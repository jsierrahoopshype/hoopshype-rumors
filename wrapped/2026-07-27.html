NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron James signs with Sixers on minimum deal, Topps breaks the news, Nuggets match Jones offer sheet **LeBron James** has officially signed a two-year, $8 million contract with the **Philadelphia 76ers**, per Shams Charania, with a player option on year two and a full 15% trade kicker attached. The deal is essentially a veteran's minimum, and ESPN's Bobby Marks notes James was not permitted to include a no-trade clause, a departure from his prior stops in Cleveland and Los Angeles, where the rule requiring four years with the signing team prevents it. Sources close to James told The Athletic the decision came down to one thing: where he thought he had the best chance to win a championship. The tipping point, according to at least three sources, was first-year president Mike Gansey's July 1 acquisition of Jaylen Brown from the Celtics for Paul George and four future picks, which turned Philadelphia from a longshot into the frontrunner in James' mind. Rich Paul called the Sixers to deliver the news late Friday morning, shortly before James and Topps posted simultaneously on social media, beating every reporter, including Charania, to the announcement. The way the news broke deserves its own sentence. Topps posted what appeared to be an already-autographed one-of-one trading card on Instagram in a coordinated post with James at 11:30 a.m. ET, before the league or any media outlet had the information. Multiple league sources told the New York Post the NBA was blindsided. The card itself was real but not yet signed; the autograph in the image was digitally modified, with the actual signature to be completed in the coming days. Here are more notes from around the league: • The reaction around the league to James joining a Sixers roster that already includes **Joel Embiid**, **Jaylen Brown** and **Tyrese Maxey** has been a mix of skepticism and grudging respect. "They do not have any role players on their team now," one rival executive told The Ringer. "Somebody will have to check their ego at the door, and I don't see Joel or Bron being the ones to do it." Both executives who raised those concerns still called the Sixers a legitimate contender, and an Eastern Conference coach added: "He's so smart he can make it work." • **Magic Johnson** offered a more bullish take, saying the Sixers are "the biggest threat to the World Champion Knicks" in the East, with four All-Stars in the starting lineup. • The Sixers are now moving pieces to clear the roster spot for James. The team has been shopping Johni Broome and may waive and stretch his salary if no trade materializes. Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, who must clear waivers first, is expected to sign once that room is confirmed. • **Stephen Curry** wanted James to come to Golden State and thought the Warriors had a real shot. His response when he heard the news? "That's why you don't envision anything until it happens. There's a lot of moving parts." Curry, 38 and entering his 18th season, is still without a contract extension, and The Athletic reports the longer that drags on, the more he appears to be using potential free agency as leverage to push the Warriors toward building a contender around him. • Warriors owner **Joe Lacob**, meanwhile, is focused on building the franchise's next era after Curry, per Tim Kawakami. That posture, combined with injuries to **Jimmy Butler** (torn ACL, January) and **Moses Moody** (left patellar tendon, March), makes the gap between Curry's goals and the front office's direction worth watching this season. • The **Denver Nuggets** are matching Oklahoma City's two-year, $12 million offer sheet on Spencer Jones, keeping the undrafted 2024 forward on a restricted deal. The match pushes Denver's luxury tax bill from $36 million to $68 million and lands them in the second apron. Their next challenge: retaining restricted free agent **Peyton Watson**, who has interest from the Hawks, Bucks and Clippers. • **Mario Hezonja** is returning to the NBA on a one-year, $2.8 million deal with the **Cleveland Cavaliers**. The former No. 5 pick hasn't played in the league since 2019-20 but shot 38.4% on catch-and-shoot threes over four seasons with Real Madrid, a significant jump from his 31.9% mark during his initial NBA run. Cleveland also had interest from Golden State before landing him. • The **Miami Heat** remain focused on **Klay Thompson**, but there is no sign when or if he shakes free from Dallas. The Mavericks have no financial incentive to facilitate a buyout since Thompson is owed $17.5 million in the final year of his deal, and he would need to take a significant cut for any departure to work. The Heat also have interest in **DeMar DeRozan**, but at least one team official wants to wait before committing one of three open roster spots. DeRozan has multiple suitors with exception money, so Miami may not have the luxury of time. • **Tyrese Haliburton** says he's essentially all the way back from his knee injury, now at the one-year mark of his recovery. "I could pretty much say I'm back at this point," he said. "There's a difference between being in shape and being in game shape," and he says playing fast again will take a little more time. • **Jayson Tatum**, speaking at a youth camp this week, said he never wants to be the smartest person in the room and doesn't want to be around anyone who thinks they know it all. The timing, one day after his former teammate Jaylen Brown signed with LeBron in Philadelphia, was not lost on the internet. • **Jonathan Kuminga** remains in free agency limbo. The sticking point, per Keith Smith, is contract length: Kuminga wants a one-year deal if he has to take less money, but a sign-and-trade requires a three-year minimum, leaving him stuck without a clear path forward out of Atlanta. • **Matas Buzelis** pushed back on the narrative that he went to the G League for money. "I knew I was going to the G League since I was 15," the Bulls forward said. "My coach kept telling me, 'You going to Ignite.' And it came to fruition." • **Karl-Anthony Towns** gave a detailed account to the New York Times of OG Anunoby's championship-winning tip-in, saying he was right next to OG ready to dunk the rebound if the ball came off the rim. "I see OG flying in the sky," Towns told the Times. "OG made it." Towns also revealed what he said to Victor Wembanyama after a big basket in the Finals: he was simply being competitive, though he acknowledged Wembanyama is "a generational player" who is "God-gifted with intangibles that none of us were able to get." • The **Trail Blazers'** broadcast team is being dismantled under new owner **Tom Dundon**. Radio play-by-play voice Travis Demers was offered a contract with a 60-plus percent pay cut, no benefits, no travel and no analyst; he turned it down. Television play-by-play man Kevin Calabro also declined to return, and analyst Michael Holton was released. Former SportsCenter anchor Neil Everett, who spent five seasons with Portland, found out he was let go by reading a sports media column in Forbes. Dundon took the same approach when he bought the NHL's Hurricanes, where longtime play-by-play announcer Chuck Kaiton left after being offered an 80% pay cut after 39 seasons. • **Dwyane Wade** opened up on a recent appearance, saying his cancer surgery at 44 has changed his perspective. "I started living a little different life, a little freer," Wade said. "I smile more. I laugh more. I appreciate more."
