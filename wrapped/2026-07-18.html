NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's free agency decision nearing, Giannis embraces Heat, NBPA targets second apron

LeBron James is closing in on a free agency decision, with sources telling ESPN that a choice could come as early as next week. Per ESPN's reporting, it is realistically down to Cleveland, Miami, and Philadelphia, with Marc Stein describing Cleveland as "the scenario to beat" while Miami and Philly remain prominently in the mix. Adam Silver made his impatience clear at Fanatics Fest in New York, saying James' destination "affects the schedule" for opening week and Christmas and that he needs a decision soon. Multiple sources familiar with James' thinking told ESPN that James is truly close to making up his mind.

The Miami pitch is gaining momentum. Brian Windhorst has been vocal about the fit, saying even the teams recruiting LeBron see Miami as the best basketball match, pointing to the open ball-handling role and the presence of Giannis and Bam as defensive cover. He added that if James wants to play two more seasons, Miami makes even more sense because it gives the Heat time to build around him. Windhorst also noted that Pat Riley has historically repaired relationships with stars who left under difficult circumstances, citing Alonzo Mourning, Shaquille O'Neal, and Dwyane Wade as examples of successful second acts.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • James used the phrase "trust the process" at a Fanatics Fest appearance, sending the crowd into a frenzy over a possible Philly clue. He also said he wants to join a franchise that shares "the same model" as himself, one built around "practicing championship habits every day."
  • James Harden said publicly "I hope he comes to Cleveland," and sources tell ScoopB.com that Harden would absolutely welcome James to the Cavs. Rich Paul told ScoopB.com of Harden, "He's actually my friend."
  • A source close to James' camp also pushed back on a report that Anthony Edwards never reached out to recruit LeBron to Minnesota. "He did. He reached out to him and it was received," the source said. Meanwhile, Edwards revealed he spoke with Karl-Anthony Towns before every game of the NBA Finals, giving Towns tips on guarding the Spurs.
  • Edwards also said LaMelo Ball has been staying at his house the last couple of days and that he plans to rush back to Minnesota after Fanatics Fest to spend more time with him. The two have apparently been working on their postgame interviews together.
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo spoke glowingly about his new home in Miami, saying his motivation to win a championship there is "at like 1,000,000% right now." He added: "It's a place I feel breaks pressure and I thrive under pressure." Pat Riley said he felt "conviction" in Giannis' voice from their first phone call, saying the conversation was "business right on that phone call."
  • Erik Spoelstra reflected on Tyler Herro's seven years in Miami following the trade that brought Giannis. "Tyler came as a teenager, and we poured everything into him," Spoelstra said. "And I think he did the same toward us."
  • The NBPA and its new executive director, David Kelly, along with president Fred VanVleet, came out swinging against the second apron at the league's Las Vegas events. Kelly argued the NBA had parity before the current system, pointing out that five different teams won titles in the five years before the 2023 CBA. The current CBA runs through 2029-30 but includes an opt-out provision a year early.
  • VanVleet told Andscape he is confident he will be ready for the Rockets' first preseason game after missing last season with a knee injury. He said he "feels like a rookie again" and is using the Knicks' 2026 championship as motivation, recalling how his 2019 title with Toronto came when "nobody paid us any attention."
  • The Spurs still view De'Aaron Fox as a core piece, multiple team and league sources say, though there is an understanding that his current contract makes him untradeable right now and that a strong playoff run next season will be key to his long-term standing. On a related note, sources say Dylan Harper is willing to accept a sixth-man role next season, following the example set by Victor Wembanyama, who passed on a supermax to give San Antonio more financial flexibility.
  • The Pistons are evaluating whether to pursue DeMar DeRozan in free agency, per Marc Stein, joining Toronto as a confirmed bidder. It is not yet certain Detroit will formally enter the race, but the Pistons have been assessing the fit this week.
  • The Nuggets are confident they can retain restricted free agent Peyton Watson and plan to let the process play out. The Bucks have emerged as a potential sign-and-trade suitor, joining the Clippers and Hawks, though Denver holds the matching rights and values Watson significantly.
  • Denver is also seen as an increasingly likely landing spot for veteran coach Dave Joerger, who interviewed with the club earlier this summer for a top assistant role under head coach David Adelman. The Nuggets are also still looking for additional frontcourt depth after losing Jonas Valanciunas, even after bringing in Marvin Bagley as a backup center option.
  • The Lakers are promoting South Bay G League coach Zach Guthrie to an assistant role and head of player development on JJ Redick's staff, per Shams Charania. Guthrie guided South Bay to the G League's best record last season.
  • The Grizzlies' Zach Edey revealed a Brandon Clarke memorial in the team's locker room. Clarke, the former Grizzlies forward, passed away earlier this year.
  • John Calipari revealed the advice he gave Karl-Anthony Towns during the NBA Finals: stop trying to draw fouls and go for and-ones instead. "They're not calling fouls so quit flopping," Calipari told Towns. "And Karl listened and it all started flowing."
