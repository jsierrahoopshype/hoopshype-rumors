NBA Rumors Wrap: Mavericks fire Kidd, Knicks stun Cavs with 44-11 run, Trail Blazers mass layoffs

The Dallas Mavericks have parted ways with head coach Jason Kidd, the team announced Tuesday, ending a five-year run that included a trip to the NBA Finals. Kidd has four years and more than $40 million remaining on his contract, but owner Patrick Dumont authorized new president of basketball operations Masai Ujiri to make the call, per Tim MacMahon. The firing caps months of tension: Kidd had sought a promotion to president of basketball operations after GM Nico Harrison was fired in November, but Dumont told him months ago that wasn't happening, and Kidd was kept out of the loop when Ujiri was hired. Sources told Marc Stein that Kidd and Ujiri had multiple conversations over the past two weeks, including a lengthy meeting with new GM Mike Schmitz, but speculation about a coaching change swirled throughout the draft combine in Chicago. The top candidates to replace Kidd are not yet clear, though San Antonio's Sean Sweeney and Minnesota's Micah Nori are both known quantities in Dallas, and sources told The Stein Line that Orlando had serious interest in Kidd back in 2021, with the same decision-makers still in place there.

Dallas also confirmed that executive Matt Riccardi, who served as co-general manager this season after Harrison's departure and was the runner-up to Ujiri in the front office search, is also departing the organization.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The New York Knicks pulled off one of the most stunning playoff comebacks in NBA history in Game 1 against the Cleveland Cavaliers, erasing a 22-point fourth-quarter deficit to win. It is the second-largest fourth-quarter comeback in the play-by-play era (since 1997) and the largest comeback win in Knicks playoff history. Landry Shamet was named by Knicks coach Mike Brown as "the difference in the ballgame on both ends of the floor" in New York's 44-11 closing run.
  • James Harden had a rough night in the loss, finishing with more turnovers (6) than made field goals (5), the 32nd time in his playoff career and the sixth time this postseason that has happened. Cleveland coach Kenny Atkinson was asked whether he considered benching Harden on defensive possessions late, and replied flatly: "No. He's been one of our best defenders in these playoffs. I trust him. Smart. Great hands. Didn't think about that."
  • The Cavs gave up 28 points off turnovers in Game 1, the sixth time they allowed 25 or more points off turnovers in a single postseason, the most such games by any team in the last 30 years. Donovan Mitchell said of preparing for Jalen Brunson and the Knicks: "No one guy can stop him. It's going to be a group effort."
  • The Portland Trail Blazers, now under new owner Tom Dundon, conducted sweeping layoffs Tuesday that affected both business and basketball operations, from senior vice presidents to PR staff to content producers. Employees learned about the cuts during a two-minute group video call before being told to check their personal email for details. Chris Mannix reported that roughly 75 people were fired, with virtually the entire PR staff let go.
  • The Bulls, Magic and Trail Blazers are all expected to make formal interview requests across their respective coaching vacancies this week. Sources tell Marc Stein that former Bulls coach Billy Donovan is in dialogue with Magic president Jeff Weltman and is viewed around the league as having an excellent shot at the Orlando job.
  • Clippers assistant Jeff Van Gundy, brother of former Magic coach Stan Van Gundy, is also on Orlando's radar, per Stein. Spurs assistant Sean Sweeney, who is also a prime candidate for the Chicago opening, is expected to interview with Orlando, though his first conversation may need to be conducted remotely given San Antonio's Western Conference finals duties. Chicago is also expected to formally request an interview with Portland interim coach Tiago Splitter, who went 42-39 and led the Blazers to their first playoff berth in five years after Chauncey Billups was placed on indefinite leave.
  • Two additional names are on Chicago's radar: the Bulls plan to seek permission to interview Charlotte assistant Lamar Skeeter and Atlanta assistant Ryan Schmidt, per Jake Fischer. Sources also say a contract extension for Atlanta coach Quin Snyder is looming, in the same range as the deal New York's Mike Brown is expected to command.
  • The Oklahoma City Thunder and San Antonio Spurs are preparing for Game 2 of the Western Conference finals. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander said Victor Wembanyama's presence is "obviously challenging," and that the key for OKC is to stay aggressive without overthinking it: "You get caught worrying about it too much, and you lose aggression." After the Spurs won Game 1 in double overtime, Wembanyama said the message from his team's performance was simple: "Our effort should be over anybody else's and tonight we were relentless."
  • Thunder coach Mark Daigneault said Chet Holmgren's underwhelming Game 1 against Wembanyama won't lock in any defensive approach for the series: "Everything is on the table. You can't just throw one pitch throughout the whole series." Teammate Luguentz Dort was direct about Holmgren's bounce-back potential: "He's a competitor. He works really hard, and he's human."
  • Detroit Pistons president Trajan Langdon expressed confidence the team will extend Jalen Duren, calling him "one of the biggest contributors to us being a No. 1 seed" and saying he looks forward to getting a deal done. Langdon also praised Cade Cunningham's toughness through a collapsed lung and fractured rib: "I thought Cade was an absolute warrior."
  • Minnesota Timberwolves president Tim Connelly said the team is optimistic about re-signing Ayo Dosunmu, calling him their most important free agent this summer: "Now seeing him day to day, we love him."
  • Draft prospect Darryn Peterson's medical evaluation remains the most important off-court piece of his pre-draft process after he dealt with serious cramping concerns attributed to high doses of creatine. Team sources said Peterson came across as "quiet but serious" in combine interviews and handled questions about his unusual season well. Michigan forward Morez Johnson Jr., who started all 40 games on a national championship team, is staying in the draft and is projected as a top-20 pick after a strong combine.
  • Oklahoma City is selling $762.4 million in revenue bonds this week to finance most of the cost of a new arena for the Thunder, backed by city sales and use tax revenue.
  • The NBA playoffs are averaging 4.5 million viewers per game across ABC, ESPN, NBC/Peacock and Amazon Prime Video through the Conference Semifinals, the highest viewership at that stage in 29 years.
  • Joe Ingles, 38, has signed a two-year deal with Melbourne United, returning to Australia's National Basketball League 17 years after leaving. "I knew, deep down, I had that itch to play," Ingles told ESPN. "I truly believe that I could come in and make an impact and help this team win games."
  • Carmelo Anthony invited Jeremy Lin on his "7PM In Brooklyn" podcast to clear the air on what he called unspecified "bullshit" between them: "Let's have a conversation, let's clear the air... Come speak the truth."
