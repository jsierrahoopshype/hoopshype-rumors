NBA Rumors Wrap: Duren weight clause removed as Pistons standoff continues, Nembhard picks Nuggets, Heat's Giannis moment

The summer-long standoff between Jalen Duren and the Detroit Pistons has cleared at least one hurdle. Per The Athletic, the weight clause that was part of Detroit's five-year, $200 million offer is no longer part of the talks, though the clause was never put in writing. The offer itself remains on the table, and Duren has not yet accepted. Detroit has continued to refuse sign-and-trade discussions with interested teams, including the Sacramento Kings and Milwaukee Bucks, the two most seriously interested parties. The Kings have not spoken to the Pistons since late June, per the report, and separately, the Washington Wizards have confirmed interest in a Duren-for-Anthony Davis swap.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Ryan Nembhard is heading to Denver. The guard agreed to a two-way contract with the Nuggets after receiving multiple two-way offers, per Shams Charania. To make room, Denver is expected to waive KJ Simpson, one of three current two-ways on the roster. Nembhard averaged 6.6 points and 5.3 assists in 60 games for Dallas last season.
  • • Erik Spoelstra reflected on the Giannis Antetokounmpo acquisition at Heat training camp. "It's surreal seeing Antetokounmpo in a Miami Heat jersey but I'm sure it's surreal for him also," Spoelstra said. "He really wanted to trust a place that would be dead ass serious about trying to win just like he is." Spoelstra gave GM Andy Elisburg credit for making it happen, saying Elisburg "went to work this summer" and "was in his bag."
  • • Kyrie Irving has a new potential trade destination. Jake Fischer reported on Bleacher Report that the Miami Heat have already shown interest in acquiring the Mavericks guard, calling Miami one of "the early spots that people around the league are looking at as potential Kyrie Irving trade destinations."
  • • MSG Sports will spin off the New York Knicks and Rangers into separate publicly traded companies, with the transaction set to close October 26. The Knicks entity will be called MSG Knickerbockers Corp. and will include the Westchester Knicks. Former team president and head coach Isiah Thomas will join the MSG Knickerbockers Corp. board of directors. Former Knicks president Steve Mills will sit on the Rangers side board.
  • • Miles McBride said he wants to finish his career with the Knicks. "I love New York. I want to continue my career here and end my career here," McBride said, adding that he will let his agency and the front office handle extension talks. Coach Mike Brown said there will "probably be a time and place" he addresses the situation with McBride directly, adding "we all want what's best not just for ourselves but for our family as well as the organization." New York also made a series of roster moves, waiving John Konchar and N'Faly Dante while signing Jaden Akins to a two-way deal and Tony Bradley to a camp deal.
  • • Kawhi Leonard told reporters at Raptors camp that he is close to being fully active. "I'm there pretty much. We're just taking our time to ramp-up," Leonard said. He acknowledged not being able to do much over the summer because his trade was in a "hold pattern."
  • • Tyrese Maxey said he wants to be judged by winning from here on out. "I've showed what I can do talent-wise," Maxey told ESPN. "I want to be judged by winning. If we win, that's what I want to be judged by." New Sixers addition Dean Wade said the way his teammates think the game is the most impressive thing he has encountered. "You get on a team like this and it's night and day being on this side with them instead of against them," Wade said.
  • • Steve Kerr said transition defense is a main focal point for the Warriors this season. "We were very poor in transition last year," Kerr said, adding that new defensive coordinator Frank Vogel is running that end. Kerr has also made clear he plans to tie most of Draymond Green's minutes to Stephen Curry this season. Last year, that pairing posted a +105 net rating in 889 minutes together.
  • • JJ Redick said the Lakers are intentionally splitting Luka Doncic and Austin Reaves in training camp drills while focusing on early offense over isolation matchups. Redick acknowledged that losing LeBron James and Rui Hachimura leaves real gaps, but expects the team to attack the paint more, shoot more threes, and generate more second chances. Reaves, for his part, said trying to score against the team's new defensive personnel in practice "is not fun."
  • • Timberwolves coach Chris Finch praised the NBA for taking a harder stance on flopping. "Hopefully the league cleans up this crap, because nobody wants to watch it," Finch said. He added: "Just don't reward these non-basketball actions. Those of us who have been in the sport long enough know what it is and what it isn't."
  • • Norman Powell set the tone for his leadership role with the Bulls on media day. "I'm not worried about what the future looks like and teams and trades," Powell said. "My job is to be here. I want to be here." Coach Tiago Splitter said Powell and the Bulls' shooters create gravity that opens up the paint for younger players like Caleb Wilson, Matas Buzelis, and the rest of the core.
  • • The NBA appears to be making concrete moves toward a European expansion. Deputy commissioner Mark Tatum attended the European Football Clubs annual conference in Copenhagen, hosted by Paris Saint-Germain president Nasser Al-Khelaifi. Next week in Italy, the EuroLeague will hold a shareholder meeting to consider an NBA proposal to acquire the league and merge it with NBA Europe.
  • • A Senate hearing took an unexpected NBA detour Tuesday. Sen. Eric Schmitt accused former special counsel Jack Smith of perjury for denying he attended an Atlanta Hawks game in 2024. Smith said the only game he recalled attending around that time was a Maryland-Iowa college game featuring Caitlin Clark. Sen. Amy Klobuchar then confirmed the date matched that college game and chided Schmitt for apparently confusing the Hawks with the Hawkeyes.
  • • Klay Thompson said he wants to prove he can still defend at a high level with the Heat. "I want to be a plus defender. I want to guard 2-5 effectively," Thompson said. "Marking the best player all night isn't my forte anymore but I can do it in spurts."
  • • Julius Randle had measured words for his former team after the Knicks won a title. "I'm happy. I had a lot of great moments there," Randle said from Nets camp. "It was a great thing not just for them, for the city. We plan to do the same thing here."
