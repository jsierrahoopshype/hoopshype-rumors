NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard nearing Clippers exit, Jalen Duren meetings set, LeBron James free agency muddied

Kawhi Leonard's time with the Los Angeles Clippers appears to be ending. Per Shams Charania, trade talks between the Clippers and the Toronto Raptors are serious and continuing, with Leonard never having been offered a contract extension by Los Angeles. The Raptors are willing to offer Leonard the two-year, $126.1 million max extension he's seeking, and Ramona Shelburne reported that Leonard is comfortable returning to Toronto, where he won a championship and never burned any bridges. A potential deal structure floated by Bobby Marks would send Leonard to Toronto along with Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick and three first-round picks heading to the Clippers. One thing the Raptors have made clear: second-year wing Ja'Kobe Walter and rookie Colin Murray-Boyles would not be included in any such deal.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Jaylen Brown trade market is active but contradictory. Shams Charania described a "stark variance" between Brown's value as a player, the weight of his supermax contract with three years remaining, and the assets teams have available. Marc Stein reported that the Clippers have not pursued Brown this offseason and are not planning to, regardless of how the Kawhi situation resolves. Charania also said Portland is not currently involved in Brown talks, calling the Morant trade "their big move."
  • The Portland Trail Blazers now have four point guards on the roster after acquiring Ja Morant from Memphis for Jerami Grant and Kris Murray. The team told The Athletic it does not feel it's essential to move one of Morant, Lillard, Jrue Holiday or Scoot Henderson, believing Holiday can shift to shooting guard to ease the congestion. Morant told the Blazers shortly after the trade that he's excited to be in Portland. One executive from another team called the deal "confusing" given the existing guard depth.
  • Donovan Clingan is essentially off limits in trade discussions following the Morant deal, per a team source. Portland is still looking to add big-man depth and has interest in retaining Robert Williams III, Matisse Thybulle and Blake Wesley from its group of unrestricted free agents.
  • Jalen Duren has meetings scheduled with the Sacramento Kings and Los Angeles Lakers when free agency opens Tuesday, with the Kings visit expected after Duren signaled his intention to pursue a sign-and-trade out of Detroit following a breakdown in contract talks. The Detroit Pistons maintain they do not intend to trade the 22-year-old and have a high degree of internal confidence he re-signs. The Lakers' meeting is expected to be by phone; Luka Dončić is known to be a fan of Duren's game, and the Lakers' best offering would likely be draft capital rather than players.
  • The LeBron James free agency picture remains genuinely murky. The Warriors are operating without any assumption that James and Anthony Davis are a package deal, per Marc Stein, and would try to sign James for around $15 million if he becomes available. Bobby Marks noted the math is tight: Golden State is $27 million below the first apron and will bring back Draymond Green, meaning unless LeBron wants to come on the minimum, the numbers don't work for a $15 million offer. Meanwhile, the Lakers have said almost nothing publicly, with no hints given even to players on the current roster. Windhorst summed up the league's prevailing skepticism: most people he's spoken to expect Draymond to re-sign in Golden State, LeBron to re-sign in LA and Davis to re-sign in Washington.
  • On the Anthony Davis front, Windhorst reported that Davis's representatives and the Wizards met Monday to discuss a possible contract extension, though no deal can be signed until August. Davis has one guaranteed year remaining plus a player option. Bobby Marks raised the question of Washington's incentive to trade him at all: the worst-case scenario for the Warriors would be giving up assets for Davis and then not landing LeBron.
  • Kristaps Porzingis agreed to a two-year, $40 million extension to stay with the Warriors through 2027-28, with a player option in year two. That deal likely prevents Golden State from using its full non-taxpayer mid-level exception, adding another layer to the LeBron math. Green is expected to return on a new multi-year deal, with the specifics to be worked out after other offseason business is settled.
  • James Harden declined his $42 million mutual option with the Cleveland Cavaliers, a move Bobby Marks called a no-brainer given the structure. Marks expects Harden to come back on a deal in the $32-33 million range with a second year attached to make up the difference.
  • Warriors lottery pick Yaxel Lendeborg said his friends spent the day texting him asking how he would fit alongside Steph, Draymond, LeBron and AD.
  • Harrison Barnes intends to sign a one-year, $8 million deal to stay with the San Antonio Spurs for a third season. The Spurs valued his leadership and production throughout the season.
  • The Indiana Pacers are targeting swingmen to support the returning Tyrese Haliburton, with Josh Okogie, Gary Trent Jr. and Kelly Oubre Jr. all on their list. Indiana can offer part of the non-taxpayer mid-level to compete for those players against minimum offers from other teams.
  • The Washington Wizards have a level of interest in Russell Westbrook, per Marc Stein. Westbrook last played for Washington in 2020-21, when he averaged 22.2 points, 11.5 rebounds and 11.7 assists.
  • The Brooklyn Nets declined qualifying offers to Ochai Agbaji and Jalen Wilson, making both unrestricted free agents, per HoopsHype. The New York Knicks are also not extending a qualifying offer to center Ariel Hukporti, cutting him loose as an unrestricted free agent after two seasons.
  • The Atlanta Hawks declined Jonathan Kuminga's team option Monday, making the 23-year-old an unrestricted free agent. The Kings, who tried and failed to acquire him via sign-and-trade last offseason, are not expected to pursue him this time around. The Lakers have been linked to a wide range of names, including Sandro Mamukelashvili, the Toronto forward/center who declined his $2.8 million option and could command $10 million or more in free agency.
  • The Cavaliers and Dean Wade are in active negotiations heading into Tuesday's free agency opening, with neither side closing the door on a return to Cleveland.
