NBA Rumors Wrap: Kuminga picks Wolves over Lakers, Jeanie Buss files court petition, Mathurin lands in New Orleans

Jonathan Kuminga has agreed to a two-year, $13 million deal with the Minnesota Timberwolves, including a player option, per Shams Charania. Kuminga will slot into the starting lineup alongside Anthony Edwards, LaMelo Ball, Jaden McDaniels and Rudy Gobert. He passed on a three-year, $12 million-plus-per-year offer from the Lakers that would have included a similar starting role, choosing instead a shorter deal in Minnesota that gives him more control over his future. President Tim Connelly traveled to Miami for a four-hour in-person meeting with Kuminga earlier this month, and Edwards was a central part of the recruiting push. To make the money work and stay under the second apron, the Wolves will likely need to trade or stretch veteran Josh Green, who is owed $14.6 million.

The deal is a significant roster piece for a Wolves team that spent the offseason searching for a prototypical power forward after trading Julius Randle and Naz Reid. Minnesota beat out the Lakers, Bulls and Trail Blazers for Kuminga's services, a recruiting win that came together quickly once Connelly and the coaching staff got face time with the forward.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Jeanie Buss has petitioned a Los Angeles Superior Court to block her five siblings from selling the family's remaining stake in the Lakers, accusing them of secretly engineering a deal to remove her as the team's governor. The petition was processed Wednesday and is the clearest signal yet that the Buss family ownership dispute is heading toward a prolonged legal fight.
  • Mark Walter's TWG Global pushed back hard Wednesday against reports connecting his companies to federal investigations, saying it has committed no fraud and insisting the Dodgers are not for sale. TWG called recent media coverage the product of "unnamed sources with self-serving interests" and said the proposed $12.5 billion Lakers sale represents a 25% premium over what Walter paid less than a year ago, hardly a fire sale.
  • Bennedict Mathurin has agreed to a two-year, $16 million deal with the New Orleans Pelicans, including a player option, per Shams Charania. The Pelicans pursued Mathurin for over a year and view him as a core piece. New Orleans plans to use the non-taxpayer mid-level exception to complete the signing, which will hard cap the team, meaning they may need to shed payroll first to get under the first apron threshold.
  • Getting Mathurin out of Los Angeles required the Clippers to withdraw his $8.8 million qualifying offer, something the team was initially reluctant to do. The Clippers ultimately agreed to work with Mathurin and New Orleans to facilitate the move, turning the restricted free agent situation into a more straightforward signing.
  • Klay Thompson said Miami was always on his radar after requesting a buyout from Dallas. "Miami was always on my mind," Thompson said. "They were great in honoring our request and understanding that those things did happen." Thompson added that the Heat were simply better positioned for a deep postseason run.
  • Draymond Green defended Thompson against critics who argue he could never have been a No. 1 option. "Everyone tries to make Klay the guy Klay was after the injuries," Green said. "I won't go for that." Green maintains there was a 90% chance Thompson was going to leave Golden State after the 2019 playoffs to become a No. 1 option somewhere before the knee injury changed everything.
  • Thompson himself said the adjustment to age and new roles has been intentional. "As you get older, you train differently," he told HoopsHype. "With Coach Erik Spoelstra and his staff, I was just really attracted to the fact that they built such a well-rounded roster."
  • Former 76ers coach Brett Brown praised the franchise's current direction after LeBron James signed in Philadelphia. "I think it's fantastic for the city," Brown said. "I think it's an endorsement to the players and Nick Nurse that LeBron chose them. They've got a team that is as good as anybody in the league."
  • 76ers president Bob Myers opened up about the Jaylen Brown trade from Boston's perspective, saying the process was anything but sudden. "It just that's not what happened. You start hearing there was a possibility, and the job then became, 'Is this a real thing?'" Myers said, crediting Celtics counterpart Brad Stevens for his honesty. When Stevens responded to an initial feeler with "What are you offering?", Myers knew a deal was possible.
  • Per HoopsHype, the Orlando Magic and Malaki Branham have agreed to a deal, per agent Deirunas Visockas of Gershon Deangelis Basketball. Branham, the 20th pick of the 2022 draft, averaged 8.0 points across four seasons with San Antonio and Washington.
  • The Denver Nuggets and Bryce Hopkins have agreed to a two-way deal, according to his agents at Priority Sports. Hopkins was the 49th pick of the 2026 draft and a two-time All-Big East selection at Providence and St. John's.
  • The Houston Rockets signed center Rafael Castro to a two-way contract and waived Tristen Newton to make room. Castro, a redshirt senior out of George Washington, measured at a 7-foot-1.25-inch wingspan and 9-foot-1.5-inch standing reach at the combine.
  • The Warriors will wear a "NELLIE" band on their jerseys this season to honor late coach Don Nelson, who died Aug. 9. Portland and Sacramento announced similar tributes for fellow Hall of Famer Rick Adelman.
  • Clippers guard Jordan Miller underwent surgery Wednesday to repair a torn left rotator cuff suffered during an offseason workout and will be reevaluated in six months.
  • Campbell Hall, the Studio City private school where former Lakers player Byron Scott allegedly sexually assaulted a 15-year-old student in 1987, has reached a confidential settlement with the woman. The settlement amount remains sealed until Scott's civil trial begins Sept. 8.
  • Joey and Jesse Buss announced their investment firm, Buss Sports Capital, has joined the San Diego Padres' new ownership group led by Kwanza Jones and Jose E. Feliciano. The brothers started the firm after their family sold its majority Lakers stake to Mark Walter last year.
  • Blake Griffin is launching a weekly late-night show called The Stepback with Blake Griffin on Prime Video and YouTube, produced with Amazon's Wondery. The show will feature Griffin behind a desk covering major sports topics.
  • A 1997 Ferrari once owned by Michael Jordan, famously seen with Jordan behind the wheel after the Bulls clinched their fifth championship in Game 6 of the 1997 Finals, will go to auction this September after disappearing from public view for over two decades.
