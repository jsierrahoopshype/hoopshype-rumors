NBA Rumors Wrap: Heat's Giannis offer is real, court-stormer banned for life, Knicks-Spurs Finals notes

The Milwaukee Bucks are weighing a significant trade offer for Giannis Antetokounmpo, and the Miami Heat are leading the charge. The Heat's offer is described as "player and pick heavy," which aligns with what Milwaukee has reportedly asked for in any potential deal. "The Heat thing is real. The Heat offer is real," said Jim Owczarski of the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel. "I think to me that's the best package." The Bucks have not yet moved off Giannis, but the Heat's willingness to part with both players and selections makes their bid stand out from the field. What happens next depends on whether Milwaukee decides this summer is the moment to move on from their franchise cornerstone.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The NBA issued a lifetime ban to the individual who entered the court during Game 1 of the Finals in San Antonio, who was also arrested. A second person received a lifetime ban for his role in the incident. Bexar County Sheriff's Office told TMZ Sports the court-stormer was a juvenile, charged with disrupting a lawful gathering and intentionally breaching court security.
  • The NBA is also looking into two courtside fans who allegedly directed vulgar "flopping" remarks at Jalen Brunson late in Game 1. Brunson met with official Scott Foster after the game to report the behavior, at which point the remarks reportedly continued.
  • Mitchell Robinson is listed as probable for Friday's Game 2 with a fractured fifth metacarpal in his right hand, per HoopsHype. Robinson's availability gives the Knicks a meaningful frontcourt boost as the series shifts to a must-win spot for San Antonio.
  • Victor Wembanyama put up 26 points, 12 rebounds and three blocks in his Finals debut, joining Shaquille O'Neal (1995), Hakeem Olajuwon (1986) and Elvin Hayes (1975) as the only players to reach that line in a Finals opener since blocks became official in 1974. Still, Bill Simmons and Doc Rivers both felt Wembanyama looked off. "Wemby looks sped up tonight," Rivers texted Simmons during the first half. "I think he had 6 or 7 turnovers." Spurs coach Mitch Johnson said the team needs to get him "moving in space and toward the rim."
  • De'Aaron Fox addressed his reduced shot volume in the Finals. "Coming here, I knew that was the way it was going to be," he said. "As a player, you sacrifice for the betterment of the team."
  • President Donald Trump confirmed he will attend Game 3 at Madison Square Garden next Monday after an invitation from Knicks owner James Dolan. "The answer is yes," Trump said. "I'll be there. It could be Monday. Maybe I'll do both." Commissioner Adam Silver said Trump is "of course" welcome, recalling the president's long history as a Knicks fan and his past appearances at NBA Draft ceremonies at MSG.
  • Get-in prices for Game 3 at MSG have jumped to roughly $8,200 as of Thursday afternoon, nearly double the $4,200 figure from three days earlier and more than triple the market level from three weeks ago.
  • Former Knicks star Charles Oakley said he has no plans to attend the Finals at MSG due to his ongoing dispute with Dolan. "He started it, he can make it go away," Oakley said of Dolan. "He's the boss." Oakley added that he has enjoyed watching Brunson's leadership: "Even though me and the owner are in a dispute, I hope the team has success, especially for the city of New York."
  • ESPN confirmed to Front Office Sports that it used artificial intelligence to create a moving portrait of Spurs legend Tony Parker during Wednesday's Game 1 broadcast, along with two other similar images. The network said it is "evaluating" whether to continue using the technology in Game 2. Parker, for his part, had only praise for Wembanyama this week: "For me, he's the best player in the NBA, and by far."
  • The Dallas Mavericks coaching search is taking shape. Marc Stein reports that Dallas has registered or plans to register interest in assistants including Minnesota's Micah Nori, Houston's Royal Ivey, Toronto's Jama Mahlalela, Boston's Tony Dobbins and Miami consultant Noah LaRoche, along with veteran coach Terry Stotts, who has a prior Dallas connection through former owner Mark Cuban. Nori, though widely coveted, said Tuesday he has had no contact from Dallas.
  • The Portland Trail Blazers have narrowed their head coaching search to three finalists: Nori, Boston's Tyler Lashbrook and acting coach Tiago Splitter. The Clippers' Jeff Van Gundy and Utah's Mike Williams did not advance from the earlier group of five.
  • The Chicago Bulls are expected to identify coaching finalists by early next week. Incumbent assistant Wes Unseld Jr., who has prior head coaching experience in Washington, is among those expected to be in that final group.
  • NBC averaged 7.2 million viewers for its return to NBA Playoffs coverage this season, up 72% over the comparable coverage last year. The Spurs-Thunder Game 7 in the Western Conference Finals drew 15.9 million viewers, the best conference finals audience since Warriors-Thunder on the same network.
  • A lawsuit seeking more than $3 million from Charlotte's LaMelo Ball is moving closer to trial. The suit stems from allegations that Ball injured a child's foot while leaving Spectrum Center following a 2023 team scrimmage.
  • The Houston Rockets unveiled new uniforms Thursday honoring the franchise's classic red-and-yellow look from its back-to-back championship era in the 1990s. "Ketchup and mustard is back," said Rockets vice chairman Patrick Fertitta.
