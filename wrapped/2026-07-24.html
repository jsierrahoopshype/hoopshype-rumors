NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's decision still a mystery, Heat emerge as frontrunners, Duren standoff nears a flashpoint

LeBron James remains unsigned, and by all accounts, no one knows when that changes, including James himself. Sources close to him told The Athletic that reports suggesting he had made up his mind, changed it, and was back to square one were "a lie," and that he is not waiting on any other roster moves to fall into place. Shams Charania confirmed as much, saying LeBron and agent Rich Paul are "not waiting for another domino right now" and not conditioning anything on an Anthony Davis trade. The field, per Shams, is down to five teams: Cleveland, Miami, Golden State, Philadelphia and Minnesota. One source put it plainly: "Nobody knows right now except LeBron, and I'm not even sure LeBron knows."

Miami has pulled ahead in the LeBron sweepstakes, at least according to one outlet with a track record worth watching. Sources told Dallas Hoops Journal that Klutch Sports initially believed it would be "super easy" to get Cleveland or Golden State to trade for Davis, making a package deal feasible. Neither team wants to pay Davis max money, sources said, and no other LeBron suitor has expressed interest in Davis either. Since Miami already has Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo, it doesn't need to add Davis, and sources say both Giannis and Bam have been recruiting LeBron hard.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Jalen Duren restricted free agency standoff with the Pistons may be more balanced than it looks. The Athletic polled 13 rival front office members on what a fair deal would look like, with answers ranging from $180 million to $230 million over five years. Detroit has the edge in that no other team has cap space to offer a big contract, but Duren can always accept the $9.6 million qualifying offer and walk as an unrestricted free agent next summer. "While Detroit might look like they have most of the leverage, given that there are no cap-space teams out there, they're royally f------ if he takes the qualifying offer and walks next year," one respondent said. "So, I think leverage is relatively even."
  • • The Clippers want to re-sign Bennedict Mathurin outright but have not ruled out a sign-and-trade if a deal with him directly falls through, per Marc Stein. That flexibility separates their approach from Detroit's harder line on Duren.
  • • Jordan Walsh is staying in Boston. The Celtics agreed to a three-year, $16 million extension with the forward, with two years fully guaranteed and a third-year team option for the 2029-30 season. Boston also picked up Walsh's $2.4 million option for the upcoming season.
  • • Cleveland is casting a wide net in free agency. The Cavaliers have registered interest in former No. 5 overall pick Mario Hezonja, the Croatian forward who has spent the last four seasons with Real Madrid. Hezonja reportedly activated his NBA exit clause before Monday's deadline and turned down backup offers from three EuroLeague clubs, signaling he fully expects to land in the NBA. Jonathan Kuminga remains another option Cleveland has been tracking, and Lakers GM Rob Pelinka has reportedly been calling about Kuminga as well, per Anthony Slater.
  • • Giannis spoke openly about his trade request from Milwaukee, saying he was scared of how Bucks fans would react. "I thought people would say 'after 13 years? You were protected by us, you were loved here,'" he said. He also acknowledged the adjustment ahead: "In Miami now, I won't lie, I don't feel comfortable. I don't know the team, but I'm going over there and I will do my best." He added, "I love the city of Milwaukee. I will love Milwaukee until I die. I bleed purple and green."
  • • The Warriors are open to giving Stephen Curry a one- or two-year max extension, per Anthony Slater. Golden State wants to preserve long-term flexibility but is treating Curry as a clear exception to that principle. On a related note, Warriors rookie Yaxel Lendeborg said Steve Kerr has already told him he might start next season, a notable signal given Kerr's history of limiting rookie minutes.
  • • The Pelicans have been exploring trade frameworks to move Jordan Hawkins for roster and financial flexibility, with the Grizzlies among the teams that have registered interest. No deal was in place as of Wednesday night.
  • • The Knicks return nine of their top 10 playoff rotation players but at least one voice inside the organization is tempering expectations. A team source flagged the possibility of a championship hangover and noted that New York's league-leading postseason three-point shooting could regress next spring. On a lighter note, Andre Drummond publicly questioned why Jalen Brunson wasn't the cover athlete for NBA 2K27.
  • • Chauncey Billups remains among the defendants in the federal rigged poker game case. Prosecutors have asked to move the trial from November 2, 2026 to early 2027, citing ongoing plea negotiations with at least seven defendants. Billups' lawyer is among those not consenting to the request to relocate the trial.
  • • The Blazers are in the middle of a stadium funding timeline and a front office transition. A county vote on committing roughly $100 million toward a Moda Center renovation has been delayed to August 6, with a more consequential Portland City Council vote on the term sheet set for August 12. Separately, longtime Blazers play-by-play voice Kevin Calabro said the team made him an offer so underwhelming that it was "not hard at all to turn thumbs down on." He said the offer was low on compensation per game, limited in games covered, and came with no benefits, telling Willamette Week the team showed "not much seriousness on the other end to get a deal done."
  • • The Pacers GM Chad Buchanan explained the team's offseason wing and frontcourt additions. On Kelly Oubre, Buchanan said Indiana believes last season was his best shooting year and that he'll see more open looks in their system. On Larry Nance Jr., Buchanan said the 33-year-old was Indiana's "best option that was gettable" at frontcourt, adding that Nance specifically targeted the Pacers and is "very motivated" after a down year.
  • • Hornets coach Charles Lee spoke glowingly about new addition Naz Reid, calling him a "big-time get" and praising his size, physicality and experience in the Western Conference Finals. Lee also offered positive early reviews of summer league performers Hannes Steinbach and Christian Anderson, saying Anderson's defensive competitiveness had exceeded expectations.
  • • Lamar Odom pleaded no contest to one misdemeanor count of DUI in Las Vegas. Under the deal, prosecutors dismissed the accompanying speeding and improper lane change charges.
  • • Ja Morant is launching a new Nike signature sneaker called the "Twelve Time" this September, priced at $90. "Growing up, not everybody could always get the top shoe," Morant said. "I want more kids to be able to wear my stuff, hoop in it, feel like they part of it."
  • • A research paper by two Texas Tech professors found that players attempted last-second heaves 94% of the time last season after the NBA stopped counting them as missed field goals, up from 58% in the prior decade. The biggest behavioral shift came among efficient shooters who previously had the most to lose from a missed attempt on their stat line.
