NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard tops trade wish lists, Kyrie-Flagg pairing faces skepticism, Knicks sweep Cleveland

Kawhi Leonard and Giannis Antetokounmpo are running neck-and-neck as the most coveted players on the hypothetical trade market, according to a HoopsHype survey of rival executives. With the Clippers trending toward a youth movement following recent transactions, league offices are watching closely to see whether Leonard, who turns 35 in June, signs an extension or becomes available. The competition at the top of that list speaks to the scarcity of All-NBA-caliber players who could realistically change teams this offseason. Leonard's decision, expected to come before the start of free agency, could reshape the Western Conference.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Rival executives around the league are skeptical of how long Kyrie Irving and Cooper Flagg can co-exist in Dallas. Mavericks president Masai Ujiri has been publicly bullish on the pairing, saying the organization "owes it" to itself to see how Irving fits alongside Flagg, and new GM Mike Schmitz called it "a match made in Heaven." Irving, a nine-time All-Star who just turned 34 and is returning from a torn left ACL, gives the Mavericks limited runway to prove the fit works.
  • Austin Reaves made his intentions clear when asked by TMZ at LAX whether he wanted to return to the Lakers: "Run it back." He also said he hopes LeBron James stays in Los Angeles, though he noted he needs to sort out his own situation first. Reaves will become an unrestricted free agent after declining his $14.9 million player option for next season.
  • How LeBron's decision lands could complicate the Lakers' roster construction beyond his own contract. HoopsHype's Jovan Buha projects LeBron expects to be paid in the $35-45 million range given his All-Star season and first-round playoff performance. If the Lakers bring back LeBron, Reaves and Luka Doncic as the top three, Rui Hachimura may be the odd man out, as the five-man lineup featuring Hachimura and Deandre Ayton was one of the worst high-minute units in the league last season.
  • The New York Knicks are one win away from a sweep after winning their 10th straight game, tied for the fifth-longest win streak in NBA playoff history and the longest for the franchise in any format since 2013. Jalen Brunson led all scorers with 30 points in Game 3's 121-108 win in Cleveland. The crowd inside Rocket Arena included enough Knicks fans in blue and orange that a "Let's Go Knicks" chant broke out on the road.
  • Donovan Mitchell is dealing with normal postseason wear and tear, per Cavs coach Kenny Atkinson, who spoke with Mitchell after the game. Mitchell told Atkinson it's "normal bumps and bruises, wear and tear" with no formal injury designation.
  • The Thunder bench has been historically dominant this postseason. Jared McCain scored a playoff career-high 24 points off the bench in Game 3 against San Antonio, part of a night where Oklahoma City's reserves put up 76 bench points, the most in a conference finals or Finals game since starters began being tracked in 1971. The Thunder bench has now topped 50 bench points in three straight playoff games, tied for the second-longest such streak in a single postseason in that same span.
  • McCain, who was traded from the 76ers, showed no bitterness toward former GM Daryl Morey when asked about the deal. "It's never to prove anybody wrong," McCain told HoopsHype's Michael Scotto. "Daryl's still the guy that drafted me, so I'll always have love for him for that."
  • Spurs guard Devin Vassell got in Ajay Mitchell's face during Game 3 after Mitchell's hard foul sent Stephon Castle sprawling to the court on a dunk attempt, drawing a Flagrant 1 and technicals on both players. "Just having my teammate's back," Vassell said. "That's my brother." Castle echoed the sentiment, saying the team's reaction was natural: "We're a pretty close group and brothers do that. It's in our nature to stand up for each other. But we have to move on and keep our composure throughout the game."
  • The Spurs will get both De'Aaron Fox and Dylan Harper back for Game 4 on Sunday, per coach Mitch Johnson. Their availability matters beyond this series: Johnson described Fox, Castle and Harper as San Antonio's "three-headed downhill monster" on offense, and with two of the three playing through pain, the Spurs have had limited ability to convert the advantages Victor Wembanyama creates. Mitchell has been ruled out for Game 4 with a right soleus strain; Jalen Williams is questionable with left hamstring soreness.
  • Per HoopsHype, Tiago Splitter is expected to be among Portland's finalists for their head coaching job, and the Bulls also have interest in the Blazers interim coach. The Bulls are simultaneously interviewing Heat assistant Chris Quinn for the vacancy and considering Cavaliers associate head coach Johnnie Bryant, who was a finalist for Phoenix's job before Jordan Ott was hired. Portland also quietly interviewed Hawks assistant Bryan Bailey and reached out to Cleveland for permission to speak with assistant coach Omar Cook.
  • The 76ers front office search is taking shape. Bob Myers is expected to remain involved in all major transactional decisions even after a new basketball operations leader is hired, and coach Nick Nurse has two years left on his contract. External candidates include Cleveland's Mike Gansey, Minnesota's Matt Lloyd and Phoenix Mercury GM Nick U'Ren, while assistant GM Jameer Nelson has positioned himself well for a potential internal promotion.
  • The Lakers are casting a wide net for their own front office openings, expressing interest in candidates including Timberwolves assistant GM Steve Senior, 76ers assistant GM Prosper Karangwa, Heat VP of player personnel Eric Amsler and Jazz VP Bart Taylor. Karangwa is being pursued by both the Lakers and Mavericks, who are also eyeing Toronto's director of global scouting Patrick Englebrecht, a former Ujiri collaborator since the 2013-14 season.
  • The Hawks hold the eighth pick after landing it via the Pelicans in the Derik Queen trade, and rival executives expect Atlanta to go either point guard (Keaton Wagler, Mikel Brown Jr., Darius Acuff or Kingston Flemings) or Michigan 7-foot-3 center Aday Mara. The Kings are high on Acuff at seventh overall, though the question is whether he'll still be available when they pick.
  • The NBA's draft lottery reform proposal has been sent to owners ahead of a May 28 vote. Under the "3-2-1" plan, all 16 picks are drawn in the lottery, the three worst teams can fall no lower than 12th, and Adam Silver gains expanded powers to punish tanking. A new wrinkle: no team can land first in two consecutive drafts or in the top five in three straight drafts, with the clock starting retroactively from the 2025 lottery. That means if Washington wins the lottery again in 2027, it would automatically be bumped to second.
  • God Shammgod has agreed to join the Pelicans as a front-of-bench assistant under new head coach Jamahl Mosley. The rest of Mosley's staff is also coming together, with holdovers Greg Monroe, Jodie Meeks, Mike Hopkins and Will Bynum expected to remain, plus Dale Osbourne and Bret Brielmaier, both Mosley carryovers from Orlando, as strong candidates to join him in New Orleans.
