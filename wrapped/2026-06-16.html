NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade talks intensify, Bulls hire Splitter, Kessler at odds with Jazz

Giannis Antetokounmpo wants to go to the Miami Heat, and the league increasingly believes a deal will get done before next week's draft, per Brian Windhorst. Windhorst reported that talks have intensified over the last seven to ten days and that Giannis has focused on wanting to be in Miami, though the Bucks have not loved what the Heat are offering and are actively seeking other teams to join or improve the package. A Giannis associate confirmed to the Miami Herald's Barry Jackson that Antetokounmpo "absolutely" wants to go to the Heat, though Jackson added it is not Miami-and-nowhere-else, similar to the dynamic that played out with Damian Lillard. Boston is also in the mix, with Windhorst reporting Giannis has expressed willingness to extend in Boston as well, though the Celtics are expected to be very cautious before making any firm offer. Marc Stein wrote that the window between the Finals and the draft is when this trade is most likely to happen, and that any acquiring team needs Giannis's signature on an extension six months after the deal closes.

Stein also reported that Trae Young has emerged as a backup option for Miami if Boston, Portland or another team outbids the Heat for Giannis, alongside Kawhi Leonard and Ja Morant as other "big fish" targets Pat Riley has been considering. On the Bucks' side, Marc Stein reported the working presumption around the league is that Milwaukee would prefer to move Jaylen Brown for assets rather than keep him after a Giannis trade, given how many picks they already surrendered in the Lillard deal, which is a big reason Portland keeps coming up as a potential third team.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Chicago Bulls are hiring Tiago Splitter as their new head coach, per HoopsHype. Splitter, 41, went 42-40 and led the Portland Trail Blazers to a playoff berth as interim coach last season after replacing Chauncey Billups, and he succeeds Billy Donovan in Chicago.
  • Walker Kessler is at odds with the Utah Jazz front office over the handling of his restricted free agency. Kessler has long wanted to stay in Utah and was already frustrated when the Jazz declined to offer him an extension last summer. Despite that friction, the expectation around the league is still that Utah will match any offer sheet he receives.
  • Washington Wizards center Alex Sarr underwent surgery to repair a fractured right foot suffered during an offseason workout. He is expected to be healthy for the start of next season.
  • The Wizards have narrowed their No. 1 focus to AJ Dybantsa and Darryn Peterson. Shams Charania reported that Dybantsa has visited both Washington and the Jazz at No. 2, while Peterson only visited the Wizards and does not plan to grant anyone else a meeting, with his camp said to be "very confident they are going No. 1."
  • Peterson's medicals have not raised major concerns with teams, keeping him firmly in the top-two conversation despite a difficult season at Kansas dealing with cramping issues. An NBA draft analyst for The Ringer said he has heard the Jazz like Peterson and that Jazz GM Danny Ainge specifically prefers him.
  • James Harden's arraignment date has been rescheduled from June 22 to August 3. Harden was released on bond Saturday morning and has hired Texas attorney Rusty Hardin, who has previously represented several high-profile athletes, to handle his case. The expectation around the league remains that Harden and the Cavaliers will agree to a multiyear deal that brings down his $42.3 million player option salary.
  • The Oklahoma City Thunder have received trade calls from rival teams about Chet Holmgren and have told those teams he is not available. Holmgren is entering the first year of a five-year, $239 million rookie scale extension.
  • Jalen Duren is expected to command more than $30 million per year in restricted free agency. The Pistons are expected to match any offer, though a big number will limit Detroit's future roster flexibility.
  • The Rockets have not been mentioned much as a player in the Giannis sweepstakes, and that may be intentional. The team is optimistic about the impact of Fred VanVleet's return next season and appears content with its current direction.
  • The Hornets are shopping for frontcourt upgrades via both the draft and trade market and could move one of their two first-round picks (Nos. 14 and 18) for the right player. Miles Bridges is among the movable expiring contracts they could use to facilitate a deal.
  • The Spurs remain committed to De'Aaron Fox as their franchise point guard. "I know there's a lot of interest in whether they might do something with De'Aaron Fox, but they are committed to him right now," Windhorst said on SportsCenter.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves are already working to re-sign Ayo Dosunmu after his midseason acquisition proved influential for the team.
  • Draymond Green was critical of the young Spurs for walking off the court without shaking hands after losing the Finals. "There's a way to win and there's a way to lose," Green said on his podcast. "And walking off the court, not looking your killer in his eyes, ain't the way to lose." He praised Jalen Brunson for immediately seeking out Spurs coach Mitch Johnson to shake his hand.
  • Knicks championship gear has already more than doubled Fanatics' previous best-selling NBA Finals champion, the LeBron-led Lakers in 2020. Fanatics took in more than 8,000 orders per minute after New York's 4-1 series win over San Antonio, also a record. Per HoopsHype, Jalen Brunson was the 47th-highest-paid player in the NBA this season, and none of the last four Finals MVPs ranked in the top 30 in salary when they won the award.
