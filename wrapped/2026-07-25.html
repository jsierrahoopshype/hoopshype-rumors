NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron signs with 76ers for veteran minimum, Cavs pivot to Kuminga, Heat target DeRozan

LeBron James is joining the Philadelphia 76ers on a two-year, $8 million deal with a player option, per Shams Charania, completing one of the most surprising free-agent decisions of his career. James goes from earning $52.63 million in his final season with the Lakers to just $3.88 million next year, the largest pay cut in NBA history. The decision came together through a sustained recruiting effort: Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and newly acquired Jaylen Brown all worked James directly, with Maxey, a fellow Klutch client who has called James a "big brother," leading the charge. President of basketball operations Mike Gansey, who crossed paths with James as far back as their high school days in Northeast Ohio, stayed in constant communication with agent Rich Paul throughout the process. A league source told The Athletic that Philadelphia became James' choice because it gave him the best shot at a fifth NBA title.

For much of the process, James and Paul thought Miami was the destination. Paul told ESPN that for a long time it seemed like the Heat were the most likely landing spot, but ultimately James decided he'd rather preserve those memories than risk diminishing them. Golden State was in the mix too — Draymond Green had opted out of $27.7 million specifically to create flexibility to chase James — but Paul acknowledged that pairing with Stephen Curry and Green, while appealing, wasn't the choice of someone who wants to win a championship. James had also been pushing behind the scenes for former teammate Kyrie Irving to join him in Cleveland, but the Mavericks had no interest in engaging on a trade, and Irving is believed to be comfortable in Dallas. One of James' ex-Laker teammates told The Athletic that James had seriously considered signing with Philadelphia back in 2018 before choosing the Lakers — eight years later, he got there.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • The Sixers have a small logistical hurdle to clear before James is officially signed. His $3.9 million salary puts Philadelphia $572,000 above the first apron hard cap, meaning the team will need to waive a non-guaranteed player. Dalen Terry (non-guaranteed) and Jabari Walker ($250,000 partial guarantee) are the most straightforward options, per HoopsHype.
  • Rich Paul made clear that Bronny James and his father are "not a package deal" and there is no current plan for Bronny to follow LeBron to Philadelphia. Bronny recently had his contract guaranteed by the Lakers, whose staff has developed a genuine fondness for him.
  • Shams Charania also reported that there is a strong likelihood LeBron does not retire until 2028, meaning the two-year deal with Philadelphia could run its full course.
  • The Cavaliers, who spent James' free agency hoping for a third reunion, are now turning their attention to free agent forward Jonathan Kuminga. Kuminga's agent Aaron Turner told The Athletic "Jonathan likes the Cavs, likes Kenny Atkinson, and Donovan Mitchell likes him", and Kuminga spent two months training in Cleveland last summer. The Lakers have also had interest, but sources say what Los Angeles is offering Atlanta in a potential sign-and-trade is underwhelming compared to what Cleveland could put together.
  • The Heat are expected to open fresh conversations with free agent DeMar DeRozan now that James is off the board. Miami is also monitoring Klay Thompson's buyout market availability and has already spoken with Bradley Beal's camp earlier this offseason. Beal opted out of a Clippers contract worth $5.5 million and would welcome Heat interest.
  • Kasparas Jakucionis was with the Lithuanian national team at 6 a.m. when the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade to Miami went through. He woke up two hours later to a flood of messages from his agent — that's how he found out he was a Buck.
  • Giannis, now settled in Miami, addressed online criticism of his wife Mariah Riddlesprigger this week. "Why is Giannis with his wife? He could be with a model. Not one model but 10!" he said people tell him. His answer: "I am with my wife because she tells me the truth."
  • Jimmy Butler's ACL recovery is trending in the right direction. People close to the Warriors forward say he has turned a corner in the last few weeks, though he still can't sprint freely. There is pressure building for him to return in the January-February window, but he becomes extension-eligible in February, which provides some personal motivation to push toward that timeline.
  • Despite circulating speculation, the Warriors and Wizards have not had substantive talks about an Anthony Davis trade. Wizards GM Will Dawkins has also been actively working to shut the rumors down in public.
  • Khris Middleton is another player now etched into the pay-cut record books. His new three-year, $17.61 million deal to rejoin the Wizards follows a year in which he earned $35.09 million, making his $29.5 million pay cut the fourth-largest in NBA history.
  • The Knicks' Jalen Brunson is expected to collect every dollar of his next contract. Brunson signed a year early at a discount, and the expectation around the league is that he gets the full $280-300 million when he hits free agency next summer.
  • The NBA's investigation into the Clippers and owner Steve Ballmer surrounding Kawhi Leonard appears to be wrapping up. The league is now deciding whether to levy a punishment, and a settlement with the Clippers and NBPA involvement remain possible outcomes.
  • The Rockets have signed forward/center Oscar Tshiebwe to a contract. Tshiebwe, who has spent the past three seasons on two-way deals with Indiana and Utah, averages 7.0 points and 6.5 rebounds in 49 career games.
  • The Trail Blazers are signing guard John Tonje to a two-way contract. Tonje appeared in six games for the Celtics last season as a rookie.
  • Caitlin Clark had just finished making her case for LeBron to join her Indiana Pacers when the news broke that he was headed to Philadelphia. "No way! I just pitched him for the Pacers — it didn't get to him fast enough," Clark said during her WNBA All-Star media availability in Chicago.
