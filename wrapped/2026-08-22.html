NBA Rumors Wrap: Klay Thompson heads to Heat, DeRozan joins Nuggets, Timberwolves get new controlling owner

Klay Thompson is heading to Miami, ending a months-long saga that reshaped the Heat's offseason. Thompson and the Dallas Mavericks reached a buyout agreement on his $17.5 million expiring contract after he made clear to Dallas he wanted to play for a contender, per Shams Charania. Thompson then agreed to sign with Miami once he clears waivers, with the Heat having waited nearly two months for his release. The deal is two years at nearly $13 million, with a player option in Year 2. Miami made Thompson its top free agent priority this summer to surround Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo with another elite shooter, and Thompson can sign as early as Sunday at 5 p.m. after clearing waivers.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • There was mutual interest between DeMar DeRozan and the Heat throughout the summer, and Adebayo and DeRozan communicated regularly, but Miami ultimately made Thompson its priority. DeRozan instead agreed to a one-year, $3.9 million deal with the Denver Nuggets after also speaking with the Washington Wizards and New Orleans Pelicans. The Nuggets had been monitoring DeRozan's situation in Sacramento since February and put together a full-court recruitment involving players, coaches and executives once he hit free agency, with Aaron Gordon and assistant coach Jared Dudley among the key voices in the process. DeRozan had already been in conversations with Nikola Jokic and Jamal Murray and was eager to join a contender.
  • With Thompson and DeRozan now settled, the Heat have turned their attention to a big man for the 14th roster spot. There is definite Miami interest in Nick Richards, though a couple of other names are also in the mix, and there have been talks about bringing back Keshad Johnson.
  • Former Milwaukee Bucks assistant Vin Baker is joining coach Erik Spoelstra's staff in Miami. Baker spent eight seasons with the Bucks and has a close bond with Giannis, which factored into the move.
  • Marc Stad has completed a deal to become the controlling owner of the Minnesota Timberwolves and Minnesota Lynx, buying the majority of co-owner Marc Lore's stake at a $4.5 billion valuation, per Shams Charania. Lore will retain a minority stake and co-owner Alex Rodriguez is increasing his equity percentage. The team's current leadership structure, including the Timberwolves and Lynx coaching and front office staffs, will remain in place. Stad's wife, Elisa, will serve as Timberwolves governor, while Rodriguez continues as alternate governor for the Wolves and governor of the Lynx.
  • Lore and Rodriguez bought into the Timberwolves in 2021 at a $1.5 billion valuation. The franchise has reached the playoffs in each of the five seasons since, advancing beyond the first round in each of the past three, and went into the luxury tax in both of the last two seasons.
  • The five-team trade that brought Peyton Watson to Cleveland is official. Watson is signing a four-year, $88 million deal with a player option and trade kicker, and the Cavaliers sent an unprotected 2031 first-round pick and a future second-rounder to Denver as part of the sign-and-trade. The deal cost Cleveland Max Strus and Dennis Schröder, with Cam Whitmore also coming back to the Cavs in the deal. Aaron Gordon described Watson to Tim McMahon as "a young Paul George," though McMahon noted that might be hyperbole — the point was Watson's length, athleticism and versatility as a 6-8 wing.
  • Watson was Cleveland's top fallback option after a failed pursuit of LeBron James, and the deal has hard-capped the Cavs at the first apron. The Cavs have had conversations about waiving and stretching Whitmore as a result. As a broader context on the cap swing: Cleveland had $392 million on their books as of January 20th and were over the second apron. As of August 20th, they were at $218 million in salary with just $9 million in tax.
  • Brian Windhorst says there is still a window for the Lakers to pursue a Jonathan Kuminga sign-and-trade using Jaden Hardy. His skepticism: for the Atlanta Hawks to do a sign-and-trade, they would likely end up in the luxury tax unless a third team absorbs salary, and he doubts Atlanta does that deal unless they are genuinely in love with what they are getting back.
  • Luka Doncic and his Lakers teammates held a two-hour workout at Stozice Arena in Slovenia, with Lakers assistant Greg St. Jean leading the session. It was the group's first practice together during Luka's privately organized trip to his home country. Meanwhile, Tim McMahon noted that teams around the league are monitoring Doncic's situation in Los Angeles regardless of his public optimism, the same way they watch Nikola Jokic's stated loyalty to Denver.
  • The Detroit Pistons' extension talks with Jalen Duren have been "an awfully nasty negotiation," per McMahon. Duren was an All-NBA player for Detroit last season and a massive factor in the team winning 60 games, and McMahon says he has real concerns about the lingering cloud the situation could create even after it gets resolved.
  • Dusty May's first coaching staff in Dallas will be entirely new after Phil Handy departed for Philadelphia. Handy and Josh Broghamer (who left for Orlando) were the only holdovers from Jason Kidd's staff, leaving no continuity on the bench from the previous regime.
  • Victor Wembanyama said he feels "a bit rusty" after France narrowly lost to Serbia in a friendly in Belgrade. Wembanyama said the biggest adjustment switching from NBA to FIBA play is the ball itself: "There are plenty of differences compared to the NBA, but this is one of the biggest."
  • Lauri Markkanen had some familiar faces in the stands as Finland hosted Iceland in a FIBA World Cup qualifying warmup. He finished with 17 points, six rebounds, three assists and three steals in a 93-78 win, with Jazz teammates Jaren Jackson Jr. and Keyonte George, head coach Will Hardy and owner Ryan Smith all in attendance after spending the previous days touring Helsinki with Markkanen.
  • Lakers legends reacted to the Buss family ownership drama at a charity event in Beverly Hills Thursday night. "I don't think he [Dr. Jerry Buss] would be very happy with the state of the Lakers right now," Byron Scott said. Robert Horry called the prospect of the Buss family losing its connection to the franchise "sad" and "strange," crediting Jerry Buss with inventing much of what the modern NBA experience looks like. "For the Buss family not to be involved in some way would be such a sad thing," Horry said.
