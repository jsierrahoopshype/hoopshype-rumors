NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks cut to Wiseman-Brown decision, Wembanyama sets GM Survey record, Warriors to limit Curry

New York Knicks center Drew Eubanks has sustained a torn pectoral muscle and will be sidelined for an extended period, per Shams Charania. The Knicks will waive Eubanks, who had been competing for a reserve center spot in training camp. Along with Eubanks, New York is also waiving Tony Bradley and Ochai Agbaji. That leaves the roster competition down to two players for one spot: center James Wiseman, who had already been the frontrunner before the Eubanks injury, and wing Bruce Brown, who would require the Knicks to make a trade to keep alongside Wiseman since New York only has room under the second apron for one additional camp veteran.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Victor Wembanyama received 77 percent of the MVP vote in the NBA GM Survey, the highest mark in the survey's 25-year history, per HoopsHype. He broke the previous record set by LeBron James ahead of the 2009-10 season, when LeBron pulled 69 percent.
  • The Warriors are intentionally ramping up Stephen Curry more slowly this year, scaling back his practice workload and looking for the right balance between rhythm and long-term durability. Steve Kerr said it's possible Curry starts the year below a 32-minute pitch count, and told fans to "be mindful" of games early in the season. Kerr said he plans to sit down with Curry, Draymond Green and team physician Rick Celebrini in the coming days to identify potential rest nights on the schedule.
  • Julian Strawther was helped off the court with a left leg injury during Denver's preseason game in Los Angeles after his leg bent awkwardly on a landing. The Nuggets ruled him out for the remainder of the game, and coach David Adelman said he would undergo an MRI the following day.
  • Phoenix Suns guard Luke Kennard left a preseason game against San Antonio with a sore left knee after an awkward landing on a floater. Coach Jordan Ott said Kennard would be further evaluated when the team returned to Phoenix.
  • Jamaree Bouyea is expected to earn the 14th roster spot for the Suns after a strong camp. Phoenix is not expected to fill the 15th spot, instead keeping flexibility for two-way players Pat Spencer and Duop Reath.
  • Karl-Anthony Towns is still carrying some frustration over the Knicks pausing his contract extension talks until later in the season. Reporter James Edwards III said Towns' vibe was "a lot more positive" in his most recent availability, with Towns emphasizing team business on the court. But Edwards noted Towns said it was news to him that talks were being stopped, and that "there was obviously still a little bit of animosity there."
  • AJ Dybantsa enters his rookie season with the Wizards surrounded by veterans brought in partly to ease his development. Wizards coach Brian Keefe said the plan is to put him in situations to grow as a handler, decision-maker and two-way player. Trae Young, who was the last freshman to lead the nation in scoring before Dybantsa, offered his own read: "AJ averaged 25 as a freshman in college. There's not very many of us. I know he knows how to score."
  • Celtics guard Mike Conley ran Boston's substitution rotations during a preseason win over Charlotte after coach Joe Mazzulla handed him the clipboard before tip-off. Conley called it harder than expected: "You're looking down, and you're trying to follow a strict regimen, but then you look up and a guy is winded." He said the night gave him a genuine window into how Mazzulla thinks about the game.
  • LeBron James said OG Anunoby was the team MVP of the Knicks' Finals run, crediting his chase-down block, a key tip-in, and consistent three-point shooting. "Obviously Jalen Brunson became the MVP," LeBron said during a conversation with Steve Nash, "but it's the small things."
  • Steph Curry said a LeBron-to-Warriors pairing was never a real conversation. "I thought there was a conversation," Curry said, "and if there was ever some momentum, it would be known based on the profile LeBron has."
  • Mavs rookie Cooper Flagg has a willing mentor in Kyrie Irving, according to Dallas coach Jamahl Mosley. "Kyrie Irving is fantastic for Cooper Flagg," Mosley said, pointing to Irving's ability to teach both on and off the court. Meanwhile, Dallas big man Moussa Cisse sat out practice as a precaution for a left ankle sprain, though coach Dusty May said Cisse would have been available if there had been a game.
  • Tyrese Maxey said Joel Embiid's health "changes everything" for the Sixers. "There's been times where he hasn't been healthy and we get into the game and now we're trying to learn on the flow," Maxey said. "If you haven't been playing with a big like Joel, there's not many."
  • Former Knick Amar'e Stoudemire graduated from the Miami-Dade Police Academy on Wednesday after months of training and will join the county's school police department. The six-time All-Star and Hall of Famer is 43.
  • On the broadcast side, Hall of Famer Jason Kidd and former NBA champion Jeremy Lin are both joining ESPN's NBA coverage roster this season. Kidd will contribute across game and studio programming; Lin will appear on NBA Today and other studio shows. Separately, Tom Haberstroh has been named sideline reporter for NBC Sports Philadelphia's Sixers coverage.
  • The Kings have converted Leaky Black to a two-way deal. The Blazers are signing Caleb Houstan to an Exhibit 10 contract, with Houstan expected to join the Rip City Remix. The Lakers have signed Kiwi forward Aniwaniwa Tait-Jones to an Exhibit 10 deal; the 25-year-old averaged 15.9 points, 6.9 rebounds and 2.7 assists per game for Valmiera Glass VIA and was named Finals MVP of the Latvian-Estonian Basketball League.
