NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron James likely heading East, Kawhi Leonard investigation nears end, Wolves courted Giannis and Brown before LaMelo

The Golden State Warriors have privately cooled on their chances of landing LeBron James, with the organization internally believing they are a dark horse and that James is probably heading to the East. That lines up with reported interest from the Cleveland Cavaliers, the Miami Heat and the Philadelphia 76ers, who are among the Eastern Conference teams connected to James this summer. LeBron has not appeared to be in any rush to decide, spending July 14 on a golf course with Draymond Green and Kevin Hart rather than making any public moves. He is scheduled to appear at Fanatics Fest in New York on Thursday, where he will record a Mind the Game podcast episode alongside Tyrese Haliburton, sitting in for Steve Nash, per Marc Stein.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard appears to be approaching its conclusion. Clippers owner Steve Ballmer met last week in New York City with the Wachtell lawyers conducting the probe, which sources described as probably one of the final steps. ESPN's Shams Charania has said the worst-case outcomes range from a lengthy suspension to the voiding of Leonard's contract entirely. Meanwhile, the Toronto Raptors, who acquired Leonard in a trade, are acting like an unworried team, with the sense being that any outcome short of a voided contract is something they can work around.
  • Before landing LaMelo Ball, the Minnesota Timberwolves held talks with the Celtics about Jaylen Brown, the Bucks about Giannis Antetokounmpo, and the Bulls about Josh Giddey, according to team sources. The Celtics were asking for a haul that included Rudy Gobert and heavy draft capital, and there were internal debates about how Brown and Anthony Edwards would fit as two ISO-heavy wings. Ball called the trade "God's plan" at his introductory press conference Tuesday.
  • The Wolves' long game with Ball is built around one goal: getting Edwards to sign a max extension a year from now. The organization, from Marc Lore and Alex Rodriguez down through the coaching staff, believes it has the infrastructure to help Ball mature, with the expectation that his presence will elevate Edwards enough to justify that next monster deal.
  • The Dallas Mavericks are actively pursuing a trade for Klay Thompson and have not given serious consideration to a buyout as of mid-July, per Marc Stein. The Miami Heat interest that has been floated is a buyout scenario, not a trade scenario, and Stein said the question of whether Thompson's representatives push for a buyout only becomes relevant if no trade materializes by August or September.
  • Jalen Brunson won two ESPY Awards Tuesday night, taking home Best NBA Player and Best Championship Performance. He also revealed on ESPN Radio that his wrist issues began during Game 2 of the Eastern Conference Finals against the Cavaliers. "I just remember being at the free-throw line in the third or fourth quarter," Brunson said. "My wrist was starting to feel weak." He recently had surgery to repair a tendon in his left hand and wrist, with a six-to-eight week recovery timeline.
  • Spurs rookie forward Jayden Quaintance underwent surgery to repair meniscus damage in his right knee, the team announced Wednesday. San Antonio was aware of the looming procedure when they drafted him with the 20th pick last month, and GM Brian Wright said the team went through a thorough review of his rehabilitation timeline before becoming comfortable with the selection.
  • Hawks forward Mouhamed Gueye fractured his left foot during a workout last week, underwent surgery, and will be re-evaluated in three to four months.
  • Kevin Durant had plenty to say this week. He called the Sixers "dangerous" after the Jaylen Brown trade, saying the Luka Doncic deal proved nothing is out of the realm of possibility in the NBA. On LeBron's free agency, Durant was straightforward: "Wherever he goes, I know he's going to ball out." Durant also went on Instagram to call out Fox Sports analysts Colin Cowherd and Nick Wright by name, writing that basketball would move forward if Wright "wasn't involved."
  • NBA Commissioner Adam Silver said Tuesday he expects the league's centralized local broadcast streaming hub to launch for the 2027-28 season, with YouTube as a leading candidate to house the platform. Following the collapse of Main Street Sports Group, 13 teams are left to patch together over-the-air and streaming solutions for the upcoming season. Silver also said Moda Center renovation talks have "gone off track", expressing concern that the deal approved in March between the Trail Blazers and Portland city officials has yet to be finalized.
  • Silver said NBA Europe expansion interest has exceeded expectations, with cities bidding even without being invited to do so. On the ownership side, Joey and Jesse Buss are making an aggressive push through their firm Buss Sports Capital to join an NBA Europe ownership group, per Dave McMenamin.
  • WNBA Commissioner Cathy Engelbert was not planning to suspend Alyssa Thomas for hitting Caitlin Clark until NBA Commissioner Adam Silver stepped in and urged her to reverse course, multiple sources told Sports Business Journal. Engelbert initially felt a suspension was too steep given that no foul was called on the court. Engelbert was not available for comment and Silver did not address his role.
  • Washington Wizards GM Will Dawkins pushed back on Anthony Davis trade speculation, saying flatly on SiriusXM NBA Radio: "AD wants to be in DC. We're going to keep him there." After 14 years in the NBA, Jonas Valanciunas is returning to Europe on a two-year deal with Zalgiris Kaunas.
  • Quentin Grimes said the Lakers' pitch to him in free agency centered on a meaningful offensive role alongside elite defensive responsibilities. "I am guarding the best players and wanting that responsibility," Grimes said. "I want to have that challenge every night."
  • Stephen Curry received the Muhammad Ali Sports Humanitarian Award on Tuesday night, recognized for his Eat Learn Play foundation, beating out Damar Hamlin and Adam Thielen for the honor.
  • The Pacers are fully guaranteeing Quenton Jackson's $2.58 million contract for next season. He had only $275,000 guaranteed had the team waived him by Wednesday's deadline.
