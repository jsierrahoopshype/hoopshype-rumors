NBA Rumors Wrap: Jokic extension watch puts Nuggets on league radar, Draymond opts back in, Peyton Watson market heats up

The Denver Nuggets have become one of the most closely watched franchises in the league this summer, and Nikola Jokic's contract situation is the main reason. Marc Stein, speaking on the DNVR Nuggets Podcast, said "there are a lot of eyeballs on the Denver Nuggets right now league-wide" because Jokic declined to sign an extension for the second consecutive summer. The curiosity around Denver extends beyond Jokic's contract status to broader questions about where the franchise is heading. Stein made clear the league is watching and waiting to see what Denver does next.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Draymond Green opened up about his offseason decision, saying he re-signed with the Warriors for the same amount he opted out of after the team's hopes of landing a player with the mid-level exception fell through. "I pretty much resigned, not pretty much I did resign for the same amount that I opted out of," Green said, confirming he also did not receive a no-trade clause. He acknowledged that the clause was something he could have pushed for but ultimately moved on.
  • Peyton Watson's restricted free agency is dragging into the late summer, with the Bucks and Clippers remaining active in their pursuit of the Denver forward. Watson and agent Rich Paul are willing to wait until early September for the right deal, with offers in the $16-18 million annual range, according to Marc Stein. Atlanta was also involved in discussions, and Cleveland has interest in both Watson and Jonathan Kuminga.
  • Jaylen Brown addressed his move to Philadelphia on a Twitch livestream Monday, trying on his new No. 7 Sixers jersey for the first time. "Yo, we got 'Bron, and what an opportunity that is," Brown said, adding that wearing a non-Celtics jersey for the first time in 10 years "honestly feels weird, you know what I mean? But it looks fire." He also revealed his championship celebration plan: skydiving. LeBron James texted back, "Boy, hell nah!"
  • VJ Edgecombe described the moment he found out LeBron was signing with the 76ers: he was mid-workout when his coach's Apple Watch kept buzzing with the news. "He's like yo we just got Bron. I'm like, you're lying." Edgecombe also said he had been repeatedly calling Tyrese Maxey asking whether LeBron was coming, and Maxey kept saying he didn't know. "He just didn't want to tell me," Edgecombe said. "That's what I feel. I might be wrong."
  • Kevin Durant doubled down on his claim that the new-look 76ers are comparable to the 2016-17 Warriors, replying to a fan on Instagram who said the two teams "ain't close." Durant listed Joel Embiid, Brown, Maxey, LeBron, and Klay Thompson by their accolades and argued Steph Curry and Draymond Green's numbers alone don't make that Warriors team clearly superior on paper. Draymond responded on his podcast, saying Durant views basketball purely through the lens of scoring, so his framing of the comparison has to be understood in that context.
  • Ron Harper Jr. spoke at length about his contract extension with the Celtics, saying a February start against Houston — when he guarded Kevin Durant — was the turning point in his season. "I felt like after that point I felt like I'd be a real contributor," Harper said. He also revealed that about an hour after the Paul George trade, George called and asked for jersey No. 13, which Harper had been wearing. They worked out the swap, with Harper taking No. 8, describing it as beneficial for "both parties." He added about Jaylen Brown's departure: "It sucks to see him go. But you know, this is a business more than anything. So I wish him luck in the future, except when he plays us."
  • Rui Hachimura explained his decision to leave the Lakers for the Clippers, saying the move came down to role and winning opportunity. "In my final year with the Lakers, I couldn't really make those cuts due to our team's structure," Hachimura said, adding that the Clippers specifically told him they want him cutting, shooting threes, and rebounding. He also received a call-up to Japan's national team for the 2027 Basketball World Cup Asian qualifiers later this month, his first with the national team since distancing himself following the 2024 Paris Olympics.
  • The Warriors signed De'Anthony Melton to a two-year, $12.4 million deal using the Taxpayer Mid-Level Exception, per HoopsHype. The contract includes a player option for the 2027-28 season. Golden State has also been linked to Georges Niang and Kelly Olynyk on veteran minimum deals as it continues to fill out the roster.
  • The Sacramento Kings are weighing whether to bring back Russell Westbrook or sign Victor Oladipo to mentor rookie Darius Acuff Jr., according to Marc Stein. Sacramento attended Oladipo's recent workout in Las Vegas during summer league. Separately, Stein also reported that Portland is not currently pursuing free agent DeMar DeRozan, despite reports to the contrary. The Heat are interested in both DeRozan and Klay Thompson, according to Jake Fischer.
  • The Toronto Raptors added a pair of players on Monday. Center Trey Jemison, who won an NBA title last season, agreed to a two-way contract with Toronto. The Raptors also reached a deal with Andre Jackson Jr., who spent the past three seasons with the Bucks and will compete for a roster spot.
  • Seven Spurs players, including new signee Tobias Harris, have joined Victor Wembanyama in France for workouts this week. The group also includes Harrison Barnes, Stephon Castle, De'Aaron Fox, Dylan Harper, Carter Bryant, and Jordan McLaughlin. Spurs CEO R.C. Buford said Wembanyama organizing the trip on his own reflects his growth as a leader.
  • Luka Doncic's ex-fiancée Anamaria Goltes has dropped her child support petition, citing an intention to resolve the matter privately and in the best interests of their children. Luka had previously moved to dismiss the California case, arguing it had no legal standing because the children live in Slovenia.
  • Knicks executive Gersson Rosas, who helped build New York's championship roster, has departed the organization after his contract expired. Sources say he left on good terms with president Leon Rose and wanted to pursue other opportunities for his own professional growth.
  • Cole Anthony has signed with Melbourne United for the upcoming NBL season. The 26-year-old, a 367-game NBA veteran, said he views the move as a chance to continue developing and compete for a championship.
  • The Cleveland Sirens is the official name of Cleveland's new WNBA franchise. The team will play home games at Rocket Arena and use the Cleveland Clinic Courts in Independence as its training facility.
  • The NBA is considering Battersea Power Station as a potential arena site for a London team in its planned European competition, which is targeting an autumn 2027 launch across 12 cities.
  • "Inside the NBA" host Kenny Smith married Croatian influencer Tia Jurcic at The Resort at Pelican Hill this week. The couple announced the news in a joint Instagram post.
