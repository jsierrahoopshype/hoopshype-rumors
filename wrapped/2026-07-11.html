NBA Rumors Wrap: Adebayo punches Herro in Vegas, LeBron decision still pending, Wemby takes the 25% max

Bam Adebayo punched Tyler Herro in the face during a confrontation at a Las Vegas hotel gym Friday morning, with the Miami Heat confirming they are aware of the incident but declining to comment. Per Shams Charania, Adebayo approached Herro about comments the guard had made on social media critiquing him after their seven-year run together in Miami ended, and the punch landed in front of Herro's AAU team, with Herro being escorted out by security personnel afterward. Charania reported there was no visible injury or noticeable cut on Herro, though the league and players union are expected to weigh in on the matter in the coming days. Former NBA player Danny Green offered a measured reaction, saying he doesn't promote violence but acknowledged that "sometimes it takes that for guys to settle things." The background context, per reporting, is that multiple former Heat players harbored some level of resentment toward Adebayo for being treated as the franchise's untouchable cornerstone while others were repeatedly dangled in trade discussions.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The LeBron James free agency circus rolled on Friday with no resolution in sight. LeBron's agent Rich Paul told ESPN that a "handful" of teams remain in the mix but named none of them, said players have been communicating with James directly, and offered no timetable for a decision. ESPN's Dave McMenamin said LeBron wants to win another championship and "is a guy who likes being liked, accepted and celebrated." Marc Stein reported LeBron is looking at Eastern Conference destinations with increased seriousness.
  • Brian Windhorst threw cold water on the Cleveland sentiment Friday, saying he had a source swearing it's a done deal in another city and calling the situation "too scattered" to draw conclusions. He also said he has not heard anything credible to support speculation that LeBron is trying to recruit Draymond Green away from the Warriors. Meanwhile, the Timberwolves have still not been told no, with The Athletic's Jon Krawczynski reporting the team still believes it has a chance.
  • Andre Iguodala said LeBron's interest in joining the Warriors is a testament to Stephen Curry. "As I've been saying since 2013, Steph Curry, no one understands how great he is," Iguodala told ESPN at the NBPA's leadership transition news conference Friday. He added that a friend's text crystallized it for him: "Steph Curry, if he's going to get another one of the greatest players of their generation to play on his team, what does it say about him?"
  • The 76ers have been floated as a legitimate LeBron landing spot, with McMenamin pointing to the relationship between LeBron and Tyrese Maxey as a genuine draw, adding that there are "few players I have seen that LeBron talks about as glowingly as a player but as a person." Coach Nick Nurse is also a Klutch client, which creates familiarity. On the Cleveland side, Charles Barkley was direct: "The only rightful farewell tour would be with the Cleveland Cavaliers."
  • Victor Wembanyama signed a five-year, $252 million maximum rookie-scale extension with the Spurs, with a player option in the fifth year, per Shams Charania. Wembanyama chose the 25% max escalator rather than the 30% supermax path that would have been worth $303 million, a decision rooted in giving the Spurs more flexibility to build around him. The organization's entire leadership group, including chairman Peter J. Holt, CEO RC Buford, GM Brian Wright and coach Mitch Johnson, traveled to Paris to finalize the deal with Wembanyama and his family.
  • San Antonio big man Luke Kornet submitted a two-minute voice memo pitch to LeBron's camp as part of Rich Paul's request that interested teams send audio pitches. Kornet re-introduced himself by reminding LeBron that he "yelled at me a little bit last year," then pitched the state income tax exemption, SeaWorld and a local bakery as perks of San Antonio life, describing himself as "a generational player."
  • Derrick White was home with his wife when he learned that Jaylen Brown had been traded from the Celtics to the 76ers. "It's tough to see someone get traded, and JB's been an amazing teammate, amazing friend to me," White said Friday night, adding that he reached out to Brown with a simple message: "Tell him thank you for everything he did for me, and just know how much he means to me." Teammate Neemias Queta, who also signed a contract extension this week, said of Brown: "He's a guy that you can't replace in the city. Really, really hard to replace on the court as well."
  • Gary Payton defended the Brown trade to Philly, saying the relationship had simply broken down beyond repair. "You cannot keep talking about trading somebody and doing things, and then all of a sudden he comes back to your team, it's not going to be a good relationship." Payton said Brown makes the Sixers a genuine threat in the East and "somebody to reckon with" for the defending champion Knicks.
  • The NBPA's new executive director David Kelly said Friday the union wants the second apron softened or removed entirely. "We are not fans of the second apron," Kelly said at ESPN. "We did not propose the second apron. We should have done a better job of fighting back against the second apron. In the future, we will have a much more unified union." Kelly is succeeding Iguodala in the role.
  • Trae Young signed a contract extension with the Wizards that raised eyebrows around the league, given he was essentially salary-dumped by Atlanta and had minimal trade market last season. Young shrugged off the skepticism: "That ain't nothing new. I don't really care about what other people think." He added that when the trade to Washington happened, "it just felt like this could be my next home."
  • The Rockets officially signed Marcus Smart on Friday. Rockets GM Rafael Stone said at Summer League he is "really excited" about Smart's versatility, calling him someone who "feels like a Rocket" and saying he is comfortable deploying Smart at any position, including center. Stone also said the team wants to reduce the minutes load on Kevin Durant this season.
  • The Mavericks officially signed forward Tarik Biberovic, whom they acquired along with Santi Aldama from Memphis as part of a six-team trade earlier this week. Dallas sent guard AJ Johnson and a pair of picks to land the two forwards.
  • Lakers GM Rob Pelinka was spotted embracing free agent target Peyton Watson before a Summer League game Friday, the latest sign of the Lakers' continued interest in the former Nuggets forward.
  • New Bucks guard Tyler Herro spoke earlier Friday about always being in trade rumors during his time in Miami, before the altercation with Adebayo. "I know how good I am and what I'm capable of," Herro said. "I just have to keep working, stay healthy, and represent this new team how I'm supposed to." New Miami acquisition Bobby Portis explained why he switched his jersey number to 95: while driving to Miami, he passed a highway sign reading "95 South" and decided that was his number now.
  • Denver GM Jon Wallace was asked about Nikola Jokic delaying his extension until next summer and kept it succinct: "We feel confident. We trust him. We've got no choice but to."
  • Victor Oladipo announced he is holding an open pro day workout in Las Vegas next Tuesday, the former All-Star's first formal step in his NBA comeback attempt. The workout is open to front office executives, scouts and coaches from any team or league.
