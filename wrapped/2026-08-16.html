NBA Rumors Wrap: Hall of Fame class enshrined, Luka child support case dismissed, Haliburton on Pistons snub

The Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame officially inducted its 2026 class this weekend, headlined by Mike D'Antoni, Doc Rivers, Amar'e Stoudemire, Candace Parker, Elena Delle Donne, Chamique Holdsclaw, referee Joey Crawford and college coach Mark Few. D'Antoni, whose Seven Seconds or Less offense with the Phoenix Suns transformed how the entire league plays, told ESPN that the revolutionary style came with a price: "We were first to go through the door, first of all, you take a lot of bullets." He added that he wishes those Suns teams had pushed even further. "We'd shoot 30-something 3s and we're going, 'Is that too many?'" he said. "While other people were saying, 'You can't win that way.' ... We just didn't quite maximize what we could have done." D'Antoni also reflected on the injuries that kept those Suns from a title, pointing to Joe Johnson's broken orbital bone in 2005, Raja Bell's hobbled calf in 2006 and the infamous 2007 suspension as the three moments that derailed what could have been a dynasty.

Rivers brought his trademark humor to his induction speech, joking that coaching had literally changed his voice: "I used to be able to really sing. Can't do that anymore." He also addressed the players he coached over his career: "I can guarantee you every time my motives were right. I was always trying to make you better or trying to get the team to win." Stoudemire, meanwhile, gave a nod to the current Knicks, saying: "Before I got there, no one else wanted to go to New York. So that's why I was like, the Knicks are back, and we're gonna do this. And so it's good to see those guys winning that championship this year in 2026."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The child support case filed by Anamaria Goltes against Luka Doncic was formally dismissed in a Los Angeles County courtroom on Friday. Doncic's attorney, Laura Wasser, moved quickly to quash the petition, and Judge Melissa C. Lyons ruled in her favor.
  • Tyrese Haliburton opened up about the 2020 draft, saying the Detroit Pistons passing on him at No. 7 hurt the most. "You need a point guard, it's got to be me," Haliburton recalled thinking, before watching Detroit select Killian Hayes instead. "Then I look at my agent behind the camera and he gives me the look. Then it flashes up Killian Hayes, and I'm like, 'What?!'"
  • Haliburton also said Giannis Antetokounmpo is the toughest player the Pacers have had to scheme against. "Whatever scheme, he averaged I think like 50," Haliburton said of their battles with the Bucks. "You can do whatever you want but sometimes it's nothing." Indiana went 4-for-5 in those matchups, per Haliburton's own count.
  • Haliburton also said he was caught off guard when the schedule came out and the Pacers are opening the season in New Orleans. "When I saw the schedule, I was like, 'Oh wow!'" he said. "Shoutout Trey Murphy, love my boy, Trey. Love New Orleans. But I was shocked to see that."
  • Draymond Green pushed back on recent comments from Monta Ellis and Jeff Teague suggesting the Warriors would have been better off without Stephen Curry. "These statements are null and void and kinda irrelevant," Green said on his podcast. "I think it also takes away what Steph Curry is as a leader, and quite honestly, that's just not how it goes."
  • D'Antoni, on the eve of his induction, also gave a shoutout to James Harden, saying: "He's one of the most skilled players I've ever coached, and I appreciate the trust he gave me." D'Antoni joked that when he told Harden he'd run the offense as the point guard, Harden was probably thinking, "Coach, just give me the ball."
  • The San Antonio Spurs will play only 36 regular-season home games in San Antonio this season due to their Global Games commitments abroad with Victor Wembanyama, including stops in Paris and Manchester. The team also added a third game in Austin to coincide with a major sports weekend in the Texas capital. Spurs CEO RC Buford issued a measured statement after the schedule release: "San Antonio is home, and we're looking forward to bringing our community together while reaching audiences in Austin, Paris and Manchester."
  • The arena situation in San Antonio remains complicated. Voters approved $311 million in taxpayer funding last November, and the Spurs have committed at least $500 million toward the estimated $1.3 billion complex. But the city is now facing a projected budget deficit, and San Antonio Mayor Gina Ortiz Jones wants residents to reauthorize the city's portion of the funding this fall, with the deadline to get it on the November ballot arriving Monday.
  • Chris Paul, retired since February after a 21-year playing career, is drawing interest for a front office role with the Lakers. One Eastern Conference front office member told the Post they could see Paul fitting in initially as a consultant, while another speculated coaching could also be in his future: "Such a student of the game, he's used to leading on the floor and in a locker room. Could just see it translating well."
  • LeBron James has launched a YouTube golf channel, with the first video clocking in at 96 minutes and featuring a Scotland golf trip reunion of the 2016 Cleveland Cavaliers championship team, including J.R. Smith, Kevin Love and Richard Jefferson.
  • The owner of Cooper Flagg's 1-of-1 numbered rookie debut patch autograph card has come forward. Jason Williams, a 50-year-old machine shop owner from South Carolina, pulled the card after paying $515 for a spot on a livestream box break. "They slid that card over and I just slid to the floor crying," Williams told ESPN. He says the card is "in a safe place for right now" and he's trying to lay low as he figures out what to do with it.
  • MSG Sports is moving forward with splitting the New York Knicks and Rangers into separate entities. Sportico reports the combined franchise value of the two teams is $13.5 billion, but the conglomerate currently trades at a 29% discount to that figure. The spin is meant to make minority stake sales easier and reduce what analysts have long called the "Dolan discount" on the stock.
  • Tony Parker's French club ASVEL, dealing with a sharp budget reduction, met last week with Joey Buss and Jesse Buss, sons of the late Lakers owner Jerry Buss. The Buss brothers, along with other investors, are reportedly interested in buying the club as Parker pivots to a coaching career.
  • Olivier Sarr has officially signed a two-year deal with Real Madrid through the 2027-28 season. The 27-year-old French center arrives from the Cleveland Cavaliers and gives Madrid additional size and rim protection after Garuba suffered a serious Achilles injury.
  • Charles Oakley posted "#ItsNotOver" on Instagram, keeping his long-running legal dispute with Knicks owner James Dolan in the public eye.
