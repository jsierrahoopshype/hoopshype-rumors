NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade still in limbo, Sabonis wants out of Sacramento, Simmons signs with Kings

The verbally agreed trade sending Kawhi Leonard back to the Toronto Raptors remains in an unusual holding pattern more than a week after the Los Angeles Clippers were hit with the league's historic salary-cap circumvention punishment. Leonard has been with Raptors players in Miami this week but is restricted to individual workouts since the deal has not been made official, per Marc Stein. The reasons for the continued delay have become something of a mystery to people around the league, though Leonard's camp and the camps of other prominent players in the trade remain confident it will go through soon. The Clippers have indicated the impact of the league's discipline on their front-office structure is the reason for the holdup. Brian Windhorst said Monday night he believes the trade will go through as is, adding that he was "scolded" for wondering whether the terms might be adjusted.

Separately, the NBA has no indication the Clippers plan to flip Brandon Ingram anytime soon following their punishment. Jake Fischer said on Bleacher Report that while the possibility exists down the line, it is not being discussed at this moment.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Domantas Sabonis wants out of Sacramento, according to Jake Fischer on Bleacher Report. Fischer pointed to Sabonis switching agents from Wasserman to CAA this summer as evidence of his desire to find a new situation, and added that Sabonis is extension-eligible. Reports of Sacramento-Charlotte talks, meanwhile, were shot down quickly, with a league source saying the Kings and Hornets have not spoken since weeks before the NBA Draft.
  • Ben Simmons has signed a one-year, non-guaranteed deal worth up to $3.5 million with the Kings. The 30-year-old three-time All-Star will earn $1 million if he makes the opening night roster, marking his latest attempt at an NBA comeback.
  • Jaylen Brown sat down with actor Mahershala Ali for The Hollywood Reporter and addressed his departure from Boston. "It was disappointing, to some degree, in terms of how things transpired at the end in Boston," Brown said. "But I'm also very excited for the opportunity. The past is the past."
  • The Lakers are hiring Miami Heat vice president of player personnel Eric Amsler as an assistant general manager, per Shams Charania. Amsler spent 22-plus years with the Heat, starting as an intern in 2004-05 and eventually becoming VP of player personnel and Sioux Falls Skyforce general manager. He joins Rohan Ramadas as assistant GMs under president Rob Pelinka.
  • Bronny James described the Lakers' recent team trip to Slovenia, where Luka Doncic served as tour guide. "He was hooking us up and getting us in the right places," James told ESPN LA 710. The trip included a night at a Ljubljana restaurant that doubles as a nightclub and a visit to Lake Bled.
  • Charles Barkley thinks the Lakers have the goods to compete. "Luka and Kyrie took the Mavs to the Finals, come on, man. This team is probably better; this team is probably just as good as that team," Barkley said on the Bill Simmons podcast. President Donald Trump, speaking at American Airlines Center in Dallas, was less impressed with the trade that made it possible. "It might go down as the worst trade in the history of sports," Trump said of the Luka deal, floating the idea of redoing it.
  • Mike Conley signed with the Celtics this offseason in part because of the franchise's history, according to Jon Krawczynski. "Mike really wanted to at least spend one year playing for a flagship franchise," Krawczynski said. "The Boston Celtics have a history and a gravitas about them that Conley wanted to experience." Krawczynski also said that during his time in Minnesota, Anthony Edwards would sit next to Conley after nearly every game to go over what he saw and what Ant could have done better, calling Conley one of the most available high-profile players he has covered in 25 years.
  • Jayson Tatum unveiled the Tatum 5s, and the right heel of every shoe features five lines acknowledging the scar from his Achilles surgery. "Anytime you go through something traumatic, you're not the same you," Tatum said. "That's not saying you're worse, you're just different."
  • Draymond Green weighed in on the debate over Amen Thompson's $200 million extension. "In this league you get paid for what you've done," Green said. "Amen Thompson has put together a body of work that says if you want to keep me, this is going to be the going rate."
  • Jalen Brunson will be the first Knick to host Saturday Night Live, making him just the fifth basketball player to do so after Michael Jordan, Charles Barkley, LeBron James, and Bill Russell. He's also only the third New York-area athlete after Eli Manning and Derek Jeter.
  • HoopsHype sat down with retired forward Danilo Gallinari for a wide-ranging exit interview. Gallinari called his final season, which saw him play center for the Wizards and Pistons, "terrible," saying he never played the five "not even in my dreams." On the what-if front, he said he may be "the biggest what-if guy in the history of the game" given his injury history, while also saying he spotted star potential early in both Nikola Jokic and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander.
  • Euroleague owners will meet on October 5 in Lake Como to evaluate an NBA offer to buy a stake in the competition as part of the NBA Europe project. The alternative is pressing ahead with the league's own expansion and franchise-system plan. The meeting comes roughly a year before the anticipated launch of NBA Europe.
  • Trey Kaufman-Renn never signed a contract with the Timberwolves, who fully support his efforts to return to school, per Jon Krawczynski. The Golden State Warriors are bringing back 2025 second-round pick Alex Toohey on an exhibit 10 training camp deal after a knee injury wiped out his first season in the organization.
  • Malik Beasley has asked a judge to ease his federal travel restrictions so he can visit his kids in California and then travel to Hawaii for a resort promotion tied to his social media work, according to court documents obtained by TMZ.
