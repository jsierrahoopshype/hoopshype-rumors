NBA Rumors Wrap: Celtics confident after Jaylen Brown trade, Kyrie returns, SGA sounds off on NBA's best

The Boston Celtics are not second-guessing themselves. Despite widespread criticism of the Jaylen Brown trade, the organization is internally confident the move will pay off, per Brian Windhorst. Boston believes newly signed center Mitchell Robinson will be a significant upgrade and that the team has a legitimate shot at winning the East this season. Windhorst noted he is not personally sold on the trade, but said the Celtics' front office is not wavering. The organization has been transparent internally about its belief in the new roster construction.

Robinson's departure from New York was not without emotion. Knicks guard Jordan Clarkson said of his former teammate: "I hope he does great things in Boston. But when we see him, we've gotta whoop his ass. But that's my dog, man. I love that dude forever." The comments capture the mix of genuine affection and competitive edge that follows a player out of a city where he spent years as a fan favorite.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander has no interest in carrying a chip on his shoulder into the new season. In a wide-ranging TIME profile, the two-time MVP said: "I think I'm the best player in the NBA. I think the production that I have night in and night out is best-player-in-the-NBA production." On the Thunder's playoff exit, SGA took personal responsibility, saying "The MVP of the league waited until Game 7 to have a good game," and adding that the fault lies with him, not Chet Holmgren. On revenge as motivation, he was equally direct: "Even as a kid, I was never good when I played mad. Never, never, never, never."
  • Holmgren, for his part, pushed back slightly when told SGA blamed himself. "I might disagree with him," Holmgren said. "Losing in a sh-tty fashion on the last game of the season, and f-ck-ing having that be your last game for the next three months or whatever, sucks. But he definitely checked on me." SGA's trainer Nem Ilic said the loss didn't fully register for SGA until the Knicks closed it out in Game 5, when SGA texted him: "I'm pissed. I can't believe I'm not here right now."
  • Kyrie Irving told reporters in Macao that a calf strain sustained last December set his ACL recovery back eight weeks, which ultimately ended his 2025-26 season in February. Irving is set to return to game action this weekend for the first time in 19 months. Meanwhile, Cooper Flagg unveiled his official New Balance logo at the NBA China store in Macao, a pine tree design he said connects to his Maine roots.
  • Mavericks owner Patrick Dumont sat down for a candid interview and addressed his relationship with former owner Mark Cuban despite a messy ownership transition that included a legal dispute. "I love Mark," Dumont said. "I consider him a great family friend. He is the most passionate Mavs fan I've ever met. I hope we can do him proud." Dumont also expressed patience with the team's rebuild around Flagg and Irving: "I believe in building things over time. That's how you make them resilient."
  • Luka Doncic opened up about the hamstring injury that ended his Lakers season, saying he regrets pushing through it. "I felt a little bit in the first half that I should have just stopped," Doncic said. "I think we had a team that could compete for the championship. I definitely regret it." The SI cover story also touches on a domestic incident last December in Slovenia that Doncic described as unresolved: "Hopefully," he said when asked if things had been settled. "I can't go through another season like that."
  • Quentin Grimes said JJ Redick was the primary reason he signed with the Lakers. Grimes described a relationship that began in 2023 when Tom Thibodeau suggested he reach out to Redick for shooting advice, and Redick quickly responded and invited him to work out at his Sag Harbor home. "He was the main selling point," Grimes said. "I saw the freedom he gave guys the last two years."
  • New details emerged on the Jeanie Buss-Mark Walter Lakers sale. Buss secured a five-year term as team governor following the sale, plus the right to remain alternate governor beyond that period as long as her affiliated entities retain at least a five-percent ownership stake in the franchise.
  • Tyrese Haliburton made his return after more than 16 months away, scoring seven points in 16 minutes during Indiana's preseason win over Minnesota. "It's the first game, but it's got to slow down," Haliburton said. "There was just a lot of adrenaline." He also revealed he weighs 25 pounds more than he did in Game 7 of the 2025 Finals, with plans to play the full season around 200 pounds after adding significant muscle during rehab.
  • De'Aaron Fox was a late scratch for the Spurs' preseason opener due to general soreness from practice, with the training staff determining he had reached his workload limit after pregame warmups. Julian Champagnie started in his place.
  • Kristaps Porzingis remains away from the Warriors' facility dealing with an undisclosed health issue, and coach Steve Kerr said he has been in contact with Porzingis as he recovers. The team is unsure when he will rejoin the group. Al Horford said he has not had a chance to speak with Porzingis and confirmed Porzingis has not been around the facility.
  • The NBA Europe expansion is generating significant financial interest but running into resistance from EuroLeague clubs. JPMorgan's global co-head of sports said he has "the highest degree of confidence" that some NBA Europe franchises will reach $1 billion in value. The NBA and FIBA released a joint statement reaffirming their commitment to a new European league, but EuroLeague shareholders rejected the NBA's initial cooperation proposal as inadequate. Real Madrid, Fenerbahce, Panathinaikos, ASVEL and Bayern Munich are among the clubs still open to talks, while the EuroLeague separately announced plans to expand to 24 teams in 2027.
  • Ja Morant and Damian Lillard are generating real optimism in Portland as the Trail Blazers' two-point-guard experiment takes shape. Coaches and teammates believe Morant can return to the level he played at before his injury history derailed him, after 19 minutes in Wednesday's preseason game provided enough encouragement.
  • A'ja Wilson won her record fifth WNBA MVP award, becoming the first player in league history to win three consecutive MVPs, per Shams Charania. Wilson also became the first player in WNBA history to average 25 points, 50 percent shooting and 40 percent from three in the same season.
  • Steve Kerr said it would be "a tragedy" if the Trail Blazers left Portland over arena lease negotiations, comparing the situation to what happened in Seattle. "I would hope we have learned as a league since the Seattle debacle just how important franchises are," Kerr said.
