NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade expected before draft, Celtics made offer, Aaron Gordon drawing interest

Giannis Antetokounmpo's time in Milwaukee appears to be ending. The window between the Finals and the draft has emerged as the most likely moment for a trade to happen, per Marc Stein, who reported that every executive, coach and agent around the league brings up Giannis's status unprompted. The Heat have a deal framework in place that would send Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jacquez Jr. and the No. 13 pick to Milwaukee along with additional draft compensation, according to league sources. Any acquiring team should know that Giannis becomes extension-eligible six months after a trade is completed, and Stein made clear that no team giving up that kind of asset package can afford to not lock him up the moment that window opens. The Celtics have also made a formal offer, with Bill Simmons reporting that Boston submitted a bid in the past week, though he said he didn't know the specifics of what was in it.

The Knicks were in on Giannis too, at least last summer. Sources told ESPN that Antetokounmpo let it be known in league circles he would welcome a trade to New York, and the Knicks were interested, but one source familiar with the discussions said Milwaukee was never truly serious about moving him. That conversation is moot now that New York has its championship, but it adds context to how broadly Giannis's availability has been floated. The Bucks' preference, per Stein, would be to move Jaylen Brown in any deal for assets rather than keep him, partly because so many of Milwaukee's picks are already gone from the Damian Lillard trade. Portland has come up frequently in those discussions for that reason.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Aaron Gordon is the Nuggets veteran generating the most trade interest around the league, per Stein. Gordon turns 31 in September and is entering the first year of a three-year, $103.6 million extension he signed after Denver won the 2023 title.
  • • The Pistons are expected to pursue shooting upgrades through trades rather than free agency, a decision tied directly to upcoming extension talks with restricted free agent-to-be Jalen Duren and Ausar Thompson, both of whom will be rookie-scale extension eligible starting July 6.
  • • The Lakers are widely expected to re-sign Austin Reaves. His comfort in Los Angeles and his developing backcourt partnership with Luka Doncic are both regarded as strong indicators the sides will reach a deal.
  • • The Raptors' front-office search last summer extended further than publicly known. Donnie Nelson was quietly interviewed by Toronto before the team announced Bobby Webster as Masai Ujiri's full-time successor in August 2025, and former Raptors coach Dwane Casey, currently in an advisory role with Detroit, also factored into the search.
  • • Oddsmakers have the champion Knicks as the fourth favorite to win next year's title despite finishing the postseason with the largest point differential (+283) in NBA playoff history, per ESPN Research. The Spurs (+250), Thunder (+260) and Celtics (+550) are all listed ahead of New York entering an offseason that could reshape the East.
  • • Jalen Brunson became just the fourth player listed at 6-foot-2 or shorter to win Finals MVP, joining Stephen Curry (2022), Tony Parker (2007) and Isiah Thomas (1990). When asked if leaving $113 million on the table to give the Knicks roster flexibility was worth it, Brunson said "100% worth it." Knicks coach Mike Brown, for his part, said he admired the gesture but was honest: "Every time they would've thrown that number in front of me, I would have said 'no.'"
  • • Brunson and his father Rick shared a moment on the court after the win. "Those drives in the car, days at the park, you dream and talk about what you can be," Rick said. Jalen added: "To be with him makes it 10x better."
  • • Victor Wembanyama finished with 19 points on 7-for-19 shooting in the series-clinching Game 5 loss. "The margin of error is very, very thin," Wembanyama said afterward. His playoff averages of 23.8 points, 10.9 rebounds and 3.5 blocks have only been matched in Finals appearances by Kareem Abdul-Jabbar and Hakeem Olajuwon. One note from the series: the Spurs outscored the Knicks by 5.4 points per 100 possessions with Dylan Harper on the court, a stat that raises questions about how head coach Mitch Johnson distributed his minutes.
  • • Spurs veteran Harrison Barnes was measured after the loss. "There's no guarantee that this group will ever have the opportunity to achieve that," Barnes said. "Some players in this room may be able to get back to the Finals, some players in this room may be able to win a championship."
  • • Jaylen Brown was gracious after the Celtics' playoff exit. "Jalen Brunson is a dog," Brown said. "It is painful to see, painful to see, but you got to tip your cap." He also left a clear message about his own future: "You have not seen the best version of Jaylen Brown."
  • • The New York championship celebration turned violent overnight. At least 63 people were arrested, with four stabbings and a shooting reported. A separate scene in Times Square saw a school bus set on fire. City officials say the victory parade, which begins early in the morning, is expected to be calmer.
  • • The Jazz hold the No. 2 pick in the draft, and draft analyst J. Kyle Mann told Bill Simmons on The Ringer podcast that Utah is likely to select Darryn Peterson with that selection. "I've heard they like Peterson, I've heard Danny likes Peterson," Mann said.
  • • The Grizzlies are committed to staying in Memphis long-term. Greater Memphis Chamber Interim CEO Duncan Williams said after a June 9 meeting with team president Jason Wexler that the Grizzlies plan to remain in the city for "multiple decades" and that a new lease agreement is not a matter of if but when.
