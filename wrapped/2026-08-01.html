NBA Rumors Wrap: Watson sign-and-trade market heats up, Cavaliers pursue multiple free agents, Wembanyama signs Nike extension

The Denver Nuggets are facing a crowded sign-and-trade market for restricted free agent Peyton Watson, with the Milwaukee Bucks, Cleveland Cavaliers, Atlanta Hawks, and Los Angeles Clippers all expressing interest, per HoopsHype. Denver's asking price has shifted to at least one first-round pick and one notable player in any deal. The Nuggets have made clear they want to retain Watson, so suitors face the challenge of meeting a steep price or watching him stay put. The Cavaliers' interest is particularly notable given they are simultaneously pursuing Jonathan Kuminga and DeMar DeRozan as they regroup after missing out on LeBron James.

The Bucks haven't offered a first-round pick yet, but they'd consider absorbing Nuggets forward Zeke Nnaji as part of a Watson deal. Nnaji is owed $14.93 million over two seasons, including a player option for 2027-28.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Cavaliers are also deep in pursuit of Kuminga, with sources saying he wants a deal in the $15-20 million annual range. Any sign-and-trade would create a hard cap at the first apron for Cleveland, and the Hawks would need to cooperate by taking back either Max Strus or Dennis Schroder. The Cavaliers have been in contact with Kuminga's representatives throughout the week trying to find a financial pathway.
  • Cleveland is also working toward adding European forward Mario Hezonja, with the team navigating the process of getting him released from his Real Madrid contract. There's no concern the agreed-to deal is in jeopardy, and Cleveland expects Hezonja to be part of their regular rotation to help fill the frontcourt void left by Dean Wade's departure to Philadelphia.
  • The James Harden situation in Cleveland looks manageable. Harden's representatives have discussed two- or three-year deal structures with the Cavaliers, with both sides working together on a contract that pays Harden appropriately while preserving some financial flexibility for the franchise.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves, after missing out on LeBron James, have expressed interest in Kuminga with their starting power forward spot available and the $6.06 million taxpayer mid-level exception in hand. That adds another team to an already crowded field chasing the 23-year-old.
  • Restricted free agent Bennedict Mathurin has a wide market, with the New Orleans Pelicans, Chicago Bulls, Bucks, and Dallas Mavericks all showing sign-and-trade interest alongside the Clippers looking to re-sign him. The Pelicans and Bulls are the most engaged suitors, while a source told Marc Stein that Dallas has not expressed interest at a serious level. Chicago's front office has a stated preference for short-term deals and roster flexibility, and one source told The Athletic that Chicago's reported interest has been overstated.
  • To create room to chase Mathurin, the Pelicans have explored trading Jordan Hawkins, including discussions with the Grizzlies involving AJ Johnson. Shedding Hawkins' $7.02 million salary would keep New Orleans below the luxury tax while freeing up their non-taxpayer mid-level exception.
  • Speaking of Memphis, both D'Angelo Russell and AJ Johnson are not expected to remain with the Grizzlies by the start of the season. Despite adding Isaiah Stewart, Quinten Post, and the return of Zach Edey, Memphis still wants to retain Olivier-Maxence Prosper, who averaged 10.0 points on 55 percent shooting and 40.5 percent from three last year.
  • The Miami Heat have had discussions with representatives for Bradley Beal and DeMar DeRozan, but their top priority is Klay Thompson, who remains under contract with the Mavericks at $17.5 million this season. Any path to Thompson would presumably require a trade.
  • Victor Wembanyama has signed a long-term extension with Nike that includes a signature shoe line, per Shams Charania. The Wemby 1 is in development, along with a new personal logo. Nike said in a statement that Wembanyama is "a generational talent who continues to inspire the game's next generation." Three NBA seasons in, Wembanyama has reached the Finals, been a Defensive Player of the Year, a max contract signee, and now a signature athlete.
  • The Brooklyn Nets could use remaining cap space to renegotiate and extend Michael Porter Jr., raising his 2026-27 salary up to $45.3 million and adding up to four seasons. The catch is it would trigger a six-month trade restriction, making August 4 a meaningful date given a February 4 trade deadline. Porter, speaking on a podcast, said he's been trying to stay out of the public eye during negotiations: "I'm in contract negotiations. I was trying to stay away from the podcast world." He also said he wants to retire within five or six years.
  • Joe Tsai and Clara Wu Tsai confirmed their divorce will not affect ownership of the Nets, Liberty, or Barclays Center. Joe will remain governor of the Nets, Clara will remain governor of the Liberty, and both will stay on as co-chairs of Brooklyn Sports & Entertainment. The couple said they plan to involve their children in the long-term ownership of the teams.
  • The ownership of the Portland Trail Blazers situation is drawing frustration at the league level. Chris Mannix said the NBA is exasperated with how owner Tom Dundon is managing the process, while reporter Bill Oram said the responsibility for how things have unfolded lies entirely with Jody Allen: "I don't think it is an overstatement to say all of it." As for relocation destinations, Mannix said Nashville is off the table and could end up as a landing spot for the Grizzlies before the end of the decade.
  • Terry Rozier has asked a federal judge to dismiss two of four charges brought by the DOJ over the past year. His lawyers argue he never agreed to manipulate his performance and did not know anyone was betting on his stats during a March 2023 game when he was with the Hornets.
  • Dwane Casey has withdrawn from consideration for a coaching role with Rome's new Serie A basketball team, choosing to remain in the NBA as a Pistons adviser, per Marc Stein. Rome had made a strong push, with Donnie Nelson, who worked with Casey in Dallas, leading the pursuit.
  • LeBron James may commute to Philadelphia by helicopter from New York City after agreeing to join the 76ers, though his living arrangements haven't been finalized. The trip would take roughly 45 minutes, though fog in the corridor and New York's restrictions on commercial helipads could complicate things. LeBron's arrival also carries big commercial weight: the Sixers' jersey patch deal with Crypto.com expires after this season, and his presence could make the next partnership one of the most coveted in the league.
