NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's Lakers future clouded by friction, Wemby powers Spurs to series lead, draft lottery shakeup

LeBron James' future with the Los Angeles Lakers is murkier than ever, and a new ESPN deep dive makes clear the tensions behind the scenes are real and personal. A source familiar with James' thinking told ESPN that the Lakers' approach in showing their interest will matter beyond the dollar amount, with one agent telling the outlet "The Lakers can't just offer James a number. They have to offer a why behind that number." Sources told ESPN that James felt the organization tried to "push him out the door" after acquiring Luka Doncic, and that he simmered over what he saw as repeated indignations, including Rob Pelinka presenting the game ball to coach JJ Redick rather than James after his historic 1,229th career win. For the first time in his career, per Tim Bontemps, James is not the Lakers' top offseason priority. The relationship with Redick, at least, appears solid, with one source calling his hiring "one thing the Lakers got right."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The San Antonio Spurs lead the Minnesota Timberwolves 3-2 in the Western Conference semifinals after Victor Wembanyama bounced back from his first career ejection in dominant fashion in Game 5. San Antonio's paint production was the most by any team in a postseason game against a Rudy Gobert-anchored defense since 1998. Wembanyama had his own read on whether he needed to come out upset or composed: "I feel they ain't mutually exclusive. I'm looking for both."
  • Dylan Harper turned 20 in March and has already crossed 250 minutes in his playoff career. Spurs teammate Carter Bryant summed up Harper's sacrifice with the deep roster: "If he played for any other team in the league, he'd be starting and probably be winning the Rookie of the Year right now."
  • Commissioner Adam Silver addressed the tanking problem directly, calling this past season's behavior "unprecedented." The league is bringing a flat-odds lottery proposal to owners at the end of May that would give teams picking fourth through 10th an equal 8.1% shot at the top pick, with worse odds reserved for the three worst records in the league. The new system would run for three years and include league authority to strip lottery balls from teams that appear to be not trying to win.
  • That proposal aligns with Marc Stein's reporting that the new "3-2-1" lottery format is expected to win approval at the May 28 Board of Governors vote. Several executives told Stein they expect the looming lottery changes to reduce the number of future picks traded for star players in the near term, as front offices wait to see how the new system plays out.
  • At the NBA Draft Combine, Washington Wizards' selection of AJ Dybantsa at No. 1 is not the done deal some broadcasts made it seem. A high-ranking scout from another lottery team told CBS Sports "Absolutely not" when asked if Dybantsa was a certainty, adding the team was "surprised the broadcast made it seem like such a foregone conclusion."
  • As for the No. 2 pick, most sources at the combine expect the Utah Jazz to take Kansas guard Darryn Peterson, with nearly the same confidence level as Washington taking Dybantsa at No. 1. The immediate post-lottery speculation about Utah trading up to grab Dybantsa given the organization's BYU ties is purely speculative at this stage, per ESPN.
  • The Philadelphia 76ers will hire a new head of basketball operations this offseason, and that person will work alongside Bob Myers, the four-time championship executive who serves as president of sports across the Josh Harris and David Blitzer portfolio, per Shams Charania. The Sixers' front office failures under Daryl Morey have drawn scrutiny, with ESPN's Tim MacMahon saying Morey's "biggest failing as a basketball decision maker is he's never been a guy who valued, or even frankly considered, the human element. He does not believe in basketball chemistry."
  • A Joel Embiid-for-Domantas Sabonis swap with the Sacramento Kings is one longshot scenario that has come up in league circles, though Stein called "longshot" an understatement given Embiid's durability concerns at 32.
  • The Boston Celtics made a call to the Milwaukee Bucks before February's trade deadline about Giannis Antetokounmpo, but Bleacher Report's Jake Fischer says Boston never loomed as a very serious suitor and he does not expect that to change. The Celtics have said publicly they love Jaylen Brown and feel good about him.
  • Speaking of Brown, he opened up in a wide-ranging conversation about the intersection of sports and politics, recalling that at 19 years old he told a Guardian reporter that "sports is a mechanism for control," which went viral and earned a rebuke from then-GM Danny Ainge: "Danny Ainge came and talked to me like, 'What is this?'" Brown said it also led to an invitation to speak at Harvard. On his future in Boston, Brown was measured: "At some point, maybe you make that decision where it's not going to work out here. Maybe I need to go somewhere else."
  • The Detroit Pistons have struck a local TV deal with Scripps Sports beginning in 2026-27, the first NBA local rights agreement for Scripps and the first time Pistons games will air over-the-air since FanDuel Sports Network Detroit folded. Meanwhile, the Charlotte Hornets are among the teams weighing a deal with streaming platform Victory+, per Sports Business Journal.
  • Terry Rozier and the NBA appeared before an arbitrator last month for a second time to argue whether Rozier should have been paid his $26.6 million salary this season, three sources told The Athletic. Rozier faces federal charges over an alleged involvement in a sports gambling scheme.
  • The basketball world continues to grieve the loss of Brandon Clarke. Jaren Jackson Jr. posted a heartfelt tribute on Instagram: "I couldn't imagine a world where this was even a possibility. i love you forever brother, this is absolutely devastating."
  • Hall of Famer Tracy McGrady has taken a role as strategic advisor for Wagner College men's basketball, joining his son Laymen who committed to the program. McGrady will lead basketball operations, recruiting and NIL efforts for the school.
