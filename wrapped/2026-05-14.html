NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's Lakers future clouded by tension, Wembanyama leads Spurs back, new lottery rules take shape

LeBron James' path back to the Los Angeles Lakers is complicated, and a new ESPN report by Dave McMenamin and others lays out why. Sources familiar with James' thinking told ESPN that the Lakers cannot simply offer him a contract number — they need to explain the vision behind it, with one agent telling the outlet "The Lakers can't just offer James a number. They have to offer a why behind that number." The report details a specific flashpoint: after a win in which James surpassed Kareem Abdul-Jabbar for the most victories in NBA history, Rob Pelinka presented the game ball to coach JJ Redick for his 100th win, not to any of the players, and James left without changing clothes. Sources close to James say the organization has repeatedly made him feel taken for granted, particularly since acquiring Luka Doncic, with one source telling ESPN the Lakers tried to "push him out the door." For his part, James has a "great" relationship with Redick, per sources, calling the hire "one thing the Lakers got right," and the Lakers' own sources describe the overall relationship as mostly "harmonious." What happens next depends on whether the front office can construct an argument that convinces James Los Angeles still has a real plan built around him.

The broader picture for James this summer is murky. Tim Bontemps said on ESPN that "for the first time maybe in his career truly, he is not the number one priority going into the summer when he's up for a contract," with Austin Reaves' opt-out complicating the Lakers' cap situation. Jake Fischer reported that the prevailing sentiment around the league is that James, wherever he lands, "would be best suited to take a minimum salary contract if he does care ultimately about winning another championship." Tim MacMahon added that Reaves is clearly set to be Doncic's long-term co-star, barring a dramatic reversal.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Victor Wembanyama responded to his first career ejection in Game 4 with a dominant Game 5, and the San Antonio Spurs now lead the Minnesota Timberwolves 3-2 in the Western Conference semifinals. Before the game, Devin Vassell said the team needed to see an "upset Vic," while coach Mitch Johnson preferred the word "mature." Wembanyama had a better idea: "I feel they ain't mutually exclusive. I'm looking for both."
  • Stephon Castle said Wembanyama's bounce-back was a reminder of his ceiling. "I feel like we got the Vic you've seen all year," Castle said. "When he's playing like that, playing aggressively, with everything he brings for us defensively, I feel like we're hard to beat." The Spurs' paint production in the win was the second-most by any San Antonio team in the postseason since 1998.
  • Rookie Dylan Harper, who just turned 20 in March, has crossed 250 playoff minutes and looks increasingly at home in October moments. Fellow Spurs rookie Carter Bryant put it plainly: "If he played for any other team in the league, he'd be starting and probably be winning the Rookie of the Year right now."
  • OG Anunoby is expected to play in Game 1 of the Eastern Conference Finals after participating in portions of practice, per Shams Charania. Sources told Charania there is genuine optimism about his availability barring any setbacks.
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo trade speculation continues to swirl around the Boston Celtics, but Jake Fischer poured cold water on that connection. "I do not think Boston ever loomed as a very serious suitor," Fischer said, adding that Boston has been consistent in saying it feels good about Jaylen Brown. Brown, for his part, said publicly that sometimes a player reaches a point where "it's not going to work out here. Maybe I need to go somewhere else."
  • The Philadelphia 76ers are restructuring their front office with Bob Myers serving as an oversight partner while the team hires a new head of basketball operations, per Shams Charania. Myers, the four-time championship executive who built the Golden State Warriors, is now president of sports across the portfolio of owners Josh Harris and David Blitzer, and will help shape the Sixers' search for Daryl Morey's replacement. ESPN's Tim MacMahon had sharp words for the outgoing executive: "Daryl's arrogance has backfired on him repeatedly over the years. He does not believe in basketball chemistry."
  • Marc Stein reports that one longshot trade scenario being floated by rival team strategists involves a Joel Embiid-for-Domantas Sabonis swap with the Sacramento Kings, though Stein says "even longshot might be underselling it." Embiid's durability concerns at 32 significantly shrink the field of potential suitors.
  • Commissioner Adam Silver addressed tanking directly at the draft combine, calling this season's level of it "unprecedented." The new lottery reform proposal, expected to win approval at a May 28 Board of Governors meeting, would create a flat-odds system where the three worst teams actually receive worse odds than teams finishing fourth through the lottery line. Silver also said the league is pursuing authority to remove draft lottery balls or alter draft order as penalties for teams not trying to win, beyond just financial fines. The system would sunset after three years.
  • At the draft combine, there is less consensus on the top picks than the broadcast suggested. A high-ranking scout from another lottery team told CBS Sports he was "absolutely not" certain AJ Dybantsa was a lock at No. 1 for the Washington Wizards: "We were surprised the broadcast made it seem like such a foregone conclusion." Most around the combine expect Darryn Peterson to go No. 2 to the Utah Jazz, with sources speaking about that with nearly the same confidence level as Dybantsa at No. 1.
  • As for whether Utah might trade up to No. 1 to grab Dybantsa given the organization's BYU ties, ESPN reported there is "no indication that it will happen at this stage." The Jazz are in a strong position at No. 2 and can afford to wait and see what Washington does.
  • The new lottery format will also affect how executives approach trades. Marc Stein wrote that multiple front office executives at the combine told him they expect to see fewer future picks traded for star players in the near term, as teams get a feel for how the looming lottery changes affect the value of those assets.
  • The Detroit Pistons have struck a deal with Scripps Sports for local broadcast rights starting in 2026-27, moving their games over-the-air after FanDuel Sports Network Detroit folded following this season. It will be the first time local Pistons games air over-the-air since the RSN era began. The Charlotte Hornets and Brooklyn Nets are among teams still sorting out their own broadcast situations, with the Hornets among those considering a deal with streaming platform Victory+.
  • Tracy McGrady has taken a role as strategic advisor for Wagner College's men's basketball program, joining his son Laymen, who committed to play there. McGrady will oversee basketball operations, recruiting and NIL efforts, joining a growing list of former NBA players moving into college administrative roles.
  • The NBA and former Miami Heat guard Terry Rozier appeared before an arbitrator last month for a second time to argue whether he should have been paid his $26.6 million salary this season, as Rozier continues to face federal charges related to an alleged sports gambling scheme.
  • The basketball community is grieving the loss of Brandon Clarke. Jaren Jackson Jr. posted on Instagram: "I couldn't imagine a world where this was even a possibility. I love you forever brother, this is absolutely devastating."
