NBA Rumors Wrap: Stacey King passes away, Trae Young nears $120M Wizards deal, Portland would trade for Giannis without extension

Stacey King, the former Chicago Bulls reserve and beloved franchise broadcaster for more than 20 years, passed away at 59, per Shams Charania. King was the No. 6 overall pick in the 1989 draft and won three championships with Chicago's first dynasty, but his legacy grew far larger in the broadcast booth, where his catchphrases and energy became a fixture for generations of Bulls fans. He had extended his career into podcasting with "Gimme The Hotsauce," now in its sixth year. Scottie Pippen said King was "a champion, a great teammate, and a true ambassador for the game," while ESPN's Nick Friedell wrote that Stacey "always left you with a smile on your face." Reporter Steph Noh noted that King would stay 45 minutes to an hour after every game for two decades just to take pictures with Bulls fans.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Trae Young is expected to sign a new deal with the Washington Wizards worth approximately $120 million over three seasons, per Marc Stein. Young has until June 23, the first night of the draft, to activate or decline his player option, and is also eligible for a three-year extension worth nearly $160 million until June 30.
  • • The Portland Trail Blazers would still pursue Giannis Antetokounmpo in a trade even without a commitment to a long-term extension, Charania said. "I'm pretty sure that Portland would do a couple players, starters, and a first-round pick for Giannis, no matter the risk-reward," Charania said. "But the price is not going to be a couple rotational players and a first-round pick." Brian Windhorst separately said he has not heard that Boston is on Giannis's wish list, and that until both that and an extension commitment are confirmed, a trade to the Celtics is not in the world of reality.
  • • Windhorst also pushed back on the idea that Jaylen Brown or Derrick White could be moved this summer. "I have not heard one iota of Jaylen Brown truly being available or Derrick White being available," he said, calling the Boston trio of Brown, Tatum and White a proven championship combination that remains under contract.
  • • With the Knicks headed into Game 3 of the NBA Finals at MSG on Monday night, Landry Shamet has become one of the hottest names in the upcoming free agency market. He's shot nearly 55 percent from the floor and 50 percent from three in New York's 13-game playoff winning streak, and Marc Stein reports that external suitors are already circling ahead of the June 30 free agency period. Coach Mike Brown had lobbied for the team to re-sign Shamet last summer, telling him directly, "I want you here."
  • • Karl-Anthony Towns has one guaranteed season left on his deal, with a player option worth $61 million for 2027-28 and eligibility for a four-year, $272 million extension. If the Knicks win it all, that conversation figures to happen quickly. Draymond Green said KAT has changed the league's perception of him entirely with his playoff intensity, calling it "the key to why they're having the success that they've had."
  • • Ticket prices for the Knicks' Finals home games at MSG have surpassed almost every Super Bowl ever tracked by SeatGeek, with the only exception being Super Bowl LVIII in Las Vegas. Josh Hart said he wishes prices weren't so high: "$7K, $8K is ridiculous," he said. "A lot of people who have been waiting for this moment for a very long time unfortunately aren't able to get into the building."
  • • Victor Wembanyama on facing elimination in the Finals: "There's really no reason to overthink it. This is what I'm built for." Rookie Dylan Harper said his mindset heading into Game 3 is simply "desperation," adding that he feels he's at his best when playing with that kind of fire. Greg Popovich addressed the team before they boarded the plane after Game 2, telling them to stop thinking about the lost opportunities and focus on the one in front of them.
  • • Donald Trump's presence at Game 3 will affect media access at MSG. The NBA notified reporters that both locker rooms and courtside sidelines will be closed to the press before the game, limiting access typically used to gather quotes and build stories ahead of tip-off.
  • • The Philadelphia 76ers are promoting Jameer Nelson to Executive Vice President of Basketball Operations under new president Mike Gansey, making the Philly native the No. 2 executive in the front office. Elton Brand will not return as GM and is working through a new role within the organization and its parent company Harris Blitzer Sports & Entertainment. Assistant GM Prosper Karangwa has also agreed to a multiyear contract extension.
  • • VJ Edgecombe talked about his rookie season with HoopsHype, saying Kyle Lowry, Eric Gordon and Paul George were his biggest mentors on and off the court. On Wembanyama: "He's not real. I don't think he's real."
  • • Nikola Vucevic is widely expected to leave Boston this summer after a fractured right ring finger in March cut short his time with the Celtics, per Stein. Meanwhile, Bogdan Bogdanovic has decided to remain in the NBA for at least another season rather than return to Europe, though whether he stays with the Clippers or moves elsewhere remains open.
  • • Jon Scheyer is not expected to leave Duke for the pros, even if it means a reunion with Mavericks star Cooper Flagg. Indications are strong he wants to stay with the Blue Devils, where he just added two coaches to his staff this week.
  • • Malik Beasley remains under investigation in the NBA's gambling probe, and no team will sign him until it is resolved. The Pistons hold his non-Bird rights and could offer a starting salary of $7.2 million if and when he is cleared. Isaiah Stewart's name will likely come up in trade talks this offseason, though the team expects him to return, with two years and $30 million left on his deal.
  • • Former Wizards assistant Ryan Richman has signed to become head coach of Alvark Tokyo in Japan's B.League Premier, widely considered Asia's most coveted coaching position. Richman previously led SeaHorses Mikawa to three consecutive playoff appearances.
  • • Kawhi Leonard and Cory Joseph met up for dinner in Athens during Leonard's offseason vacation. The two have been friends since entering the league together as first-round picks in 2011. Separately, Giannis attended the Monaco Grand Prix with the Mercedes-AMG Petronas team over the weekend.
