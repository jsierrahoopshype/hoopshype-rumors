NBA Rumors Wrap: Spurs steal Game 3 at MSG, Hawks extend Snyder, Raptors set to extend Rajakovic

The San Antonio Spurs pulled off a statement win at Madison Square Garden on Monday night, cutting the New York Knicks' series lead to 2-1. Stephon Castle hit clutch free throws down the stretch, saying afterward, "It's just you and the rim." De'Aaron Fox also came up big in the final minutes, and said of his late shot: "Get to your spot, and if you miss that shot, like, is what it is." The Knicks had won 13 straight games heading into Monday but Karl-Anthony Towns acknowledged they simply didn't execute: "We turned the ball over, didn't execute, didn't do what got us 13 straight wins in a row." Game 4 remains in New York, with the Spurs now firmly believing they can win this series.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Victor Wembanyama credited Castle after the game, saying "He might be the most mature player on our team, and he's nowhere near the oldest." Castle responded with a playful deflection: "Vic's probably number one. I don't go to sleep at 9pm, but I'm probably a close second."
  • • Wemby also had a clear gameplan on offense: Spurs coach Mitch Johnson said before the game that while the team never told Wemby to avoid open threes, the directive was to put pressure on the paint and the rim. It showed Monday night.
  • • The Knicks lost both the possession battle and the shooting battle in Game 3 for the first time since March 29 against OKC. Separately, New York has been outscored by 24 points in the 37:30 that Towns has been off the floor through three Finals games.
  • • Castle on the Spurs' mindset after stealing a road game: "I think our confidence has been the same throughout this series, regardless of what happened. I feel like we're just looking forward to next 48."
  • • Fox spoke pregame about the defense living in scramble situations: "If you're playing 20, 21, 22 seconds of great defense, and they make a shot, tap them on the butt, good job."
  • • Johnson on his young group's composure heading into Game 3: "This group is excited for the moment. This group will be ready to go, and if we execute to the level we can, time will tell." Johnson also said his one-on-one conversations with Wemby were about giving him clarity: "I think he's looking for what my job is, which is to help him, support him, and give him clarity."
  • • Knicks coach Mike Brown had warned before the game that San Antonio's desperation level would be heightened: "You know that your opponent, the level of desperation is going to be heightened, which means their physicality, how hard they play, is going to be heightened." He also invoked Gregg Popovich's "bunker mentality" concept as a frame for handling the noise of a Finals in New York.
  • • President Donald Trump, invited by Knicks owner James Dolan, attended Game 3 in a bulletproof-glass suite near midcourt, where NBA commissioner Adam Silver joined him in the second quarter. The motorcade drew protests along the FDR Drive on the way to the Garden. Spurs coach Johnson said pregame he had felt no inconvenience from the presidential visit: "We have a big game tonight. There's a lot going on, and I'd much rather be part of it than not."
  • • Wemby also shared that he spent his off day in the city drawing in Gramercy Park to clear his head, saying the playoffs feel like "a whirlwind." His self-review of the sketch: "Not bad. Pretty good."
  • • The Atlanta Hawks officially signed coach Quin Snyder to a multi-year extension, per HoopsHype, which first flagged the deal as one to monitor back in April. Hawks president Onsi Saleh said the extension reflects the organization's "commitment to stability and sustained growth." The next move in Atlanta is a similar deal for Raptors head coach Darko Rajakovic, who is also entering the final year of his contract. Multiple sources told Sportsnet an extension is in the works, though the timing has not been set.
  • • Oklahoma City GM Sam Presti addressed the team options on Isaiah Hartenstein, Luguentz Dort and Kenrich Williams, leaving the door open to picking up all three: "Is it possible that we just pick up the options for everybody and roll into next season? That's certainly possible. I certainly wouldn't rule that out." Presti also said Thomas Sorber is unlikely to be ready for Summer League after his ACL rehab, though he praised how Sorber handled his first pro season.
  • • Presti spoke warmly about midseason acquisition Jared McCain, saying he had followed him since high school and believes he "contributes to winning." He also defended Chet Holmgren after a difficult playoff run: "He's an underdog. We've seen that everywhere. He's been questioned every time. That's why he fits here so well."
  • • The New Orleans Pelicans officially named David Cosgrave as VP of Health and Performance and Joe Sharpe as head athletic trainer. Cosgrave spent more than 12 years in the Premier League, including stints at Tottenham, Liverpool and Fulham. A report from In the NO noted that GM Joe Dumars has no intention of being constrained by budget in rebuilding the department, and is prepared to spend whatever is required.
  • • The Philadelphia 76ers' new front office under Bob Myers is expected to use the non-taxpayer mid-level exception this summer, the larger of the two MLE options, suggesting the team plans to spend meaningful money in free agency.
  • • Gary Payton brushed off concerns about the Seattle SuperSonics' expansion process, saying he is not worried: "There's nothing that's going to snag them. We should've never lost it but we're gonna be back."
  • • Draft prospect Kingston Flemings has drawn mixed evaluations. A current NBA agent and former scout told The Sacramento Bee that Flemings projects as a "10-year starter" but not an All-Star, ranking him behind another point guard in the class.
