NBA Rumors Wrap: Euroleague rejects NBA takeover bid, Hukporti tears Achilles, KD blamed for Rockets collapse

The Euroleague owners met Monday at Villa d'Este on Lake Como and rejected the NBA's offer to acquire the tournament, though the two sides left the door open for continued talks. The NBA's proposal came with a firm deadline of October 6 and required the Euroleague to freeze its expansion plan, staying at its current 13 clubs rather than growing to 24 teams. No shareholder backed the offer, but the Euroleague agreed to pause its expansion plan as a goodwill gesture to keep negotiations alive. A new meeting between the two sides is expected to be scheduled soon, with the financial terms of any future proposal likely to look different from what was on the table Monday.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Philadelphia 76ers big man Ariel Hukporti suffered a season-ending ruptured right Achilles tendon, per Shams Charania, and underwent surgery Monday. The 76ers will likely be awarded a $1.7 million Disabled Player Exception, though cap restrictions prevent them from using it on a standard contract. They do have an open two-way slot available.
  • Kevin Durant was at the center of the Rockets' fourth-quarter collapses and locker room connectivity problems last season, according to ESPN's Tim MacMahon. "Kevin Durant was part of both of those problems. I would say you could even say Kevin Durant was at the top of the list of both of those problems," MacMahon said, adding that KD was placed in a role that didn't play to his strengths as both primary ball-handler and default locker-room leader down the stretch.
  • Anthony Davis wants a contract extension or a new deal at or near maximum salary, and that desire is well known around the league. Davis holds a $62.8 million player option for 2027-28. If he declines it, he would be eligible for an extension worth up to $275 million over four years through 2030-31.
  • Karl-Anthony Towns reiterated Monday that he does not expect to reach an extension agreement with the Knicks before the season starts. The Knicks have offered approximately four years and $200 million, which is $77 million below Towns' maximum value.
  • The San Antonio Spurs internally believe Dylan Harper is already their second-best player, according to ESPN's Brian Windhorst. "That's not a slight on De'Aaron Fox or Stephon Castle," Windhorst said. "Harper is a stud." Harper himself said his focus this season is on having a voice, knowing everyone's assignments and elevating every part of his game.
  • Fired Portland Trail Blazers broadcaster Braiden Bell, hired and let go on the same day after the team discovered old derogatory tweets, has retained legal counsel and is seeking reinstatement. His attorney Arnie Herz said in a statement: "What he is asking is whether a boy's words from 14 years ago should outweigh everything the man has done since."
  • LaMelo Ball is making an early impression on the Minnesota Timberwolves. Anthony Edwards said Ball "plays like his hair is on fire" and talked more than Edwards expected. Teammate Jonathan Kuminga added: "He's happy all the time. I haven't seen him mad yet." Coach Chris Finch said he wants the two stars to push each other, and with Ball in the fold, Minnesota plans to play significantly faster after ranking 11th in pace and 14th in offensive efficiency last season.
  • Kristaps Porzingis has been out with an undisclosed health issue and Warriors coach Steve Kerr said Monday he hasn't seen Porzingis yet and finds the situation "definitely concerning." On a related note, Warriors veterans Stephen Curry, Draymond Green and Al Horford will play Monday against the Lakers but be limited to around 20 minutes, and none of the three will travel to Portland for the back-to-back Tuesday.
  • The Warriors honored the late Don Nelson at their first preseason home game Monday, with Kerr toasting "Nellie Night" with a pregame Bud Light. Former Warriors GM Larry Riley, who worked under Nelson and helped build the dynasty that followed, said he expects the evening to feel like a celebration. "The way Nellie lived, he would want it that way," Riley said. Stephen Jackson, when asked how people knew Nelson was different, offered his own answer: "When he stopped practice because his dog took a shit on the court."
  • Kerr also said Curry told him at the end of last season he had no interest in leaving Golden State: "First thing he said was, 'I'm not moving my family.'" Kerr added he cannot imagine a luckier coaching circumstance. "I cannot even imagine a coach being luckier ever in the history of the game than I am to coach Stephen Curry," he said.
  • Bruce Brown is competing for a roster spot with the Knicks after logging 82 games last season with a title contender. He said the second apron was the common excuse he heard from teams that passed on him in free agency. "I heard that all summer," Brown said. "I'm fighting for a job right now. Any role they give me, I'd be happy to do it."
  • Dalton Knecht is not expected to make the Lakers' regular-season roster, with the team carrying 16 guaranteed contracts and room for only 15. Bronny James and Jaden Hardy have reportedly outplayed him in camp, and Draymond Green pointed to Knecht's representation as the reason behind his difficult situation in LA.
  • Victor Wembanyama spent part of his offseason training with French rugby club Provence, saying he was inspired by the sport's unity and camaraderie. It follows his 2025 offseason trip to train with Shaolin monks in China.
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander said he was caught off guard by Russell Westbrook's retirement, given Westbrook averaged 15.2 points, 6.7 assists and 5.4 rebounds in his final season with Sacramento. "He had a little bit more in the tank," SGA said. "He deserves his flowers."
  • Clippers coach Tyronn Lue said losing Max Strus will hurt the team's spacing, movement and ability to run plays off timeouts. "We are going to miss that tremendously," Lue said. Strus departed in a five-team trade that sent him to Cleveland, where the Cavs' Craig Porter Jr. now has a clear path to the backup point guard job he has been working toward his entire career.
  • The Brooklyn Nets' G League affiliate, Long Island, acquired DJ Rodman in a trade. Rodman, son of Hall of Famer Dennis Rodman, shot 55 percent from the floor and 46.5 percent from three across 39 games for Greensboro last season.
  • The Chicago Bulls signed guard Jordan Hawkins to a two-way contract. The Thunder signed Anthony Pritchard to an Exhibit-10 deal and waived Robert McCray V.
