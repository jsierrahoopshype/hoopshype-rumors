NBA Rumors Wrap: Jalen Duren feels disrespected by Pistons, Knicks load up for camp, Kawhi explains Toronto return

Jalen Duren's contract standoff with the Detroit Pistons has spilled into training camp with real tension on both sides. The 22-year-old center feels disrespected by the franchise, according to ESPN's Marc Spears. "It's not about money right now. He feels disrespected," Spears said on NBA Today. "There's a black cloud going into camp. They gotta try to fix it, and I don't know if they can in time." Duren is one of the youngest centers in the league with legitimate upside, which makes the breakdown in trust harder to paper over with a contract number alone. How Detroit manages this relationship in the coming weeks will set the tone for what is supposed to be a pivotal season for the franchise.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The New York Knicks are bringing a crowd to training camp. Bruce Brown, John Konchar, James Wiseman, Drew Eubanks, and Ochai Agbaji have all signed Exhibit 9 deals to compete for the team's two open roster spots. The Knicks would need to move a player on a standard NBA deal if they want to keep more than one of the camp invitees, so the competition will be real.
  • Kawhi Leonard addressed why he returned to the Toronto Raptors after his time with the Clippers. "I'm not really championship chasing or trying to find the best team that can win," Leonard said on the Raptors' Open Gym YouTube channel. "I didn't want to put on a thousand jerseys. My family took a liking to the city. I enjoyed it. My son was born here and he's been asking me forever when he's going to come back." Raptors head coach Darko Rajaković also shared a story with Leonard about being in San Antonio's draft room the night the Spurs traded George Hill to acquire Kawhi's rights.
  • LeBron James will earn more from his Polymarket partnership than from his Philadelphia 76ers contract, Front Office Sports reports. The prediction-market platform is paying James $15 million a year as an endorser, not an investor. Derek Jeter and Eli Manning also appeared in the Polymarket commercial but are under smaller deals, and the NBA is reportedly closing in on its own agreements with prediction-market platforms.
  • The Phoenix Suns are signing center Duop Reath, per Shams Charania. With Mark Williams sidelined for several months with a shoulder injury, the former Trail Blazers big man gives Phoenix some needed frontcourt depth while it waits for Williams to return.
  • The Sacramento Kings and Elfrid Payton have agreed to a deal, per HoopsHype. Payton, who has averaged 9.8 points and 5.8 assists over nine NBA seasons, will compete for a roster spot. Kings GM Scott Perry previously worked with Payton in Orlando and New York.
  • A potential Domantas Sabonis trade to Charlotte faces a significant obstacle: Sacramento's asking price remains too high for the Hornets and most other teams to meet, and Sabonis's desire for a contract extension is further complicating the market. "I'm skeptical of this juncture how much market there were for Sabonis at the Kings' current asking price," Jake Fischer told Bleacher Report. Fischer added that Charlotte would re-emerge as a suitor if Sacramento lowers its price, but does not expect anything to happen soon.
  • Steve Kerr is asking Draymond Green for patience as the Golden State Warriors enter a transitional season. "He's going to have to adapt a little bit, and it's probably going to be a little frustrating at times," Kerr said on the Tom Tolbert Show. If Green struggles with that adjustment, Warriors insider Marcus Thompson II has raised the possibility that the franchise could consider moving him at the trade deadline.
  • Danilo Gallinari said he was traded from the Clippers because of a player request, not from the front office or coaching staff, referring to Kawhi Leonard wanting to play alongside Paul George. "It was clearly a player request that didn't like what he saw," Gallinari said on YouTube. "I didn't say anything bad."
  • Kevin Love is seeking a spot on a contending team rather than returning to the Utah Jazz, where there was mutual interest. "I think there's some real desire from his side to get to a winning situation," Fischer said on Bleacher Report.
  • The Atlanta Hawks have added some notable names to Quin Snyder's staff. Coaching legend Ettore Messina joins as a consulting coach, while veteran executive Sergi Oliva has been hired as a basketball strategy and development advisor, and Jason Love comes on as an assistant coach.
  • Myles Turner has been elected as a vice president on the NBPA Executive Committee, replacing retired player Garrett Temple. Turner also made a $2 million investment in the HEB ISD Education Foundation in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, with the district's new arena renamed Myles Turner Fieldhouse in his honor.
  • NBA commissioner Adam Silver said the search for the WNBA's next commissioner is generating significant interest since Cathy Engelbert announced her retirement. "I can tell by the calls I'm getting how much more attractive this job is and will be in 2027 than it was in 2019," Silver told The Athletic. Indiana Fever star Sophie Cunningham said Silver reached out to her directly for input on the search, calling him "very knowledgeable and full of wisdom."
  • Michael Porter Jr. worked out with Klay Thompson before the season and came away humbled. "I used to think I was the best shooter, but not anymore, dog," the Brooklyn Nets forward said. Still, Porter is bullish on Brooklyn's season, predicting the Nets will surpass 50 wins if the young roster develops the right chemistry.
  • Brian Windhorst offered a sobering note on the business side of the league. "I have seen some of the books of the NBA teams and I am not sure that there are 30 healthy teams," Windhorst said on YouTube.
  • Mark Walter's ownership stake in Chelsea FC is being acquired by Clearlake Capital as part of an ownership transition at the club. Walter, who is also a co-owner of the Los Angeles Lakers, is exiting his Chelsea position as Clearlake takes full control.
  • A Michael Jordan jersey worn during Game 3 of the 1998 NBA Finals has already drawn a $10 million bid at auction, which with Joopiter's buyer's premium exceeds $12 million and will set a new record for a basketball jersey when the auction closes September 29.
