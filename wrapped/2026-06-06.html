NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks take 2-0 Finals lead, Kawhi investigation deepens, Giannis-Miami buzz grows

The New York Knicks are two wins away from their first championship since 1973 after holding on for a 105-104 Game 2 victory over the San Antonio Spurs in San Antonio on Friday night. Mitchell Robinson, who had been listed as questionable with a fractured right hand, came up with the biggest play of the night, stopping Victor Wembanyama twice in the final 30 seconds. "It just speaks to his resiliency, making the biggest play in the game," Karl-Anthony Towns said. The Knicks have now won 13 straight postseason games and tied the 2000-01 Lakers for the longest road winning streak in a single postseason, with eight straight road wins. Game 3 is Monday at Madison Square Garden.

The Knicks' run has been historically dominant by every measure. Per HoopsHype, their point differential of +282 this postseason is the best in NBA playoff history, topping the 2016-17 Warriors (+230) and the 2013-14 Spurs (+214). Wembanyama, for his part, took the blame for the final-seconds turnover that sealed the loss. "I threw that one away. I messed up," Wembanyama said after the game. "Am I going to regret it? Yes, of course. Am I going to use that to fuel me and to fuel us next game? Absolutely."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The NBA's investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers over alleged salary cap circumvention has reached Kawhi Leonard directly. Leonard and his uncle and business adviser Dennis Robertson have both been interviewed by NBA investigators, as has owner Steve Ballmer and other team executives. Clippers officials privately believe they did nothing wrong but are less certain about the outcome, with one source telling ESPN: "If Wachtell delivers the report that says, 'Well, we don't have it,' I don't think Adam is going to push it further."
  • The Miami Heat remain aggressive in their pursuit of Giannis Antetokounmpo, but there is a complication on his end. According to Sam Amick, Giannis has questions about what the Miami roster would look like after a trade package is assembled. "You don't want to gut your roster and go to the place you're excited to be in and have a hard time contending for a championship," Amick reported. Jaime Jaquez has been mentioned as part of Miami's potential offer, though he told reporters in Brazil this week he is focused on growth and staying ready, "whether it's in Miami or somewhere else."
  • Jaquez is also eligible for a five-year, $245 million maximum extension with Miami this summer. "I think that'd be incredible," Jaquez said of a potential deal. "It's the second contract that really sets you up for a great life after basketball."
  • Austin Reaves is drawing interest from multiple teams this offseason, but his priority is clear. "Austin wants the max," a source said, with the Lakers able to offer five years, $241 million. Teams like the Bulls and Nets could offer four years at $178 million. Whether Reaves gives the Lakers a discount remains an open question: "Is he willing to give the Lakers a hometown deal? I'm not so sure about that."
  • One speculative take on LeBron James' future has him returning to the Lakers on a two-year deal worth $25 million per year with a player option and a no-trade clause, per B.A. Turner. That structure would give LeBron flexibility while keeping his value on the open market if circumstances change.
  • The Sacramento Kings intend to move one or more large contracts this offseason, and Domantas Sabonis carries the most trade value of anyone on the roster, according to James Ham. Ham also pushed back on a separate report, saying a Kings-Hornets Sabonis conversation at the deadline never happened, per a high-ranking league source.
  • Daniel Gafford says new Mavericks president Masai Ujiri called him personally after making front-office changes but the two have not yet sat down face to face. "I'm just sitting back and watching from afar," Gafford said, adding that Ujiri "made sure he called me and we had a good conversation on the phone."
  • The Mavericks are also looking for a new jersey patch sponsor. The franchise's seven-year partnership with Chime has officially ended, the team confirmed Friday.
  • Security around Game 3 at Madison Square Garden on Monday will be extraordinary. A full perimeter closure is expected around the arena, with no bags permitted, Secret Service-level screening for all ticketed attendees, and major street closures along Seventh and Eighth Avenues. President Trump confirmed he will attend Monday's game. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said he has also bought a ticket, and made clear the two will not be sitting near each other: "I will be in a very different section of the stadium."
  • The man who ran onto the court during Game 1 in an attempt to take a selfie with Wembanyama is now facing serious consequences. Authorities say the stunt was tied to a cryptocurrency promotion scheme, with the goal of generating attention around a digital coin to attract investors, then cashing out and leaving buyers with worthless currency.
  • Adam Silver told The Athletic that Wembanyama's global profile would have been enormous in any era. "In the old days, I think a player like Victor Wembanyama might not have attracted the kind of attention he has had until he won," Silver said. "But these days, given social media and given even the uniqueness of who he is, his background, his size, maybe even a world before social media, he still would have commanded enormous attention." Silver added that Wembanyama understands he will be defined by championships, not commercial opportunities.
  • Spurs forward Harrison Barnes had a warning for his younger teammates after Game 2. "Every single year you have to begin at the start of the journey, the start of the mountain and climb, and health is a major part of it," Barnes said. "This may be the last time both teams are in the Finals for a while. We have no idea."
  • FIBA says the 2027 World Cup in Qatar is proceeding on schedule despite the ongoing war in Iran. Secretary general Andreas Zagklis said Qatari officials view the tournament as "a project of national importance" and that preparations with the 32-team field are "going according to plan."
  • Julius Erving says modern NBA basketball is just the old ABA in a new uniform. "When you watch basketball now, you are watching the old ABA game," Erving said on a recent press call, pointing to the three-point line, up-tempo play, six fouls and three referees as ABA inventions that survived the 1976 merger. He also called Wembanyama "the future," adding: "The future is now. We're going to see things we've never seen before."
  • Serge Ibaka thinks Montreal deserves an NBA team. At an NBA House event in the city on Friday, the 2019 champion with the Raptors said the market is ready, adding that playing in a francophone city would have been a great experience during his career. "I'm sure it would be amazing to live and play here," Ibaka said.
