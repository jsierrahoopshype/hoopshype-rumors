NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade timeline takes shape, LeBron's Warriors interest, Wembanyama cleared after Brunson shove

The Milwaukee Bucks are in no rush to move Giannis Antetokounmpo, and the NBA Finals may be the reason why. There is an increasing belief leaguewide that the Bucks prefer to wait until after the Finals before completing a deal, partly to see whether the Knicks squander their 2-0 series lead and potentially re-enter the bidding. The Miami Heat are widely regarded as the frontrunner, with a package centered on Ware, Tyler Herro, future draft capital and other prospects, while Portland remains the other team with a framework the Bucks are at least entertaining, per Marc Stein. Milwaukee also wants to loop the Trail Blazers into any multi-team structure in hopes of recovering some of the draft capital surrendered in the Damian Lillard trade before the 2023-24 season. Boston has been mentioned in the conversation, but one reporter said he has been told "rather reliably" that the Celtics have not been actively pursuing Giannis at this point.

The timing of any deal may also depend on how Sam Amick put it: Giannis is looking at what the Miami roster could look like after a trade and comparing it to what Boston's might look like. Bucks GM Jon Horst is known for keeping negotiations tight, and at least one team has wondered whether Miami is so far out in front that Milwaukee is not getting the package it was hoping for. Bill Simmons predicted a deal could come after Game 4 but before Game 5, saying he does not think "it gets super close to the draft."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Golden State Warriors remain legitimately interested in adding LeBron James to their core of Stephen Curry, Jimmy Butler and Draymond Green coached by Steve Kerr. The pitch would reportedly include the idea that LeBron could commute from Los Angeles to some degree without having to move his family. Staying with the Lakers is still believed to be his preference after eight seasons with the franchise, and James himself declined to comment when asked if he would take a discount to give L.A. more financial flexibility.
  • LeBron addressed his own longevity in a TIME profile, saying retirement will come when he falls out of love with the preparation. "It's up to the mind," he said. "Where the mind goes, the body will lay." On the GOAT debate, he did not hesitate: "I'm not taking nobody over me. There's no question."
  • The NBA will not issue additional sanctions against Victor Wembanyama for his first-quarter shove of Jalen Brunson in Game 3. The play was not upgraded to a flagrant foul, leaving Wembanyama at two flagrant foul points this postseason. Jose Alvarado had a pointed response: "He got away with one. That'll be the last one."
  • Knicks coach Mike Brown said after Game 3 that getting Karl-Anthony Towns more involved throughout games is a priority heading into Game 4. "It's extremely important that he's getting touches, that he's involved, not just in the fourth quarter but obviously throughout the ballgame," Brown said at practice Tuesday. He added that no one in the locker room is panicking, but the team knows it can play better.
  • Spurs guard Stephon Castle did not sound like a team planning to go quietly into Game 4. "I expect us to win the next three, but we're just taking it game by game," he said. "Those first two games really hurt, and I feel like it was an eye-opener for us. We want to punch first."
  • Wembanyama and Towns both condemned videos circulating on social media showing apparent Knicks fans physically attacking Spurs supporters in the streets and ripping off their jerseys. "We can't forget it's a game," Wembanyama said. Towns added: "Leave the physicality to everyone on the court." MSG Sports also released a statement criticizing the NYPD's "frozen zone" restrictions around the arena for Game 4, calling the actions of the mayor and police commissioner "difficult to understand."
  • Game 3 drew 16.43 million viewers for Game 2, ABC's best number for that slot since 2018 and an 88% jump from last year. The series has also become a cultural spectacle at MSG, where Spike Lee wore a custom Pope Leo XIV Knicks jersey he received at the Vatican, and Ben Stiller has been filming an HBO documentary from his courtside seat on his iPhone.
  • Jeremy Lin praised Brunson for giving up $113 million to help build the current Knicks roster. "I don't think enough people are talking about that," Lin said. "That's also historic." He also credited Towns for rising to the occasion on both ends against Wembanyama, saying "three different areas of KAT that had certain narratives already written about him" are being rewritten in this series.
  • AJ Dybantsa, the projected No. 1 pick expected to go to the Washington Wizards, watched the Spurs in the Finals and said he sees a similar path to quick success. "It won't take that long, especially with my adaptability and my work ethic," Dybantsa said Monday. The first wave of green room invites for the June draft also went out Tuesday, including Dybantsa, Darryn Peterson, Cam Boozer, Caleb Wilson, Aday Mara, Karim Lopez, Nate Ament and others.
  • The Brooklyn Nets worked out Lopez and Ament on Tuesday. Lopez, speaking on the Kevin O'Connor Show, said: "I'm super excited for that. I feel like it's going to be a good one." Meanwhile, BYU teammate Keba Keita has been impressing teams throughout the predraft process, with his agent saying he has been "outplaying bigs projected to go in the first round" and is a "no-brainer for the mid-to-late second round."
  • Charlotte is expected to make Miles Bridges available in trade discussions around the upcoming draft, after essentially doing the same at the February trade deadline. Separately, Trey Lyles is expected to part ways with Real Madrid and pursue a return to the NBA. Lyles recently changed representation agencies with that goal in mind.
  • The New Orleans Pelicans conducted a sweeping overhaul of their performance staff at the end of the season, parting ways with the majority of their medical and performance personnel. The restructuring covered athletic training, strength and conditioning, performance science, mental health, nutrition and rehabilitation. The team's G-League affiliate staff has been brought in temporarily while a full-time replacement staff is assembled.
  • The family of Chicago Bulls broadcaster and three-time champion Stacey King released a statement after his death, remembering him as "an even greater father, friend, and man." The family thanked fans in Chicago and around the world for the outpouring of support.
