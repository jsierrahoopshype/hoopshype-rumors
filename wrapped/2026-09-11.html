NBA Rumors Wrap: Federal probe targets Clippers, Kawhi-Toronto trade still on, Jalen Duren faces extension deadline

Federal prosecutors have opened a criminal investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and Kawhi Leonard, examining whether the team helped arrange secret sponsorship contracts that circumvented the salary cap, according to people briefed on the matter. The investigation is being conducted by the U.S. attorney's office in Brooklyn, which has a long history of probing the NBA and professional sports leagues. Spokesmen for the Clippers and the league did not respond to requests for comment on whether they received a subpoena. Pablo Torre, whose reporting helped surface the original scandal, said on his podcast that there is a lot more to come. The league has already handed down the harshest punishment in NBA history against the Clippers, and Torre reported that owner Steve Ballmer was fundamentally uninterested in settling before those sanctions were imposed, telling his audience: "This is what you get when you don't bargain."

Despite the criminal probe, the Kawhi-to-Toronto trade is moving forward. Per Marc Stein, the Raptors are eager to consummate the deal after placing it on hold July 9, and both sides still firmly believe it will happen as agreed upon. ESPN confirmed that if the trade goes through, the Clippers will receive two first-round picks, and Leonard will write a $700,000 check but face no suspension and keep his contract and Bird rights intact. Kawhi's odds of playing for Toronto in 2026-27 dropped from 93.7% to 84.2% on Polymarket in the hours after the federal investigation became public.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • With Clippers president Lawrence Frank suspended, GM Trent Redden is expected to emerge as the team's lead decision-maker, though no formal announcement has been made. Meanwhile, the ESPN reporters behind the original August 17 Clippers story faced renewed scrutiny after the Wachtell report came out. Don Van Natta pushed back in an email to Puck's John Ourand, writing that readers who compare the facts will see the story held up, though Ourand noted ESPN declined to make its reporters available for interview.
  • Jalen Duren is increasingly prepared to play out the season on his $9.6 million qualifying offer and become an unrestricted free agent next summer if no extension with the Detroit Pistons is reached by end of September, per a source briefed on the process.
  • Deni Avdija is coming off his first All-Star season with the Trail Blazers, averaging 24.2 points, 6.9 rebounds and 6.7 assists, but Portland may not be in position to keep him. "The Blazers are kind of a sinking ship," one insider said on the Third Apron podcast. "They are not setting themselves up to preserve the cap space they're gonna need to renegotiate and extend Deni Avdija."
  • The Knicks and Miles McBride's representatives have been in regular contact ahead of the season, with both sides hoping talks produce a multi-year extension. Cap constraints remain a complication, though a trade that clears future salary could open a path.
  • Josh Hart stopped by the ESPN booth at the U.S. Open and made clear the Knicks are motivated by being overlooked again. "I don't think we have a big target because I don't think anyone is picking us to win again," Hart said. "We're the hunters and we're not satisfied with one."
  • Dirk Nowitzki said he was skeptical when Jalen Brunson went to New York as a franchise player, but came around quickly. "I didn't think he's going to have a franchise player type ceiling in him," Dirk said. "But man, has he been unbelievable on and off the floor." On his own team, Nowitzki said the early conversations with new Mavericks president Masai Ujiri have been positive and that he hopes to be part of the franchise's future, though nothing is imminent.
  • Nowitzki also offered his read on the Mavericks' upcoming season, saying a healthy Kyrie Irving alongside Cooper Flagg could be "a dangerous combo." He added that Maxi Kleber's health is a big key and that he's heard great things about the new coaching staff's relationship with players.
  • Dallas also made an unsuccessful run at hiring Amber Nichols away from the Wizards for their overhauled front office. Washington promoted Nichols to vice president of basketball operations and personnel before the Mavericks could get her, per HoopsHype.
  • Nikola Jokic opened up in a wide-ranging offseason interview about the grind of fame. "I'm getting ruder to people who approach me. Honestly, it's tiring," he said. He also said he wants to play every game this season, pointing to the rhythm he finds from playing an 82-game slate. This summer he organized things so he could visit the stables every day to ride horses and decompress.
  • Luka Doncic is expected to remain in Europe until September 20 before returning to Los Angeles, according to a Slovenian outlet. He has been working with his longtime personal trainer and is reportedly in the best physical shape he has been in for years, with his weight at approximately 105 kilograms.
  • The San Antonio Spurs will hire Chris Kent as head coach of their Austin Spurs G League affiliate, sources told HoopsHype. Kent previously served as an assistant and director of player development at Florida State.
  • Lonnie Walker officially signed a non-guaranteed camp deal with the Denver Nuggets. Elsewhere on the roster moves front, Bennedict Mathurin officially signed with the New Orleans Pelicans, and the Atlanta Hawks signed Lamont Butler and waived Keyshawn Bryant.
  • The Detroit Pistons' G League affiliate, the Cruise, hired Steve Scalzi as head coach. Scalzi joined Detroit's staff as an assistant in 2023 and coached their summer league team in July.
  • Dirk Nowitzki also reflected on the passing of Don Nelson, saying it hit him hard when Nelson declined so quickly after appearing well at a June ceremony. "He meant so much to me in my career," Nowitzki said.
  • Dwight Howard is heading to Europe at 40, signing with Black Fortress Tbilisi in the top tier of Georgian basketball. Howard made the announcement with a social media post playing on the fact that he was born in Georgia.
