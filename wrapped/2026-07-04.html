NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's six-team shortlist takes shape, Knicks land Drummond, Lakers trade Ayton

LeBron James' free agency is down to six teams, with his agent Rich Paul speaking extensively about the process in a wide-ranging interview with ESPN. Paul told ESPN he has spoken to all 27 other teams about James, with the final contenders being the Cavaliers, Heat, Nuggets, 76ers, Timberwolves and Warriors, per Shams Charania. Paul called this the first time James is making a decision "pressure-free," telling ESPN: "He's won already. He's made good on his promise — he won in L.A. This is strictly for his happiness." Paul also pushed back on the idea that this is James' final season, saying: "No one said this is going to be his last year. Once he got the green light from Savannah and Zhuri — cause they're the only ones left in the house — once he got the green light, you can do whatever you want to do." The Timberwolves have ramped up their push, with league sources telling The Athletic that Minnesota has full belief they are in the hunt, pitching James on joining Anthony Edwards, LaMelo Ball, Jaden McDaniels and Rudy Gobert.

Paul called the Cavaliers a strong fit, citing the roster of Donovan Mitchell, James Harden and Jarrett Allen, along with ownership and front office ties. He singled out assistant GM Brandon Weems, James' childhood friend and high school teammate, as a "big, big, big X factor." Paul also flagged Philadelphia's appeal, pointing to front office executives Jameer Nelson and Mike Gansey as personal connections, and said the 76ers' potential acquisition of Jaylen Brown is the kind of roster move that could shift the calculus. The one team Paul effectively crossed off the board was the Knicks: "The Knicks have a good thing going. If the Knicks hadn't won, this wouldn't even be a question. There would be no board. He'd be going to the Knicks."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Lakers are trading Deandre Ayton to the Wizards for Jaden Hardy and two second-round picks in 2031 and 2032. The deal opens roughly $2 million in financial flexibility for Los Angeles, with the Lakers now targeting both a wing defender and a backup big man this summer. Centers Jonas Valanciunas, Kevon Looney and Andre Drummond are among those being considered for the backup role.
  • The Knicks landed Drummond on a one-year, $3.9 million deal, per HoopsHype, installing him as the backup center behind Karl-Anthony Towns following Mitchell Robinson's departure. Drummond released a statement welcoming the move: "I'm coming in with gratitude, hunger, and a mindset to work. Let's make this chapter special." If he becomes available, Jonas Valanciunas is also on New York's radar as a third center option.
  • The Rockets are trading Dorian Finney-Smith and three second-round picks to the Hornets. No additional return was reported for Houston.
  • Simone Fontecchio became the second Heat player to speak publicly about the not-yet-announced Giannis Antetokounmpo trade, telling reporters on a Zoom call Friday that it was an easy decision to re-sign with Miami on a minimum deal worth $2.6 million. "There is a real big opportunity for the Heat with the Giannis trade and what it's going to mean for the franchise and city," he said. Fontecchio added that he spoke directly with coach Erik Spoelstra before re-signing: "Hearing his voice, hearing his thought process and wanting me to stay helped me."
  • The Mavericks signed Spanish rookie Sergio De Larrea to a four-year, $16.3 million contract at the full 120% of the rookie scale. De Larrea, who helped Valencia win the Spanish ACB crown last week, is expected to join Dallas' summer league team in Las Vegas next week and come directly to the NBA next season.
  • Dusty May's first Mavericks coaching staff is taking shape. Marc Stein reports that Joe Boylan, who was previously expected to join Taylor Jenkins in Milwaukee, is now Dallas-bound alongside Willie Green, and will coach the Mavericks' summer league team. May is also pursuing Garrett Temple for a staff role if the 16-year veteran, who spent the past three seasons with the Raptors, decides to move into coaching.
  • Josh Okogie agreed to a two-year, $12 million deal with the Jazz, including a team option on the second year. Okogie shot 38.5% from three last season with the Rockets before his camp fielded offers from multiple teams.
  • Jordan Miller re-signed with the Clippers on a three-year, $15.3 million deal. The restricted free agent scored double digits in 20 of his final 26 games last season, averaging 10 points on 53% shooting.
  • Caleb Love agreed to a two-way deal with the 76ers. Separately, Celtics assistant coach Sam Cassell addressed the Jaylen Brown trade, saying: "Jaylen has done everything possible for this city. He'll be missed. But it's the NBA. Wilt Chamberlain got traded after he averaged 50 points one year." On Paul George, Cassell added: "He doesn't have to try to be Jaylen Brown. He's gotta be Paul George. We'll be fine."
  • The Thunder officially signed Otega Oweh, Brooks Barnhizer and Josh Dix to two-way contracts, filling out a full 15-man roster and all three two-way slots. Oklahoma City also announced the signings of rookies Aday Mara and Bennett Stirtz to multi-year deals.
  • The Hawks signed second-round pick Henri Veesaar to a four-year, $9.3 million deal, with the first two years fully guaranteed and the final two non-guaranteed, per HoopsHype. Veesaar was acquired by Atlanta in a trade after being selected 52nd in the 2026 draft.
  • Keyonte George drew up a successful end-of-game play during a Jazz practice and the jokes about putting him on the coaching staff turned into something real. George and the Jazz agreed for him to serve as an assistant coach for one summer league game, an arrangement that came together in the last 24 hours.
  • French guard Sylvain Francisco signed one of the richest EuroLeague contracts in history, a three-year deal with ASVEL worth up to €15 million gross, but retained an NBA exit clause that keeps his options open through July 30.
  • A 19-year-old Portland resident paid $3.07 at a Hillsboro Goodwill and walked away with a Wilt Chamberlain warm-up jacket now authenticated to the 1972 NBA Finals. Sotheby's is currently auctioning it with an estimated value of $150,000 to $250,000.
