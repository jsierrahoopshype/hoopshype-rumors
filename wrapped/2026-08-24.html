NBA Rumors Wrap: Klay Thompson signs with Heat, Giannis opens up on Miami move, Kanter ejected from WNBA game

Klay Thompson is officially a member of the Miami Heat. Thompson cleared waivers Sunday afternoon and his agent Greg Lawrence finalized a two-year deal with a player option, per Shams Charania, completing the signing for the four-time champion. Thompson had been waived by the Dallas Mavericks, and teams had the option to claim him at his full Dallas salary using trade exceptions before he hit the open market. None did. Miami now adds a proven shooter and championship-tested wing to a roster already built around Bam Adebayo and Giannis Antetokounmpo, giving the Heat one of the more star-studded lineups in the Eastern Conference heading into next season.

Giannis addressed his move to Miami in an interview posted Sunday, and his words carried real weight. "The easy thing would be to stay in Milwaukee, but I don't want to be comfortable," he said. "From a small child, I want always to have new, new challenges." He was equally emotional about his old home, saying he loves Milwaukee and will love it until he dies, and left open the door to returning there: "When I finish this cycle with Miami, which I don't know when it will end, and if, I will definitely return to Milwaukee."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Enes Kanter Freedom was escorted out of Wintrust Arena on Sunday after an altercation with Chicago Sky guard Natasha Cloud. After Cloud scored a layup late in the third quarter, she appeared to say something toward Freedom's courtside seat; Freedom stood and raised his hands before security moved in. As he was removed, Freedom told security, "I'm supporting women, and that is the reason they are kicking me out. That's unacceptable." Freedom had declared his intent to enter the 2027 WNBA Draft, arguing the league's own rules would allow it.
  • • Breanna Stewart spoke out over the weekend on the broader transgender debate swirling around the WNBA. "Knowing that rallies are happening on both sides shows the platform that the W has and the stunts for attention are just continuing to try and ride the wave that we have," she told reporters Saturday. She added that the bigger concern is people trying to divide teams and players, saying the league is "incredibly inclusive."
  • • Victor Wembanyama and Nikola Jokic put on a show in a FIBA World Cup qualifying exhibition Sunday, with France edging Serbia 91-90 at CO'Met Arena. Jokic dominated the third quarter and finished with 34 points, 13 rebounds and five assists in a brilliant individual performance; Nikola Jovic added 13 for Serbia. Wembanyama scored 19 for France, with Sylvain Francisco leading all scorers with 20. Afterward, Wemby acknowledged what he takes from those matchups: "You always learn when you play against Jokic."
  • • Cade Cunningham is leading a players-run minicamp in San Diego this week, a good sign of buy-in and leadership for a Detroit Pistons team that won 60 games last season before falling in a Game 7 against Cleveland in the second round.
  • • Rich Paul says he worked to keep Peyton Watson out of the playoffs while he was dealing with a strained left hamstring. "Guys want to play, and you have to protect them from themselves in that moment," Paul said. "Because you're up for a contract and it's the playoffs, and I've had a pretty good year, so I need to play. And it's important to protect that." Watson was eventually dealt to the Cleveland Cavaliers in a sign-and-trade that also sent $4.35 million to the Charlotte Hornets and $3 million to the Washington Wizards, per HoopsHype.
  • • Payton Pritchard is looking forward to having Mitchell Robinson on the Celtics, telling reporters "the rebounding, the toughness inside, he is going to bring it." Pritchard also mentioned his own offseason priorities: getting to the free throw line more. "Not being a flopper but drawing fouls," he said. "I have been working on it." On the departure of Jaylen Brown, Pritchard kept it brief: "It always sucks to see a brother and a teammate that you played with for a long time go, but you move on and you are ready to go to war."
  • • Nikola Vucevic said in an interview with SKWEEK that he saw the signs of Boston's rebuild coming. "With Tatum and Brown both on such big contracts, it wasn't easy for them to fit the rest of the team around those two," he said. Vucevic also weighed in on LeBron James in Philadelphia: "Maybe his role won't be the same as it used to be, when he dominated and controlled the entire game, but he can still contribute a lot."
  • • Toronto Raptors coach Darko Rajakovic isn't shy about how he feels about Scottie Barnes. In an interview in Serbia, Rajakovic said, "There's no Phil Jackson without Michael Jordan. My Michael Jordan is Scottie Barnes."
  • • Sandro Mamukelashvili, fresh off meeting his new Lakers teammates in Slovenia, helped Georgia beat Israel 101-90 in a FIBA exhibition Sunday. He finished with 20 points alongside Goga Bitadze's 22. Meanwhile, Marc Stein reported that Holger Geschwindner, the longtime shot coach who worked with Dirk Nowitzki, was spotted at a Luka Doncic-led Lakers workout in Slovenia. The explanation: Geschwindner works with Doncic on his Total Hoops Approach youth basketball initiatives.
  • • Dennis Schroeder says he wants to play another two to four years and hasn't ruled out an NBA return. "I've heard that in 2028, NBA salaries are going to increase even more," he said. "If they put 10, 15, or 20 million a year on the table, then I might think about it a little longer." If he does return to Europe, though, he says he'd go home to Braunschweig rather than boost an established EuroLeague club.
  • • Jordan Clarkson hosted his second annual JC Cares Charity Golf Invitational in San Antonio over the weekend, returning to his hometown fresh off winning his first NBA title with the Knicks. The championship celebration, he said, is already in the rearview. "A lot of us already have; the celebration is done. Back to work and the goal is to do it again."
  • • A Michael Jordan 1998 NBA Finals jersey could fetch $100 million at auction, according to the current owner, sports investor Rob Gough. Gough has held the Game 3 jersey for four years and says it would shatter the record for the most expensive piece of game-worn sports memorabilia ever sold. "I think this will be the first jersey to hit $100 million," he said. August 23 is also Kobe Bryant's birthday, and the NBA marked the occasion on social media: "Remembering Kobe. 8.23.78."
  • • NBA franchise sales are accelerating rapidly, with 11 change-of-control sales since 2020 totaling a combined $54.1 billion in value. The Sports Business Journal reports that Commissioner Adam Silver is expected to try to help smooth over the Buss family situation surrounding the Lakers, while the Timberwolves' sale to Marc Stad was described as organic, with Stad having quietly increased his stake even before Marc Lore began contemplating selling.
