NBA Rumors Wrap: Sharpe tears meniscus, Heat build out roster, Nuggets stretch Valanciunas

Portland Trail Blazers guard Shaedon Sharpe suffered a lateral meniscus tear in his right knee during an offseason workout last week, the team announced. Sharpe had surgery yesterday, performed by Dr. Kristofer Jones at the UCLA Department of Orthopedic Surgery and Sports Medicine, and will be reevaluated in six months. That timeline essentially rules him out for the start of the 2026-27 season and puts his availability for the bulk of the year in question. Sharpe was entering what many expected to be a breakout season as one of Portland's building blocks in their ongoing rebuild.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Miami Heat have been busy filling out their roster. Free agent center Nick Richards agreed to a one-year deal to fill the team's 14th roster spot, joining Klay Thompson as the Heat's major additions this offseason. Richards' contract is non-guaranteed, which gives Miami flexibility to pursue any available player for either their 14th or 15th spot as the season approaches. Only $500,000 of Myron Gardner's $2.6 million is guaranteed, adding another layer of roster flexibility.
  • The Heat also re-signed guard Keshad Johnson to a two-way deal. Johnson, a former Slam Dunk Contest winner, has spent the past two seasons in Miami. Forward J'Vonne Hadley, undrafted out of Louisville, was signed to an Exhibit 10 contract after playing for Miami's summer league team.
  • Joe Johnson weighed in on Thompson's move to South Beach, saying it reminded him of Ray Allen's similar decision years ago. "Draymond is right, it's more of a Ray Allen effect of him going to Miami," Johnson said. "To have him on a team when you have Giannis and Bam who's going to eat up a lot of space and create a lot of different opportunities. You need a guy like Klay on your team."
  • The Denver Nuggets are stretching Jonas Valanciunas' remaining $2 million salary for 2026-27. The move spreads his cap hit into $667,000 per year over the next three seasons, giving Denver more financial room this year.
  • The Golden State Warriors have been active on the roster-building front. Dalen Terry, a former 18th overall pick who spent four seasons with the Bulls and 76ers, agreed to a deal with Golden State, per HoopsHype. The Warriors also signed Georges Niang to a partially guaranteed deal worth $1.84 million of his $3.88 million salary, and Brandon Williams to a non-guaranteed deal that can grow to $2.63 million if he sticks on the opening-night roster.
  • Draymond Green offered a notable defense of Klay Thompson's legacy this week. "Everyone tries to make Klay the guy Klay was after the injuries," Green said. "I won't go for that." Green added that before Thompson's knee injury in 2019, there was a "90 percent chance" Klay was going to leave Golden State to become a No. 1 option somewhere else, injuries or not.
  • Kevin Durant walked back comments that initially seemed to slight his Warriors teams. After further reflection, Durant said the 2019 Warriors with DeMarcus Cousins was probably the best team he's been a part of on paper. "Obviously, the best team I played on was the Warriors," Durant said. "I want to say the 2019 team was crazy, but we got hurt. With Cousins at the five, that's probably the best team I've been a part of on paper."
  • Durant also spoke about LeBron James joining the 76ers, saying LeBron's decision to take a minimum contract "says a lot about his mindset." "He understands how to put a winning basketball team together," Durant said. "He's been in the league for 24 years. He should know how to understand how to do that."
  • Victor Wembanyama led France to a 92-67 win over Slovenia in the 2027 FIBA World Cup European Qualifiers, finishing with 22 points, nine rebounds and one block in just 21 minutes. He shot 6-of-10 from the field and went 9-of-10 from the free-throw line. Rui Hachimura also had a big night, scoring 29 points on 11-of-13 shooting as Japan beat Saudi Arabia 106-78 in the Asian Qualifiers.
  • The Oklahoma City Thunder announced a hybrid local broadcast approach for 2026-27 that puts non-national games on cable, satellite, over-the-air and the NBA App through a platform called Thunder+. The service costs $19.99 a month or $108.99 for a season pass. The setup is different from the DAZN digital-only model being used this season by the Knicks, Nets, Wizards, Magic and Hornets.
  • NBPA executive director David Kelly said in an interview with Front Office Sports that players deserve a share of the revenue from the incoming NBA Europe league. "It's clearly being built on the backs of current NBA players, and prior NBA players, on the whole cachet that they brought to this league," Kelly said. He also took aim at the second apron, saying it has made it "harder to keep teams together."
  • The Toronto Raptors announced they will retire Kyle Lowry's No. 7 on January 10 against Philadelphia.
  • The Sacramento Kings held a workout for Ben Simmons as he pursues an NBA comeback. Someone present at the sessions texted a reporter that Simmons "looked fantastic," though the source offered no further detail on his basketball conditioning.
  • Former NBA player Michael Carter-Williams is joining Boston-based Brunswick Sports Management as a partner to lead its basketball practice, transitioning fully into NIL representation.
