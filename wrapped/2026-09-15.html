NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade finally official, Nuggets eye Cam Whitmore, Jalen Duren situation grows complicated

The long-delayed trade sending Kawhi Leonard back to the Toronto Raptors is officially done. The LA Clippers and Raptors agreed to complete the deal Monday, with Leonard heading to Toronto for Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks, one first-round pick swap and two second-rounders, per Shams Charania. The trade was originally agreed upon on June 30 but sat on hold for more than two months while the NBA investigated the Clippers organization and Leonard faced potential suspension. The league's trade call was held Monday night, and the deal is now officially approved. Raptors lead decision-maker Bobby Webster said: "We're thrilled to welcome Kawhi back to Toronto. His impact on our organization is undeniable. We believe his leadership, experience and talent will help us build toward our next championship."

According to TSN, league sources say there was never real consideration to altering the terms of the deal, with neither side having the leverage to renegotiate even if they wanted to. The Clippers' primary delay was tied to restructuring their front office following the NBA's punishment. Trent Redden and Mark Hughes are expected to move forward as the main decision makers in LA going forward. Leonard will wear No. 2 in Toronto, a number last worn by Jonathan Mogbo.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Denver Nuggets have emerged as a team to watch in the pursuit of Cam Whitmore, per Marc Stein. Whitmore, the 20th overall pick in 2023, was recently waived by Cleveland after the Cavaliers acquired him as part of the five-team megatrade that landed Peyton Watson in Northeast Ohio.
  • The Jalen Duren situation in Detroit has gotten messy. Marc Spears said Sacramento GM Scott Perry has a longstanding relationship with Duren dating to when Perry tried to get the Knicks to draft him, and Duren likes Perry and the Kings organization. The Pistons remain focused on bringing him back and have some optimism, but Spears reported the situation is complicated: "It's not about money right now. He feels disrespected. There's a black cloud going into camp. They gotta try to fix it, and I don't know if they can in time."
  • Taj Gibson is retiring after 17 NBA seasons and will return to the Chicago Bulls as an assistant coach under head coach Tiago Splitter. Gibson wrote on social media: "From Fort Greene Projects to USC, to the Chicago Bulls, and every team and city that came after, I'm grateful for every moment."
  • John Konchar is drawing significant free-agent interest, with league sources telling Stein that Miami, New York, Orlando and Toronto have all shown interest in the wing. He's considered one of the top remaining free agents available.
  • The Boston Celtics have added a pair of camp bodies in recent days. Per HoopsHype, Devin Carter agreed to a deal with Boston and will compete for a roster or two-way spot; Carter averaged 8.9 points in 38 games for the Kings last season. The Celtics also signed guard Caleb Grill to a training camp deal.
  • Former Mavericks assistant Igor Kokoskov unloaded on Jason Kidd in a wide-ranging interview. "Jason Kidd the player and Jason Kidd the coach and person are two completely different stories," Kokoskov said. "I didn't have a good experience with him, nor do I have a particularly high opinion of him off the court or as a head coach." He also recalled leaving Dallas for Brooklyn under Steve Nash: "I was working for the worst guy in the NBA and now I have a call from the best guy in the NBA." Kokoskov did say Luka Doncic is "a gem, a diamond, a gift from God" and that it took only two practices to recognize his generational talent.
  • Danilo Gallinari blasted the NBA's $700,000 fine on Kawhi Leonard, saying Leonard should have paid the full $50 million. "He changed the trajectory of my career and put me in a difficult situation by sending me away from Los Angeles," Gallinari said on a podcast. "What happened to my NBA career wasn't decided by a general manager or an owner, but by a player who decided." Gallinari left the Clippers in 2019 as part of the trade that brought Paul George to LA, a move that helped secure Leonard's original arrival.
  • Cooper Flagg's trainer says the Mavericks rookie has had a productive offseason, spending time in Durham, Maine, LA and Dallas getting acclimated to the new coaching staff and teammates. On the Flagg-Kyrie Irving fit: "Kyrie and Cooper have had an opportunity to spend a little time together this offseason. They complement each other really well. I know that Kyrie has a ton of respect for the player that Cooper is." The trainer added that Flagg has been focusing on micro details including ball control, finishing and using the three-point line off both the catch and the dribble.
  • Draymond Green weighed in on Ben Simmons signing with the Kings, saying the move should benefit young guard Darius Acuff by taking pressure off him. "Let's face it, Ben Simmons is Ben Simmons," Draymond said. "For a GM that knows as much as Scott Perry does, that's one of the deciding factors, if not THE deciding factor behind this move."
  • The NBA's board of governors met Monday and Tuesday in New York, with Las Vegas and Seattle expansion among the expected agenda items. The league hired investment bank PJT Partners to evaluate ownership bids and arena plans tied to both markets. Commissioner Adam Silver has said expansion is not guaranteed.
  • The NBA's planned NBA Europe league is facing skepticism from stakeholders abroad about whether it can launch on schedule, though the league office pushed back directly on Monday. A league spokesman said: "We continue to target a rollout beginning in October 2027." Separately, EuroLeague is expecting an offer from the NBA to buy a controlling stake, and EuroLeague owners are scheduled to discuss any potential proposal at their Oct. 5 meeting. NBA Europe bids for the proposed 16-team league have run from $500 million to as much as $1 billion per team.
  • The Sacramento Kings have added Brendan Suhr and Jason Maxiell as assistant coaches. Suhr has spent more than 25 years as an NBA coach and executive; Maxiell previously served on the Stockton Kings' G League staff.
  • The Indiana Pacers are handing out training camp deals to Wade Taylor IV, Keion Brooks, Cameron Hildreth and M.J. Iraldi. The latter three finished last season with Noblesville; Taylor's G League rights were recently acquired via trade.
  • Houston Rockets GM Rafael Stone on getting Amen Thompson's five-year, $208 million extension done: "It was pretty easy. He wants to be here and we want to be with him."
  • Duke coach Jon Scheyer said he had no interest in leaving for the NBA, three months after Dallas pursued him during their coaching search before hiring Dusty May. "I wouldn't be able to live with myself, not seeing this thing through," Scheyer said. "I'm at a one-of-one place where not often you get your dream job when you're 33."
