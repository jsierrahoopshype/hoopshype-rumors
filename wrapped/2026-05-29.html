NBA Rumors Wrap: NBA overhauls draft lottery, Spurs force Game 7, Robinson breaks finger

The NBA's Board of Governors voted 29-1 to overhaul the draft lottery in a sweeping set of anti-tanking reforms, per Shams Charania. The new format expands the lottery from 14 to 16 teams, flattens the odds, and creates a relegation zone where the three worst teams in the league face reduced chances at the No. 1 pick. The lottery itself is also expected to become a live, televised event as soon as next year, ending the current practice of airing only the results. Commissioner Adam Silver framed the penalties for bottom-three finishers as appropriate: "There should be a penalty for performing poorly." The league also gains expanded authority to reduce lottery odds, modify draft positions, and levy significant fines on teams found to be tanking.

The lone dissenting vote was the Memphis Grizzlies, whose objection came down to one specific rule: the new system prohibits a team from picking top-five in three consecutive years. The Grizzlies own the most favorable 2027 first-rounder from Cleveland, Minnesota, and Utah, and because the Jazz hold a top-5 pick again this year, that pick cannot land in the top five in 2027 under the new rules. Grizzlies GM Zach Kleiman had participated in the league's GM committee discussions throughout the process, while team president Jason Wexler attended the Board of Governors meetings before the club cast the sole no vote. One GM around the league summed up the uncertainty the new format creates for trade planning: "I'm not trading any picks beyond 2029."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Spurs blew out the Thunder 118-91 in Game 6 to force a Game 7 in the Western Conference Finals. Victor Wembanyama finished with 28 points and 10 rebounds, while Stephon Castle added 17 points and 9 assists. SGA was held to 15 points.
  • Castle credited the Spurs' defensive focus for the blowout, saying their offensive struggles against Oklahoma City had never been the issue. "Our self-inflicted mistakes, like turnovers and allowing them to get offensive rebounds and easy buckets, is what slows us down." He also took personal responsibility for his turnover issues: "I think a lot of my turnovers were unforced. I know that they like to pressure a lot, but I don't think a lot of my turnovers came from their pressure."
  • Thunder coach Mark Daigneault said Jalen Williams is clearly not 100% but praised his determination to get back: "He's been hellbent on trying to get himself to this point." Williams was active for Game 6 with OKC's lineup otherwise unchanged from Game 5.
  • Wembanyama joins five players to post 350 points and 150 rebounds in a first career postseason. He also hit three 3-pointers in the first quarter of Game 6, a new career high for any single quarter in the playoffs. Oklahoma City had shot 49% against him in Game 5, the best field goal percentage any team has managed against the 22-year-old this postseason, per ESPN Research.
  • Spurs rookie Carter Bryant drew praise from both Castle and coach Mitch Johnson after a strong Game 6 showing. Daigneault called him "as coachable as anybody," adding that Bryant sometimes tries too hard to do the right thing. Castle put it simply: "For him to be a rookie and come in and there's no dropoff defensively — that alone says a lot."
  • Mitchell Robinson broke his right pinky finger earlier this week, and there is no timetable for his return. Robinson's injury clouds the Knicks' Finals preparation. Despite that, an East executive told ESPN: "If they win the Finals, they're not losing anybody."
  • On the Giannis front, ESPN's Brian Windhorst poured cold water on Boston as a destination: "I have not heard that Giannis, Boston, and Milwaukee are serious about it." Separately, insider Chris Haynes teased a "mystery team" still in play for the Bucks star, saying the Knicks remain involved and adding he plans to discuss the mystery team the following day.
  • The 76ers are expected to re-sign Quentin Grimes at or below the midlevel exception. A West executive put it plainly: "I do think he stays there out of pressure. You have to keep at least one of [Grimes or Oubre], if not both, after that trade. And if they don't go into the tax, they're going to get crushed."
  • The Timberwolves are expected to bring back Ayo Dosunmu on a deal slightly above the midlevel exception, roughly $15 million, though doing so may require them to move off Donte DiVincenzo to avoid the second luxury tax apron. DiVincenzo is expected to miss most of next season with a torn right Achilles.
  • Austin Reaves is drawing projections in the range of five years and $200 million from scouts and executives this summer, below his five-year max of $239 million. The Brooklyn Nets are worth watching as a potential bidder, with sources noting the team will enter the offseason with enough cap space to accommodate a max-type player.
  • A defendant in the federal NBA gambling case, Marves Fairley, pleaded guilty Thursday to seven felony counts and admitted in court to paying an NBA player to change his performance so he could bet on him. A federal prosecutor identified that player as former Charlotte Hornets and Miami Heat guard Terry Rozier, who disputed the claim through his attorney.
  • Wembanyama was not fined for skipping his required media availability after a recent game. Chris Haynes reported the league gave him a warning and cited his reputation as one of the most accommodating players to media, as voted by the Pro Basketball Writers Association, as the reason no penalty was issued. The league told him fines would follow for any future violations.
  • The NBA Draft saw more movement Thursday. Michigan State guard Jeremy Fears Jr. withdrew and will return for another season with the Spartans. Baylor forward Tounde Yessoufou, a projected first-round pick, withdrew and committed to St. John's to play for Rick Pitino. Kansas wing Dennis Parker Jr. also pulled his name and will head to Lawrence for the 2026-27 season. The NBA announced that 38 players total have notified the league of their intent to withdraw ahead of the June 13 deadline.
  • Prospect AJ Dybantsa said the No. 1 pick matters to him personally: "I been number 1 since my first rankings came out. ESPN ranked me number 1 in 9th grade. I was like 14 and I ain't drop. So I don't plan on dropping in the draft."
  • Rockets owner Tilman Fertitta reached a $17.6 billion deal to acquire Caesars Entertainment, combining his Fertitta Entertainment empire with one of the country's largest casino and hotel companies.
