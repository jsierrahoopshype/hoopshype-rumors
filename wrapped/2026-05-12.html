NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis expected to reject Bucks long-term commitment, LeBron's future uncertain after sweep, Fox questionable for Game 5

Giannis Antetokounmpo is expected to tell the Milwaukee Bucks this summer that he will not commit to a long-term future with the franchise, per Shams Charania. Antetokounmpo has one guaranteed year remaining on his contract, giving him significant say over his next destination, and the list of teams expected to pursue him is long: the Miami Heat, Golden State Warriors, Los Angeles Lakers, Minnesota Timberwolves, and Cleveland Cavaliers are all in the mix. Charania, speaking on the Pat McAfee Show Monday, framed the Bucks' situation bluntly: "Any conversation that the Bucks are going to have with Giannis Antetokounmpo, from my understanding, is going to be a very simple one: 'Do you want to stay here long-term and commit long-term in June?'" He added: "The answer, from all accounts, is going to be 'no.'" Milwaukee will now have to decide whether to trade Antetokounmpo before he can walk, or risk losing him for nothing when the window closes.

The timing matters for the Lakers specifically. LeBron James enters the offseason as a fully unrestricted free agent for the first time in his eight years in Los Angeles, and his future is genuinely unclear. ESPN's Dave McMenamin said on NBA Today that "all indicators" point to LeBron continuing his career, but the question of whether that will be in L.A. remains open. Asked directly about his future after the Oklahoma City Thunder swept the Lakers 115-110 Monday night, LeBron was careful: "None of us know what the future holds... I'll take time to recalibrate and see what's best for my future." Dwyane Wade, who won two championships alongside LeBron, said he doesn't believe anyone outside LeBron's circle has real answers: "I think one of the things that him and his entire team have been great at is they hold their cards close to their chest." Wade also said "history shows that Mr. James ain't taking a lot of discounts."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The San Antonio Spurs listed De'Aaron Fox as questionable for Tuesday's Game 5 of the Western Conference semifinals with right ankle soreness. Fox was hurt with 7:42 left in the third quarter of Monday's Game 4 loss at Minnesota when Ayo Dosunmu landed on his ankle diving for a loose ball. The series is tied 2-2 heading back to San Antonio.
  • Donovan Mitchell opened his halftime locker room talk Monday with an apology to his Cavaliers teammates after a slow first half. "I set the tone in Game 3, getting downhill in transition, and I don't think I did that one time in the first half," Mitchell said. He then responded with a historic second half to even the series.
  • The Lakers postgame had a valedictory feel regardless of what LeBron decides. Austin Reaves, himself a free agent, said playing alongside LeBron would "mean the world" to him, and recalled how LeBron found him after he missed the potential game-tying shot and told him: "Helluva shot. Keep your head up." Reaves added: "He's meant a lot to my career."
  • LeBron's numbers this postseason made the moment harder to watch. At 41, he scored more playoff points than every NBA player 41 or older combined in league history, 232 to 212. He also recorded more assists at that age, 73, than all historical peers combined, who totaled 22. He averaged 23.2 points, 6.7 rebounds and 7.3 assists and led the Lakers in both scoring and assists this postseason.
  • Luka Doncic, still recovering from his hamstring injury, said he was not close to being cleared to play and pushed back on reports that he was. "I know some people wanted me back, but obviously I wasn't close to clearing," he said, adding that there were "a lot of false rumors" about the injury online. He expects to be cleared for contact next week. Doncic also confirmed he will not play for Slovenia this summer, as he focuses on securing joint custody of his daughters. On his first full season as a Laker: "I feel very comfortable. I like being a Laker."
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander had a generous word for LeBron after the Thunder closed out the series: "It's amazing what he's doing out there at this age. It's hard to put into words. I'm not sure we'll see anything like that." SGA also had high praise for teammate Ajay Mitchell, calling him arguably the Thunder's best player of the series. The Thunder finished the Lakers 8-0 across the regular season and playoffs.
  • Coach JJ Redick declined to call the season a disappointment and brushed off questions about whether he coached LeBron for the last time. "I haven't even thought about that," he said. "We'll deal with the offseason in the offseason." On the gap between the Lakers and a championship: "That's what we got to figure out this offseason."
  • Draft combine measurements rolled in Monday, with Michigan center Aday Mara emerging as one of the big winners. Mara measured in at 7'3" and 260 pounds with a 7'6" wingspan and a 9'9" standing reach, tying Mark Williams for the second-longest standing reach in combine history. Tennessee forward Nate Ament also impressed at 6'9.5" with a 6'11.5" wingspan, and was told by a doctor after X-rays that he could still grow another two inches.
  • The No. 1 overall pick debate got sharper Monday. ESPN's Jonathan Givony told Zach Lowe he has Darryn Peterson going first, not AJ Dybantsa, who has emerged as the consensus favorite. "Not once did I come away thinking AJ Dybantsa is a better prospect," Givony said after seeing them together 8-10 times in person. Dybantsa, for his part, said at the lottery: "I'm betting on myself to be the No. 1 pick, but I can play anywhere."
  • Former NBA player Desmond Mason was arrested last Thursday in Oklahoma City on a felony theft warrant out of Texas. A couple in Collin County, Texas, filed a police report in January, alleging they hired Mason in March 2025 to frame a piece of sports memorabilia valued at roughly $40,000, paying him $9,822.86. Mason allegedly sent proof he completed the work and then never delivered the finished product.
