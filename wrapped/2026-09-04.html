NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers fallout continues, Amen Thompson signs $208M extension, Lakers ownership clarity

The fallout from the NBA's historic punishment of the Los Angeles Clippers is still reverberating, with independent journalist Pablo Torre at the center of the conversation a day after the league dropped its 35-page findings. Torre, whose yearlong investigation into the Clippers, Steve Ballmer, and Kawhi Leonard preceded the NBA's own probe, said in a Variety interview that the Clippers and a crisis PR manager hired by Ballmer were "shopping around a story" that would tell "a version of events helpful" to a man who had "repeatedly lied." Torre specifically accused ESPN of being fed that story, saying the outlet "may have known it was not true" and questioning why reporters never contacted him before publishing a piece that he says undermined his work. When Dan Patrick asked whether ESPN had apologized, Torre said: "I don't expect an apology. I'm a big boy. They got fed a story by the people who are lying this whole time, and I got implicated as a result of it." Miami Herald investigative reporter Julie K. Brown, whose work helped expose Jeffrey Epstein, backed Torre publicly, writing that it is "shameful" that the Times and The Athletic buried his role and called the characterization of his work as a mere "podcast episode" a "slap in the face."

The broader Clippers picture remains grim. Zach Lowe put it plainly, saying "the Clippers are somewhere beyond f---ed," adding that as recently as the day of the ruling, a front-office exec from another team was still floating the Clippers as a potential landing spot for Nikola Jokic. "Forget that," Lowe said. "This team is dead." Former Clippers employees told NBC News that losing five first-round picks between 2029 and 2033 is the most punishing element of the penalty: "They would pay $30 million to have their five draft picks back." Team sources also told NBC that a fire sale is being discussed, with some wondering whether Ballmer, who previously found tanking "unpalatable," might now have no choice but to strip down the roster and accumulate future assets.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Clippers-Raptors Leonard trade is still happening. Both sides spoke after the ruling and intend to follow through, with Los Angeles set to receive Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick, two unprotected first-round picks (2031 and 2033), a 2027 first-round pick swap, and two second-rounders. Shams Charania added that an extension for Leonard in Toronto is expected. The New York Times reported that Leonard's decision to cut ties with his uncle Dennis Robertson over the summer, after being shown early findings from the report, appears to have helped reduce his culpability in the league's eyes.
  • Torre disagrees with the league's conclusion on Leonard's culpability. "Kawhi Leonard is 35 years old and we have documents," Torre said, pointing to a $28 million Aspiration contract bearing Leonard's signature and specific references to Leonard in the NBA's own report. Shams, for his part, offered a more measured framing, saying Dennis Robertson "ran Kawhi Leonard's life" and that investigators simply "don't have the proof that Kawhi had the knowledge of what was going on."
  • On what comes next legally, Torre said Ballmer's options are limited. Because the NBA settled with the Players' Union, arbitration is off the table. Ballmer's remaining path is to sue the NBA outright, which Torre said would open the door to broader document discovery. Suspended owner Ballmer is barred from attending any games or practices, visiting team offices, or influencing personnel decisions for any of the league's 30 franchises during the suspension period. Clippers president of basketball operations Lawrence Frank is also suspended, for six months.
  • Thaddeus Young made the case that the Clippers are not as damaged as they look, saying the team made three trades before the ruling landed, which he believes shows the front office knew something was coming. The trio of moves, swapping James Harden for Darius Garland, trading Ivica Zubac, and moving Leonard to Toronto, all brought back draft capital the Clippers would not have otherwise had. Young said Garland alone makes the Clippers "not in a bad position."
  • Pablo Torre also confirmed in the Variety interview that the Knicks and Jalen Brunson are not targets of his current reporting. "They are not a target," he said. "At the moment I'm absolutely not focusing on the Knicks or Jalen Brunson."
  • Amen Thompson is staying in Houston. The Rockets and Thompson agreed to a five-year, $208 million rookie-scale extension, per Shams Charania. Thompson, 23, led the NBA in minutes last season while posting career highs of 18.3 points, 5.3 assists, and 1.5 steals per game. Zach Lowe reported the deal contains no escalator clauses tied to All-NBA selections or Defensive Player of the Year awards, leaving money on the table compared to Thompson's max potential. Marc Stein added that Houston will hold Media Day on Sept. 22, a week ahead of the rest of the league.
  • The report on the Lakers' ownership situation requires a correction. Bob Iger's role has been overstated, according to the New York Post. It is Josh Kushner, not Iger, who will be the lone controlling shareholder. Under NBA rules, Kushner must personally own at least 15% of the franchise, which means he will put up at least $1.875 billion. The deal remains under league review.
  • Anthony Edwards is pleased with Minnesota's offseason. Brian Windhorst reported that Edwards "is very pleased by the Wolves' offseason," including the additions of LaMelo Ball and Jonathan Kuminga.
  • Stephen Curry's contract situation in Golden State is still unsettled. Anthony Slater said he would not be surprised if Curry takes the max rather than a discounted deal, adding that the Warriors have built significant flexibility around him with short-term contracts for Draymond Green and Kristaps Porzingis. "Steph Curry gets what he wants," Slater said.
  • Kyrie Irving is back on the court in Dallas pickup runs, and Mavericks head coach Dusty May has been watching closely. "To see his level of preparation, attention to detail, and IQ," May said, "it's something that I've never seen at the level that he works at." Irving tore his left ACL against Sacramento last season and missed the entire 2025-26 campaign.
  • Luka Doncic flew his Lakers teammates to Slovenia this summer for a team bonding trip, and the new faces on the roster took notice. Kevon Looney called it a first in his career: "to go into somebody's country and bring us all out there and spend that much time together, that just shows his character." Collin Sexton added that the trip showed Luka is embracing a leadership role heading into the season.
  • A HoopsHype research project polled 112 active and former players on the GOAT debate, and the results split along generational lines. Among active players, LeBron James leads Michael Jordan 59.6% to 29.8%. Among retired players, Jordan flips it, winning 52.7% to James' 27.3%. One wrinkle: eight former teammates of James' picked Jordan as their GOAT, while 11 chose James.
  • The NBA is closing in on prediction-market deals. Sources told Front Office Sports there are "deal terms on the table" with platforms including Kalshi and Polymarket, and one industry executive said announcements could come before the end of September. The league had reportedly been close to announcing something during the playoffs before pulling back.
  • The person who sent Sparks forward Dearica Hamby a racist text has been fired. An Acclaro Medical employee was terminated after Hamby posted a screenshot of the message, with the sender's phone number, on social media. The company had initially suspended the employee pending an investigation before confirming the termination on Sept. 1.
  • Tony Parker could barely contain himself when Patty Mills walked into the ASVEL practice facility. "It was almost like a surreal moment," Parker said at NBA House Philippines. Mills headlines a veteran group at the French club that also includes TyTy Washington, Joel Bolomboy, and Jae Crowder.
