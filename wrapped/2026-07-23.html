NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's free agency drags on, Warriors rebuffed on Anthony Davis, Kuminga draws four sign-and-trade suitors

LeBron James remains unsigned more than three weeks into free agency, and the ripple effects are freezing the entire league. According to a Cleveland radio source relayed on 92.3 The Fan, LeBron's camp was prepared to announce his decision last week while in New York for the World Cup, but held back after feeling pushed by commissioner Adam Silver. "There's a little bit of irritation... Irritated was the word I was told, by the commissioner pushing them... so they decided to hold back," the source said, per Yahoo Sports. James Harden and Draymond Green are among the notable free agents still sitting on the sideline waiting for LeBron to move first. ESPN has remained consistent that the Heat, Cavaliers, or 76ers are the most likely destinations, while the Warriors are pessimistic about their chances. A Heat YouTube video titled "LeBron James Introductory Press Conference | July 27, 2026" briefly went viral, but a Miami spokesperson said the video was accidentally made public by the team's social media department while preparing contingency content.

The Warriors' pursuit of LeBron is further complicated by Washington's refusal to engage on Anthony Davis. Golden State has expressed trade interest in the 33-year-old Davis, who becomes extension-eligible on August 6, but the Wizards have rebuffed all inquiries, per HoopsHype. Washington plans to open extension talks with Davis before that deadline, and Trae Young said he isn't putting any pressure on his teammate about the money. "The last thing we're talking about is what we're making," Young said. "I know what I've got to do and what I want to do with this team."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Jonathan Kuminga has drawn sign-and-trade interest from four teams: the Lakers, Clippers, Cavaliers, and Bucks, per HoopsHype. Atlanta remains in the picture as well, with the Hawks still a candidate to bring Kuminga back on a potential one-plus-one deal. The Lakers and Hawks had preliminary sign-and-trade discussions involving Jarred Vanderbilt and a 2032 Lakers first-round pick swap, but Atlanta has balked at taking on Vanderbilt's $13.29 million salary for 2027-28, which carries a player option.
  • The Clippers remain interested in re-signing restricted free agent Bennedict Mathurin, but are hesitant to commit to major roster decisions while Kawhi Leonard's Aspiration investigation is still ongoing. That investigation has also held up Leonard's expected trade to Toronto.
  • D'Angelo Russell is not considered a long-term piece in Memphis, and both sides are expected to work toward a resolution that gets the 30-year-old to a new team this offseason, whether through a buyout or a trade.
  • The Suns hope to sign Miles Bridges to a long-term extension after acquiring him via trade. Bridges is on an expiring $22.83 million deal for the upcoming season, and Phoenix GM Brian Gregory said trading an unprotected 2033 first-round pick to Charlotte was worth it. "We got Miles Bridges," Gregory said. "That, in my opinion, tipped the scales for us." Bridges, for his part, said he wants to earn the trust of his new locker room first. "Making the locker room comfortable, having guys that want to be around me... is huge," he said.
  • Several teams, including the Lakers, Nets, Raptors, and Heat, expressed interest in Luguentz Dort before he was traded to Atlanta. The Hawks want to keep Dort beyond his expiring $17.22 million deal this season. The Dort trade also opened a starting spot for guards Ajay Mitchell and Cason Wallace in Oklahoma City. Wallace, an All-Defensive Team selection, is extension-eligible, with comparable deals for guards like Dyson Daniels and Christian Braun landing at $25 million per year and Jalen Suggs at $30 million annually.
  • The Charlotte Hornets have been linked with interest in Dennis Schröder, with Hornets executive Jeff Peterson a known admirer of the veteran guard dating back to their time together in Atlanta.
  • The Rockets are adding Chip Engelland as an assistant coach on Ime Udoka's staff, per Shams Charania. Engelland spent four seasons with the Thunder after a 17-year run in San Antonio, where he built a relationship with Udoka.
  • Victor Wembanyama has been named a cover athlete for NBA 2K27. He joins a list that includes Michael Jordan, Shaquille O'Neal, LeBron James, and Kobe Bryant. "I want to make sure it's never our case," Wembanyama said of teams failing to fulfill their potential because of financial decisions. "We have a good team, we had a very good playoff run, and we had a very good regular season. But what's even better and greater is our potential."
  • The Portland Trail Blazers are shedding their broadcast team under owner Tom Dundon. TV analyst Michael Holton, statistician Tom Haberstroh, TV host Neil Everett, and host Jamie Hudson have all been let go after their contracts lapsed. On top of that, veteran play-by-play man Kevin Calabro is stepping away after receiving what he called a "subprime" contract offer. "Easy decision. Not worth my time," Calabro said. "Which is sad because it's a great roster and the people there are great." Multiple NBA ownership-level sources told Yaron Weitzman they believe Dundon is bluffing on relocation and simply pressuring Portland's city government for arena funding.
  • Jalen Brunson had surgery to repair a tendon in his left wrist after winning the NBA Finals MVP. He wore a cast at a recent interview and said he expects to be fully healthy after about six more weeks in the cast and subsequent rehab.
  • A Wilt Chamberlain warmup jacket found in a Goodwill bin by a Portland teenager sold at Sotheby's for $89,600. Quinn Brown paid $3.07 for it in January after spotting "Chamberlain" on the back. Sotheby's photomatched it to three images from the 1972-73 season, including during the Lakers' championship run.
  • Tyrese Haliburton and Jade Jones got married at an Indianapolis courthouse surrounded by family and friends.
