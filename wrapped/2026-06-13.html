NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade timeline tightens, Austin Reaves draws max offers, LeBron's future murky

The Milwaukee Bucks are moving toward a decision on Giannis Antetokounmpo, and the clock is ticking. Multiple sources indicate a trade will likely happen shortly after the NBA Finals conclude, with the draft set for June 23 creating a hard deadline. Miami remains the frontrunner, per reporting from The Athletic, with a package expected to include Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., a guard prospect and up to three first-round picks. The Bucks are also calling rival teams in search of a third top-20 pick and potentially another pick in the top 10, with Miami's No. 13 selection expected to be part of any deal. Boston, Minnesota and Orlando are among the teams the Bucks are seeking clarity from before making a final call.

Minnesota's offer would center on Jaden McDaniels, Naz Reid, Terrence Shannon Jr. and two first-round picks, though Giannis is known to prefer an Eastern Conference destination. The Athletic also reports Portland is more likely to factor into a deal as a third team than as a direct suitor. Sam Amick floated an interesting distinction between Boston and Miami as landing spots: "In Miami you'd maybe get a little more celebrated if you get them another one," given Boston's 17 championship banners. If Miami does complete the deal, a salary structure that keeps them below the first apron would give them more flexibility to build out the rest of the roster.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Lakers' pursuit of Austin Reaves has gotten complicated fast. The Nets are expected to offer a four-year, $178.5 million contract, and the Pistons and Hawks are also among teams with the cap space to compete. One Western Conference executive put it plainly: "You can't let a talent like that walk. That would be a disaster." Internally, the Lakers view Reaves as a key fit next to Luka Doncic as a secondary playmaker, with a team source saying defensive concerns about that pairing are overstated provided Los Angeles addresses other needs on the wing and at the rim.
  • LeBron James' future remains genuinely open. For the first time in eight years, there is a real chance he will not be in a Lakers uniform next season, whether through retirement or a move elsewhere. His agent, Rich Paul, told the Pat McAfee Show that he has not yet spoken with LeBron about next season but has received calls from roughly 10 to 12 teams expressing interest. "Believe nothing that's out there because I haven't had one conversation with him," Paul said.
  • On the Kyrie Irving front, the Mavericks have received trade inquiries but are telling teams he is not available, per Marc Stein. Detroit and Minnesota are known to have interest, but Houston has yet to make any overtures to Dallas despite being frequently mentioned as a suitor. The Mavericks' messaging has been consistent: Irving is off the table, at least for now.
  • Trey Murphy III has drawn intense trade interest, with the Pistons and Pacers among teams known to have expressed interest in the 25-year-old shooter. The key context, per Stein, is that New Orleans has not shopped Murphy anywhere. The Pelicans under new president Joe Dumars are fielding inbound calls for the first time, but teams are the ones coming to them, not the other way around.
  • Jaylen Brown has drawn interest from Portland, Houston and Atlanta as a trade target, but three years and $183 million remaining on his deal adds real complexity. Brown turned in a career season and turns 30 in October, which will factor into how aggressively teams pursue a deal.
  • The Bucks are also building out their coaching staff, adding Vince Legarza and Brad Jones as assistants. Legarza previously worked under Mike Budenholzer with the Suns and Bucks, while Jones was a Grizzlies assistant under Taylor Jenkins.
  • Game 4 of the NBA Finals drew 20.9 million viewers on ABC, peaking at 23.2 million, making it the most-watched Finals Game 4 since 1998 and up 123 percent from last year's Game 4. The 2026 Finals are the most-watched through four games since 1998.
  • Victor Wembanyama spoke about the Spurs' position heading into Game 5 down 3-1: "The fact that we've proven game after game that nobody beats us, we beat ourselves. The confidence comes from knowing that our destiny is in our own hands." Coach Mitch Johnson was equally direct about De'Aaron Fox: "I've probably been fired 212 times, and we've traded Fox 72 times. People have their opinions. I don't care. De'Aaron Fox will have the ball in his hands at the end of the game tomorrow."
  • Luke Kornet is listed as questionable for Game 5 due to illness after playing just four minutes in Game 4. Meanwhile, Karl-Anthony Towns has drawn scrutiny for appearing to motion courtside fans to crowd rookie Dylan Harper on the game-ending inbound play, a moment some in San Antonio have objected to. Spurs forward Julian Champagnie addressed reports of Knicks fans throwing eggs at the team's hotel: "At the end of the day, it's just a basketball game. I don't think anyone should be getting eggs tossed at them."
  • OG Anunoby has been the standout performer of the Finals so far. Paul Pierce did not hold back in his assessment: "I'm not giving OG Anunoby no goddamn flowers. I'm giving him the Finals MVP." Anunoby said it is "really cool" to know his tip-in will be immortalized in New York sports history. Josh Hart offered a more measured note: "We can't keep getting into a hole and trying to dig ourselves out of a hole."
  • Zach LaVine is widely expected to opt into the final year of his deal with the Kings. HoopsHype's Michael Scotto said it would take something like a three-year, $100 million offer to change that calculus, "and barring anything of that caliber, that was kind of the expectation."
  • Former NBA coach Dave Joerger is in talks with Paris Basketball about becoming the club's next head coach, per BasketNews and ESPN. Current coach Julius Thomas is expected to leave this summer to pursue an NBA staff position. Former Timberwolves executive David Kahn oversees basketball operations in Paris.
  • Jeremy Lin and Carmelo Anthony have ended a 14-year feud stemming from the "Linsanity" era in New York. Lin said the two had an "honest and direct" conversation on Anthony's podcast where he asked "every last question I had." Anthony said it took years before he was ready: "It took me to go through what I went through to have the time to come back and be at peace with being able to answer all your questions with actual facts."
  • The Big3 league co-founded by Ice Cube is targeting a $290 million valuation as it plans to go public via a SPAC deal later this year, with the goal of trading on a major exchange in the fourth quarter of 2026.
