NBA Rumors Wrap: Lakers sold to Iger and Kushner at $12.5B valuation, Chris Paul eyed for front office role, Luka visits Dallas on his birthday

Bob Iger and Josh Kushner have agreed to buy Mark Walter's majority stake in the Los Angeles Lakers at a $12.5 billion valuation, a sale that came together in three business days and stunned people around the league. "The news shocked all of us," one agent with a client on the current Lakers roster told ESPN. The deal still needs approval from the NBA and its Board of Governors, but Iger and Commissioner Adam Silver have a long relationship, built through years of TV rights negotiations and the 2020 bubble restart at Disney's Orlando properties. "Bob and Adam are close," one league office source told ESPN. "They're not just business close. They're vacation-together close." Multiple sources also raised questions about whether an ongoing federal investigation into Walter's company, TWG Global, factored into the timing of his decision to sell.

Kushner will be required to sell his 5 percent stake in the Miami Heat before the Lakers purchase can be finalized, per league rules barring ownership in multiple franchises. He was previously a minority owner of the Memphis Grizzlies before selling that stake in 2024. On the basketball operations side, multiple sources told ESPN that recently retired Chris Paul, a close friend of Iger's since his playing days with the Clippers, could have a role in the new front office. ESPN's Ramona Shelburne described Iger as "sort of a mentor to Chris Paul" and said she expects the two will be in contact. Coach JJ Redick received an extension after his rookie year on the bench, and sources say his contract now aligns with GM Rob Pelinka's deal, giving both men some stability under the new ownership group.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Luka Doncic was not consulted about the Lakers sale, but he plans to have a video call with Iger and Kushner next week from Slovenia, where he is hosting teammates for a minicamp. A source close to Doncic put the ownership carousel in perspective: "The only constant in life is change. He's had five owners now."
  • The Lakers sale is already pushing projected expansion fees higher. An entry fee once pegged between $6 billion and $8 billion for a Las Vegas franchise could now climb toward $10 billion, with a prospective owner needing roughly another $2.5 billion to build an arena.
  • Bradley Beal agreed to a two-year, $13.2 million deal to return to the Los Angeles Clippers, with a player option in Year 2, per Shams Charania. Beal had declined his $5.6 million player option, giving the Clippers additional salary space to work with, before the sides landed back on a reunion. He spent most of last season rehabbing from hip surgery.
  • The new NBA schedule has a few reunion games worth circling. Luka Doncic makes his lone visit to Dallas on Feb. 28, which happens to be his birthday. LaMelo Ball's Minnesota Timberwolves visit Charlotte on Feb. 15, and LeBron James brings the Philadelphia 76ers to face the Lakers on Christmas Day. Giannis Antetokounmpo's Miami Heat visit Milwaukee on Jan. 28, and Ja Morant's Portland Trail Blazers return to Memphis on Feb. 6.
  • The Milwaukee Bucks will appear on national television just twice in 2026-27, both games against Giannis's new team, the Heat. That ties for the fewest national TV appearances in the league, a sharp fall from 19 appearances just three seasons ago.
  • New Dallas Mavericks coach Dusty May told The Athletic that a phone call from GM Mike Schmitz on May 24 started the process that led him to leave Michigan, where he'd just won a national championship. May said he had returned frustrated from Big Ten spring meetings where he expected progress on issues facing college basketball. "I go there and I was just like, 'Oh my God, nothing's getting fixed. Ever,'" he said. He added that college coaches now spend most of their time on fundraising and administrative work rather than coaching, and the NBA offered a chance to refocus. Asked if he would ever return to college, May said: "I hope not, because that would mean I did sh—y here."
  • Golden State Warriors owner Joe Lacob confirmed the team was in discussions about signing LeBron James before he chose Philadelphia. "We certainly were in that narrative and that discussion," Lacob said. He also revealed the Warriors made at least two significant trade offers this offseason that ultimately did not come together.
  • The Warriors were heavily interested in trading for Kawhi Leonard at the deadline but cooled on a deal this summer because of his looming contract extension. Leonard, for his part, wanted to land in Toronto, where he eventually signed.
  • Kevin Durant paid tribute to Russell Westbrook after his retirement, writing that Westbrook led by example and "let everything out" when the lights were bright. "He didn't say much, he just showed up. For 18 years," Durant wrote. Draymond Green was more pointed about the response to Westbrook's career, saying "basketball fans suck" for spending years calling Westbrook a liability only to turn sentimental at his retirement. Warriors owner Ted Leonisis also offered a tribute, comparing Westbrook's competitive drive to Michael Jordan and Alex Ovechkin.
  • The Indiana Pacers are waiving two-way guard Ethan Thompson, per HoopsHype. Thompson averaged 7.0 points in 20.4 minutes across 32 games last season. He is expected to sign a one-plus-one year deal with Partizan Belgrade in the EuroLeague.
  • Tyrese Haliburton said he has had "zero court time" with newly acquired center Ivica Zubac but is not concerned. "He's very smart," Haliburton said, adding that Zubac will be available once he finishes playing internationally in September.
  • Doc Rivers is being inducted into the Naismith Memorial Basketball Hall of Fame on Saturday. He told ESPN that writing his speech taught him the ceremony isn't really about him. "People are happy for you, and I am happy for them because they got me there," Rivers said. He also reflected on his time with the Clippers during the Donald Sterling era, calling the organization "the worst" he had seen before Silver stepped in and forced a sale.
  • Front Office Sports reports that the final year of the late Brandon Clarke's contract will not be paid to his estate because it was not protected for death. Clarke did earn his full $12.5 million from the 2025-26 season. He had a life insurance policy, though sources say it is unclear whether the circumstances of his death could affect the payout.
  • NBC Sports announcer Jason Benetti will add NBA play-by-play work to his MLB schedule this season, handling between 10 and 20 telecasts alongside the network's existing roster of Mike Tirico, Noah Eagle, Terry Gannon and Michael Grady.
  • Jalen Brunson may soon have a TV credit to go along with his NBA Finals MVP trophy. "Law and Order: SVU" star Mariska Hargitay told the "Today" show that Brunson will appear on the show. "Don't tell anyone but see him on 'SVU,'" Hargitay whispered on air.
