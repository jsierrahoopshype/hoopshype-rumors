NBA Rumors Wrap: Lakers sale saga deepens, Shams defends reporting, Hartenstein ankle injury

The fallout from the Los Angeles Lakers ownership saga continued Tuesday, with the Buss siblings releasing a joint statement confirming that Johnny, Jimmy, Janie, Joey and Jesse Buss have decided to sell the family's remaining stake in the franchise. The statement said their "focus has always been on what we believe is best for the Lakers, the fans who have supported this franchise for generations and the greater Los Angeles community." Meanwhile, per Shams Charania on the Pat McAfee Show, the ESPN reporter pushed back against a letter from Jeanie Buss's attorney that characterized his original report as saying all six Buss siblings voted to sell. "The first line of the statement from the lawyer said that I, quote, reported the six Buss siblings voted to sell. That key sentence right there is inaccurate," Charania said. "And so I fully stand by my reporting." The situation has broader basketball consequences: league sources told The Athletic that there is a belief around the NBA that the team cannot complete the buildout of its basketball operations department until the ownership situation is resolved, including planned hires for assistant general manager roles focused on analytics and player scouting. Inside the team facility, sources told The Athletic, it was "weirdly enough, business as usual Monday."

The legal complexity around the sale is significant. Sportico reported that the siblings could potentially seek a court order to remove Jeanie as co-trustee, and that the durability of Mark Walter's agreement with Buss granting her controlling-owner status through 2030 is now in question, particularly whether incoming buyers Josh Kushner and Bob Iger would be bound by governance arrangements Walter negotiated.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Isaiah Hartenstein will miss Germany's FIBA qualifying window this summer after suffering an ankle injury. The Oklahoma City Thunder and the German Basketball Federation agreed it was in his best interest to focus on rehabilitation rather than risk aggravating the injury.
  • The New Orleans Pelicans are signing forward Malik Dia to a two-way deal. Undrafted out of Ole Miss, Dia averaged 10 points and 5.8 rebounds for New Orleans in summer league. On a related note, the Pelicans have waived two-way center Hunter Dickinson, per HoopsHype. Dickinson averaged 17.6 points on 59% shooting, 10.9 rebounds and 2.3 assists in the G League last season.
  • The Kawhi Leonard-Clippers situation remains unresolved ahead of training camp. ESPN's Baxter Holmes identified two key dates to watch: a mid-September NBA Board of Governors meeting, which he described as an "inflection point" for potential clarity, and media day and training camp shortly after. The league's probe into the Los Angeles Clippers and a pending trade with the Toronto Raptors remains ongoing.
  • Media personality Pablo Torre questioned the reported negotiations between Clippers owner Steve Ballmer's camp and the NBA to reach a resolution to the investigation. "This is typically not how investigations work, whether in the criminal justice system or in journalism," Torre said. "The subject of the investigation does not get to negotiate with the group that is doing the investigating."
  • The Houston Rockets have signed Sean Pedulla to a two-year, two-way contract.
  • The NBA's first season under its new media rights package generated a record $2.11 billion in advertising revenue, up 38% year-over-year. ABC/ESPN led all partners with $824.5 million, followed closely by NBC Sports at $782.2 million. Average viewership hit 1.78 million per game, up 16% from the prior season and the highest regular-season number in seven years.
  • Victor Wembanyama is back with the French national team ahead of 2027 FIBA World Cup qualifying and told L'Equipe the national team jersey carries even greater weight than his San Antonio Spurs uniform. "It means more because you wear it for much shorter periods of time," Wembanyama said. His former coach at Metropolitans 92, Vincent Collet, separately said Wembanyama is on his way to being the best player in the world, while calling Nikola Jokic the best offensive player: "If you have Jokic, you have a good team, regardless of who plays with him."
  • Guard TyTy Washington is in advanced talks with ASVEL Villeurbanne about a move to the EuroLeague, where former NBA star Tony Parker is preparing for his first season as a head coach. Washington, 24, has appeared in 74 NBA games across stints with the Clippers, Phoenix Suns, Milwaukee Bucks and Rockets.
  • Brad Stevens spoke about the realities of building the Boston Celtics under the NBA's new apron rules, acknowledging the team knew it would eventually have to reshape the roster. "Last summer, when we made the moves we made, we knew we would ultimately have to do that with the new apron rules, the thresholds, and the challenges of trying to operate in that neighborhood," Stevens said. "And those challenges are not the financial part; it's the limitation on trades, the future draft pick issues."
  • OG Anunoby said the Knicks' championship last season hasn't slowed his offseason work. "Our goal is the same: to win the championship next season," Anunoby said. "It's a feel thing. I think you can feel when you've improved."
  • Patrick Beverley said media coverage ended Russell Westbrook's career prematurely. "I think the media killed his game," Beverley said. "I think it all turned when he went to the Lakers and left. I think he had a cloud over his head that was dark." Westbrook's former high school coach, Reggie Morris Jr., offered a different frame on Westbrook's legacy in the Los Angeles Times, saying his impact on the community far outweighs the record 209 triple-doubles.
  • Suns owner Mat Ishbia's mortgage company, United Wholesale Mortgage, suspended its quarterly dividend and posted roughly $452 million in losses in the second quarter of 2026, following net income of about $170 million in the first quarter.
  • Jamal Crawford said publicly for the first time that he expects to become an NBA head coach. "I 1,000% know I'm going to be an NBA coach one day," Crawford said, adding that he turned down three NBA jobs this summer, including going through a head coach interview process, because his son made USA Basketball and the timing wasn't right.
  • Tim Connelly on the Timberwolves' championship ceiling: "There's only five or six rooms in the league that really think they have a chance, and we're one of them."
  • Bill Rasmussen, who founded ESPN in 1979 as the world's first 24-hour sports television network, died Tuesday at his Florida home from the effects of Parkinson's disease. He was 93.
