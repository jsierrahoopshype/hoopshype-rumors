NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks table extension talks with Towns, Hart, Kyrie Irving set for Macao return, LeBron makes Sixers debut

The New York Knicks have shut down contract extension negotiations with their eligible players for the foreseeable future, and the gap between the team and Karl-Anthony Towns is wider than the organization's statement let on. The Knicks announced Thursday they are tabling all extension talks to focus on the season, but per Shams Charania, the real issue is that both sides are "fully divided" and negotiations with Towns have been idle for some time. Towns holds a $61 million player option for next season and is eligible for a four-year, $276 million max extension. The Knicks have offered him around $200 million, roughly $76 million below max. Towns is willing to take a discount, but Chris Haynes reported that the two sides remain "nowhere close," with the Knicks' offer still too steep a pay cut for the reigning champion's MVP-caliber center to accept. Josh Hart, Miles McBride and Tyler Kolek are also among the four players whose extension windows close June 30.

ESPN's Bobby Marks said there is still a viable path to keep both Towns and Hart while staying under the second apron for 2027-28, and that scenarios were presented to the front office within the last month where both players sign long-term deals and New York remains compliant. The wrinkle for Towns specifically: he would have to decline his player option for 2027-28 to sign any in-season extension, and the Knicks would have to decline their team options on Hart and Kolek to extend them mid-season. Bruce Brown, speaking to SNY, put a finer point on the CBA frustration: "The new CBA is good for max guys, new free agents and rookie extension guys. The middle guys, it kind of sucks for us."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Kyrie Irving is expected to play Sunday in Macao against the Rockets, his first game action in nearly 20 months, after being held out of Houston and Dallas's first China Games matchup Friday. Kevin Durant will not play in Sunday's game, per Rockets coach Ime Udoka.
  • • LeBron James made his preseason debut with the Philadelphia 76ers Thursday, logging 18 minutes in a 114-108 loss to Brooklyn. "It felt a little different," James said. "Obviously, just wearing a new uniform, but the game is the game." LeBron also said the transition away from family will be a challenge, but that everyone in the organization has been "super welcoming."
  • • Joel Embiid pushed back on a report that LeBron confronted him about being late to practice, calling it flatly untrue. "I don't know where that came from. It never happened," Embiid said after the loss. "I guess I'm Joel Embiid. I've been in that situation. It feels like I'm an easy target."
  • • The Detroit Pistons spent the offseason swinging for major additions that never materialized. The team inquired about trading for Kawhi Leonard and Kevin Durant, according to league sources, and was positioned to offer Austin Reaves a big free agent deal if the Lakers opened the door. Leonard had no interest in Detroit amid the Clippers' cap-circumvention scandal, the Rockets declined to engage on Durant, and the Lakers locked up Reaves with a max offer before he could look elsewhere. Meanwhile, center Jalen Duren entered contract negotiations having heard he was considered the team's fourth-best player, one of the reasons for his reported frustration in those talks.
  • • Giannis Antetokounmpo is already reshaping how the Heat operate offensively. Miami's uptempo system has created easy looks for him in transition, and he's also setting ball screens for Davion Mitchell in pick-and-roll actions, something the Heat rarely ran last season. "Last year, we led the league in 140-point games and that's with no Giannis," Bam Adebayo said with a laugh. "But we see the potential."
  • • Damian Lillard overhauled his training routine this offseason and says he feels like he's 29 all over again after Chris Haynes visited him at home to watch a workout. Separately, Tyrese Haliburton said he regrets what his relationship with Lillard has become after stealing Dame's clock-watching celebration during the 2023 NBA Cup. "I would love to have a conversation because I just think it became more than it ever needed to be," Haliburton told ESPN's Zach Lowe. "Put it on me." He added that he hopes they can "at least be cordial again."
  • • LaMelo Ball's energy has already made an impression on his new Timberwolves teammates. The Athletic's Jon Krawczynski said his vibe is "permeating the rest of the team," describing "this vibrancy about him" that was evident even before training camp games began.
  • • Kawhi Leonard, now back with the Raptors after his time in Los Angeles, said a new contract extension meant security for his family more than anything else. "I never really look at the money," he told TSN. "I could've stopped playing today with the money I made. So I'm just fortunate enough to be healthy and able to play the game."
  • • Rockets GM Rafael Stone spoke at length about what the team envisioned when it traded for Durant, saying the Rockets made clear they weren't going to surround him with a veteran roster and expected Durant to embrace developing alongside younger players. Stone was equally effusive about Amen Thompson, saying the team has been "materially better" when Thompson is on the floor since roughly halfway through his rookie year and that he "helps our team in literally every way you can."
  • • Victor Wembanyama credited Devin Vassell and Keldon Johnson with anchoring the Spurs' culture heading into what he called their rise as a contender. "Devin's increasing leadership role coincides with our increase as a contender," Wembanyama said. Rookie Dylan Harper added that the Spurs' trio of himself, Stephon Castle and Wembanyama will be "really hard to guard" given their shared ability to get downhill and create mismatches.
  • • Walker Kessler gave a brief update on his finger injury, which he jammed on the backboard during Lakers training camp. "Finger's good," Kessler said during ESPN's broadcast of a preseason game. "Just kinda going through all the protocols right now." The Lakers have added undrafted rookie center Rafael Castro to their training camp roster after he was briefly on a two-way deal with the Rockets this offseason.
  • • Adam Silver confirmed the NBA is moving forward with NBA Europe regardless of where negotiations with the Euroleague land, saying franchise bids are coming in from existing basketball clubs, major soccer brands and new startups, and that valuations are "very positive." EuroLeague clubs voted to reject the NBA's initial investment offer at a meeting in Lake Como this week, though NBA sources say talks will continue. The league is planning to invest roughly $10 billion over 10 years in the project, with franchise prices expected to range from $500 million to more than $1 billion for the London spot.
  • • Silver also addressed prediction markets and sports betting at his CNBC appearance in Macao, saying the NBA's position is the same regardless of what the product is called. "There's issues around integrity first and foremost," Silver said, adding that the league wants federal regulation rather than managing roughly 40 different state jurisdictions.
  • • The Pelicans have waived Christian Koloko, per HoopsHype. The Nets signed Dion Brown to an Exhibit 10 deal, and the Cavaliers signed Jaxson Robinson and Noam Yaacov to training camp deals.
  • • An emergency protective order has been filed against former NBA player J.R. Smith after he was accused of causing "serious bodily injury" to a woman during an incident in Texas last month. Smith has been ordered to have zero contact with the woman and is barred from her home and workplace.
