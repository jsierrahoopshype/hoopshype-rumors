NBA Rumors Wrap: Lakers sold to Kushner and Iger for record $12.5 billion, Russell Westbrook retires, Cavs pursue Peyton Watson

The Los Angeles Lakers are being sold to businessman Josh Kushner and former Disney CEO Bob Iger for a record $12.5 billion, per Ramona Shelburne of ESPN. The deal covers only Mark Walter's shares, not the Buss family's remaining stake, and came together in a matter of days after Kushner reached out to Walter last Friday. Walter had purchased controlling interest in the team from the Buss family just over a year ago at a $10 billion valuation, making his $2.5 billion return one of the fastest appreciation stories in sports ownership history. The quick turnaround raised eyebrows around the league, with Front Office Sports reporting that Walter has been under federal investigation for alleged tax fraud involving insurance companies he controls, and that Bloomberg reported Walter had been in talks with investors to raise money to pay down the loans in question. Iger told the New York Post the deal is still sinking in: "Josh and I are avid NBA fans. And to have an opportunity to own not just any NBA team, but the LA Lakers is…it's extraordinary."

Kushner runs Thrive Capital and its brand-focused arm Thrive Eternal, which holds a minority stake in the San Francisco Giants. Iger was a partner at Thrive between his stints as Disney CEO before rejoining as an advisor. Jeanie Buss will remain the team's governor under the agreement Walter originally struck with the Buss family, and Iger said he and Kushner "have every intention to honor" that arrangement. One wrinkle for Lakers fans: a 2021 podcast clip resurfaced in which Iger admitted rooting for the Knicks growing up and later adopting the Clippers because "it just was not in my DNA" to root for LA. Luka Doncic welcomed the new owners on social media: "Being a Laker means everything to me, and I'm excited to get back on the court and bring a championship to LA."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Russell Westbrook has retired after 18 NBA seasons, per Shams Charania. The 2017 MVP, nine-time All-Star and all-time triple-double record holder turned down offers from the Kings and Wizards to call it a career. Oklahoma City Thunder GM Sam Presti said Westbrook "helped establish the foundation of our organization from day one in 2008," while Sacramento posted a tribute on social media: "Grateful Sacramento was part of an unforgettable career."
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers have been exploring trades involving Dennis Schröder and Max Strus in an effort to clear enough salary to make a competitive four-year offer to restricted free agent Peyton Watson. Schröder is owed $14.8 million next season and $15.5 million in 2027-28, with only $4.4 million guaranteed in the latter year; Strus has an expiring $16.7 million deal. The Nuggets, who currently hold Watson's rights, have offered four years and $70 million with a team option in year four, a structure Watson's camp has resisted. The Bucks and Clippers are also in the mix for a sign-and-trade.
  • A return to the Clippers has not been ruled out for Bradley Beal, who also has drawn interest from the Heat, per Marc Stein. The 33-year-old played in only six games last season before needing surgery to repair a fracture in his left hip.
  • The Warriors are showing legitimate interest in free agents Georges Niang and Brandon Williams for their open roster spots, per Marc Stein. Dalen Terry has also been mentioned as a potential Golden State target. Meanwhile, new Warriors signee Yaxel Lendeborg watched Stephen Curry work out and was left speechless: "I was like, this dude don't really miss at all. That shit was damn near making me mad."
  • The Suns have waived Haywood Highsmith, per HoopsHype. He was partially guaranteed $1 million of his $3 million salary for 2026-27. Highsmith averaged 5.4 points on 38% from three and 3.1 rebounds across six NBA seasons.
  • Suns owner Mat Ishbia's mortgage company UWM is having a rough stretch. The firm posted a $603 million loss tied to a bad bet on falling interest rates and a net loss of $452 million in the second quarter alone. UWM also filed a $500 million lawsuit against Two Harbors over an alleged breach of a merger contract. The quarterly dividends from UWM, which were suspended last week, had been a key source of funding for Ishbia's investments in the Suns.
  • Portland City Council approved an amended Moda Center term sheet in an 8-4 vote, adding $3.17 million in annual rent to a proposal that Blazers owner Tom Dundon had already pushed back on. The term sheet also includes up to $120 million for renovations and $275 million for ongoing capital expenditures. Several councilors who voted yes said publicly they don't believe Dundon is operating in good faith, meaning tougher negotiations still lie ahead.
  • The NBA Cup group play schedule is set, with action running from October 30 through November 27. East Group B, featuring the Knicks, Sixers, Cavaliers, Heat and Pacers, will be the most nationally televised group, with five of the 15 broadcast games coming from that pod. West Group C, featuring the Lakers, Warriors, Spurs, Kings and Blazers, will also get five nationally televised slots.
  • The NBA announced an NBC Sports quadrupleheader for Martin Luther King Jr. Day on January 18. Games include a rematch of the 2026 Eastern Conference Semifinals between the Pistons and Cavaliers, plus 76ers-Hawks, Timberwolves-Knicks and Rockets-Grizzlies.
  • MarJon Beauchamp is heading to Europe after 155 NBA appearances, signing with FC Bayern Munich in the EuroLeague.
  • Hapoel Tel Aviv owner Ofer Yannay has invited Dennis Rodman to fly to Israel and run a rebounding clinic for the squad. The offer includes a $100,000 appearance fee, business-class airfare and luxury hotel accommodations for Rodman and up to three guests. Separately, reports of Russell Westbrook pursuing a deal with the club turned out to be a social media hoax traced to a parody account presenting itself as a reporter.
  • The Hornets announced that all non-nationally televised games will be broadcast on WSOC-TV or TV64, both free over the air and available on YouTube TV and major cable carriers.
  • Former NBA player Royce White lost Minnesota's Republican Senate primary to former NFL sideline reporter Michelle Tafoya, who took 52.1% of the vote. White responded by announcing a presidential campaign on X: "Now the real fun begins — I will be starting my campaign for President next week."
