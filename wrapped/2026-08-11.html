NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade expected before training camp, Harden-Cavs deal closing, Pistons' Duren situation

The NBA's investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and the stalled trade that would send Kawhi Leonard to the Toronto Raptors appears to be approaching a resolution. Per Marc Stein, there is a belief in league circles that the investigation is closer to completion than publicly acknowledged, and that a resolution involving some level of sanctions against the Clippers, along with clearance for the trade to proceed, could come within the next six weeks. Leonard's agent, Harrison Gaines, has maintained frequent contact with the NBA Players Association throughout the 11-month process. The trade was agreed to with the idea that the 35-year-old would also negotiate a multiyear extension with Toronto to extend the reunion beyond the coming season. The Clippers, for their part, are prepared to go to arbitration depending on the severity of any penalties the league hands down.

Jake Fischer separately reported that Leonard is working under the belief the investigation will wrap before training camp opens, and that he remains on course to join the Raptors.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers are still working toward a James Harden re-signing, with a resolution expected soon. "The Cavaliers are working through different iterations of a deal, trying to come up with something that's best for James and also best for them," Marc Spears said. "I hear that they expect a resolution to come soon for him to re-sign." On a related note, Cleveland is also a serious candidate to land restricted free agent Peyton Watson, per Dave McMenamin, though other teams are also in the mix.
  • The Detroit Pistons' contract extension talks with Jalen Duren have been complicated by an internal view that Ausar Thompson is actually the second-best player on the roster. Per Vince Goodwill, a significant faction within the organization holds that belief, and it is affecting how the two sides are approaching Duren's deal.
  • The San Antonio Spurs remain committed to De'Aaron Fox despite his rough showing in the NBA Finals, with team sources pointing out he played through a high right ankle sprain. Still, sources say the decision between Fox and his competition for playing time will ultimately belong to the coaching staff and will be decided on the floor.
  • Victor Wembanyama said in a quick-fire Q&A with a French content creator that he believes he could score 100 points in a single game. When asked which French compatriot he'd most want to bring to the Spurs, he went with Evan Fournier.
  • The Miami Heat are still trying to fill their final two standard roster spots while operating under a hard cap created by the Giannis Antetokounmpo acquisition. Among the names being considered are Bradley Beal, DeMar DeRozan and Klay Thompson, the last of whom would need to come from the Dallas Mavericks via trade or buyout. Once those spots are filled, Miami intends to offer Exhibit 10 camp contracts to several undrafted players.
  • The New York Knicks explored trades for big men this offseason, targeting New Orleans' Karlo Matkovic in trade talks that went nowhere, and making an offer for restricted free agent Moussa Cisse that Dallas matched. As for Karl-Anthony Towns, the expectation is that an extension will get done, though the Knicks are unlikely to offer a max.
  • New Orleans Pelicans GM Joe Dumars addressed the team's quiet offseason, saying the price for moving up in the draft was too steep and that he passed on deals that did not make sense. "You don't want to make a move for the sake of making a move," Dumars said. On Zion Williamson, Dumars said the message has been straightforward: "To be great, you have to be obsessed with this," and that Williamson's summer work has been encouraging.
  • Jaylen Brown will host a public block party in Boston on August 30 before heading to Philadelphia. The event, called "741 Frequency Fest," will take place at Boston Common and feature music, games and giveaways. Former Celtics teammate Payton Pritchard said of Brown's departure: "You lose close friendships, brotherhoods. He kind of took me under his wing, pushed me, made me better. He'll be missed mightily."
  • Pritchard also said he is not focused on a contract extension despite being eligible. "I have two years on my contract and there's no rush on it," he said. "We'll talk, obviously, but we have a whole next year to talk about it."
  • D'Angelo Russell said he would love to see Stephen Curry join the Celtics. "I just think he needs that type of help around him now," Russell said on a podcast, adding that Boston is a veteran team that knows how to win in the closing minutes of games.
  • Panathinaikos owner Dimitris Giannakopoulos said his goal is to sign Nikola Jokic next summer. "There is one player who remains a dream of mine," he told the Euro Insiders podcast. "It's Jokic, the best player in the world. If I don't sign Jokic, I'm not going anywhere."
  • Luka Doncic and Jaxson Hayes will both skip Slovenia's upcoming FIBA window for the 2027 World Cup Qualifiers. Neither player was included in the federation's 19-man preliminary squad for the late-August games.
  • Shaquille O'Neal said the Lakers' decision to let LeBron James go was simply business. "When they brought in Luka Doncic, they were looking to start over," O'Neal said. He was less diplomatic about former Laker Deandre Ayton, saying he is glad the Lakers no longer have "Dominayton, who didn't do anything."
  • The Philadelphia 76ers' young core gathered for the first day of assistant coach Rico Hines' annual summer runs at UCLA, with VJ Edgecombe, Labaron Philon, Adem Bona, Dominick Barlow and Caleb Love all on the floor together.
  • The New York Knicks appear likely to keep second-round pick Tyler Nickel rather than send him back to college. There is momentum toward him joining the team this season, a league source confirmed.
  • Former NC State center Cozell McQueen, a member of the Wolfpack's 1983 national championship team, has died at 64. McQueen appeared in three games with the Detroit Pistons in 1987, scoring six points and grabbing eight rebounds in seven total minutes.
