NBA Rumors Wrap: Lakers buyers project $30B valuation, Keyonte George signs in Utah, Heat keeping roster spot open

The group purchasing the Los Angeles Lakers is telling prospective investors it expects to nearly triple the franchise's value within a decade, according to a presentation reviewed by the Wall Street Journal. The buyers, led by Joshua Kushner's Thrive Capital and former Disney CEO Bob Iger, project a valuation of at least $30 billion within 10 years, assuming TV and streaming rights double in value — with more favorable assumptions placing the long-term figure at $62 billion. The group struck a deal to purchase the team from finance mogul Mark Walter at a $12.5 billion valuation last month. Near-term plans include identifying around $150 million in incremental revenue by 2028, partly by reclaiming roughly 6,000 broker-held season tickets for single-game sales, which would push average single-game ticket prices from $217 to $361. International growth and new local media rights opportunities are also part of the pitch.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Utah Jazz made Keyonte George's contract signing an event, and the turnout reflected how the team feels about him. Jusuf Nurkic, Svi Mykhailiuk, Jaren Jackson Jr., Lauri Markkanen, Darryn Peterson, Mo Bamba, and Blake Hinson were all there to support him at the signing. George kept it brief but meaningful: "Special shout out to the city. I got drafted here. All I know is Utah."
  • Jazz GM Austin Ainge spoke about George's leadership style ahead of the signing, pushing back on narrow definitions of the word. "He's maybe not a screamer and a yeller type of guy," Ainge said, "but he shows up for everybody, he is there for everybody, smile and work ethic every day." Coach Will Hardy added a personal note on their bond: "It got really good when we realized we're really similar." Both men said they don't trust easily, but now trust each other immensely.
  • The Miami Heat are keeping their 15th roster spot open for now, in case a significant free agent becomes available. No specific target was named.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves had to work for Jonathan Kuminga. GM Matt Lloyd revealed that between July 15th and the day they signed Kuminga, he and Kuminga's agent logged 94 phone calls. "My wife asked me at one point, how many times has he called you?" Lloyd said. He checked his phone and gave her the answer.
  • Kyle Lowry received a key to the city of Toronto on Saturday, presented by Mayor Olivia Chow at a free block party in Jurassic Park outside Scotiabank Arena. "Kyle Lowry gave Toronto more than unforgettable moments on the court, he gave our city his heart," Chow said. The celebration drew a who's-who of Raptors alumni, including DeMar DeRozan, OG Anunoby, Pascal Siakam, Fred VanVleet, Scottie Barnes, Immanuel Quickley, Kevin Love, Danny Green, and coach Darko Rajakovic, among others.
  • Former Trail Blazers player Chris Dudley weighed in on the Portland arena situation, saying the state made a mistake by agreeing to a break-up fee rather than demanding relocation be taken off the table entirely. "If your franchise is worth a couple of billion dollars more in another city, and you have to pay a break-up fee of a couple of million, that's nothing, pennies on the dollar," Dudley said. He added that he's an optimist about a deal eventually getting done, but said his central condition would be owner Tom Dundon committing to Portland for at least 20 years.
  • Marc Stein reports that Dwight Powell attended the Dallas Mavericks' Kyrie Irving-led minicamp in Los Angeles this week, and league sources expect Powell to receive a formal training camp invite even though Dallas currently has a full 15-man roster.
  • The Memphis Grizzlies marked what would have been Brandon Clarke's birthday on Saturday with a statement confirming they will honor his memory with a pre-game tribute and moment of silence on Opening Night, and a BC jersey patch throughout the season.
  • Per HoopsHype, the New Orleans Pelicans have waived guard Markquis Nowell, the Timberwolves have waived guard Stephen Thompson, and the Warriors have waived forward Elias Ralph while adding Obi Agbim on a training camp deal. The Lakers also added Meechie Johnson and Chase Ross to their training camp roster while waiving Anton Watson.
  • The Indiana Pacers have announced Exhibit 10 signings for Keba Keita, Rienk Mast, Kowacie Reeves Jr., and Jalen Warley, all of whom played for the summer league Pacers.
  • Shaquille O'Neal graduated from the FBI National Academy's session 299 and was named an honorary FBI Special Agent, according to FBI Director Kash Patel. Patel called Shaq "one of the best supporters of law enforcement in the country."
  • Dwyane Wade said on the Conversations With Cam podcast that he changed his behavior in the Heat locker room after his daughter Zaya began her transition, though before the family went public. "I realized that toxic environment that we grew up in," Wade said, adding that he stopped laughing at certain jokes and began leaving the room when he heard things that bothered him. He also said he had a private conversation with longtime teammate Udonis Haslem who, Wade said, eventually changed his thinking, tone, and actions on the subject.
  • Dirk Nowitzki and Steve Nash opened up about the rough patch in their friendship after Nash left Dallas. "I think it went from super hot to very cold to super hot again," Nowitzki said. "There was a phase when he left us where things were a little chilly." Nash, for his part, joked: "Yes, I'm sick of Dirk. Entirely way too much time together."
  • Tony Parker lost his first game as head coach of ASVEL to his own brother, TJ Parker, whose Chorale Roanne squad beat them 73-71 in the French SuperCup semifinal. ASVEL has gone 1-5 in the preseason so far. Tony said he chose to start his coaching career in France rather than jumping straight to the NBA out of humility, crediting a conversation with Zinedine Zidane for the mindset: "Zizou explained to me how much it helped him to start again from zero, to put himself in a position where you make yourself a little vulnerable."
  • The Brooklyn Nets had an unexpected visitor at practice: Manchester City manager Pep Guardiola stopped by to spend time with coach Jordi Fernandez.
