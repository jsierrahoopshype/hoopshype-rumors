NBA Rumors Wrap: Gansey lands 76ers president role, Sweeney hired by Magic, Williams ruled out for Game 7

Mike Gansey is heading to Philadelphia. The Cleveland Cavaliers general manager has agreed to become the new President of Basketball Operations for the 76ers, per Shams Charania, filling the vacancy created when the team fired Daryl Morey on May 12. Gansey joined Cleveland in 2012 and rose through the ranks, winning G League Executive of the Year along the way, and is credited with identifying under-the-radar contributors like Sam Merrill and Max Strus. He and Nick U'Ren of the WNBA's Phoenix Mercury were the two finalists, with both holding final-stage meetings with Philadelphia ownership this week before the decision came down Friday. The hire gives the 76ers a front-office leader with a track record of player development and roster construction, though the bigger question now is who fills Gansey's role back in Cleveland.

The Sixers search had been closely watched since Marc Stein reported earlier in the week that Sixers assistant GM Jameer Nelson was considered a leading candidate alongside Gansey, with a growing expectation that Nelson would be elevated to general manager at minimum if he didn't get the top job. That still appears to be the likely outcome on the basketball operations side in Philadelphia.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Orlando Magic have hired Sean Sweeney as their head coach, per HoopsHype. Sweeney was an associate head coach with the San Antonio Spurs and will remain on San Antonio's bench through the rest of the postseason, including Saturday's Game 7 against Oklahoma City. Former Bulls coach Billy Donovan and Clippers assistant Jeff Van Gundy were the other two finalists for the vacancy.
  • Jalen Williams has been ruled out for Game 7 of the Western Conference Finals against San Antonio. Williams gave it a go in Game 6 after missing three straight games with a left hamstring strain, and Chet Holmgren was asked about his teammate's toughness afterward: "That's a tough dude right there. For him to go out there and fight through it and try to give us what he can, that's who he is. There's no quit in him."
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's minus-28 plus-minus in Game 6 is tied with 2023 Joel Embiid for the lowest plus-minus by an NBA MVP in a clinching opportunity in the play-by-play era (since 1997). The Spurs disrupted him in the first half with ice coverages that kept him on the right side of the floor, preventing his stepback and limiting his cross-court passing lanes.
  • Victor Wembanyama showed up to the Spurs' home arena Thursday in a long robe, worn to celebrate an Islamic holiday, with freshly cropped hair. Devin Vassell said he saw a photo pregame and knew immediately Wembanyama was locked in. Wembanyama's pregame message to his teammates, as recalled by Lindy Waters III, centered on the idea of playing with "life after": earlier in the series there was always a next game, but once they reached Game 6, there was no more life after.
  • Veteran forward Harrison Barnes said Wembanyama's leadership behind the scenes has grown in ways that aren't always visible. "You don't see a lot of guys in their third year, at this stage, with this level of responsibility that he has on our team, be able to step into that moment with the comfort that he has," Barnes said. Meanwhile, Dylan Harper said his little sister called him before Game 6 and told him, "You gotta make it to Saturday." He delivered.
  • Mitchell Robinson has undergone surgery on a broken right pinky finger but fully plans to play in the Knicks' Game 1 of the NBA Finals while wearing a brace. New York will face either the Thunder or Spurs beginning next Wednesday.
  • The Knicks' Mikal Bridges, Jalen Brunson and Josh Hart could become the fifth set of teammates in history to win both an NCAA championship and an NBA title together, joining alumni of programs like UCLA, Kentucky, and Ohio State who pulled it off previously at the pro level with their college teammates.
  • Donovan Mitchell will be eligible for a contract extension on July 7, and Cavs GM Koby Altman left little doubt about the team's intentions. "There hasn't been any question on if he wants to be here or if he will be here," Altman said. On the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade rumors swirling around Evan Mobley, Altman declined to engage: "Since Evan's been here, we have the third-best record in the league." Altman also praised second-year guard Jaylon Tyson, saying he will be "a big part of what we do" going forward.
  • The Chicago Bulls are casting a wide net in their head coaching search, with early conversations including BYU's Kevin Young, who spent four seasons as a lead assistant with the Phoenix Suns before taking the college job. Other candidates include Tiago Splitter, Micah Nori, Johnnie Bryant, Jerry Stackhouse and Lamar Skeeter.
  • The consensus around the league is that Walker Kessler will re-sign with the Jazz in the range of $25 million to $30 million annually. "It appears Utah is gearing up to keep him," one East scout told ESPN. "I don't see an obvious fit in free agency for someone to try to get him." The Jazz already added Jaren Jackson Jr. via trade and have Lauri Markkanen, but Kessler remains the full-time center in their long-term plans.
  • To make room to re-sign Peyton Watson, the Denver Nuggets are expected to move either Cameron Johnson (on an expiring $23 million deal) or Christian Braun (signed to a five-year, $125 million extension last fall). That belief is widespread around the league, though Watson's recurring hamstring injuries, which cost him a significant chunk of the second half, add a layer of uncertainty to the negotiations.
  • Jalen Duren could draw a heavy offer sheet this summer with the Pistons expected to match, per ESPN. "If he wants to get a max, they'll tell him to go get one," one East executive said. "But he's Cade's guy, so they'll have to play it the right way." The two sides had a contentious extension standoff last summer and fall.
  • Mavericks GM Mike Schmitz said Kyrie Irving has been doing "everything humanly possible" to return from his torn ACL, citing his daily presence and work ethic at the practice facility. Schmitz made the comments Friday at a roundtable with reporters. Separately, one analyst said he isn't putting much stock in a Kyrie-Luka reunion with the Lakers at this time, even with Austin Reaves' free agency adding roster uncertainty in Los Angeles.
  • Luka Doncic and former Mavericks executive Donnie Nelson are among the lead investors in a group that has purchased Italian club Vanoli Cremona and plans to move the franchise to Rome. The group submitted a bid to represent Rome in the NBA's planned European league. The league has received dozens of applications, with franchise fees ranging from $500 million to over $1 billion, and will request a second round of bids by late next month.
  • ESPN's Mike Breen will call his record-extending 21st straight NBA Finals this year, and for the first time will be broadcasting the team he grew up rooting for. "I've been broadcasting Knick games for over half my life," Breen said. "So, when you see a team have a chance to do something they haven't done forever, there's certainly emotions there." Broadcast partner Walt Frazier, 81, is reportedly rejuvenated by the run, with Breen saying he had worried the Knicks legend might retire after years of watching bad basketball.
