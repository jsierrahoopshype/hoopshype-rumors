NBA Rumors Wrap: Don Nelson dies at 86, Ben Simmons eyes comeback, Tyler Herro embraces fresh start in Milwaukee

Hall of Fame coach Don Nelson died Sunday at age 86, surrounded by family, per Marc Stein. Nelson spent 31 seasons as an NBA head coach with the Milwaukee Bucks, Golden State Warriors, New York Knicks and Dallas Mavericks, finishing with a regular-season record of 1,335-1,063, the most wins in NBA coaching history before Gregg Popovich passed him in March 2022. He made 18 playoff appearances and won five NBA titles as a player with the Boston Celtics. A Celebration of Life is scheduled for Thursday, August 13 at Preston Hollow Presbyterian Church in Dallas, with an ashes ceremony in Maui to be confirmed at a later date.

The tributes poured in from across the NBA. Dirk Nowitzki "I don't think my career could have turned out how it did without you being my first coach and giving me the freedom to play my game," Nowitzki wrote. Stephen Curry, who Nelson pushed Golden State to draft with the No. 7 pick in 2009 against consensus, said in a statement: "One of the big reasons I was drafted by the Warriors was because of Don Nelson. The story is he never liked rookies, but from Day 1 he challenged me and gave me an opportunity to be my best self on the court." Luka Doncic called it "a sad day for basketball" and said he was grateful for the time he spent with Nelson. NBA Commissioner Adam Silver said Nelson "revolutionized NBA basketball through his fearlessness, ingenuity and deep conviction."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Warriors coach Steve Kerr said Nelson "consistently challenged conventional thinking, experimented with different lineups and styles of play and saw possibilities in the game that others often did not," adding that "so much of what is now commonplace in the modern NBA can be traced back to the way Nellie approached the game." Brent Barry put it simply: "His fingerprints on the modern NBA: space, movement, creativity."
  • Baron Davis, reflecting on the Warriors' 2007 upset run, said Nelson "understood what made us go and made us believe we could beat anybody, ultimately helping us make history as the first No. 8 seed to defeat a No. 1 seed in a seven-game series." Jason Richardson also paid tribute, writing that Nelson "saw basketball differently, trusted his players, and wasn't afraid to do things his own way."
  • Nick Van Exel remembered Nelson as "the first and only coach to say 'if you don't shoot I'm taking you out,'" and praised his willingness to try unconventional lineups. Steve Nash wrote that Nelson "could see things differently than everyone else" and that when Nash made his first All-Star game, "it was zero surprise to him."
  • Jason Kidd called Nelson "a great player, a coaching genius and a great person," while former Bucks star Michael Redd described him as "a basketball genius among basketball geniuses." Mark Cuban kept it to two words: "RIP Nellie."
  • Ben Simmons is expected to be a full participant with "zero restrictions" in a player-led Australian men's national team minicamp in Melbourne beginning Monday, with sources telling Andscape he paid his own way to attend. A coach who worked Simmons out recently said he has regained his athleticism, and Simmons told Andscape this is the best he has felt in years. Several NBA teams have expressed interest, including one Western Conference team that has offered a training camp invite, and Simmons is also open to a veteran minimum deal.
  • Simmons also posted to Instagram from a gym this week, writing: "I wouldn't change the story. The highs, the lows, the pain, the time, the lessons. I embrace all of it. Every chapter built something in me that I couldn't have built any other way. Grateful for where I've been. Proud of where I stand. Ready for wherever this goes."
  • Tyler Herro said he is carrying "the biggest chip on my shoulder" he has had in his career as he prepares for his first season with the Bucks, his hometown team. Herro, who has one year left on his contract and could become an unrestricted free agent after 2026-27, said he is not focused on his extension situation: "I'm not really worried about it right now. It's a new slate for me, for the coaches, really for the organization as a whole."
  • Former Pistons center Isaiah Stewart, now with Memphis, said Detroit should pay Jalen Duren. "He was an All-Star, third-team All-NBA. I feel like he should get paid," Stewart told Andscape. "He's a huge core piece. Bring him back. He's been getting better every year. Third-year All-Star? Like come on, give him the money."
  • Shaquille O'Neal defended LeBron James' move to the 76ers, saying the criticism that LeBron is ring-chasing does not hold up. "The man is not chasing rings. The man has already gotten rings the right way," Shaq told FTW and USA TODAY Sports. Shaq did add some perspective on LeBron's legacy in the rings conversation: "Kareem got six, Magic got five, Kobe got five, I got four, Rambis got three. So you know, he's at the party, but he's not on stage."
  • Austin Reaves is looking forward to the Lakers' upcoming minicamp in Slovenia and has a very specific priority: beating Luka Doncic in golf. "I feel great, I'm going to kick his a** in golf," Reaves said jokingly at a media availability. Beyond the golf course, he said the camp is about getting to know the team's new faces: "We've got a fairly new team, a lot of new guys. So it'll just be good to be around them, learn to get to know one another off the court."
  • The Clippers have been busy with roster-building moves, signing Jamarion Sharp to a two-way contract and inking Yuki Kawamura to an Exhibit 10 deal.
  • Former Atlanta Hawks guard Keaton Wallace is heading overseas, signing a two-year, $2.4 million contract with Maccabi Tel Aviv. Wallace appeared in 53 NBA games last season, averaging 3.5 points in 10.1 minutes per game.
  • Timberwolves teammates Anthony Edwards, LaMelo Ball and others flew to Paris to link up with Rudy Gobert, who is spending the offseason in his home country. The team photo in front of the Eiffel Tower made the rounds Sunday.
  • Former NBA forward Monta Ellis said the Warriors teams he played on in the pre-dynasty years set the table for what Curry and company accomplished, but he never had the supporting cast to win at that level. "If you switched me and Steph out with the team that won the championships, it'd be the same thing," Ellis said.
