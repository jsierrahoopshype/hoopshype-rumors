NBA Rumors Wrap: Cavs trade Schroeder to Hornets, Clarke's contract protected for death, Lakers sale fallout continues

The Cleveland Cavaliers traded Dennis Schroeder to the Charlotte Hornets for Tre Mann on Friday, per Shams Charania. Cleveland also sent roughly $4 million in cash to make the deal work, having spent months resisting other teams' requests for draft picks as part of any Schroeder trade. HoopsHype first reported Charlotte's interest in Schroeder back on July 22, with Hornets president Jeff Peterson a known admirer of the veteran point guard from their time together in Atlanta. With Schroeder's contract off the books, the Cavs are now in active pursuit of free agent forwards Jonathan Kuminga and restricted free agent Peyton Watson, while staying in contact with James Harden's camp about a new deal to return to Cleveland.

The trade is not a prelude to any imminent move for Kuminga or Watson specifically, sources say, but it gives Cleveland financial flexibility and room to pursue sign-and-trade scenarios. Bobby Marks laid out the cap picture after the deal: the Cavs are now $32 million below the first apron and $44 million below the second, though any sign-and-trade bringing in salary would hard-cap them at the first apron. For what it's worth, this was Schroeder's ninth trade in his NBA career, making him the second-most-traded player in league history.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • An erroneous report surfaced this week claiming the estate of Brandon Clarke would not receive the remainder of his $12.5 million contract following his death in May. That report was wrong. NBA sources confirmed that Clarke's deal was fully protected for death, with the $12.5 million expected to be paid through life insurance. His agent, Mark Bartelstein of Priority Sports, said in a statement: "Brandon's contract was fully protected for a God-forbid occurrence. So Brandon's family and his estate are fully protected in Brandon's contract. The support that the entire Grizzlies organization has shown to Brandon's family during this devastating time has been beyond words."
  • The Lakers sale to Bob Iger and Josh Kushner continues to generate reaction around the league. A source close to LeBron James told ESPN that if the sale had been completed sooner, it might — just maybe — have influenced LeBron's decision to stay in L.A. "Maybe," the source said. "Communication could have been better. Just a different relationship. But the basketball piece was the most important, so maybe not from that standpoint." Separately, sources told ESPN that Luka Doncic was not consulted about the sale, but plans to have a video call with the new owners next week from Slovenia, where he's hosting teammates for a minicamp.
  • On the broader league business front, a league office source told ESPN the NBA is prioritizing a Las Vegas expansion franchise sale before Seattle, wanting to lock in a premium fee that sets a financial precedent. As of now, a vote on any Las Vegas sale would not pass at the next Board of Governors meeting in September. Commissioner Adam Silver and many owners also view NBA Europe, a 16-team league set to launch in October 2027, as a bigger organizational priority.
  • New internal emails obtained by Pablo Torre show the Clippers' marketing team actively pushed their scoreboard vendor, Daktronics, to schedule activations with Kawhi Leonard in 2021. Steve Ballmer had publicly claimed Kawhi's sponsorship deals were independent of the team, but the emails suggest a much closer relationship. In one exchange, a Daktronics exec responded to the Clippers' prodding with: "Thanks for the reminder! Go Clippers!"
  • A federal judge dismissed Charles Oakley's lawsuit against Madison Square Garden on Friday, ruling the former Knicks forward had not proven he was assaulted during his 2017 removal from a game. Judge Richard Sullivan wrote that "ample evidence, including videos from multiple angles and the unanimous testimony of every witness, shows that Oakley had a reasonable opportunity to depart the Garden, that he instead resorted to physical violence, and that he was never pushed to the ground." MSG called it the third dismissal of claims its lawyers say were always false, and said it is pursuing legal action against Oakley's attorneys.
  • Peyton Watson's future in Denver is looking murky. A source told ESPN's Ben McMahon that Watson's situation is "very uncertain" with the Nuggets, that other teams have interest, and that his asking price, roughly in line with Christian Brown's contract, is probably too high. "I will also believe that he plays for the qualifying offer when I see it," McMahon said.
  • Miles Bridges did not sign an extension with the Suns before the deadline and is expected to wait until later to revisit one. Brian Windhorst said on The Hoop Collective that Phoenix has every intention of giving Bridges a significant role and paying him accordingly. Bridges said separately that the city and fanbase made the decision to stay easier: "The atmosphere we have in our arena. The fans made it kind of easier for me to stay."
  • Also on the Suns: Dillon Brooks, who just signed a $73 million extension, was asked how he'll stay motivated after getting paid. "My reward will be a championship," he said. "Obviously $73 million showcases that you're a pretty good player, but accolades from the NBA, that always keeps my engine going." Brooks also said he's looking forward to facing LeBron James on December 30, which happens to be LeBron's birthday. "That's more ammunition for me to win that game."
  • The Knicks and Rangers are heading for a corporate split. MSG Sports announced it plans to spin the two franchises into separate publicly traded companies by the end of October. James Dolan will serve as executive chairman and CEO of both. The Knicks entity will also include the Westchester Knicks; the Rangers entity includes the Hartford Wolf Pack and MSG Training Center.
  • Doc Rivers, who is on deck for the Hall of Fame conversation after a career that spanned Orlando, Boston, the Clippers, Philly, and Milwaukee, is getting a closer look from The Athletic. One former staffer said Rivers doesn't get enough credit for his offensive creativity. "I used to look at his play designs and think, 'Oh, that puts the defense in a real tough spot,'" former Clippers video coordinator Mo Dakhil said.
  • The basketball world gathered to honor Don Nelson, who passed away recently. At a Dallas memorial service, Rick Carlisle credited Nelson as the architect of modern NBA terminology: "Terms like 'small ball, stretch-big, point-forward, positionless, cross-matching' are all common terms today. Nellie invented this stuff." Gregg Popovich, speaking at the service, was equally candid: "Most of the stuff that I ran in San Antonio I stole from you. And I just tweaked it because I had different players."
  • The Bulls are hiring Jordan Brink as assistant coach and director of player development, per HoopsHype. Brink won an NBA championship with the Knicks as part of coach Mike Brown's staff and previously worked with Detroit.
  • The Indiana Pacers have waived Ethan Thompson from his two-way deal, clearing the path for Thompson to join Partizan in Serbia and freeing up a roster spot for Braden Smith to sign a two-way contract.
  • Trading card company Panini is escalating its legal fight with Fanatics, alleging in a new court filing that Fanatics illicitly obtained confidential Panini licensing information and used it to secure exclusive trading card deals with the NBA, NBPA, NFL, and NFLPA. Fanatics had previously moved to seal the new allegations.
  • Victor Wembanyama spent part of his offseason training with a professional rugby team in the south of France, according to French rugby media. Harrison Barnes was also part of the group. Meanwhile, a New York pizza spot has been making the rounds online for posting a "Heimknick Maneuver" choking-hazard sign featuring Wemby's likeness in his Spurs jersey.
  • Patrick Beverley, 38, is heading to France for the 2026-27 season after Boulazac Basket Dordogne officially announced his signing.
