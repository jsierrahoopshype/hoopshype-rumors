NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron still deciding, Bam-Herro altercation details emerge, Kawhi investigation grows

LeBron James' free agency is now in its second week with no decision in sight, and the noise around it keeps building. Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown have all been in contact with James, with Maxey leading the recruitment effort for the 76ers, per ESPN. A return to the Cavaliers remains the most frequently cited destination among rival teams, but the field is wide open enough that Golden State, Miami, Philadelphia, Minnesota and Denver are all still actively involved. Marc Stein reports that Warriors owner Joe Lacob appears determined to stay in the chase until the end, even as Cleveland is widely regarded as the frontrunner. Multiple Western Conference executives told Front Office Sports they expect James to sign with Golden State, though the consensus is that only two teams are truly in play: the Warriors and the Cavaliers.

One wrinkle in the LeBron sweepstakes: The Athletic reports that any assumption that Bronny James would follow his father to whatever team he signs with is false, according to league sources. Meanwhile, Shams Charania said on ESPN that LeBron has been reaching out to players on interested teams, with owners checking in with each other and hearing nothing. The Timberwolves have been in regular contact with agent Rich Paul, and Minnesota president Tim Connelly confirmed they are still working on the pitch.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Bam Adebayo-Tyler Herro confrontation in Las Vegas last week got physical, with Adebayo striking Herro near the chin after Herro responded verbally to a conversation Adebayo initiated about social media comments Herro had made criticizing him, per ESPN. Herro, now with the Bucks after the Giannis trade, said "I'm just trying to move past all of it. I'm focused on Milwaukee and building something special."
  • The Kawhi Leonard-Clippers investigation has grown in scope, according to The Athletic. Investigators from Wachtell Lipton are looking into whether the Clippers circumvented the salary cap through a sponsorship deal between Leonard and Aspiration, a deal sources say was worth significantly more than what Drake and Robert Downey Jr. received from the same company. Commissioner Adam Silver said the league did not pause the Kawhi trade to the Raptors, and that he hopes the investigation wraps up this summer: "It's going on longer than I would have hoped."
  • Victor Wembanyama signed a five-year, $250 million extension with the Spurs without the Rose provision escalator, meaning he left potentially $53 million on the table. Brian Windhorst noted the extension does not create cap space but does give San Antonio meaningful flexibility under the second apron, which he described as "so dreaded" in the current CBA era.
  • Peyton Watson is drawing serious outside interest as a restricted free agent. The Bucks have joined the Clippers and Hawks on the list of teams attempting to pry Watson away from Denver via sign-and-trade, per Marc Stein. Nuggets teammate Cam Johnson acknowledged the situation directly: "Peyton Watson is in contract negotiations. The only reason we're in this position is because he balled out."
  • Austin Reaves took slightly less than originally reported to give the Lakers more future flexibility. Final terms on his new deal are four years and $180 million with a player option in 2029-30, down from the $185 million figure that had circulated earlier.
  • New Pelicans signing DeAndre Jordan got a fully guaranteed two-year minimum deal because New Orleans was convinced he had genuine one-year interest elsewhere and did not want to lose him, per Stein. The two-year structure costs the Pelicans the luxury tax savings they would have received on a one-plus-option deal.
  • The 76ers president of basketball operations Mike Gansey went public with Philadelphia's interest, telling The Athletic: "We've had conversations with Rich, really since the Jaylen trade." "In my opinion, he's the best player of all time. I think his decision is dragging out because he has a lot of good options."
  • USA Basketball is expected to name Thunder coach Mark Daigneault, Pistons coach JB Bickerstaff and Gonzaga's Mark Few as assistants on Erik Spoelstra's national team staff, per Shams Charania.
  • Charlotte Hornets GM Jeff Peterson said trading LaMelo Ball to Minnesota was never going to be a popular call. "I knew that it was not going to go over well with a lot of the fanbase," Peterson told Andscape. On the return, he singled out Naz Reid as underrated in the deal: "Chip on the shoulder, got it out the mud, found a way to have success."
  • The Timberwolves signed LaMelo Ball partly to address a team culture issue. Connelly told ESPN the organization became too moody last season: "This is supposed to be fun. He enjoys life. He enjoys playing. You don't want to make this anything but a joyous atmosphere." Connelly also said Minnesota is working with one or two free agents to fill out the power forward spot.
  • The Spurs withdrew the qualifying offer for Harrison Ingram, making him an unrestricted free agent, per Keith Smith and Spotrac.
  • DeMar DeRozan would welcome a return to Toronto "if everything works out," according to a source close to the former All-Star, and the Raptors front office has been thinking about the possibility for some time, per the Toronto Star.
  • Jazz guard Trey Alexander was taken off the court on a stretcher during a summer league game against the Bulls after appearing to injure his side or abdomen on a drive to the basket. Alexander signed a two-way deal with Utah last week.
  • Commissioner Adam Silver said Las Vegas NBA expansion is moving forward, with "multiple very serious groups" presenting plans to the league's bankers. "It's music to my ears," Silver said. No votes have been taken.
  • Brooklyn Nets GM Sean Marks said the organization values Michael Porter Jr. and wants to keep him. "I love Mike, the guy, the person, the player, and I hope he is part of this team," Marks said, adding there is no immediate hurry on extension talks.
  • David Steele, the play-by-play voice of Magic basketball for 37 seasons, has announced his retirement.
