NBA Rumors Wrap: Wemby's historic Game 1, Fox injury update, Giannis suitors revealed, Pelicans hire Mosley **Victor Wembanyama** delivered one of the most astonishing playoff performances in NBA history Monday night, finishing with 41 points, 24 rebounds and 2 blocks across nearly 49 minutes in the **San Antonio Spurs'** double-overtime Game 1 win over the **Oklahoma City Thunder** in the Western Conference Finals. The 24 rebounds were more than the entire Thunder starting lineup combined, and Wembanyama's 41-20 game made him the youngest player in NBA history to hit that line in the playoffs, the first Spur to do it since David Robinson, and the first player to do it in the conference finals since 1993. It came on the same night **Shai Gilgeous-Alexander** was presented with the MVP trophy, a sequence Wembanyama acknowledged afterward with a simple, "I'm number 1… I told you." The last player with a 40-20 game this deep in the postseason was prime Shaq in 2001. SGA, who shot 7-for-23 from the field and scored just 2 points in the two overtimes, said afterward, "We just got to be better, me in particular." Wembanyama's impact on OKC's offense was staggering in the film. The Thunder shot 35.5% in the paint with him on the court, going just 9-for-21 at the rim. When Wemby sat for the nine minutes he was off the floor, OKC converted 8-of-13 paint attempts. Jalen Williams summed up the futility simply: "If anyone has 41, you probably didn't make him work that much." Spurs coach Mitch Johnson confirmed Wembanyama was motivated by watching SGA accept the MVP award before tip-off, saying "One hundred percent. He's competitive." Asked afterward if he feels like the best player in the world, Wemby said, "Do I feel like it right now? I feel tired… We'll see." Here are more notes from around the league: • **Dylan Harper** had a coming-out moment of his own in Game 1, recording seven steals, the most by a rookie in a Conference Finals game since Magic Johnson. Harper said afterward the performance was less about individual heroics and more about execution: "My seven steals was kind of a team thing, it was more just rotations, and just for me, being in the right place and just let my instincts take over." Teammate Stephon Castle had a shorter review of Wembanyama's night, offering a colorful locker room endorsement that left no room for ambiguity. • There is optimism that **De'Aaron Fox** will play in Game 2 after missing Game 1 with an ankle injury. Fox's return would significantly change the Spurs' offensive picture, adding a pick-and-roll threat that forced OKC to game-plan differently in the regular season. • Before the series even tipped, league executives and scouts were already treating this matchup as a marquee event. "This is the Finals," one West assistant coach told ESPN, while an Eastern Conference executive said it "has a chance to go down as one of the best series of the past decade." After Game 1, that framing looks accurate. • **Shai Gilgeous-Alexander** celebrated his back-to-back MVP award by gifting his Thunder teammates Audemars Piguet watches, Burberry trench coats and golf clubs. The trench coats were worn to SGA's MVP press conference, though one needed alterations: "Chet, we gotta extend Chet's, but we'll figure his out," SGA said of **Chet Holmgren**. "My bad Chet, I apologize. You're going to get two, don't worry." • **Draymond Green** was openly critical of Shams Charania for reporting the MVP result before the league's official announcement. "It makes our league look like we have no organization," Green said. Charania, for his part, defended the decision, saying that when he has news vetted and confirmed, he reports it regardless of the occasion. • Multiple teams expressed interest in **Giannis Antetokounmpo** before the trade deadline, per Shams Charania. Miami and Minnesota had the most extensive conversations, while Cleveland, the Lakers, the Celtics and the Knicks all checked in at varying levels. On Boston specifically: "They've never gotten into the particulars because it takes two to tango," Charania said. Expect those conversations to resume this offseason. • **LeBron James** will be an unrestricted free agent this summer, and his agent Rich Paul told Shams Charania that essentially every contender in the NBA has already placed a call since the season ended. Charania said LeBron will likely need time with his family and inner circle before making any decision. • The **New Orleans Pelicans** have hired Jamahl Mosley as their new head coach on a five-year contract, per Shams Charania. Mosley spent five seasons in Orlando, guiding the Magic to three straight playoff appearances after taking over a rebuilding team following a 21-win season, and the Pelicans see a similar trajectory ahead for their young roster. • The **Detroit Pistons** plan to retain **Jalen Duren** this offseason, though at a lower rate than the max given his disappointing playoff showing. Duren could theoretically qualify for a five-year max worth 30% of the cap with an All-NBA nod, but the belief around the league is that he has no leverage for that figure after this postseason. • Detroit is also moving to lock up **Ausar Thompson** on a rookie-scale extension this offseason. The Pistons view Thompson as part of their core, and his postseason showing backed that up: he led all players with 28 steals and 25 blocks combined. Despite his offensive limitations, his defensive impact was impossible to ignore. • Despite Detroit's second-round exit, the front office's faith in coach **JB Bickerstaff** has not changed. The expectation, per league sources, is for Bickerstaff to be around long term. He signed an extension the day after Detroit eliminated Orlando in the first round. • **James Dolan** is moving to split the **Knicks** and New York Rangers into two separate, publicly traded companies via a tax-free spin-off, with an initial Form 10 registration filed confidentially with the SEC. Analysts say the move could unlock billions in franchise value that has long been tied up under MSG Sports' structure. • The **Knicks** open the Eastern Conference Finals on Tuesday and **Josh Hart** says the long layoff has been a mixed bag. "Obviously, you'd rather not have a 9-day break," Hart said, adding that the lessons from previous late-series collapses remain front of mind: "You see 10-, 15-, 20-point leads dwindle in four, five minutes. Play until there are zeroes on the clock." • Marc Stein reports that Cavaliers GM **Mike Gansey** and Phoenix Mercury GM **Nick U'Ren** have emerged as names to watch in Philadelphia's front office search. U'Ren worked for the Warriors before moving to the WNBA. • **Joe Ingles** has officially signed a two-year deal with Melbourne United in the NBL, returning to the city where his professional career began nearly 20 years ago. "Coming home to Melbourne feels incredibly special," Ingles said.
