NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's Sixers deal, Draymond staying in Golden State, Kuminga sign-and-trade stalled

LeBron James made his decision at 1 a.m. Friday, and the world found out from a trading card. Topps posted what appeared to be an already-autographed one-of-one card on Instagram in a collaborative post with James before any reporter or the league itself had been notified, blindsiding the NBA and beating ESPN's Shams Charania to the scoop. Sources told the Post the card's signature was digitally modified, with the actual autograph to be completed in the coming days. Rich Paul, James' agent, said the process was "an emotional roller coaster every single day," with James seriously considering Cleveland, Miami and Golden State before landing on Philadelphia. Paul said the Jaylen Brown trade by first-year Sixers president Mike Gansey was the turning point: "Now you got Jaylen Brown. Now hold on, wait a minute now. This looks a lot different."

James did contemplate retirement after the Lakers were swept by Oklahoma City in the second round, but felt "incomplete" about the season. Rich Paul told his podcast that if L.A. had lost to the Rockets in the first round, it could have been a different story: "I think if they somehow got swept or lost 4-1 in that series, it could be different. I think that gave us another ride." Three factors ultimately worked against Cleveland: LeBron's family situation was not a factor in the decision, the Cavs failed to land Brown, and Darius Garland was not on the roster. The Warriors' situation also got complicated, as Bob Myers appeared on Rich Paul's podcast to make his pitch directly, which one rival coach called "brilliant" even if it ultimately wasn't enough.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • With James headed to Philly, the Warriors are now expected to re-sign Draymond Green to roughly the same $28 million he would have received on the player option he declined last month. Joe Lacob, meanwhile, is focused on something longer term: building the next Warriors team after Stephen Curry.
  • Kentavious Caldwell-Pope has agreed to a buyout with the Memphis Grizzlies and plans to sign a one-year, $3.9 million deal with the 76ers after clearing waivers, per Shams Charania. The two-time champion won the 2020 title alongside James in Los Angeles and gives Philadelphia a proven 3-and-D wing.
  • Charles Barkley told Stephen A. Smith that if the Sixers win back-to-back titles in LeBron's two years in Philadelphia, he'd be ready to call him the GOAT. "If he wins back-to-back championships and walks away this thing at 44, 45, I'll say something I ain't never said before." Barkley also noted his standing criticism: "He stacked in Miami. He stacked in Cleveland. He stacked in LA. Now he's stacking it in Philly."
  • Jonathan Kuminga's sign-and-trade situation remains stuck, and the core issue is contract length. Kuminga will only accept a one-year deal if he has to take less than his asking price, but a sign-and-trade requires a minimum three-year contract. "He doesn't want to be on a smaller contract that devalues himself," per Keith Smith.
  • Restricted free agent forward Spencer Jones has agreed to a two-year, $12 million offer sheet with the Oklahoma City Thunder, per HoopsHype. The Denver Nuggets have the right to match, but doing so alongside a Peyton Watson deal in the $23 million range would put Denver at $238 million in luxury tax penalties alone, not counting payroll. Jones announced the offer sheet on LinkedIn, writing: "Bet you've never seen a free agency announcement on LinkedIn. Neither have I."
  • The Miami Heat are prioritizing Klay Thompson, but there is no sign Dallas is ready to move on from him. The Mavericks are well below the luxury tax line and have no financial incentive to release Thompson, who is owed $17.5 million in the final year of his deal, unless he takes a significant buyout haircut. The Heat spoke with Bradley Beal's camp earlier this summer and have interest in DeMar DeRozan, though at least one Heat official wants to be patient before committing any of the team's three open roster spots.
  • Mario Hezonja's agent hinted on social media that his client could be headed to the Cavaliers as Cleveland now looks to reshape its roster following James' departure.
  • Tyrese Haliburton is 52 weeks removed from his injury and says he's back. "I'm playing five-on-five, doing hard workouts every day. I could pretty much say I'm back at this point," the Pacers guard said, adding that returning to game shape will still take some time.
  • Guerschon Yabusele appears headed back to Europe. In a phone call clip, he said the NBA offer he received was less than expected and that the Panathinaikos offer "really speaks to me."
  • The New Orleans Pelicans and Kobe Bufkin have agreed to a training camp deal, with the former first-round pick competing for the final roster spot. Bufkin averaged 21.3 points in Summer League with New Orleans.
  • The Houston Rockets have signed forward Julian Phillips, who was originally the 35th pick in the 2023 draft and spent time with both the Bulls and Timberwolves before landing in Houston.
  • New Trail Blazers owner Tom Dundon has gutted the team's broadcast operation. Radio voice Travis Demers was offered a contract that included a 60-plus percent pay cut, no benefits, no travel and no analyst, and he declined. TV veteran Kevin Calabro turned down an offer to return, and analyst Michael Holton was released. Neil Everett, who lost his hosting role this week, found out not from a phone call or email, but by reading a Forbes column about it. Dundon followed a similar playbook when he took over the NHL's Hurricanes, where longtime play-by-play man Chuck Kaiton left after being offered an 80 percent pay cut following 39 seasons.
  • Dwyane Wade opened up about his health, saying at 44 he has already had cancer surgery. "I started living a little different life, a little freer. I smile more, and I always smile. I laugh more. I appreciate more."
  • FIBA mourned the passing of former president Horacio Muratore, who led the organization from 2014 to 2019 through the introduction of international windows in 2017.
