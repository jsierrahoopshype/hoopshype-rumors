NBA Rumors Wrap: Thunder trade Lu Dort, Mavs land Risacher, LeBron free agency heats up

The Oklahoma City Thunder have traded Luguentz Dort to the Atlanta Hawks in a three-team deal that also sends former No. 1 pick Zaccharie Risacher to the Dallas Mavericks and Ryan Nembhard to Atlanta, per Shams Charania. Rather than simply declining Dort's $17.7 million team option, OKC picked it up, moved him, and in doing so created a $17 million trade exception while collecting three future second-round picks. The Thunder have now acquired seven second-round picks this offseason and shed $224 million in combined payroll and luxury tax obligations, positioning themselves comfortably under the second apron. With Dort eligible for an extension worth up to three years and $67 million, Atlanta now has a decision to make on whether to keep him long-term. On the Dallas end, the Mavericks now have three former No. 1 overall picks on their roster at the same time: Cooper Flagg, Kyrie Irving and Risacher.

The cap math behind the deal is striking. OKC saves roughly $100 million and still has enough second apron space to sign a 15th player without crossing the threshold. That flexibility matters because Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's supermax kicks in next season and Cason Wallace is also extension-eligible. The three picks heading to OKC are a 2027 Chicago pick via Dallas, a 2031 most-favorable Atlanta pick, and a 2032 most-favorable Atlanta pick. Dallas also holds $7 million remaining of their Anthony Davis trade exception and has Risacher's $17.4 million team option for 2027-28 slightly more likely to be picked up after the move, though the Mavericks are now out of second-round picks to trade.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Mavericks are still fielding offers on Daniel Gafford and would prefer to trade Klay Thompson rather than buy him out. Teams calling about Kyrie Irving have been told he is not available, but Gafford, Thompson, and PJ Washington are regarded as the most moveable of Dallas's veterans. Whether any of those deals actually materialize is another question: Dallas appears to be holding out for a first-round pick for Gafford, and no one has yet put together an offer that moves them.
  • Thompson himself offered a hint about his future when a fan at a recent event told him they missed him on the Warriors. "Never say never," Thompson said. The Mavericks have made clear they want to trade him, not absorb the buyout cost.
  • The Sixers' recruitment of LeBron James is picking up. Owner Josh Harris had a brief conversation with James at a VIP event during Fanatics Fest in New York on July 17, and players Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and Jaylen Brown have all told James they want him on the team. LeBron remains unsigned and has not indicated publicly where he is leaning.
  • James Harden made clear what he is hoping for in Cleveland. "That's the goal, that's the plan," Harden said of forming a Big Three with Donovan Mitchell and LeBron James on the Cavaliers. Whether LeBron shares that vision remains to be seen, with Philadelphia and other teams still in the mix.
  • Anthony Davis made two notable predictions about the Washington Wizards: he plans to play all 82 games next season and said the team will win 40-plus games. Davis also predicted that No. 1 pick AJ Dybantsa will win Rookie of the Year.
  • Luka Doncic said he is ready to move on after last season. "I think we have a really good team. I'm very excited to start the next season," Doncic told Golf Channel. "Obviously, last season didn't end how we wanted, so we're excited for the new season."
  • The Memphis Grizzlies and Golden State Warriors met in the Las Vegas Summer League championship game Sunday, with Cameron Boozer entering the final as one of the stars of the tournament alongside Cedric Coward, making Memphis the betting favorite. Before tip, the league held a moment of silence for Brandon Clarke, who led the Grizzlies to the 2019 Summer League title and was named MVP before his career was cut short by a torn ACL.
  • Brooklyn Nets GM Sean Marks spoke about the signing of Julius Randle, saying Randle "understands where he is in his career" and will provide both scoring and veteran leadership for a young group. Marks also said the organization is intentionally preserving roster flexibility: "You just never know what's going to come your way."
  • Denver Nuggets wing Julian Strawther is approaching a contract decision point. He is eligible for a rookie-scale extension and will become a restricted free agent next summer if Denver does not reach a deal before the season. Strawther also said he is excited about the NBA's expansion to Las Vegas and acknowledged the appeal of eventually playing in his hometown.
  • Free agents Trevor Keels and Jahmir Young both had strong Las Vegas Summer League performances for the Miami Heat but remain unsigned unrestricted free agents. Keels finished last season on a two-way deal; Young was on a standard contract.
  • The Boston Celtics are encouraged by the development of big man Hugo Gonzalez this offseason. Celtics assistant Amile Jefferson said Gonzalez has grown physically and praised his defensive versatility: "You saw it last season, putting him on team's five, play him one through five, just whoever the best player is."
  • Jalen Brunson and Karl-Anthony Towns made appearances at WWE Saturday Night's Main Event at Madison Square Garden over the weekend. Brunson, wearing a brace on his left wrist, emerged alongside Roman Reigns. Towns went further, choke-slamming JD McDonagh to help Danhausen pick up the win in his match.
  • Duncan Robinson hosted a charity basketball event in New Hampshire this weekend in honor of his late brother Eli, who died in April 2025 after battling schizophrenia, addiction, and mental illness. "It's OK to not be OK," Robinson said at the event. "I think the most important thing you can do is lean into a community."
