NBA Rumors Wrap: Heat and Celtics lead Giannis chase, LeBron uncommitted to Lakers, Trae Young's option deadline looms

The Milwaukee Bucks are closing in on a Giannis Antetokounmpo trade, and the two teams with the most advanced talks are the Miami Heat and the Boston Celtics, per Marc Stein. The Heat remain in full pursuit, while the Celtics have not abandoned hope of landing the two-time MVP. Boston would need to include players and future picks, and sources say the Bucks have specific interest in Celtics young guard Hugo Gonzalez. Minnesota checked back in with Milwaukee last week about pairing Antetokounmpo with Anthony Edwards, only to be told the Bucks want even more than what was discussed in the winter. As for the Lakers, Marc Stein said flatly on the ALL NBA Podcast: "I have not heard that," when asked about LA being part of any trade framework.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • LeBron James has not fully committed to returning to the Lakers next season. Per Ramona Shelburne, his representatives described the initial conversations with the team as "we're keeping in touch," with no salary figures discussed. Separately, The Athletic's Lakers beat notes the team remains "on the clock" with Luka Doncic, who missed the entire playoffs with a hamstring injury and said this summer, "if you don't win the title, it's a bad season."
  • • Trae Young's future with the Washington Wizards remains the most-watch storyline heading into Tuesday's draft. Marc Stein has reported since March that Young would stay in Washington on a three-year deal, but there have been "rumbles in the last week" of possible external interest. Young's option deadline is Tuesday, the same day as the draft.
  • • CJ McCollum is staying in Atlanta after agreeing to a one-year, $21 million contract extension with a 7.5 percent trade kicker, per Shams Charania. The Hawks went 19-4 with McCollum as a starter from Feb. 22 through the end of the regular season. One financial note worth flagging: because it's an extension rather than a new deal, McCollum is immediately trade-eligible with no waiting period.
  • • The Oklahoma City Thunder traded Aaron Wiggins to the Atlanta Hawks for two second-round picks, and the financial math behind it is significant. Oklahoma City reduced its total roster expenses from $549 million to $476 million by moving off Wiggins' $9.2 million salary, and its projected luxury tax penalty dropped from $213 million to $152 million. The move also gives the Thunder a cleaner path to using both of their first-round picks at No. 12 and No. 17 on Tuesday.
  • • Dallas and Oklahoma City have discussed swapping the No. 9 pick to the Thunder for Nos. 12 and 17, per Stein. The Mavericks have mutual interest with guard Brayden Burries at No. 9, but if Burries is gone by then, Dallas sliding back gains more appeal. One name to watch if they do trade down: Washington big man Hannes Steinbach, whose father played alongside Dirk Nowitzki in their shared hometown of Wurzburg roughly 30 years ago.
  • • The Detroit Pistons have expressed interest in both Kyrie Irving and Trey Murphy, per Stein, but neither appears attainable. Dallas is telling teams Kyrie is not available, and New Orleans' asking price for Murphy is described as "really, really high." If Tyler Herro ends up going to Milwaukee as part of a Giannis trade, he could become another name Detroit looks at.
  • • The Lakers are shopping for a rim-running center this offseason and have been linked to Mitchell Robinson and Robert Williams III, though both players are open to staying with the Knicks and Trail Blazers respectively. The Lakers also called the Thunder about Lu Dort's availability. Portland is expected to pursue re-signing Williams after he appeared in 59 games last season, second-most in his injury-shortened career.
  • • Rich Paul issued a warning to the Celtics front office about any potential move involving Jaylen Brown, reflecting on what happened with his client LeBron James when Kyrie Irving was traded from Cleveland. "I seen the effect that it had on LeBron when Kyrie was traded," Paul said. On the Tatum-Brown pairing specifically: "How can it be a problem when you making $300 million, and I'm making $300 million?"
  • • Jordan Goodwin intends to sign a three-year, $19 million deal to return to the Phoenix Suns, with a player option in year three, per Shams Charania. Goodwin led the team with 106 steals last season after making the roster out of training camp on a non-guaranteed contract. The Suns' next priority is re-signing restricted free agent Mark Williams.
  • • Rudy Gobert addressed his public perception in a recent interview, saying the "big media tend to show more negative things than positive things" about him, and that fan opinion often reflects what they see on social media. On retirement: "I'm still very far from thinking about it."
  • • Guerschon Yabusele said the NBA is his first priority in free agency, despite reports linking him to French club ASVEL. On his time with the Knicks, where he barely played, Yabusele said: "I said to myself, 'Ah, but I feel like I am in Boston, actually.' When I look at Boston, we are in 2026, and it was ten years ago."
  • • Dwyane Wade opened up about depression after retirement, saying the first thing he did was start therapy. "I was the Black man that was like, 'Therapy ain't for me,'" he said, but added that basketball had been his emotional outlet since age five, and losing it hit harder than expected.
  • • Jose Alvarado said he was disappointed that Victor Wembanyama did not shake hands after the NBA Finals. "I'm a competitor too, but I also like to stare my enemies down. I look forward to them, and I shake their hand," Alvarado said. The basketball from OG Anunoby's game-winning tip-in in Game 4 of the Finals is heading to Sotheby's, where it could fetch up to $3 million.
  • • The Brooklyn Nets brought in Morez Johnson for a workout as they figure out what to do with the sixth pick in Tuesday's draft. Johnson averaged 13.1 points and 7.3 rebounds while helping Michigan win the NCAA title.
  • • Michael Beasley and Lance Stephenson will each serve one-game suspensions in the BIG3 for their altercations during Saturday's game. Stephenson serves his suspension in Week 2, Beasley in Week 3, timed to ensure Miami 305 still has four eligible players available each week.
