NBA Rumors Wrap: Rich Paul says LeBron's decision has no timeline, Pistons-Duren talks at standstill, Blazers-Portland arena deal in jeopardy

LeBron James' free agency remains in a holding pattern with no end in sight, and his agent is done pretending otherwise. Rich Paul went on his "Game Over" podcast with Max Kellerman on Monday and made clear that nobody, including himself, knows when a decision is coming. "I don't know when his choice is going to be made," Paul said. "It could be 48 seconds, it could be 48 minutes." He dismissed reports that an announcement was imminent and said he has not told any teams to hold cap space or make roster moves in anticipation of James' choice. Per Paul, money is not the deciding factor since all suitors are offering the same amount.

The ripple effects of the delay are real. Commissioner Adam Silver has acknowledged the league cannot finalize next season's schedule until James picks a team. The Cleveland Cavaliers, Golden State Warriors and Miami Heat are each sitting on two separate offseason plans, one for if LeBron signs, one for if he doesn't, and neither column can be executed. Veterans like Draymond Green, James Harden and Jonathan Kuminga remain in limbo as a result. Paul's message to the teams in pursuit: be patient. "My advice has been don't just value the paper," he said. "The paper in July is different than when you get into training camp, when you get into January."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Portland Trail Blazers' arena situation is deteriorating fast. A top team official shredded Portland's proposed term sheet for a taxpayer-funded Moda Center renovation, calling it a near-categorical rejection of the city's offer. "The deal doesn't get better. It gets worse with time," the official said. The team ranked last in the league in sponsorships last season and was the only franchise without a jersey patch sponsor, financial realities that ownership underestimated when it bought the team.
  • Making things harder, two Multnomah County commissioners are now seeking significant changes to the county's nine-figure contribution to the renovation, which could further destabilize a deal already close to collapse. An August 12 vote looms, and the Blazers have asked the city to agree in writing not to sue before any architectural plans are drawn up, a timeline that many city officials see as unworkable. One councilmember compared owner Tom Dundon's approach to that of a businessman looking for an excuse to blame the city and move the team.
  • Negotiations between the Detroit Pistons and Jalen Duren on a new contract have stalled. ESPN's Ramona Shelburne reported the talks are at a standstill and could drag deep into August. Duren still has the option of signing his qualifying offer if a long-term deal doesn't come together.
  • Mario Hezonja is expected to return to the NBA for the first time since the 2019-20 season, per Marc Stein, with multiple teams showing interest in the Real Madrid forward. Among those teams are some also pursuing LeBron James.
  • Klay Thompson, in his contract year with the Dallas Mavericks, told a fan at an autograph signing that a return to Golden State would be nice. "Shoot I don't know," Thompson said when asked if he'd go back to the Warriors. "I got one year on my deal. It'd be nice to end it in Golden State."
  • Draymond Green, meanwhile, has been thinking about what a Warriors roster with LeBron would actually look like on the court. On his podcast, he said he'd essentially become a corner three specialist if LeBron and Stephen Curry were running pick-and-rolls together. "If you got LeBron James and Steph Curry in a pick and roll, you better go find a role. You better go find a spot so you can be valuable," Green said.
  • HoopsHype has learned the Los Angeles Clippers and Jamarion Sharp have agreed to a two-way deal. Sharp was the G League's Defensive Player of the Year last season and led the league with 4.1 blocks per game. With Yanic Konan Niederhauser hurt, Sharp adds needed frontcourt depth.
  • The Indiana Pacers and Jalen Slawson have agreed to a two-way deal, per HoopsHype. Slawson averaged 7.3 points, 4.4 rebounds, 2.8 assists, 1.5 steals and 1.1 blocks in 13 games for Indiana last season. The Atlanta Hawks and Jalen Wilson have also agreed to a two-way, bringing Wilson back to the NBA after three seasons with Brooklyn.
  • Yaxel Lendeborg was named MVP of the 2026 NBA Summer League in Las Vegas after averaging 14.8 points, 6.8 rebounds and 4.0 assists in six games for the Warriors, who won the championship. Lendeborg led a 12-point, fourth-quarter comeback in the title game. Draymond Green said on his podcast he was impressed: "I've seen many guys over the years tighten up when the moment gets there and not be able to get it across the finish line."
  • Tyrese Haliburton says he's fully past his shingles illness. He put on 15-20 pounds of unwanted weight from the illness and its medication but said he's worked it off and is feeling better than he has in a long time. "Shingles is mostly beyond me now. I've lost the weight. I feel great. This is the best I've felt in a long time," Haliburton said.
  • Khris Middleton, heading into his 15th NBA season with the Washington Wizards, said he's excited about Washington's young roster. "Young guys have made a huge jump development-wise since they've been drafted. Year three for a lot of them... throw in the No. 1 pick, AJ [Dybantsa]—really excited to see what this team can do," he said. Middleton also laughed recalling how he found out about his trade to Dallas last season: "We all found out a couple minutes before it went live."
  • Trae Young's agent Aaron Mintz told Fadeaway World there's nothing complicated about Young's extension with the Wizards. "This is a no-brainer move for both parties," Mintz said. He pushed back on contract criticism by pointing to the last two champions, both of which were led by star point guards, and called that position the hardest to find in the league.
  • Jalen Brunson says the Knicks haven't discussed as a team whether they'll visit the Trump White House following their championship. When told they'd be the first NBA team to do so, he responded: "Interesting… Interesting." Owner James Dolan had indicated the team would go.
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo addressed his absence from Greece's national team after they dropped matches to Romania and Portugal. "We underestimated them," he said, adding he wasn't called up for those games but intends to return for the critical upcoming FIBA window if selected.
  • A Wilt Chamberlain warmup jacket found in a Portland Goodwill by a 19-year-old reseller sold for $89,600 at Sotheby's on Monday. The jacket was matched to the 1972 NBA Finals, when Chamberlain won his second title and Finals MVP with the Lakers. Quinn Brown, who bought it at a bargain bin, had previously never netted more than $250 on a resell.
