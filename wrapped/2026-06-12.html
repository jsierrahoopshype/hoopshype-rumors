NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks one win from title after historic comeback, Celtics emerge as real Giannis threat, Harden eyes Cavs extension

The New York Knicks are one win from their first NBA championship since 1973 after pulling off the greatest comeback in NBA Finals history Wednesday night, erasing a 29-point deficit to beat the San Antonio Spurs 107-106 and take a 3-1 series lead. Jalen Brunson scored 36 points and OG Anunoby finished with 33, including the game-winning tip-in, as New York outscored San Antonio by 33 in the fourth quarter. De'Aaron Fox compounded a brutal fourth quarter with a late-game decision to attack the rim rather than run out the clock, and former teammate Iman Shumpert said Fox will kick himself for that fourth quarter for a while. Game 5 is in San Antonio, where the Spurs will need to win three straight to take the title.

Victor Wembanyama also missed two free throws late with the Spurs ahead by one and blamed his team's collapse on "greediness": "We clearly weren't the hungriest in the second half." He also logged his second flagrant foul of the postseason in the third quarter, and now sits one flagrant foul point away from an automatic suspension.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Boston Celtics have emerged as a genuine threat to Miami's pursuit of Giannis Antetokounmpo, with multiple sources now saying Boston has to be monitored. Bill Simmons, who previously said the Celtics were out of the running, reversed course Thursday, saying: "I think they are in on Giannis Antetokounmpo. Maybe they felt like Miami's offer is an offer they could beat." He also said Giannis "wants to end up in Boston because he would have the best chance to win a title."
  • Miami remains the favorite, and the Bucks are intrigued by their offer. Per Marc Stein, the package widely presumed to be on the table calls for Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware and Jacquez Jr. along with prime draft capital. Stein also reports that Giannis' interest in Miami dates back to the 2020 offseason, before he signed the first of his two extensions with Milwaukee.
  • Teams and agents around the league are operating as if a Giannis trade is a done deal, per Zach Lowe. "They're looking at Milwaukee as a rebuilding team. They're looking at Milwaukee as a team that might have another first round pick in the lottery and scheduling their draft workouts around it." Some sources expect the deal to happen as soon as the NBA Finals conclude.
  • Minnesota and Golden State were prominent suitors in February but have not factored into Milwaukee's recent trade talks. There also has not been any significant recent dialogue between the Bucks and Orlando. On the Boston angle, Kevin O'Connor said on a recent podcast that the Bucks don't want Jaylen Brown and a deal to Miami would be simpler: "It seems like it would probably just be Milwaukee and Miami."
  • James Harden and the Cleveland Cavaliers are widely expected around the league to agree to a multiyear deal, potentially three years under the over-38 rule, that lowers his $42.3 million player option and gives him more guaranteed money, per ESPN. Getting under the second apron is a priority for Cleveland.
  • The NYPD arrested 15 people and issued 41 criminal court summonses Wednesday night outside Madison Square Garden. Video also showed at least one egg thrown toward Wembanyama as he entered his hotel after the game, flanked by security. No arrests have been made in connection with the egg throwing.
  • The NBA's two-minute report found two officiating errors in Game 4, neither of which was OG Anunoby's game-winning block on Fox. The league determined Anunoby made a legal play. The two mistakes: Wembanyama should have been called for a defensive three-second violation with 1:26 left, and Josh Hart should have been whistled for a foul on Stephon Castle with 1:03 remaining.
  • Wembanyama told reporters after the game he is convinced the Spurs are "built this way" and will recover. Fox said the team "has to figure out a way to sustain" leads after building double-digit advantages in all four games. Rookie Dylan Harper said the loss hit him but left him in shock.
  • LeBron James to the Golden State Warriors remains a live rumor, with sources telling NBC Sports Bay Area that there is "at least curiosity on both sides." No principal figure has gone on record to deny it, and some are saying off the record it cannot be dismissed. The financial obstacles are significant, but the conversation has not died.
  • Jalen Duren is expected to command more than $30 million per year in free agency, per ESPN. How high that number climbs is the key question for Detroit, where a large extension would cut into the Pistons' future flexibility considerably.
  • The Dallas Mavericks have told rival teams they are open to trades in either direction around their No. 9 pick. The Atlanta Hawks are similarly willing to field offers for their No. 23 selection, though they plan to hold onto their No. 8 lottery pick.
  • Sacramento and Charlotte have had preliminary talks about a Domantas Sabonis trade, with the Kings focused on acquiring the Hornets' No. 14 or No. 18 pick. Charlotte is not currently willing to give up either pick. Sacramento is also said to have interest in Toronto's No. 19 selection.
  • A federal judge has asked the NBA to weigh in on whether Terry Rozier should be allowed to play next season. Rozier, who has pleaded not guilty to all four charges stemming from an alleged illegal gambling investigation, has a trial date set for Feb. 8, 2027. He is currently barred from contact with the Hornets while his case proceeds.
  • The NBA, EuroLeague and FIBA held what all three described as "constructive discussions" Wednesday on a potential NBA Europe collaboration, with a follow-up meeting scheduled for early July. The meeting was held in Mies, Switzerland, and while one outlet reported no significant progress, other sources said the negotiations are picking up.
