NBA Rumors Wrap: Klay Thompson trade search stalls in Dallas, Knicks' Rosas departs, Kings won't buy out LaVine

The Dallas Mavericks have been shopping Klay Thompson but haven't found a taker yet, per The Athletic. Thompson is owed $17.5 million this season and has been Dallas's leading three-point shooter in each of the past two years, but the team has yet to find a deal despite exploring the market with multiple rival teams. Whether Thompson returns for a third season in Dallas or gets moved before camp opens is one of the more pressing questions of the Mavericks' offseason. The team currently holds 16 standard contracts and needs to shape its roster before the season tips off.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The New York Knicks are losing a key front office voice. Senior Vice President of Basketball Operations Gersson Rosas has departed the organization after four seasons that included one NBA championship, per HoopsHype. Rosas hinted at the move in an Instagram post before the news was confirmed.
  • The Sacramento Kings have no plans to buy out Zach LaVine, and the two sides are aligned on him returning for the 2026-27 season. The Kings had been willing to facilitate a sign-and-trade before LaVine opted into his deal in June, even one that paid him less money on a longer-term contract, but no deal materialized. LaVine is now entering a contract year with Kings management expecting a big season from him.
  • Chris Bosh opened up about a serious health scare in a new HoopsHype interview. "I had an episode in January. I almost dropped dead pretty much and came back to life," Bosh said, confirming he suffered another pulmonary embolism and spent a week in the hospital. He said he is managing his condition daily and urged Victor Wembanyama, who also dealt with a blood clot last season, to stay disciplined with his treatment: "It only takes one more time. It happened one more time for me, it happened twice, and I couldn't play anymore."
  • Luka Doncic declared himself fully healthy in a letter to Lakers fans, saying his recovery from the hamstring injury that ended his season early went great. "I took a real break from basketball the first few weeks after the season ended to rehab my body," Doncic wrote. "When I let myself miss the game a little bit and try some new things, it only makes me more excited and motivated to get back to playing." Marc Stein also shot down a social media rumor that Doncic and Cooper Flagg had a golf summit planned in LA, clarifying that Doncic is in Slovenia for the entire offseason and Flagg is training in Maine and Dallas.
  • Bam Adebayo said he can't wait to play alongside Giannis Antetokounmpo in Miami. "I feel like Pat [Riley] has been trying to get somebody at that star level for a while now," Adebayo told the Miami Herald. When asked about concerns over the Heat's playmaking and shooting depth around him and Giannis, Adebayo kept it brief: "We'll figure it out."
  • The Sixers' rookie class is thrilled about LeBron James arriving in Philadelphia. Labaron Philon said he woke up to about five missed calls on July 24 when the news broke, describing it as "a big 'Wow.'" He told the Inquirer he's eager to learn from LeBron's approach to longevity, hoping his own career stretches into his 40s. Meanwhile, VJ Edgecombe found out the news mid-workout when a coach's Apple Watch kept buzzing. "I had to go check my phone and see if it was real. And then I went and finished my workout," Edgecombe said.
  • Former Laker Byron Scott offered a skeptical take on Philadelphia's title odds. "Talent on paper don't mean a whole lot," Scott said on podcast, drawing a comparison to the 2004 Lakers team with Kobe, Shaq, Karl Malone and Gary Payton that lost to the Pistons. He also speculated that James Harden's presence in Cleveland is what kept LeBron from returning to the Cavs for a third time, adding that reports of a poor fit with Donovan Mitchell were also a factor.
  • The Denver Nuggets would prefer to trade Zeke Nnaji rather than simply release him, but any deal would require attaching draft capital the team doesn't have much of, Marc Stein noted. Stein also said that if Denver keeps Peyton Watson, a trade elsewhere in the roster is likely coming, and one he suggested the DNVR Nuggets fanbase probably won't like.
  • Gregg Popovich is making encouraging progress in his recovery. Spurs CEO RC Buford said Popovich "continues to rehab with more purpose and diligence than I think any of us could have imagined" and that he is improving. Buford also said Popovich's presence remains a source of support for the organization.
  • Four second-round picks from June's draft may have the option to return to college after a Colorado federal judge ruled the NCAA cannot block athletes who enrolled in 2022 from using a fifth year of eligibility. The players affected include Tyler Nickel (Knicks), Braden Smith (Pacers via Bulls), Dillon Mitchell (Celtics) and Lajae Jones (Warriors). Smith has already decided against returning to school, though he remains unsigned by Indiana.
  • The Timberwolves have added veteran wing Will Barton to their coaching staff in a player development role, according to Charlie Walton. Barton spent eight years in Denver under GM Tim Connelly, who is now Minnesota's president of basketball operations. Barton coached with the Iowa Wolves last season and with the Timberwolves' summer league squad this year.
  • The Portland Trail Blazers have made two front office hires: James Brocato as Director of Basketball Innovation and Ameer Bahhur as head coach of their G League affiliate, the Rip City Remix, per HoopsHype. Brocato has been the Mavericks' director of basketball analytics since 2015, and Bahhur previously worked as an assistant with the Magic from 2022-24. Portland will also host an unusual preseason game in October when the London Lions visit the Moda Center, the first time a British club has ever faced an NBA team. Lions head coach Tautvydas Sabonis has a personal connection to Portland: his father Arvydas played seven seasons there.
  • The New Orleans Pelicans have agreed to a one-year deal with wing Caleb Houstan, who shot 52.4 percent from three with Atlanta last season. Houstan will compete for the final roster spot and reunites with former Magic coach Jamahl Mosley, who is now New Orleans's head coach.
  • Tyrese Haliburton got married and had a memorable musical moment at the reception: 50 Cent performed "I Get Money," which includes the lyric "I Run New York," a not-so-subtle jab at the Knicks from the Pacers star's big day.
  • The Oklahoma City Thunder will be inducted into the Oklahoma Sports Hall of Fame as a Team of Legend in the Class of 2026, honoring the franchise's 2025 NBA championship.
