NBA Rumors Wrap: League bewildered by Celtics' Brown return, LeBron's suitors emerge, Kuminga draws Bucks and Cavs interest

The Boston Celtics' decision to trade Jaylen Brown to the Philadelphia 76ers for Paul George, two first-round picks, and two second-round picks has left front offices around the league scratching their heads. "They couldn't have gotten better than that?" one neutral executive told HoopsHype, speaking on behalf of a team that was not involved in the Brown sweepstakes. Brown, 29, finished sixth in NBA MVP voting this season, while George is 36. Per Shams Charania, the Celtics never seriously considered trading Jayson Tatum despite rival interest, and instead determined weeks ago that it needed to move on from Brown, with sources telling ESPN the franchise felt the bond between Brown and the team was "irreparable" after his name had been dangled in trade talks previously. Brown said he was still processing the trade, telling HoopsHype: "I put my heart into this city. Because that's what I said I would do when I got drafted. 'I'm gonna go to war for this city.' And I feel like that's what I did."

One side effect of the deal: the Celtics now have additional draft capital that the New Orleans Pelicans have coveted in talks around wing Trey Murphy III. Boston has monitored Murphy's trade market, and teams including the Pistons, Warriors, Clippers, and Hawks are also in the mix to varying degrees, per HoopsHype.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • LeBron James' free agency is moving quickly. Rich Paul has already fielded a dozen pitches from teams, and Shams Charania identified the Golden State Warriors, Cleveland Cavaliers, and Miami Heat as the teams that make the most logical sense right now. The Warriors have received indications from James that they remain on his short list, though team sources believe they may not be at the top of it. Philadelphia has also emerged as a strong suitor, with Tyrese Maxey's connection to Klutch Sports viewed as a significant recruiting tool, and the New York Knicks have also expressed interest, according to Jake Fischer.
  • • Jonathan Kuminga is drawing interest from multiple teams after the Atlanta Hawks declined his $24.3 million team option. The decision was strategic: Atlanta retained his Bird Rights and created financial flexibility, leaving the door open for Kuminga to return on a lower number. Beyond Atlanta, he has been discussed with the Cavaliers and Bucks in possible sign-and-trade scenarios, and the Lakers are also exploring the feasibility of adding him to fill a wing need.
  • • The Detroit Pistons are holding firm on Jalen Duren, maintaining their stance that they will not engage in sign-and-trade scenarios and instead plan to bridge the gap and get a deal done directly. For context, Duren shares an agency with Domantas Sabonis, whose Kings have been exploring the trade market for him, Zach LaVine, and DeMar DeRozan.
  • • DeRozan is hoping to accelerate his exit from Sacramento, either through a trade or by being waived and testing the open market. The 37-year-old is expecting to be waived and stretched by the Kings, a sentiment shared by rival executives. His contract carries $25 million for next season, with only $10 million guaranteed.
  • • Restricted free agent Tari Eason has agreed to a five-year, $81.5 million deal to stay with the Houston Rockets, per Shams Charania. The deal is fully guaranteed, and also includes a player option in year five and a 10 percent trade kicker.
  • • Houston's Dorian Finney-Smith trade talks are described as fluid, with conversations spanning multiple possible structures. Some iterations would move him to clear cap space; others would bring back a player. The Rockets' free-agency activity has lessened the urgency somewhat compared to earlier this summer.
  • • Anfernee Simons has agreed to a two-year, $12.3 million deal with the 76ers, including a player option in year two, per Shams Charania. Simons chose Philadelphia over the Warriors, Heat, Nuggets, Mavericks, and Pacers, believing his fit with the revamped roster was ideal.
  • • Billy Donovan has agreed to join the San Antonio Spurs as lead assistant coach under head coach Mitch Johnson, per Shams Charania. Donovan spent 11 seasons as a head coach with Chicago and Oklahoma City before accepting the role with the defending Western Conference champions.
  • • The Los Angeles Lakers don't control their own first-round pick until 2032 and their own second-round selection until 2033, leaving them with fewer draft assets than any other team in the league. Luka Dončić, meanwhile, was reportedly "excited" about the team's offseason moves, with sources saying the Lakers kept him in regular communication and addressed his top roster priorities by retaining Austin Reaves and acquiring rim protector Walker Kessler.
  • • The Knicks tried repeatedly to trade for Pelicans center Yves Missi after losing Mitchell Robinson in free agency, but New Orleans rejected every offer. The Pelicans view Missi as a core building block and the message from the organization is that he is not available. The Knicks have also had documented interest in Kevon Looney and Nick Richards, and per HoopsHype, Andre Drummond is another center worth watching in their search.
  • • Tobias Harris, who chose the Spurs on a two-year, $31 million deal, texted HoopsHype about why: "The urgency to win a championship. It felt like both our goals aligned." Harris had drawn interest from the Pistons, Lakers, Clippers, Hawks, and Hornets before landing in San Antonio.
  • • The Minnesota Timberwolves, following the LaMelo Ball blockbuster, are expected by executives around the league to now pursue a floor-spacing big man. They could use the expiring $14.68 million contract of Josh Green as a trade chip or dip into their taxpayer mid-level exception. Minnesota also remains high on 19-year-old center Joan Beringer as part of their long-term vision.
  • • The Mavericks will officially acquire the draft rights to Tarik Biberović next week and have already opened talks with his representatives about bringing him to Dallas as early as next season, per Marc Stein. Biberović plays for Fenerbahçe and his contract includes a $2 million buyout.
  • • Tyus Jones has agreed to a one-year deal to return to the Denver Nuggets, per Shams Charania. Jones played a reserve role with Denver after joining on the buyout market last season and enters his 12th NBA season. Separately, JJ Barea will serve as Denver's summer league coach.
  • • Ja Morant will wear No. 1 with the Trail Blazers next season, switching from No. 12, which he had worn since high school. Memphis rookie Cameron Boozer acknowledged the weight of losing Morant: "Obviously it sucks to lose a guy as good as Ja, what he's done for the city and the team, but it's part of the business."
  • • The Toronto Raptors have agreed to a multi-year extension with head coach Darko Rajakovic. Toronto also signed Kyle Anderson to a one-year, $3.9 million deal, reuniting him with former San Antonio teammate Kawhi Leonard.
  • • The NBA's basketball-related income for 2025-26 reached $11.68 billion, up from $10.25 billion the prior year. The Knicks' NBA Finals run was a significant revenue driver, and the season also marked the first year of the league's new 11-year, $76 billion media deal. Total player salaries and benefits came in at $6.27 billion, triggering an escrow split of 54.8% to owners and 45.2% to players.
