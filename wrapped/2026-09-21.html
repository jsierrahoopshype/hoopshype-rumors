NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers wouldn't extend Kawhi, Hawks-Hornets in Hield-Finney-Smith talks, Westbrook comeback chatter

The Los Angeles Clippers never offered Kawhi Leonard an extension beyond his $50.3 million contract for 2026-27, and that refusal was the original trigger for trade talks with the Toronto Raptors, per Marc Stein. The deal then sat in limbo all summer while the NBA wrapped up its yearlong external investigation into allegations of salary cap circumvention by the Clippers. That investigation created an unusual backdrop for the trade, tying the resolution of Leonard's situation directly to the league's findings. Now that the deal has been done, the Raptors get Leonard and a complicated situation clarifies, at least on the surface. What his availability and conditioning look like as he heads back to Toronto remains the real question.

The Atlanta Hawks and Charlotte Hornets have had on-and-off trade discussions this month centered on a framework involving Charlotte's Dorian Finney-Smith and Atlanta's Buddy Hield. One version of the framework included former lottery pick Devin Carter, but Atlanta waived Carter on Sept. 11, which opened the door for him to sign with the Boston Celtics. Whether those discussions are still alive without Carter in the mix is unclear.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Former guard Gabe Vincent had the option to sign with the Knicks but passed on it, looking for a more secure opportunity. New York's wing battle continues without him.
  • Zach Randolph says Russell Westbrook might be eyeing a comeback, telling Out the Mud TL: "This man is supposed to be in the league for another 3-4 years at least. I'd like to see him with the Heat. Brodie got a lot left in him. He might be back." Dejounte Murray echoed the sentiment on Westbrook, saying he wished the former MVP could have had a proper farewell tour. "The person he is is just… he's second to none."
  • Jimmy Butler is progressing well in his ACL recovery with the Golden State Warriors, spending his birthday in Brazil with Neymar this summer. Warriors reporter Anthony Slater said he has heard good updates on the knee's strengthening, but Butler remains months away from returning to an NBA court.
  • The Dallas Mavericks are bonding ahead of training camp, with Cooper Flagg, Kyrie Irving, coach Dusty May and Dwight Powell among those spotted together in Santa Monica. On a related note, the Mavericks are expected to hire former Canadian soccer player Nico Berg to lead their medical department, according to Stein. Berg has studied under Alex McKechnie in Toronto, which would naturally fit the vision of new Mavs president of basketball operations Masai Ujiri, who ran the Raptors for years.
  • Dirk Nowitzki says he stays in contact with Flagg and occasionally sends messages after watching him play. "He knows that I'm in his corner whenever I want him to succeed and do well. But like I said, he's so far ahead of the curve already at that age."
  • Cade Cunningham organized a team workout in San Diego in August to help new Pistons arrivals like John Collins and Isaiah Joe get acclimated before training camp opens Sept. 29. Cunningham also trained at altitude in Colorado this summer, running and hiking in the mountains with third-year wing Ron Holland.
  • Timberwolves coach Chris Finch says the addition of LaMelo Ball gives Minnesota a chance to play faster and become a better passing team, but doesn't want to overhaul what works. "Your identity is driven by your best players, for sure, and we still have them," Finch said, adding that he sees a lot of the offensive identity still flowing through Ant. He also said Jaden McDaniels needs to take another step forward, a refrain Wolves fans have heard before.
  • Lynx rookie Olivia Miles broke the WNBA single-season rookie scoring record on Sunday, surpassing Caitlin Clark's mark of 769 points set during her 2024 Rookie of the Year season. Miles hit the milestone on a free throw in the second quarter against the Connecticut Sun for her 770th point of the year. Meanwhile, Angel Reese became the first player in WNBA history to grab 500 rebounds in a single season, reaching the mark Saturday during Atlanta's win over Chicago.
  • Lisa Leslie became the first Sparks player to have a statue unveiled outside Star Plaza. In her speech, Leslie said, "Every opportunity I had was a moment to change the mind of a man who was a hater. It was a chance to convince somebody who didn't believe in women's basketball."
  • Tony Parker confirmed that discussions with the Buss Sports Capital fund, run by Joey and Jesse Buss, are "more than advanced" regarding a potential investment in ASVEL. Parker said he has signed NDAs and can't go into detail, but confirmed the Buss brothers visited Lyon and are the people they are talking with.
  • Luka Dončić posted an emotional message on social media as he returns to the U.S. after spending the offseason in Slovenia, leaving behind his two daughters with ex-fiancée Anamarie Goltes. The caption read: "G+O, no matter where in the world your daddy is. No day passes when you wouldn't be in my heart."
  • RJ Barrett said he felt "a little salty" when the Raptors lost in the first round while the Knicks went on to win the championship, but was genuine in his congratulations to his former team. "If anybody else were to win it, I'm happy they won it," Barrett said. "I'm very happy for them, but hoping to bring some of that success over here."
  • Kevin Garnett's "Anything is Possible" jersey, worn during the clinching Game 6 of the 2008 NBA Finals, sold at auction for $701,500.
  • The Charlotte Hornets have waived Bez Mbeng.
  • New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani took a shot at the Knicks' ticket prices when asked if he'd attend the season opener at MSG: "Hopefully at cheaper ticket prices. Brother, I do not have $2,000 ready for that ticket."
