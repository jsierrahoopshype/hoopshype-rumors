NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis lands in Miami, Celtics offered Jaylen Brown, Dosunmu re-signs with Wolves

The Milwaukee Bucks are trading Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis to the Miami Heat in a blockbuster deal that will reshape both franchises, per Shams Charania. Miami is sending Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis, three first-round picks including No. 13, a 2030 pick swap and a 2033 second-rounder to Milwaukee. The trade is structured as a one-to-one deal with no additional teams involved, though both sides have until July 6 to execute it, leaving the door open to expand the framework. Antetokounmpo and his agent, Alex Saratsis, informed the Bucks repeatedly over the past 13 months — before, during and after the season — that he would not sign another long-term extension with the organization. Giannis becomes the first multi-time MVP traded away since Moses Malone.

The Boston Celtics were the other finalist, offering Milwaukee a package featuring Finals MVP Jaylen Brown and two first-round picks, but the Bucks wanted more young players and assets than Boston was willing to part with. Sources told Kevin O'Connor that Bucks owner Jimmy Haslem was the driving force behind choosing Miami's offer, wary of acquiring Brown only to face another trade demand down the road after dealing with Giannis and Myles Garrett's exits. The three teams Giannis was open to signing an extension with were Miami, Boston and Minnesota, per Chris Haynes. Celtics president Brad Stevens has not actively shopped Brown, but Boston has now offered him twice for future Hall of Famers in the past four offseasons, the first time being the 2022 Kevin Durant pursuit in Brooklyn.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Herro, a Milwaukee native, is thrilled about a fresh start with his hometown Bucks and always envisioned returning home at some point in his career. On a related note, Herro carries the highest scoring average of any Wisconsin-born player in NBA history, per HoopsHype.
  • Brian Windhorst said on First Take that Brown is accepting the situation and sees it as a chance to lead his own team, something he has thought about for some time. Brown had a second-team All-NBA season while Jayson Tatum rehabbed his torn Achilles through March.
  • The Heat will absorb Bobby Portis via the Duncan Robinson trade exception and will be hard-capped at the first apron once the deal is completed in July. People in Miami are excited about how Portis fits into the lineup, sources told Ramona Shelburne.
  • Milwaukee clears $20 million in the current framework and creates a $25.5 million trade exception that could grow if the deal expands. The Bucks also go from one of the worst draft-pick situations in the league to a slight surplus of first-rounders, mostly in the 2030s.
  • Miami is effectively out of first-round picks they could trade going forward. The Heat can still re-sign Norman Powell using his Bird rights, but will need to shed payroll to create room for the full $15 million mid-level exception.
  • If Powell re-signs and Andrew Wiggins opts in or re-signs on a new deal, Miami's projected rotation would be Giannis, Bam, Mitchell, Powell, Wiggins, Larsson and Portis plus players added through exceptions. That's before the draft.
  • Giannis can sign an extension starting January 6: four years at $275 million if he opts out of his current deal, or three years at $214 million if he opts in. The opt-in path is slightly more lucrative.
  • Myles Turner figures to be a natural trade candidate as the Bucks reshape their roster, though his $21.6 million trade bonus complicates deals. The Toronto Raptors are among the teams that could have interest if Milwaukee moves into fire-sale mode.
  • Ayo Dosunmu intends to sign a five-year, $112 million deal with a player option in year five to return to the Minnesota Timberwolves. Minnesota had cleared salary by shipping Julius Randle and the No. 28 pick to the Brooklyn Nets in a three-team deal that routes Nic Claxton to the Chicago Bulls.
  • Dosunmu's deal comes in around $22 million per year, higher than most expected. Chicago gets a 27-year-old Claxton who has averaged 69 starts over the past four seasons and shoots 62.2 percent from the field.
  • Dallas Mavericks owner Patrick Dumont hired Masai Ujiri to run the organization, then fired coach Jason Kidd within weeks, and has now landed Dusty May as head coach. John Scheyer and May were the top two targets throughout the process, per Shams Charania, with executives around the league believing May was ready for the jump. The Mavericks hold picks No. 9, 30 and 48 in this week's draft and have discussed trade-back scenarios including one that would net them the No. 16 pick from Memphis.
  • Michigan forward Yaxel Lendeborg, who played under May in college, had a message for his former coach after the hiring: "I'm going to tell him he better pick me up. If he doesn't, I'm going to be mad, I might block him."
  • Restricted free agent Mohamed Diawara intends to sign a multiyear, $10-plus million deal to return to the New York Knicks. Diawara felt welcomed in New York from his first day and was comfortable with his teammates, which gave the Knicks an edge even if a larger offer came in from elsewhere.
  • The New Orleans Pelicans are actively trying to trade Trey Murphy, though Murphy himself was laughing about the rumors from France when Marc Spears texted him. Whether a deal happens may come down to who is available at No. 11 on draft night.
  • The Raptors are expected to announce a multi-year extension for head coach Darko Rajakovic early this week. The Atlanta Hawks are the most active team in pre-draft trade discussions, exploring both trade-up and trade-down scenarios.
  • Anthony Davis and his camp have been seeking a max extension from the Washington Wizards, and is considered a plausible trade candidate if Washington does not satisfy those demands. The Portland Trail Blazers have been monitoring Davis as a potential pivot target after being involved in Giannis conversations.
