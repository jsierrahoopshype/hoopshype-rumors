NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers face fire-sale talk, Ben Simmons lands with Kings, Barkley's Inside the NBA future

The Los Angeles Clippers are staring down a potential roster teardown after the NBA handed down the largest punishment in league history over the Kawhi Leonard cap circumvention scandal. Some team sources are now wondering whether the Clippers might begin a fire sale, trading away veterans to replenish future draft assets after losing five successive first-round picks between 2029 and 2033. "They would pay $30 million to have their five draft picks back," one former Clippers employee said. "You're never going to be young enough." The penalties also raise an open question about the two first-round picks the Clippers are reportedly set to receive from the Toronto Raptors in exchange for Leonard, with some wondering whether the league might try to seize those assets as well. The punishments follow an investigation whose roots trace directly to independent reporting by journalist Pablo Torre, who spent more than a year documenting the Clippers' alleged scheme, and who has been vocal about what he calls ESPN's willingness to carry water for Steve Ballmer's crisis PR operation.

The media fallout from the Clippers investigation has been as dramatic as the NBA's ruling itself. Torre says Ballmer and the Clippers fed ESPN a version of events they may have known was false, an allegation ESPN has not publicly addressed. Sports Illustrated's Chris Mannix was blunter: "I like our colleagues over at ESPN, but they just swallowed a big bunch of bulls—t that clearly came from the Clippers." Torre, asked by Dan Patrick whether he expected an apology from his former employer, said: "I don't expect an apology. I'm a big boy. They got fed a story by the people who are lying this whole time, and I got implicated as a result of it."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Leonard's standing in all of this is more complicated than his $700,000 fine suggests. His decision to part ways with his uncle and former adviser Dennis Robertson, after reportedly being shown early findings from the investigation, appears to have helped reduce his culpability in the league's eyes. Leonard is now expected to reunite with the Raptors, who paused the trade with the Clippers after fearing his contract might be voided. Draymond Green, for his part, said he's taking Leonard at his word: "If you got a deal coming in from Daktronics, you're not running to your representation like, 'Yo, how I get this?'"
  • Torre also raised the prospect of Ballmer suing the NBA to contest the punishment, noting that arbitration is off the table because it can only be triggered by the players union. "What can Steve Ballmer do? He can literally sue the NBA. He can sue Adam Silver. And that opens the door to more discovery of documents." The Clippers have not said publicly whether they intend to challenge the ruling.
  • Chris Paul called the Clippers situation "probably one of the most disrespectful things that I've endured" during an appearance on the "Build or Break" podcast. Paul did not elaborate on specifics, but said his family, who are closely attuned to that organization, shared his feelings.
  • per HoopsHype, a survey of 112 active or former players found LeBron James leading the GOAT debate among active players, 34-17 over Michael Jordan, while retired players sided with Jordan 29-15. Among former teammates, eight ex-teammates of James still picked Jordan, while 11 chose James. Four of Jordan's five former teammates picked him.
  • Ben Simmons is back in the NBA. He agreed to a one-year, $3.5 million deal with the Sacramento Kings, per Shams Charania, returning for a 10th season after sitting out all of 2025-26. Simmons worked out for general manager Scott Perry, underwent medical exams and met with coach Doug Christie in recent weeks, and is expected to provide veteran depth at point guard behind rookie Darius Acuff Jr.
  • The Kings were not Simmons' only suitor. The Knicks and Warriors were also in the mix, according to league sources. Sacramento had previously explored bringing in veterans like Russell Westbrook, who opted to retire, and Victor Oladipo before settling on Simmons.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves officially signed Jonathan Kuminga, who will wear No. 24. Meanwhile, with Mike Conley, Kyle Anderson and Joe Ingles all gone, Minnesota could have interest in signing veteran Kevin Love, who could fill a locker-room leadership role in a long season.
  • The Lakers' offseason team-building in Slovenia is drawing strong reviews internally. Kevon Looney said the trip to Luka Doncic's home country was unlike anything he'd experienced in his career: "I've been in the league for a while and that was my first time doing something like that, to go into somebody's country and bring us all out there." Collin Sexton added that Doncic's decision to organize the trip showed the kind of leader he wants to be this season. Bronny James, asked about his individual goals, said he is focused on being ready off the ball alongside Doncic and Austin Reaves: "I need to be able to knock down shots and create for others."
  • The hearing in Jeanie Buss's lawsuit to prevent her siblings from selling their ownership shares of the Lakers has been postponed from November 5 to December 8 in Los Angeles Superior Court, after Jeanie's attorney filed a continuance request that was granted.
  • The NBA is closing in on prediction-market deals ahead of the new season, with a league proposal already circulated to multiple platforms. "I would be shocked if they don't try to announce something before the season," one industry executive told Front Office Sports. The league came close to announcing during the playoffs but ultimately held off.
  • A second group has entered the running for a Seattle NBA expansion franchise. BlackSun Private Equity is working with the Tulalip Tribes to build a new arena on tribal land near Marysville, Washington, about 35 miles north of Seattle. "We are coming in with the Tribes, who were the original inhabitants of that land," said Nick Rathod, the group's chief global strategy officer.
  • Charles Barkley told Bill Simmons on a podcast visit that he and Ernie Johnson had planned to do Inside the NBA for only two more seasons after moving to ESPN, meaning 2026-27 would be their last year together "unless Ernie changes his mind." Barkley also pushed back on the LeBron Father Time narrative, pointing to the Lakers' back-to-back playoff sweeps as context: "Just because you're putting up good numbers don't mean you're being effective."
  • The Brooklyn Nets and Grant Nelson have agreed to a two-way contract, according to his agent. The 24-year-old, 7-footer had impressed on a 10-day deal late last season.
  • Dirk Nowitzki says he and new Mavericks president Masai Ujiri have already had discussions about a potential future role with the franchise. "He obviously wants me to be a part of the Mavericks," Nowitzki said, adding that he had stepped back from the organization previously over disagreements with how things were being run.
  • Kevin Durant's trainer Dash Lovell defended Durant after the Rockets' first-round exit, saying this offseason has focused on making KD a better passer out of double teams. Durant is also tracking toward passing Kobe Bryant for fourth on the all-time scoring list, sitting at 32,597 career points with Bryant's mark of 33,643 in range.
