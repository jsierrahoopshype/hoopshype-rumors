NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's decision imminent, Bam-Herro fallout settles, Heat eye Klay Thompson

LeBron James is closing in on a decision about his next team, with his agent Rich Paul telling NBA on Prime's Chris Haynes that LeBron "has all the information needed" and that the choice is now entirely his. Per Shams Charania, the leading suitors are the Cavaliers, Heat, Warriors, 76ers and Timberwolves, with LeBron privately expressing that he wants to go somewhere he can compete for a championship and find the right team culture. The Warriors connection carries real weight: Draymond Green is reportedly doing everything he can to recruit LeBron to Golden State, though Shams noted that Green himself is expected to return and has said publicly he wants to retire a Warrior. LeBron will appear at Fanatics Fest in New York this week, including a live recording of his "Mind the Game" podcast Thursday with Pacers star Tyrese Haliburton as guest co-host, giving the 41-year-old a large public stage that could be used for an announcement.

The 76ers picture is complicated. Sources close to the situation told ScoopB that while LeBron has seriously considered Philadelphia, a pairing with Joel Embiid is viewed as a non-starter internally, with concerns about Embiid's long-term health and mobility weighing on LeBron's side of the equation.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Heat's reported interest in Klay Thompson exists independent of where LeBron lands. Multiple sources told the Miami Herald that the five-time All-Star and fourth-most prolific three-point shooter in NBA history has emerged as a strong target for Miami regardless of what happens in free agency.
  • The Bam Adebayo-Tyler Herro situation in Miami appears to be cooling. Shams Charania reported there is surveillance footage to be reviewed but no NBA investigation underway, and that both players want to move on. Heat assistant coach Wayne Ellington added more context, saying he spoke with Bam directly and that the incident was "hyped up more to what it was. Sometimes you got to check your little brother, that's it." Herro is not pursuing legal action as of now.
  • The Lakers are still pursuing Jonathan Kuminga, but there is a real obstacle: Marc Stein reported that any deal would require Jarred Vanderbilt, and the Hawks have shown resistance to including him. Jake Fischer said he does not get the sense the Lakers are actively shopping Vanderbilt around, and that if a trade does not materialize, Kuminga has other options including returning to Atlanta.
  • The Lakers officially signed Kevon Looney, who will wear No. 55. Looney said playing alongside elite passers like Stephen Curry prepared him well for what is coming with Luka Doncic: "Luka is one of the best passers in the league. He can create so many opportunities for me as a pick-and-roll player."
  • The Lakers also locked in Ziaire Williams on a one-year, $3 million deal. New Orleans made a late push to sign the 25-year-old, but the chance to play for his hometown team in a winning situation brought him to LA.
  • Walker Kessler said he is "100% cleared" after left shoulder surgery that limited him to five games last season. On expanding his three-point game, Kessler said JJ Redick has been encouraging the shot, explaining: "For a big to be able to stretch the floor like that or even have the threat of it, I think it makes other teams scout really difficult." Austin Reaves, who was teammates with Kessler during the 2023 World Cup, gave a quick endorsement: "He's a big, goofy dude that just enjoys life and has fun."
  • Jaime Jaquez Jr. learned he was being traded to the Bucks while playing pickup ball at an LA Fitness with friends. A bystander recorded the moment and it went viral. "I knew that it was definitely a possibility," Jaquez told the Miami Herald, "but you're never truly prepared for what it does." He called the news "devastating for sure" and said Miami felt like home. On a contract extension with Milwaukee, he was measured: "We'll see what happens when it comes."
  • The Golden State Warriors signed Charles Bassey to a one-year, $2.85 million deal partially guaranteed for $1.4 million, per HoopsHype. Bassey helps fill the void left by Quinten Post, who recently signed with the Grizzlies.
  • The Grizzlies officially signed Cameron Boozer to a multi-year contract after selecting him in the 2026 draft.
  • Denver signed second-round pick Trevon Brazile to a four-year, $9.3 million deal with the first two seasons fully guaranteed, per HoopsHype's Michael Scotto. Brazile was the 35th pick out of Arkansas and was acquired via trade with the Spurs.
  • Christian Braun on his mindset after a difficult end to the season: "Sometimes it's good to get punched in the mouth a little bit." He said he is focused on getting his ankle right and remains confident: "I know who I am as a player."
  • AJ Dybantsa scored 23 points and grabbed seven rebounds as the Wizards improved to 2-0 in summer league with a 104-85 win over the Kings. It may have been Dybantsa's last summer league game, as teams typically shut down top lottery picks after two games for precautionary reasons.
  • The NBA hired former Bulls executive Arturas Karnisovas as a consultant for NBA Europe, where he will advise commissioner Adam Silver on governance, game rules and talent development. The NBA and FIBA are said to be in the final stages ahead of a planned October 2027 launch, with the Board of Governors set to review a second round of bids from potential owners this week.
  • The Trail Blazers' Moda Center renovation proposal is drawing scrutiny from a new state-commissioned report, which found that most comparable NBA arena deals included significant private investment or long-term revenue sharing that Portland's plan currently lacks. At least one city councilor called private investment her "number one issue" and said she cannot support giving public money without it.
  • John Wall said his role as Howard University's President of Basketball Operations is a deliberate step toward his long-term goal: "One day I want to be a GM." Unlike most ambassador-type arrangements athletes take with college programs, Wall said he is involved in the day-to-day operations and constantly in contact with the coaching staff.
