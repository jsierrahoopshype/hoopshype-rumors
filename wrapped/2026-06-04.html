NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks take Game 1, Giannis trade buzz heats up, Curry lands $400M Li-Ning deal

The Milwaukee Bucks appear content to let the market fully develop before moving Giannis Antetokounmpo, and the Miami Heat remain the most aggressive suitor. A Heat package built around Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, additional players and multiple first-round picks — first offered in early February — is still on the table, though it wasn't enough to get Milwaukee to pull the trigger before the trade deadline. The Athletic reported that "the noise surrounding the Miami Heat and Antetokounmpo remains louder than all the rest," and, aside from Bam Adebayo, no player has been deemed off limits in Miami's pursuit. The Bucks are expected to wait until the playoffs conclude before seriously engaging suitors, with a trade not anticipated until closer to the draft. Most around the league believe Giannis prefers to stay in the Eastern Conference, given the dominance of the San Antonio Spurs and Oklahoma City Thunder in the West.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Knicks took Game 1 of the NBA Finals over the Spurs, extending their winning streak to 12 straight playoff wins. Jalen Brunson briefly left in the second half with a right knee issue but returned, and New York has not lost since April 23. The Knicks are 8-8 when trailing by double digits over the last two postseasons, the most double-digit comeback wins in the playoffs in that span.
  • Mitchell Robinson played in Game 1 despite a broken right metacarpal. When asked how he got hurt, Robinson declined to elaborate. "I'm here to talk about basketball," he said. Robinson had told reporters he'd been with the organization for eight years and there was no way he was missing this one.
  • Victor Wembanyama wasn't dwelling on falling behind in the series. "I'm not worried the slightest," Wemby said. The Spurs went 2-5 in clutch situations this postseason but are 10-2 in non-clutch games, which explains how they got here.
  • Dylan Harper had a record-setting first quarter, scoring 10 points in the first frame of Game 1 — the most by a rookie in the first quarter of a Finals game in the play-by-play era. He also surpassed David Robinson's record for most points by a Spurs rookie in a single postseason. The Spurs missed 32 three-pointers in Game 1 alone — for context, they missed 41 threes total in their entire five-game Finals run in 1999.
  • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson got high marks from Knicks coach Mike Brown before the series. "He's lightyears in front of me," Brown said. "He's a way better coach than I was when I was a young guy." Brown also credited assistant Rick Brunson for keeping the bench composed during a tough stretch in Game 1 when the staff was getting too wound up at the officials. "He told me to shut the hell up," Brown said.
  • Stephon Castle said guarding Brunson will be a different challenge than defending Shai Gilgeous-Alexander in the previous round. "Brunson being a smaller guard is probably gonna be amplified a lot more," Castle said, adding that Shai's pump fakes and sweep-throughs made keeping him off the line difficult but Brunson presents a different set of problems.
  • President Donald Trump plans to attend Game 3 of the NBA Finals at Madison Square Garden, per four league sources briefed on his likely attendance.
  • Stephen Curry has reportedly signed a 10-year, $400 million-plus deal with Li-Ning after parting ways with Under Armour last November. Draymond Green called it "unheard of" for a 38-year-old, adding that Curry's willingness to test competitors' products before signing — as he did when he evaluated Under Armour by watching how they treated then-teammate Kent Bazemore — played a role in the Li-Ning decision, with Jimmy Butler and Dwyane Wade's comfort in the brand reportedly a factor.
  • Commissioner Adam Silver said the investigation into the Los Angeles Clippers and their dealings with Aspiration needs to wrap up soon. "We're close to the point where we need to wrap this up," Silver said, though he added he wouldn't issue a decision based on perception and needed to wait for the facts. There is no set timeline for the report's conclusion.
  • Silver also addressed tanking, the 65-game rule and the league's international ambitions at his pre-Finals press conference. On tanking, Silver said what surprised him was "how quickly it became acceptable behavior in this league." On the 65-game rule, he said he's not ready to support changes so soon after implementation, calling it working as intended. On the NBA's European league, Silver said final franchise bids are due by end of June, with a target launch of the 2027-28 season.
  • Mark Cuban opened up about his fractured relationship with former Mavericks GM Nico Harrison, saying Harrison was "talking trash behind the scenes" and taking credit for moves Cuban said he made himself. "I'm the one that made the decision," Cuban said of the Kyrie Irving trade. Cuban added that he tolerated the tension because the team was winning, and compared Harrison unfavorably to Masai Ujiri: "Nico didn't really know what he was doing."
  • There is no discussion between the Celtics and Pelicans on Jaylen Brown at this moment, though New Orleans is expected to be aggressive in reshaping their roster this summer. Derrick White spoke glowingly about Brown's work ethic, saying "I don't think people understand how hard he works."
  • The Washington Wizards, holding the No. 1 pick, are looking at AJ Dybantsa and Darryn Peterson as the top two options, with Duke's Cameron Boozer still on the radar. President Michael Winger has said publicly the team will consider trading down to accumulate assets.
  • South Korean guard Lee Hyun-jung will join the Spurs for the 2026 NBA Summer League, his agency announced Thursday.
