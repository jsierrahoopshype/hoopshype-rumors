NBA Rumors Wrap: Nuggets-Watson fallout detailed, Jalen Duren extension gap narrows, Lakers roster takes shape

The messy split between the Denver Nuggets and Peyton Watson is coming into clearer focus. The Nuggets made only one contract offer — $18 million AAV over four years with escalating salary and a team option — and never countered after Watson's camp declined it. Behind that standoff was serious organizational frustration: five league sources told The Denver Post that the Nuggets believed Watson could have returned before the end of the playoff series but was slow-playing his injury to protect his contract value. A source close to Watson disputed that characterization. People at every level of the organization were left frustrated by how the situation played out, per the Post, with the contrast drawn to Christian Braun, Michael Porter Jr. and Aaron Gordon all pushing through injuries in recent postseasons.

With no player coming back in the deal, the Nuggets ended up taking Cleveland's first-round pick as the centerpiece of the Watson trade, viewing it as more valuable than any player available to them. The pick is intended as a trade asset to be moved well before 2031. Denver also had preliminary conversations about Jaime Jaquez with Milwaukee, but those talks never progressed to an actual sign-and-trade offer from the Bucks. Separately, Kris Dunn came up in discussions with the Clippers, but Denver wouldn't have moved him without draft capital also heading their way.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The gap between Jalen Duren and the Pistons on a contract extension is real but smaller than expected. "It seems like there's a window here to get something done and put him back in a Pistons jersey," one reporter said after recent conversations. The qualifying offer looming over both sides makes it a high-stakes negotiation for everyone involved.
  • Amen Thompson's rookie-scale extension with the Rockets makes him the second-latest second-year extension in any draft class since 2018, a sign of a broader trend. Teams are negotiating harder and handing out fewer maximum extensions as the salary cap rises, raising the bar for who qualifies for a max deal. It's possible Thompson would have been an automatic max in an earlier era.
  • Charles Barkley says Stephon Castle and Dylan Harper are going to be the next Steph Curry and Klay Thompson. "If I'm a guard in the NBA, I do not want to see them boys the next 10 years," Barkley said.
  • South Korea's Lee Hyun-jung has signed an Exhibit 10 contract with the Portland Trail Blazers ahead of the 2026-27 season. It's a non-guaranteed, minimum-salary deal that doesn't secure a roster spot but keeps him in camp competition.
  • Anthony Edwards sat down with Formula 1 driver Kimi Antonelli and didn't hold back on his approach to trash talk. "A lot of players don't talk trash, but I do," Edwards said. "If I'm scoring a lot of points, I'm talking crazy." He extended that to his teammates, too: "If my teammate is having a good game, I'll talk trash for him. I'll be like, 'Don't say nothing, I got you!'" Edwards also named Madison Square Garden and TD Garden as the toughest road environments in the league.
  • Lakers coach JJ Redick reflected on his journey in a recent video. "This journey has been beyond my wildest dreams," Redick said. "I am pinching myself every day that I get to coach the Lakers."
  • Former Lakers champion Danny Green likes what he sees from Los Angeles this offseason but has questions about their ceiling. "I think they're going to be a pretty good regular season team. Playoffs, I don't know because of the youth and experience," Green said. He singled out Cameron Carr, Ziaire Williams, Sandro Mamukelashvili and Collin Sexton as potential X-factors off the bench.
  • Robert Horry believes Walker Kessler could be the difference-maker for the Lakers this season, though he has one concern. "We haven't had a big since AD that can block shots like that, pop out for threes," Horry said. "I just hope he stays healthy. Him missing damn near all season, how much rust has he developed?"
  • Delonte West recalled LeBron James recruiting him to the Cavaliers mid-game at the foul line. "I thought he was just doing a veteran trick," West said. "But he was serious. He asked me to come play with Cleveland."
  • Julius "Dr. J" Erving weighed in on LeBron's move to Philadelphia, stopping short of endorsing his all-time legacy. "That particular team can set some records and win some championships," Erving said, "but they have to do it on the court, not with lip service."
  • Olden Polynice fired back at Tracy McGrady's rings-versus-MVP argument in blunt terms. "T-Mac, I've never read your book, Things Losers Say," Polynice said. "Jack Haley had three rings and he can carry that ring anywhere and show it. Nobody gives a damn about no damn MVPs."
  • Amari Bailey has joined Hapoel Jerusalem of Israel after his time with the Hornets. Former Trail Blazers guard Elijah Hughes is also heading overseas, signing with Riesen Ludwigsburg in Germany. On the domestic front, former NBA forward Reggie Perry has signed with EuroCup's KK Bosna in Sarajevo.
  • Victor Wembanyama, Caitlin Clark and Derrick Rose are the cover athletes for NBA 2K27. "Three number one draft picks. Three players that speak loud on the court," Ronnie 2K said of the trio.
