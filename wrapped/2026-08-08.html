NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard faces second cap circumvention allegation, Kuminga-Lakers talks stalled, Blazers arena funding moves forward

A new report has added a second layer to the NBA's investigation into Kawhi Leonard. Former ESPN contributor Pablo Torre, on his podcast "Pablo Torre Finds Out," reported that Leonard had an undisclosed sponsorship agreement allegedly worth millions of dollars with Daktronics, the company that designed the videoboard inside the Clippers' Intuit Dome. An anonymous source identified as a high-level Intuit Dome contractor alleged the deal "was 1,000% a way to circumvent the salary cap. It was funneling money from the Clippers through Daktronics back to Kawhi." A Daktronics spokesperson told Hunterbrook Media, which collaborated on the report, that Leonard does not have an active contract with the company. The alleged arrangement is separate from the earlier investigation into Leonard's deal with a now-bankrupt green banking company, which remains ongoing.

On the broader investigation, Sportsnet's analysis suggests a Leonard suspension is unlikely regardless of how the probe resolves. The CBA's cap circumvention penalties can include team fines, draft pick forfeitures and the voiding of player contracts, but the language does not allow for a player suspension, only the requirement to "disgorge" any benefits gained. Per league sources cited by Sportsnet, the investigation has moved past its fact-finding phase, with the Clippers and NBPA working quietly toward a negotiated settlement. The Raptors trade is still expected to go through, with a collective will from all parties, including the league itself, to resolve everything before training camps open September 28.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Jonathan Kuminga free agency saga is now in its second month. The Lakers met with Kuminga early in free agency and pitched him on a high-minutes role, and Kuminga was drawn to the opportunity, but the sign-and-trade offer did not satisfy all sides. "The Lakers remain interested in him, league sources said, but movement toward a deal has remained stalled for weeks."
  • Kevin Love's agent Jeff Schwartz has been in contact with the 76ers since LeBron James chose Philadelphia, per ESPN. The Sixers are already at roster capacity after signing Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, and carry two backup centers behind Joel Embiid in Adem Bona and Ariel Hukporti. Love, who averaged 6.7 points and 5.8 rebounds for Utah last season, has interest in chasing a championship alongside James, but Philadelphia would need to clear a roster spot to make it work.
  • VJ Edgecombe was not exactly calm when LeBron called after signing in Philadelphia. "I FaceTimed Bron. I was in disbelief, but I had to act cool," Edgecombe told reporters at the ABCD Basketball Camp. "My headphones on, I'm playing the game. Had to act all cool, but it's really Bron. I was really geekin' I ain't gone lie."
  • The cause of death for former NBA player Brandon Clarke has been confirmed. The LA County Department of Medical Examiner ruled that Clarke, 29, died from the effects of heroin and cocaine, with the manner of death classified as accidental. Clarke was found unresponsive in a San Fernando Valley home on May 11.
  • The Dallas Mavericks have explored trading Klay Thompson, and Thompson would welcome a return to Los Angeles, where he spent part of his childhood and still owns a home. Klay's father Mychal Thompson weighed in on social media, suggesting his son could team up with Luka Doncic as he originally wanted when he went to Dallas.
  • Multnomah County voted 4-1 to approve a term sheet offering up to $101.6 million toward upgrades to the Trail Blazers' Moda Center, an increase from the $88 million originally proposed. The vote is not a final funding package but a framework to begin formal negotiations with the Blazers and the city of Portland. The lone dissenting commissioner raised a pointed question: "How real is this commitment for money, if we can't agree on a way to fund it?"
  • Isaiah Stewart opened up about being traded to the Grizzlies during June's draft, saying he and his wife prayed for a move to a team that valued him after feeling unappreciated in Detroit. "I wasn't mad about it. I was happy that I was moving on to a new chapter," Stewart told Andscape. "I'd rather be where I'm wanted than headed back into a situation where it's not the best."
  • Per HoopsHype, the Spurs and second-year guard Taelon Peter have agreed to a training camp deal. Peter, the 54th pick of the 2025 draft, averaged 4.5 points in 38 games for Indiana last season on a two-way contract.
  • Free agent guard Alondes Williams is returning to the Wizards after reaching an agreement on a new deal, per Chris Haynes. Williams scored a career-high 25 points for Washington last season.
  • The Magic are hiring Josh Broghamer away from Dallas to join Sean Sweeney's first coaching staff in Orlando, per Marc Stein. Broghamer coached Cooper Flagg during summer league in July 2025 and ran Flagg's pregame workouts last season under Jason Kidd.
  • Taylor Jenkins has finalized the Bucks' coaching staff for 2026-27. Darvin Ham returns as an assistant, joined by T.J. Ford, Brad Jones, Pat St. Andrews, Vince Legarza, Johnny Carpenter and Schuyler Rimmer.
  • Lithuania's preliminary FIBA World Cup qualifying roster is missing several notable names. Domantas Sabonis, Matas Buzelis, Kasparas Jakucionis and Jonas Valanciunas are all absent from the 14-man group for the August window.
  • German prospect Jack Kayil, who had drawn attention from the Knicks, has committed to a multi-year contract with ALBA Berlin. The club officially confirmed a five-year deal. "Last season was incredibly successful, and I had a blast. That's why I'm even more excited to be staying in Berlin," Kayil said.
  • Veteran guard Garrison Mathews is heading to Armani Olimpia Milan for the 2026-27 EuroLeague season, concluding a seven-year NBA career. The 29-year-old sharpshooter signed with the Italian club.
  • Former NBA guard Monta Ellis has been named General Manager of the Alcorn State men's basketball program. The Jackson, Mississippi native spent 12 seasons in the NBA with Golden State, Milwaukee, Dallas and Indiana.
  • Rajon Rondo has been selected for the Kentucky Sports Hall of Fame's Class of 2026. The Louisville native and two-time NBA champion headlines a class that also includes sprinter Tyson Gay and former Cy Young winner Brandon Webb.
