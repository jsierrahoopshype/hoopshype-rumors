NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade imminent, Jalen Duren weighs qualifying offer, Suns lose Mark Williams to shoulder surgery

The long-delayed Kawhi Leonard trade between the Los Angeles Clippers and the Toronto Raptors appears close to finally crossing the finish line. Per Marc Stein, sources tell him the Clippers and Raptors are free at any time to co-submit the agreed-upon deal for NBA approval and that there is no holdup at the league level. The trade, which would send Leonard to Toronto along with two first-round picks in exchange for Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick, was first agreed to on June 30 but has sat in limbo ever since. The main procedural sticking point has been the Clippers still needing to appoint a new team governor and top basketball executive to formally sign off, according to Sportsnet. Training camp is roughly two weeks away, and those close to the situation remain confident the deal will go through sooner rather than later.

A separate layer of concern had emerged around the Justice Department's criminal investigation into the Clippers' salary cap violations, but league sources told the Toronto Star that the probe is expected to focus on the financial and corporate side of the Clippers' business, not the basketball transaction. Leonard himself will not be suspended and his contract and Bird rights remain intact, per ESPN, which also reported the Clippers and Raptors will be allowed to move forward with the trade process.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Jalen Duren contract standoff with the Detroit Pistons is approaching a decision point. The 22-year-old All-NBA center has until Oct. 1 to decide whether to accept Detroit's qualifying offer of $9.6 million and become an unrestricted free agent next summer, and league sources say he is very seriously considering that route. Detroit's last offer was a five-year deal in the $190 million range; Duren, who is eligible for up to $287 million, is seeking at least $200 million.
  • If Duren does hit the open market, the Sacramento Kings appear to be at the front of the line. The Kings were identified as his top suitor as early as July, and sources say Duren has three serious suitors in all. The financial gap between staying in Detroit and signing elsewhere next summer works out to roughly $6 million over five years, which may not be enough to keep him.
  • Mark Williams underwent surgery to repair a torn labrum in his left shoulder and will miss an extended period, per Shams Charania. The Phoenix Suns center suffered the injury in an offseason workout, a significant blow heading into training camp.
  • The Atlanta Hawks have waived Devin Carter, per HoopsHype. The 24-year-old guard averaged 8.9 points in 38 games for the Sacramento Kings last season. The Boston Celtics have shown interest in signing Carter once he clears waivers.
  • Jonathan Kuminga is expected to sign with the Minnesota Timberwolves, and he's explaining why Minnesota felt right. "Them wanting me to be here, it felt real," Kuminga said. "Me and [Anthony Edwards] sat on the phone and spoke about certain things, and that just made me feel very comfortable." The Chicago Bulls also expressed interest, though the front office made clear it could not promise him a starting role given the team's existing young forwards.
  • Stephen Curry is re-signing with the Golden State Warriors, full stop. "Steph is re-signing. Steph isn't going anywhere. He never was," Warriors beat writer Marcus Thompson said flatly. Thompson added that Curry values being included in the organization's decision-making process and at 38 has no realistic championship alternative that would serve his legacy better than staying put.
  • Draymond Green opened up about the negotiations that nearly sent him to the Memphis Grizzlies. "I called the Warriors and said 'Yo I'm gonna go to the Grizzlies, can y'all do all y'all can do to work that sign-and-trade,'" Green said on his podcast. He said the Warriors then engaged Memphis in negotiations and moved the goalposts enough that it became clear they genuinely wanted him back.
  • Kevin Durant wants to suit up for Team USA at the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics. The Rockets forward told The Athletic "it's just fun, man" and has been encouraging other players to chase the Olympic experience when they can, including telling Bol Bol to represent South Sudan. Steve Kerr said Durant's FIBA passion is genuine: "He loves eliminating the NBA bulls— that you have to go through for eight months."
  • The Clippers also face a five-year compliance and monitoring program as part of their league punishment, a penalty rival executives are watching closely. "Are they going to be reading all their emails, reading their texts, listening to their phone calls?" one general manager told ESPN. The terms of the monitoring program were described as nebulous, with the details still to be worked out.
  • On NBA expansion: Las Vegas and Seattle are not expected to move in lockstep or land at the same price. Preliminary Vegas bidders who came in around $8 billion quickly learned the number would be higher, while a Seattle franchise could sell for considerably less, per ownership sources.
  • The NBA's European league is moving forward, with the league ready to launch a 16-team competition starting in 2027. Bidding groups are in place for 12 of the new teams, and franchises in London and Paris are expected to fetch over $1 billion each. A potential conflict with the Euroleague is set to be a major topic at the upcoming owners meetings.
  • Rockets GM Rafael Stone spent two weeks this offseason in Turkey visiting Alperen Sengun. Stone said the trip was partly sparked by Sengun pushing him to communicate more consistently. "Part of it was Alpi putting pressure on me," Stone said, adding that Sengun asked to hear from him more often, "good, bad, or ugly."
  • Luka Doncic is expected to remain in Europe until around September 20 before heading to Los Angeles, according to a Slovenian report. His personal trainer has overseen a focused offseason recovery and conditioning program, with his weight reportedly in the desired range entering camp.
  • Derrick Jones Jr. has not requested a trade from the Clippers. Both sides have a good relationship and are open to extension talks to keep him in Los Angeles, per a source.
  • The civil sexual assault trial of former Laker Byron Scott is on hold after Scott filed for bankruptcy last week. The accuser's attorney told the Los Angeles Times she plans to file a motion seeking relief from the stay to return the case to state court.
  • The estate of the late Jason Collins, the first openly gay player in NBA history, has been made public. Collins signed a will in October 2025, months before announcing his cancer diagnosis. His estate, which includes over $1.8 million in personal property, was left to family members and two LGBTQ+ charities, Glisten and Point Foundation.
