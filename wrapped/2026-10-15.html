NBA Rumors Wrap: Brandon Miller seeks max deal from Hornets, Kyrie Irving expected to be trade target, Giannis settles into Miami

Brandon Miller is pushing for a max contract extension from the Charlotte Hornets, and it sounds like it will be difficult for the two sides to close the gap. Miller is seeking a deal close to the rookie max he's eligible for, which would put him near the five-year, $200 million threshold, per Jake Fischer on Bleacher Report. Fischer said on Bleacher Report he believes "it's gonna be very challenging for the Hornets to get anywhere below that" number. Charlotte has until October 19 to make decisions on several roster situations before the deadline, so the Miller negotiation is happening in an already active week for the organization.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Fischer also expects Kyrie Irving to be one of the NBA's most discussed trade names between now and the February 11 deadline. "He just does not fit the timeline," Fischer said, pointing to the age gap between Irving and Mavericks rookie Cooper Flagg, with Irving also heading toward the end of a lucrative contract.
  • The Clippers waived guard Cam Christie, and Fischer says that move shifts attention to Charlotte, Milwaukee, Memphis and the Lakers as the most-watched roster spots before October 19. Fischer reported that the Bucks, Lakers and Grizzlies are all exploring trades to fill out their rosters. The Hornets, meanwhile, have to weigh the locker room value of retaining veteran Pat Connaughton.
  • Julius Randle was "one of the culprits" behind a moody Timberwolves locker room last season, according to reporting from Jon Krawczynski. Randle reportedly did not react well when his name came up in Giannis Antetokounmpo trade talks, and other players including Naz Reid and Jaden McDaniels contributed to the tense atmosphere. The arrival of LaMelo Ball is said to have already shifted the vibe: "He's much more upbeat and energetic, so there's definitely been a vibe shift."
  • Rudy Gobert is working on his pick-and-roll chemistry with Ball, and his advice to his new teammate is simple: slow down. "You don't need to go fast, because I'm going to give you an advantage," Gobert said. He told Ball to watch film of his old sets with Joe Ingles to understand how to use him properly.
  • Giannis explained why he switched from No. 34 to No. 7 with the Heat: "Had to get closure. It was a huge chapter in my life. 34 will only be worn in Milwaukee." He added that he might change his mind next year. On adjusting to Miami life, Giannis said the city moves faster than Milwaukee, but he stays grounded: "I am a dad, I am a regular guy, I go to Costco and I try to get stuff."
  • De'Aaron Fox opened up about playing through a right high-ankle sprain he suffered in Game 4 of the Western Conference semifinals last May. "I played more games in the playoffs hurt than I did healthy," Fox said. He averaged just 11.2 points on 36.5% shooting in the conference finals while hampered, and said it took two months this summer to mentally move on from the experience.
  • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson said he believes Fox, Dylan Harper and Stephon Castle can coexist in the backcourt. "They are our best players; they know how to dribble, pass, shoot, defend, and make game-changing plays," Johnson said. His one note: with San Antonio playing at a very high pace, the trio will need to rebound and run.
  • Kristaps Porzingis posted an update on his health situation, saying he is "getting better and better" but needs to stay patient. "It has definitely been frustrating dealing with some health stuff that you can't exactly pinpoint, on top of injuries throughout my career, so it has added up for sure," Porzingis wrote. No timeline for his return with the Warriors has been given.
  • There is real concern in Denver about what happens after this season, when Nikola Jokic becomes an unrestricted free agent. Jokic has said he wants to re-sign with the Nuggets on a projected five-year, $360 million deal, but The Athletic noted that "29 other teams are scheming for how they might lure him away come July."
  • Tyrese Haliburton returned to Oklahoma City for a preseason game, his first time back since tearing his Achilles in Game 7 of the 2025 NBA Finals at that building. "I thought I would feel maybe a little more emotion coming in here," Haliburton said after a 24-point outing. "It wasn't nearly as bad as I thought it was gonna be."
  • Timberwolves president of basketball operations Tim Connelly does not have social media apps on his phone. The only NBA-related site he checks is HoopsHype, per The Athletic. On his willingness to make unpopular moves: "If it doesn't work and it's my job, I'll get fired. That's fine. But I don't ever want to be in a position where we're reluctant to take big swings."
  • VJ Edgecombe revealed at Sixers media day that the group chat among Philadelphia's projected starting five was his idea, though he had to go through Tyrese Maxey to get LeBron James added because he didn't have James' number. "It was my idea, so if any one of them tell you that it was their idea, they're lying," Edgecombe said.
  • Payton Pritchard agreed to a contract extension with the Celtics, and coach Joe Mazzulla praised him the day after: "When people think of a Celtic, he's one of the first people that comes to mind," Mazzulla said. Mazzulla added that Pritchard "doesn't have an ego," a quality the coach clearly values.
  • The Lakers added guard Jahmir Young on an Exhibit 10 contract ahead of their preseason finale Friday, waiving forward Cole Swider in a corresponding move. The Nuggets, Clippers and Cavaliers also made roster cuts this week as teams trim toward opening-night rosters, per HoopsHype.
  • The Trail Blazers have settled their television broadcast situation, at least for now. Travis Demers will handle play-by-play duties on a game-by-game, trial basis to open the 2026-27 season, according to The Oregonian.
