NBA Rumors Wrap: Pistons, Duren $5M apart on extension, Curry ready for Year 18, Warriors sued by ex-employees

The standoff between the Detroit Pistons and center Jalen Duren has a clear number attached to it: Detroit is offering around $35 million annually on a five-year deal, while Duren and his agent Chafie Fields are pushing for north of $40 million, per ESPN. The gap is real but not wide enough that a deal looks impossible. One Western Conference executive told ESPN you can see both sides, and analyst Keith Smith predicted the two will likely settle somewhere around $37-38 million, comparing it to last summer's Josh Giddey negotiation with the Bulls. Sacramento has been floated as potential leverage for Duren's camp, with the threat of a max offer sheet on a qualifying offer lingering. Tim MacMahon noted on ESPN's podcast that the Kings represent at least some bargaining power for Duren's reps, even if the details around that market remain unresolved.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • Stephen Curry made clear he wants to finish his career with the Warriors, telling NBC Sports Bay Area in an exclusive interview, "There's no question I want to be here the rest of my career, and I'm pretty sure that's going to happen." On the contract extension talks, Curry added: "Extension stuff, we'll figure that out at the right time. But I'm ready for Year 18." That comes alongside reporting from Anthony Slater that the Warriors are not actively negotiating with Curry right now, instead presenting him a menu of options, from a one-year max to a two-year max to waiting, and letting him choose.
  • There is lingering concern about Curry's knee following last season's injury. Monte Poole of NBC Sports Bay Area said the knee "can be managed, but it has to be managed, and that's a new thing for Steph", adding that Curry and his trainers have devised ways to maintain his conditioning around it.
  • Two former Warriors employees have sued the franchise in San Francisco County Superior Court, alleging gender and race-based discrimination, workplace harassment, and retaliation leading to their termination. One of the plaintiffs, former associate general counsel Lisa Shelley, claims the Warriors cost her a job with the Clippers this year by providing "false and damaging" statements when contacted as a reference, after she had already received a conditional offer from Los Angeles. In a statement to the San Francisco Chronicle, the Warriors called the allegations "false, misleading, and unsupported by credible evidence."
  • The Timberwolves are hiring Daniel Brady as an assistant coach, per HoopsHype. Brady spent this past season with the RGV Vipers after three seasons on Tom Thibodeau's staff with the Knicks, and has previously worked with the Thunder, Hawks, and Rockets.
  • Luguentz Dort on getting traded to the Hawks: "Nickeil Alexander-Walker is one of my closest friends. Played on the same team with Nickeil in high school and against him our whole life. When I got the call and I knew I was heading there, I hit him right away. I was really happy." Dort said Alexander-Walker has helped him throughout the transition and with Atlanta's style of play.
  • Anthony Davis and the Washington Wizards mutually decided to wait on extension talks until after the season starts. If Davis is healthy and returns to form, Washington could be a factor in the East, but if a deal doesn't materialize, a trade request could be on the table.
  • Kyrie Irving's trade market remains complicated by his $42 million player option in 2027-28. A Western Conference executive told Heavy.com that a deal likely depends on some handshake understanding around that option year, similar to how James Harden's situation played out in Cleveland: "If you have some sense that he opts out and we can do a two-year deal at a lesser number, then I think you can do something."
  • Trey Murphy's trade market is active but the Pelicans haven't gotten serious about moving him yet. New Orleans has asked for as many as four first-round picks, pointing to the Desmond Bane and Mikal Bridges deals as comps, though one executive said the market has shifted and "a good young rotation player and two first-rounders is probably where you land."
  • Michael Porter Jr. heads into the final year of his $40 million deal with the Nets in an uncertain spot. One Eastern Conference executive told Heavy.com "the problem you have is if you trade for him, are you going to extend him and what is the number? The Nuggets overpaid him when they gave him that extension, and if he wants to be paid based on that, that is not going to be an easy sell."
  • Peyton Watson opened up about his departure from Denver, saying the Nuggets were "between a rock and a hard place financially" and that when free agency opened, "the cap dried up super quick and a lot of teams were not willing to go through the war that restricted free agency is." Watson called the experience bittersweet.
  • Victor Oladipo published an open letter to NBA general managers in The Players' Tribune, making one last pitch for a roster spot at 34. "I believe my body is finally right. I believe I still have GREAT basketball in front of me," Oladipo wrote, adding: "Maybe a team out there will give me a chance. Maybe no team will. That's really up to you all."
  • Victor Wembanyama was asked last week about the 2027 French presidential election, with Marine Le Pen leading polls to succeed Emmanuel Macron. "As the years go by, I have fewer and fewer filters, and I'm less and less afraid to say what I think," Wembanyama said in French. "I'm not afraid of upsetting people."
  • Thanasis Antetokounmpo has signed with ARIS BC in Greece on a 2-plus-1-year deal. Former NBA guard Josh Primo, the 12th pick in the 2021 draft, has signed with BC Parma in Russia on a one-year deal with a second-year option.
  • The Cavaliers announced DAZN as their new broadcast partner on a multi-year deal, with 15 games this season available free over the air. On a related note, Kroenke Sports & Entertainment, which owns the Nuggets, is acquiring the Los Angeles Angels from the Moreno family, adding the MLB club to a portfolio that already includes the Rams, Avalanche, and Arsenal.
  • The Hornets have signed forward Trey Townsend and waived Josiah Allick. Charlotte also announced that Mike Pires and Zach Peterson have been named general manager and head coach, respectively, of the Greensboro Swarm.
  • Michigan's prized freshman guard Brandon McCoy Jr., a top-15 recruit in the class of 2026, has torn his ACL on the team's overseas trip and is out for the season. It is a significant blow for the Wolverines, who are also pursuing a fifth year of eligibility for Roddy Gayle through the courts.
  • The Massachusetts Gaming Commission is reviewing Bill Simmons's admission that he had his daughter's boyfriend place bets for him on his FanDuel account. FanDuel has already voided the bets and sanctioned the account.
