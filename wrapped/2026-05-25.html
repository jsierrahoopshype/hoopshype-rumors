NBA Rumors Wrap: Spurs tie West finals with blowout win, Giannis trade chatter heats up, Fox's future in San Antonio in question

Victor Wembanyama put together a dominant performance and San Antonio leveled the Western Conference finals at 2-2 with a 103-82 win over Oklahoma City, the Thunder's lowest-scoring game in any context since December 2021. Wembanyama finished with 33 points and 8 rebounds, while Stephon Castle added 13 points and 6 assists and De'Aaron Fox posted a 12-point, 10-rebound double-double. The series now shifts to a best-of-three, with the Spurs having answered back after falling behind 2-1. Wembanyama had reflected after Game 3 that he needed to be "more of a team player," and Game 4 looked like a response, per The Athletic.

The win comes with a subplot that won't go away. There is serious league-wide chatter, per The Athletic's Shams Charania, that Fox's time in San Antonio could be short, with rival executives and media both circulating the idea that rookie guard Dylan Harper is simply too good to stay on the bench long-term and will eventually need to start alongside Castle at the expense of Fox's role. Fox forced his way to San Antonio last season as part of his Sacramento Kings exit plan, and this is his first postseason with the Spurs, adding to the awkwardness of the speculation surfacing now.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Giannis Antetokounmpo trade market is getting more crowded with interested parties, and more complicated. Bill Simmons said he believes Giannis wants to go to Boston, but isn't sure the Celtics want him back. "I think that's the push and pull right now," Simmons said. Meanwhile, two league sources told The Athletic that Cleveland has shown no interest in trading Evan Mobley for Giannis, even as the Cavs face a difficult offseason after being eliminated.
  • The Miami Heat remain the most aggressive suitor, with the Milwaukee Bucks having set a draft-night deadline for a deal. As the Sun Sentinel's Ira Winderman explained, if Miami can't blow away Milwaukee with an offer, they lose the option of using their No. 13 pick in the package, and would pivot to trade or free agency alternatives or wait for 2027 cap space. The Washington Wizards are also exploring a run at Giannis, per a source close to the situation, though questions remain about how much say Giannis has over his destination and how invested Washington's ownership actually is.
  • Giannis himself was in Athens for the EuroLeague Final Four and was asked whether fans might one day see him in a EuroLeague Final Four as a player. He didn't rule it out: "Yeah, yeah. Maybe one day it's gonna happen, but right now my focus is to play in the NBA for as many years as I can."
  • Oklahoma City's Game 4 collapse was historically bad. The Thunder's 82-point total was the fewest they had scored in a playoff game since Game 5 against the Rockets in the bubble, and the first time they failed to reach 90 in any game since the 2021-22 tank season finale, a 50-point loss to the Clippers. Spurs coach Mitch Johnson said the Thunder had "handled it well" physically and that the coaching chess match had him working through a 24-hour stretch where, if he wasn't sleeping, he was preparing. "Selfishly it's a lot of fun," he said.
  • A fan at Frost Bank Center brought a fake MVP trophy to Game 4 mocking Shai Gilgeous-Alexander's reputation for embellishing contact, turning the ongoing debate into a courtside prop. SGA finished with 19 points and 7 assists in the loss.
  • The Knicks lead the Eastern Conference finals 3-0 after dispatching Cleveland in Game 3, and the numbers behind this run are historic. Per HoopsHype, the 2025-26 Knicks have the best point differential in NBA playoff history at this stage. They also join the 2013 Heat and 2017 Warriors as the only teams to record 10 wins by 10 or more points before reaching the Finals, both of whom went on to win the title.
  • Cavs coach Kenny Atkinson didn't look for excuses after Game 3. "I did think their physicality and energy level was much higher than ours," he said. "They were the more physical team tonight." Cleveland has been outscored in almost every hustle category this postseason, including minus-123 in fast break points and minus-72 in points off turnovers.
  • Knicks coach Mike Brown praised Mikal Bridges and OG Anunoby after both went off in Game 3, with Bridges posting 22 points, six rebounds, three steals and two blocks, and Anunoby adding 21 points and seven rebounds. "I told him and OG, because I don't call a ton of play-calls, you guys got to find different ways to impose your will on the game," Brown said. Brown also made clear after the game that he believes Anunoby got robbed of First Team All-Defense: "Freakin' OG got robbed. I hope the voters get it right next time around."
  • Donovan Mitchell, who grew up in New York, wasn't bothered by the Knicks fans filling Rocket Mortgage FieldHouse. "I'm from New York. This doesn't shock me. They do it in every arena," he said. "It's like Cowboys fans, just who they are."
  • Jalen Duren making All-NBA has real financial weight. By earning the honor, Duren is now eligible to sign a five-year max deal with Detroit worth a projected $287.1 million, up from a previous max of $239.3 million over five years. Jaylen Brown made All-NBA Second Team and said he wasn't surprised: "I'm not the most liked player in the media, at times."
  • Anthony Edwards was candid in his exit interviews after the Timberwolves' series loss to the Spurs. On congratulating the Spurs with eight minutes left: "I ain't trying to be kicking with y'all after y'all done whooped my ass," he said. On the rivalry: "Hell yeah. Hell yeah. Hell yeah, it's a rivalry. I tip my hat to them young guys." On the season's lesson: patience, specifically after playing through a badly swollen toe that left him barely able to walk.
  • Jalen Duren's All-NBA selection has a contract parallel across the league: Tyrese Maxey is now super-max eligible with the 76ers, but only if he earns All-NBA again in 2026-27.
  • Draft prospect Darryn Peterson shared what happened when he suffered a serious medical scare during his college season. "Everybody gets a cramp in their calf or something in the middle of the night, but my whole body," he said. "I'm laying on the training table just not able to move. Felt like I was going to die that day." ESPN's Brian Windhorst said Peterson will not fall past No. 2 in the draft, with either Peterson or AJ potentially going first overall.
  • Kentucky big man Malachi Moreno has withdrawn from the NBA Draft and will return to the Wildcats for another season.
  • Tony Parker is expected to become head coach of ASVEL Villeurbanne for the 2026-27 season, with a projected salary of around €1.2 million annually, which would be the richest coaching contract in French basketball history. Former NBA head coach Dave Joerger has also interviewed for the Melbourne United head coaching vacancy in Australia's NBL, alongside Coby Karl.
  • Jimmy Butler got on stage at the BottleRock Napa Valley music festival and sang "A Thousand Miles" and "Beautiful Soul" for the crowd. The Warriors traded for Butler in February 2025, and it's clear the Bay Area relationship is working out off the court too.
