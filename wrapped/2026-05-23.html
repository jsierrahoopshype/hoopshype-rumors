NBA Rumors Wrap: Thunder bench erupts in Game 3, Giannis trade market takes shape, Bulls add coaching candidate **Oklahoma City's** bench did something Friday night that hasn't been done in over half a century of tracked NBA data. **Jared McCain** dropped a playoff career-high 24 points off the bench in Game 3 as the Thunder rolled past **San Antonio**, and the performance was part of a broader bench explosion: OKC's reserves scored 76 points, the most by a bench unit in a conference finals or Finals game since starters began being tracked in 1971. It was also the third straight playoff game in which the Thunder bench topped 50 points, tied for the second-longest such streak in a single postseason. McCain, acquired in a trade from **Philadelphia**, was asked about being dealt by **Daryl Morey** and kept the tone generous: "It's never to prove anybody wrong. Daryl's still the guy that drafted me, so I'll always have love for him for that." The Thunder lead the Western Conference finals 3-0. Here are more notes from around the league: • **Shai Gilgeous-Alexander** finished Game 3 with 26 points, 12 assists and only two turnovers despite the Spurs throwing two defenders at him on nearly every possession. When asked about San Antonio's crowd booing him, SGA kept it straightforward: "I don't expect their fans to love me. It's not how it works. They want their team to win. It's that simple." On his relationship with McCain, Shai said he didn't imagine the rookie playing a huge role in the WCF at first, but that changed quickly: "After the first couple of games, yeah. It just oozes confidence." • **Victor Wembanyama** has been magnificent across the series despite the team falling behind 0-3, putting up 41 points and 24 rebounds in Game 1, then 47 points and 21 rebounds combined in Games 2 and 3. After Game 3, he turned the mirror on himself: "I feel like I have trouble making my teammates better right now. I need to be more of a team player." On the series deficit: "There's no place for frustration. It's annoying, of course, but we gotta use that as energy." • **De'Aaron Fox** and **Dylan Harper** both played in Game 3 after injury scares. Fox dealt with a right ankle sprain that kept him out of the first two games, while Harper was dealing with a right adductor injury that made him a game-time decision. Spurs coach **Mitch Johnson** offered no update on their condition afterward: "I'm sure they're beat up. They're giving us everything they got." • The Spurs opened Game 3 with a 15-0 run, the longest start to a conference finals game in the play-by-play era dating to 1997-98. The Thunder became the only NBA team in the last 20 years to allow the first 15 or more points of a game and win by 15 or more. • **Julian Champagnie** had a message before Game 3 about how the Spurs can help Wembanyama against physical defense: "Hit back. Honestly, hit back." He added that holding opposing players for grabbing Wembanyama's arm and jersey is a job for the officials, not the players. • **Shams Charania** reported that the **Lakers** expressed interest in trading for **Giannis Antetokounmpo** at the deadline, but the package Los Angeles could realistically offer is three first-round picks and cap space to absorb Giannis's contract. Per Shams on a recent YouTube appearance: "My sense is they're going to get better in the marketplace than that." Three anonymous executives told The Athletic that Giannis's physical profile makes him a logical trade target for teams trying to contend with Wembanyama, with one Western Conference executive calling him "a matchup solution for Wemby." • LeBron James's free agency timeline is coming into focus. ESPN's Brian Windhorst said on air: "I think by the draft, which is one month from today, we will know whether LeBron will be a Laker or not." A separate ESPN cap analysis found that if LeBron wants $50 million and the Lakers also re-sign **Austin Reaves**, the math simply does not work, but a $30 million figure would allow Los Angeles to re-sign **Luke Kennard**, **Rui Hachimura** and deploy the full $15 million non-tax midlevel exception. • The **Knicks** lead the **Cavaliers** 2-0 in the Eastern Conference finals. **Josh Hart** has been the matchup nightmare Cleveland can't solve, and **Donovan Mitchell** acknowledged as much, drawing a comparison: "You see a similar situation in the other series with Alex Caruso. They're guarding him kind of the same way. You just got to tip your cap." **Jalen Brunson** finished with a playoff-high 14 assists and wasn't even hunting Hart on purpose: "I'm really not trying to look for him, he just happens to be open." • The Cavs shot 9-for-26 from three in Game 2, including 6-for-20 on open looks. **Sam Merrill**, shooting 40% from deep in these playoffs, went 0-for-7 from three, the first time in his career he's attempted more than five threes in a game without making one. **Evan Mobley** pointed to the second-round comeback as a reason not to panic: "We're in the same place as last round. We've been here before." • **Mikal Bridges** is averaging 18.7 points, 3.0 assists and 1.4 steals over his last seven games, shooting 68.3% from the field and 44.4% from three. Coach **Mike Brown** credited the turnaround: "He just started to impose his will on the game a little bit more." • The **Bulls** will interview Miami Heat assistant Chris Quinn as part of their head coaching search, per HoopsHype. Quinn has been on **Erik Spoelstra's** staff since 2014 and has a strong player development background. • **Jeff Van Gundy** interviewed for the Orlando Magic's head coaching vacancy, confirmed by a league source. Van Gundy earns roughly $3 million annually as **Tyronn Lue's** top assistant with the **Clippers**, one of the highest figures for any assistant in the league, which raises questions about what Portland would need to offer to lure him to the Trail Blazers job as well. Two more names have emerged as finalists in Portland's search: Boston's **Tyler Lashbrook** and Utah's **Mike Williams**, with candidates expected to fly into Portland this weekend. • The **Mavericks** coaching search took an unexpected turn when one NBA executive told The Athletic it wouldn't be shocking if Dallas considered interviewing South Carolina's **Dawn Staley**. Staley previously said a prior NBA interview process made her believe "no NBA team is ready for a female coach right now," leaving her interest unclear. • **Nikola Jokic** is expected to play for Serbia in both the July and August FIBA windows. **Bogdan Bogdanovic**, still under contract with the **Clippers**, will skip July but plans to participate in August, according to Serbian national team coach Dušan Alimpijević. • Former NBA player and current BC Siauliai head coach **Darius Songaila** was attacked in Lithuania earlier this week. Songaila was walking his dog when an unleashed dog approached and he picked up his pet to protect it. The attacking dog's owner then struck Songaila with a heavy chain leash, cutting his nose and head and chipping a tooth. Police have launched a pre-trial investigation and the suspect has not been identified. • One more note from the Spurs bench: **Carter Bryant** made news during Game 2 when Thunder fans spotted him using a public restroom on the concourse mid-game. As The Athletic explained, Bryant chose a bathroom roughly 100 feet closer than the visitors' locker room facility rather than making the full trip down the tunnel. Teammate Julian Champagnie defended the decision: "He had to go to the bathroom. I think, if anything, we should stop recording people in the bathroom."
