NBA Rumors Wrap: Jalen Duren breaks silence on extension drama, 2023 draft class extension deadline looms, Tobias Harris injury puts opener in doubt

Jalen Duren finally spoke publicly about his long contract standoff with the Detroit Pistons, and he wanted to set the record straight. The 22-year-old center addressed reporters at training camp after agreeing to a five-year, $200 million fully guaranteed deal, saying he never leaked anything to the press during the process. "I have never spoken to anyone in the media, made any statements or comments about anything or everything that's been out there," Duren said. "So you can accept those as rumors and they do not represent how I feel or what I think." The extension saga dragged on through the entire summer, generating plenty of speculation about his fit and desire to stay in Detroit, all of which Duren flatly rejected. Draymond Green confirmed the deal's terms on his podcast, noting the contract includes no options and no bonuses.

Duren said his main emotion coming out of the process is "relief and excitement," and that his family hearing the constant noise made it harder than just the negotiating itself. "I never looked at it as a dreadful situation because people are going through way worse," he said. "We're talking about negotiating a lot of money." He described last season as "humbling" and said he spent the summer watching film and looking in the mirror. "I'm man enough to acknowledge my flaws," he added, "and I'm also man enough to get my ass in the gym and work on those things."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The deadline to sign rookie extensions for the 2023 draft class is fast approaching, and only four players have locked in deals so far: Victor Wembanyama, Keyonte George, Amen Thompson and Ausar Thompson. The strongest candidates to sign next, per league sources, are Brandon Miller, Anthony Black, Cason Wallace and Brandin Podziemski.
  • Tobias Harris is dealing with a left calf strain that occurred during Spurs training camp, and his status for the regular season opener is questionable. Coach Mitch Johnson said the team will not rush him: "Is he ready for the season opener? Totally questionable, and we will not push that at all."
  • Wembanyama looked sharp in his first preseason action, putting up 16 points in 21 minutes and drawing MVP chants from the Frost Bank Center crowd, nearly four months after San Antonio lost the Finals to the Knicks. This offseason, Wemby trained with France's Provence Rugby team, working on movement patterns, following last summer's viral stint with Shaolin monks in China.
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo played his first preseason minutes in a Heat jersey Saturday, going for 14 minutes in a 129-105 win over the Raptors. Coach Erik Spoelstra drew a striking comparison afterward, saying the way Giannis has played with the ball and delivered passes reminds the staff of "a great big point guard that played for Pat back in the '80s." Giannis himself kept expectations measured and long-term: "It's going to happen. It's going to take time. We're preparing for something that will take place in June."
  • Bobby Portis called his pairing with Giannis in Miami "a match made in heaven," saying both players want championships and the Heat organization is built around winning, not tanking for picks.
  • Max Strus left the Clippers' preseason opener in Hawaii early with a right foot injury suffered on a non-contact play. Coach Ty Lue said the team planned to do testing once they flew back home. The timing is unfortunate given Lue had said earlier in camp he is excited to use Strus and Rui Hachimura the way he used Kyle Korver in Cleveland.
  • Alex Sarr is close to returning to full contact work with the Wizards after sitting out contact drills in the first week of camp following mid-June surgery on a fractured foot. He could begin participating as early as Monday.
  • Spencer Jones was diagnosed with a hamstring strain and is considered day-to-day for the Nuggets. Coach David Adelman said the goal is to get Jones healthy for the season opener in Oklahoma City.
  • Dalton Knecht is not expected to make the Lakers' regular season roster, per Draymond Green. With 16 guaranteed contracts and only 15 allowed, Green said on his podcast that Bronny James and Jaden Hardy have been outplaying Knecht in camp and that Knecht is viewed as the odd man out.
  • There is interest around the league in a Bronny James trade, per Heavy.com. One executive said "he's gotten much better in a short period" and knows his role, making him attractive to teams looking for young, moldable players. The Lakers are not actively seeking to move him, but could use him to bring back draft assets.
  • LeBron James said the 76ers have the potential to be a top-five defensive team if they commit nightly. Joel Embiid pushed back on the notion of sacrifice, saying after the Blue-White Scrimmage: "Everybody keeps talking about sacrificing, but I don't really see it as sacrificing." LeBron praised Tyrese Maxey as a cerebral player who speaks up on the floor: "He is not quiet, and I am saying that in a good way."
  • Jimmy Butler has become the biggest fan of Warriors rookie Malevy Leons at Golden State's training camp, jumping off the bench to celebrate whenever Leons does something positive and pumping him up throughout the week.
  • The Kings waived guard Sean East and center Viktor Lakhin, per HoopsHype, as the roster trimming process continues across the league heading into the preseason.
  • The City of Paris reaffirmed its political commitment to Paris Basketball over the weekend, directly strengthening its case to host a future franchise in NBA Europe. The announcement came ahead of a key EuroLeague summit where member clubs will vote on whether to accept the league's proposed partnership with the NBA or maintain the current structure.
