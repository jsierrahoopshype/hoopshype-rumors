NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade in limbo, LeBron James free agency heats up, Clippers sign Baba Miller

The Los Angeles Clippers-Toronto Raptors trade centered on Kawhi Leonard is on hold until the NBA completes its ongoing investigation into the Clippers organization, per Shams Charania. The league informed both sides that the Raptors would need to assume the risk of any penalties tied to Kawhi's contract that could theoretically stem from the investigation before the deal closes. Both franchises issued statements confirming the delay, with the Raptors saying they "remain eager to bring Kawhi back to Toronto." The NBA told Marc Stein it has no specific timeline for concluding the investigation but expects it to wrap "in the coming weeks." Early reporting from Michael Grange suggested the hope was to have the matter resolved by the Board of Governors meeting Tuesday, with all sides still expecting the deal to ultimately go through.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • LeBron James' free agency remains wide open with no timetable for a decision, according to sources. Several teams have had executives record voice memos sent to LeBron via agent Rich Paul, though it's unclear whether Cleveland or Miami are among them. Shams Charania said those two teams "kind of don't need to" since James had already visited both buildings.
  • • The Cavaliers are widely viewed as the favorite. Bill Simmons said on his podcast that the Warriors believed they were in contention for LeBron and potentially Anthony Davis, but were being used as leverage. "The Cleveland thing is done," Simmons said. Adding to the noise, Brandon Weems, a childhood friend of James who works in Cleveland's front office, was the only team executive believed to have had direct contact with James during this free agency, per The Athletic.
  • • ESPN Cleveland's Emmett Golden reported hearing that both LeBron and Bronny James are headed back to Cleveland. Meanwhile, LeBron was spotted golfing with Draymond Green in Puerto Rico, keeping the Warriors speculation alive on social media.
  • • Jaylen Brown told IShowSpeed in a candid exchange that "there's no loyalty" after his trade from Boston to Philadelphia. When Speed asked where LeBron is going, Brown was straightforward: "He's going to Philly, bro. He's going to Philly trying to get a ring." Brown also described his own situation: "I just got packed up."
  • • The Celtics moved Brown partly because they believed he was going to seek an extension this summer, after which he would have become effectively untradeable. "Not only did they think they weren't getting $57M value, but then he'd become untradeable," Brian Windhorst said. Kevin Garnett compared the decision to breaking up a great thing, saying Boston will look back on it the way Minnesota rued the KAT trade.
  • • Per HoopsHype, the Clippers signed second-round pick Baba Miller to a four-year, $9.61 million deal with two guaranteed years. It is the largest overall contract for any second-round pick in the 2026 draft so far, per agents Deirunas Visockas and Guillermo Bermejo.
  • • AJ Dybantsa scored 27 points in his Las Vegas Summer League debut for the Wizards, matching Blake Griffin's record for the most points by a No. 1 overall pick in a summer league debut. Anthony Davis also worked out at the Wizards' practice facility setup Thursday morning and looked in great shape, with owner Ted Leonsis in town for what figures to be a milestone moment for the franchise.
  • • Utah Jazz rookie Darryn Peterson had nine personal fouls in his Las Vegas Summer League debut. That game aside, Peterson has looked sharp overall, putting up 25 points and 12 assists in a subsequent win over the Grizzlies. Teammate Keyonte George said "you tell him something, he does it right away" and called Peterson's ceiling "special." Off the court, George says Peterson has been largely a homebody since being drafted, too busy walking his dog around downtown to explore Salt Lake City.
  • • There is also new optimism around a contract extension for George himself. A source told ESPN's Tim MacMahon there is "a little bit more optimism" about a deal happening than there had been even a week or two ago, with the two sides potentially meeting in the middle.
  • • The Mavs preference regarding Klay Thompson is to find a trade rather than buy him out, Marc Stein told Bleacher Report. The Heat are among the teams hoping Thompson eventually shakes free as a buyout candidate.
  • • Nikola Jovic said he was "happy that I stayed" after the Heat traded Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis and more to Milwaukee to acquire Giannis Antetokounmpo. Jakucionis was not included in the deal. Heat summer league guard Trevor Keels said the upcoming summer league matchup between Miami and Milwaukee "is going to be fun."
  • • The Golden State Warriors re-signed center Charles Bassey to a one-year deal, bringing him back for his sixth NBA season after Quinten Post signed an offer sheet with Memphis.
  • • Jordan Clarkson is returning to the Knicks on a one-year, $3.9 million deal. The contract comes in at $2.4 million against the cap and includes trade veto rights, leaving New York $3.3 million below the second apron with 13 players signed.
  • • A hacked database from Madison Square Garden revealed that the arena assigned risk scores to roughly 400 celebrities who attend Knicks games. Fat Joe was labeled "medium risk" reportedly due to his association with rapper Jadakiss, who had been critical of owner James Dolan in the past. The database contains nearly 40,000 entries dating back years, and a source described the system to Wired as "a really, really paranoid, terrible system."
  • • The Pacers have signed Kobe Brown to a two-way contract, per a league source.
  • • The Clippers' Yanic Konan Niederhauser had an offseason procedure to remove hardware from his right foot as part of his recovery from March Lisfranc surgery. He is still in a boot, but the team says it is a normal part of the process.
