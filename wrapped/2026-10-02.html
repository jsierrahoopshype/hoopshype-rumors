NBA Rumors Wrap: Jalen Duren signs $200M deal with Pistons, Donovan Mitchell extends with Adidas, Warriors down three starters

Jalen Duren is staying in Detroit. The All-NBA, All-Star center agreed to a five-year, $200 million fully guaranteed contract to remain with the Pistons, ending what had been a prolonged restricted free agency standoff between the two sides, per Shams Charania. Agent Chafie Fields of THE•TEAM negotiated the deal. Chris Haynes also confirmed the agreement. Duren was the most prominent restricted free agent still unsigned heading into training camp, and the resolution gives Detroit its franchise centerpiece locked up through 2030-31.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Donovan Mitchell has signed a multiyear contract extension with Adidas that will carry him through a 10th signature shoe with the brand, making him the seventh active NBA player to reach that milestone. The deal also includes resources for his alma mater, Greenwich Country Day, to expand access to educational programs for students from underserved communities. Cavaliers teammate Sam Merrill said Mitchell had always been committed to Cleveland, recalling that Mitchell's response to questions about his future was always, "Is it not obvious?"
  • • The Warriors are entering training camp without three starters. Jimmy Butler and Moses Moody are both expected to miss until mid-season, and Kristaps Porzingis is also unavailable. The two-year, $40 million deal Golden State signed Porzingis to has only $3 million guaranteed in year two, giving the team an exit if needed next summer. Gary Payton II said the roster gives him 2022 championship vibes, citing a mix of veteran winners and players coming off NCAA titles, but staying healthy is the obvious condition attached to any postseason ambitions.
  • • Extension talks between the Warriors and Brandin Podziemski are ongoing, with both sides expressing optimism ahead of the October 19 deadline. Podziemski's cap hold sits at roughly $17.5 million, and given how carefully Golden State manages future flexibility, an extension figure above that number seems unlikely.
  • • Jamal Shead is staying in Toronto on a three-year, $24 million extension, with the deal keeping him under contract through 2029-30. Per HoopsHype, the Raptors included a third-year team option. Shead led the league with 354 bench assists last season, a franchise record for reserves, while playing all 82 games. "I thought the offer they gave me was really fair. Honestly, above fair," Shead said. "It's just good to be wanted."
  • • The 76ers are generating some early training camp buzz around their new-look roster. New addition Dean Wade said the basketball IQ of his new teammates is unlike anything he has experienced: "Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid, LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, everyone, the way they think the game is at such a high level. As an NBA player, you think you know the game well, and then you get on a team like this and it is night and day." Coach Nick Nurse said both LeBron James and Embiid have been involved in everything but are managing their workloads smartly in early sessions.
  • • Jaylen Brown said younger Sixers players have been seeking him out for mentorship. "Being a teacher. I was the guy that was always asking questions, so I appreciate when the young guys ask me questions," Brown said. He also credited Tyrese Maxey for going out of his way to make him feel comfortable in the new setting: "He doesn't have to do that. I'm the type of guy that I'll figure it out regardless. But for him to try and make sure I feel welcomed, I appreciate it."
  • • Bam Adebayo pushed back on anyone doubting the Heat this season. "People are still doubting this team for whatever reason. Somebody said we'll be a play in team. I was like 'wow that's crazy.' We just got one of the best shooters ever and one of the top 3 players in our league," Adebayo said. New addition Bobby Portis called the move from Milwaukee to Miami "a breath of fresh air."
  • • Jayson Tatum is entering his 10th NBA season feeling healthier and more confident than he did during last season's injury return. "I feel so much more confident from a mental standpoint and physically," Tatum said. He spent the summer getting his body fully right after playing through an injury late last year. "It's been a hell of a journey," he added. "It definitely has flown by."
  • • The Knicks have a straightforward injury situation with Josh Hart: his damaged finger is not getting fixed until he retires. Hart laid out his options at training camp Thursday with characteristic humor. "So I guess it's either wrap it till I retire, cut it off," Hart said. "Or I guess amputation." On a related note, Jalen Brunson said the team is focused on letting Andre Drummond be himself rather than trying to replace Mitchell Robinson: "He's not coming in to be Mitch, he's coming in to be himself."
  • • The Clippers have Darius Garland, Max Strus, Derrick Jones Jr., Rui Hachimura and Isaiah Jackson working with the first unit through two days of camp, with Brook Lopez running with the second group for now. Coach Tyronn Lue said that could change. Second-year guard Keaton Wagler said he has been challenging himself by guarding Garland every chance he gets, with coach Jeff Van Gundy pushing him to pick up full court.
  • • The NBA's European expansion plans got more public attention Thursday. AEK Athens owner Makis Angelopoulos said the league has submitted a formal proposal to the EuroLeague that he believes would lead to the EuroLeague being absorbed into a new European structure. The NBA's Europe managing director will deliver a live update on those plans at a London summit on October 8, the day after the EuroLeague shareholders' meeting.
  • • Commissioner Adam Silver said he was surprised not by the $12.5 billion valuation of the Lakers sale, but that there was a reason to sell at all. "My initial surprise went less to price and more that there was a reason to even sell the Lakers," Silver said at Puck's In The Arena conference. He added he did not know the sale was coming when it happened.
  • • Mark Daigneault offered his thoughts on Russell Westbrook's retirement during Thunder training camp. "He's, obviously, one of the giants of the game, not only in Oklahoma City, in the history of the league," Daigneault said. "When you got guys that are of his caliber that have passed through the organization, there's a certain standard that they set that when new players come in, they feel an obligation to live up to."
  • • The Pistons plan to increase Ausar Thompson's minutes this season after he averaged a career-high 26 per game in 2025-26. Coach JB Bickerstaff said the expectation is clear: "I definitely see his minutes going above 26."
  • • "Inside the NBA" will air on 25 regular-season dates this year, five more than last season and on a more consistent weekly schedule. The Ernie Johnson, Charles Barkley, Kenny Smith and Shaquille O'Neal crew will appear on ESPN's first five broadcasts of the season, return for the Thanksgiving Eve tripleheader, and then anchor ABC's "NBA Saturday Primetime" launch in December.
