NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers lose five first-round picks, LeBron's Sixers draw Dr. J's eye, Lakers roster gets Barnes treatment

The NBA has stripped the Los Angeles Clippers of five first-round draft picks spanning 2029 through 2033 as part of the league's punishment in the Kawhi Leonard salary cap scandal, per the Philadelphia Inquirer. The picks being taken are a first-rounder the Indiana Pacers owe the Clippers from the Ivica Zubac trade, plus four of the Clippers' own future firsts. A league source confirmed that the Sixers' right to swap the 2029 Clippers pick, which they retained from the James Harden trade in 2023, remains intact and untouched by the punishment. The league essentially threaded a needle that protects Philadelphia's existing draft capital while hitting Los Angeles hard on future assets.

Draymond Green used the Clippers scandal as a jumping-off point to question the salary cap itself. "Why is there a salary cap? Why are guys being prevented from making money from team sponsors or league sponsors, or whoever else?" Green said on his podcast. "We talk about, we're in a league that is player-centric." Green stopped short of defending Leonard specifically but redirected the conversation toward broader structural criticism of how the league compensates players.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Julius "Dr. J" Erving has publicly questioned LeBron James's place on the all-time list in the past, but he was generous about LeBron's move to the Philadelphia 76ers. "It's going to be very exciting for the fans of Philadelphia and for LeBron," Erving told ScoopB. "That particular team can set some records and win some championships, but they have to do it on the court, not with lip service."
  • Sandro Mamukelashvili said the Lakers' offseason trip to Luka Doncic's home country of Slovenia helped the new roster gel quickly. "First of all, shoutout to Luka. I think the organization was amazing, the way they planned everything," Mamukelashvili said. "They showed us a lot of great places, and Ljubljana is a really, really nice city." Former NBA player Matt Barnes has been less reverent about the overhauled roster, joking in a video with TMZ Sports that it's "the whitest Laker team since the Minneapolis Lakers." Barnes quickly pivoted to genuine praise, saying that with Doncic and Austin Reaves leading the way as "two of the best scoring guards in the league," he wants to see it play out before forming a real opinion.
  • Julius Randle congratulated his former team the Knicks on their championship while making clear his focus is now on Brooklyn. "What the organization accomplished is great. Been a long time coming," Randle said. "Definitely look forward to creating that in Brooklyn."
  • South Korean guard Lee Hyun-jung has signed an Exhibit 10 contract with the Portland Trail Blazers ahead of the 2026-27 season, according to his agency, Epic Sports. An Exhibit 10 is a one-year, non-guaranteed deal at the minimum salary and does not guarantee a roster spot.
  • Former NBA guard Amari Bailey has reached an agreement with Hapoel Jerusalem on a long-term contract, with plans to be loaned to Hapoel Beer Sheva/Dimona for the upcoming season.
  • Former NBA forward Reggie Perry has signed with EuroCup's KK Bosna, the Sarajevo-based club announced. On a related note, Isaiah Thomas posted on X that he wants to play overseas: "I want to hoop overseas for a year!!! I want that experience!"
  • G League prospect Dink Pate turned down an NBA contract to potentially preserve his college eligibility and has signed to play at Providence, but is awaiting NCAA clearance. His agent argues the precedent supports him: "Dink never enrolled in college and left. So why should he be punished?"
  • Celtics forward Jordan Walsh said a recent contract extension has him more motivated heading into year four, not less. "I want to be able to be called on when we need to get a bucket, and also still do the same things on defense that I've been doing because that's what got me here," Walsh said. He has spent the summer focused on developing his offense and creating his own shot.
  • Derrick Rose reflected on his legacy in a Sports Illustrated interview, saying he fears he will eventually be forgotten. "Truth to be told, given how my story has unfolded, in X amount of years, they probably won't remember me anymore," Rose said. He added that if he is remembered, he wants it to be for two things: "That I played hard. That I was a winner."
  • Glen Davis spoke candidly in a new video about his time in solitary confinement. "Nothing but time to reflect, like nothing but time every single day thinking about all the mistakes and everything that I've done," Davis said. "Sometimes I wanted to pull my eyes out my head because of just like, bro, you're in jail." In a separate interview, Davis also reflected on his 2008 championship with the Celtics, saying he took the experience for granted at the time: "I didn't understand the situation I was in, the opportunity that I truly really had."
  • Knicks forward Mikal Bridges shared his off-season training philosophy, singling out Bulgarian split squats as the exercise he hates most, and therefore trusts the most. "If it hurts and it's painful, then it's probably a good thing," Bridges said.
  • Bam Adebayo has a somewhat unusual moviegoing experience these days. Teammate Gabe Vincent revealed that Adebayo rents out the entire theater when he goes to see a film, a perk Vincent questioned: "When you go to the movies, do you really wanna spend $1,500?"
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander visited SickKids hospital in what appeared to be a charity appearance through his SGA Foundation.
