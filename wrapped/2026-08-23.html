NBA Rumors Wrap: Klay Thompson gives back $9.8M in Mavs buyout, LeBron's 76ers fit, Timberwolves ownership shift

Klay Thompson gave back roughly $9.8 million in his buyout agreement with the Dallas Mavericks, league sources told HoopsHype's Michael Scotto. Thompson will join the Miami Heat once he clears waivers, a move that has been widely expected for weeks. The buyout figure gives some context to just how motivated Thompson was to get out of Dallas and land in South Beach. The Athletic also reported that one of the reasons Thompson chose the Mavericks over the Lakers back in 2024 was concern about Anthony Davis's availability, a source with knowledge of his thinking said. That decision did not work out as planned, and Thompson is now starting fresh with his third NBA franchise.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Free agent forward Keshad Johnson continues to work out with the Heat and has talked to the team about a return. Miami could bring him back on a standard contract or a two-way deal, giving Thompson a familiar supporting cast to walk into.
  • VJ Edgecombe said any concerns about his role shrinking with LeBron James joining the 76ers are overblown. "I'm just excited to play alongside my teammates. I think we have a chance to do something really special," Edgecombe told the Inquirer. He also admitted he'll have to hold himself back from peppering LeBron with questions: "I'm going to have to control myself when it comes to asking questions. I'm totally excited. It's such a blessing. I'm going into year two, playing alongside my favorite player ever."
  • Bronny James was asked about his father's departure from the Lakers and kept it brief. "It's whatever, it's my Dad," Bronny told a group of young fans while signing autographs.
  • Josh Hart joked about his looming contract extension on Instagram after a report suggested the deal might fall short of the $132 million maximum. "Darn was hoping for that 132! I'll take 131 instead," Hart wrote in the comments. Meanwhile, Hart and Jalen Brunson are still riding the championship wave from June, with their "Roommates Show Block Party" live podcast event moving from Central Park to Randall's Island on Sept. 24 after more than 4,000 tickets sold out in 90 seconds, leaving nearly 8,000 fans on the waitlist.
  • New Timberwolves controlling owner Marc Stad is taking over from Marc Lore, who steps into a limited partner role. Stad, 47, founded Dragoneer Investment Group, a San Francisco-based firm with over $35 billion in assets under management and investments in OpenAI, Anthropic, Spotify and Airbnb, among others. He was practically anonymous within the ownership group before this transition. His wife Elisa will serve as governor of the franchise to allow Stad to remain focused on Dragoneer.
  • The NBA and YouTube remain in advanced talks on a local broadcast streaming hub covering 20 to 25 teams starting in 2027-28, with a price tag north of $1 billion. Sources believe if the league can deliver 29 clubs, YouTube would pay roughly $1.2 billion. If the package comes in at 25 teams or fewer, that number drops to around $850 million. Every NBA team is currently on one-year local TV deals or deals with one-year opt-outs while the league finalizes its plan.
  • Dejounte Murray opened up about his Achilles recovery in a wide-ranging Q&A with HoopsHype. "You see your calf get small, and then you can't do a calf raise in month two, and it's just like, 'Damn, it's over,'" Murray said. He described the mental challenge as the hardest part of the first couple months, but said he tried to reframe the injury as motivation for others going through similar setbacks.
  • Celtics owner Bill Chisholm said Boston does not necessarily need a new arena to stay competitive, but wants a building that matches the franchise's standards. "We need to have the ability to influence that agenda," Chisholm said of TD Garden, which is owned by Delaware North. "If we need to own the building, we'll do that."
  • Celtics rookie Chris Cenac Jr. left an impression on his college coach Kelvin Sampson, who said Cenac responded to every piece of harsh criticism the same way for 10 months. "He'd just say, 'My fault, Coach. My fault. I'll try to do better,'" Sampson said. "He's one of the players I wish I could have coached a little longer."
  • DeMar DeRozan has started every game since his sophomore season, a streak of 1,250 regular-season and playoff games, the eighth-longest such run since starters were first tracked on box scores in 1970-71. The question now is whether he continues that streak as a Nugget.
  • Dennis Schroder posted a double-double as Germany won the DBB Supercup over Croatia, 96-87. Schroder had 10 points, 10 assists, seven rebounds and three steals, though he shot just 3-of-16 from the field. Teammate Daniel Theis said he believes Schroder will eventually finish his career with his hometown club in Braunschweig: "I feel like he'll play for the national team until he's 40."
  • Shaquille O'Neal, promoting his new professional dunking league, said he has a simple explanation for why NBA stars skip the dunk contest. "What's the word… soft?" O'Neal said. "You can tell them I said it. They're soft, and they're scared." O'Neal also told Variety that Marvel turned him down for a role in an Avengers film because executives worried audiences would immediately recognize him and break the illusion.
  • Mavs rookie Morez Johnson Jr. spent nearly three hours stuffing and distributing backpacks with school supplies at Washington Elementary School in Riverdale, Illinois, the school he attended as a child.
