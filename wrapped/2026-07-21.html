NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron James' free agency drags on, Lakers land Thybulle, Pistons-Duren talks stall **LeBron James** remains unsigned with no timetable in sight, and the list of potential destinations keeps widening. Shams Charania told ESPN Cleveland that "there is no decision, no choice yet, no timetable on a choice as of yet," after speaking with LeBron's agent **Rich Paul** Monday morning. LeBron himself addressed the delay in a YouTube interview, saying family factors beyond basketball are central to his thinking: his daughter, his wife, and what fits him as a person, not just as a player. **Miami**, **Cleveland**, and **Golden State** have all surfaced as serious options, and when Patrick Beverley posted that LeBron was making his decision Monday, LeBron responded on Instagram with a Kevin Hart video and a comment reading "BINGO STFU." Here are more notes from around the league: • Brian Windhorst said Miami is "absolutely" on the board for LeBron, and that the story arc in Cleveland remains compelling given where his family is. Bill Simmons still bets Cleveland, saying the Cavs are trying to figure out how to get LeBron onto the roster without paying him the minimum. Meanwhile, Tim Hardaway Sr. said at SiriusXM that the longer this drags out, the better Miami's chances get, calling it the best skill-set fit because they need another ball handler. • **Pat Riley** opened up about LeBron's history with the **Heat** in an interview taped Friday. "I was very disappointed because I thought we were looking at a dynasty, something dynastic," Riley told the Sun-Sentinel, referring to LeBron's 2014 departure. He added that he and LeBron haven't talked much, but said "you let go of that stuff." • Riley also addressed the Bam Adebayo-Tyler Herro confrontation that got physical on a practice court during Summer League in Las Vegas last week. Herro had posted on social media casting **Adebayo** as a limited offensive player and overpaid, and **Bam** confronted him over it. "I didn't like what I read," Riley said, adding that social media has consequences. Herro was subsequently dealt to Milwaukee in the Giannis Antetokounmpo trade. • **Giannis Antetokounmpo** made clear how he feels about his new boss. "I call him Mr. Pat," Giannis said on Instagram. "He told me 'just call me Pat.' Nah, he's a legend. You got to show people like that respect." Giannis described visiting Riley's office as "kind of intimidating" and said he's excited to do whatever he can to help the Heat be great. • The **Lakers** agreed to a one-year, $3.3 million deal with defensive specialist **Matisse Thybulle**, per Shams Charania. Thybulle is a two-time All-Defense team member who spoke with both JJ Redick and Luka Doncic about his role before choosing Los Angeles. He shot 41.8% from three over the last two seasons and led the NBA in steals and deflections per 36 minutes last season among players who appeared in at least 20 games. • The Thybulle signing pushed the Lakers to 16 guaranteed contracts, and they will need to move or waive one player before opening night to accommodate him. The roster is currently at full capacity, though the team is still monitoring the market for big men via trade or signing. • The Lakers also signed forward Arthur Kaluma to a two-way deal. Kaluma spent the 2025-26 season in the Lakers' G League program and averaged nearly 19 points per game with 61% shooting and 50% from three in Las Vegas Summer League. Signing Kaluma will require waiving one of the team's current two-way players, with Peter Suder or AK Okereke the most likely candidates. • The clincher for the Lakers' signing of Ziaire Williams on a one-year minimum deal: a four-person contingent including Rob Pelinka, Kurt Rambis, and assistant coaches Nate McMillan and Greg St. Jean traveled to Las Vegas to watch the 6-foot-9 Williams work out at trainer Joe Abunassar's Impact Basketball gym. The visit was designed to assess Williams' game and his recent work sharpening his perimeter shooting. • **Jonathan Kuminga's** market remains complicated. The Lakers showed interest since late June, but the sides are far apart and LA is not motivated to push significant draft capital into a sign-and-trade, per The Athletic. Kuminga could re-emerge as a target if his market stays tepid. Cleveland is reportedly the team most active in his camp at this stage. • **Detroit** and restricted free agent big man **Jalen Duren** remain far apart, with progress "modest" since negotiations opened five weeks ago, per Marc Stein. The Pistons are also brushing aside any sign-and-trade discussions, making a straightforward deal the only path being seriously explored. • **OKC**'s Sam Presti spoke at length about the Lu Dort trade. "This was a financial decision," Presti told The Oklahoman, adding that savings generated this summer will be reallocated to future teams. Presti also said the money freed up, along with new revenue streams, will allow more basketball-driven decisions over the next several years. On the extension front, a reason behind the Dort move is that the Thunder want to extend **Cason Wallace** this summer and **Ajay Mitchell** next summer. • The **Atlanta Hawks** have 17 players under contract after adding Dort and John Nembhard, meaning they must cut two by the start of the season. Buddy Hield at $9 million is likely on his way out, and Devin Carter and Mo Gueye (who is out three to four months after foot surgery) are also candidates for the roster crunch. The official pick details in the Dort/Zaccharie Risacher trade: the Hawks sent a top-55 protected 2027 pick to Dallas and two second-rounders (2031, 2032) to OKC, per Brad Rowland. • The Kawhi Leonard/Brandon Ingram/Gradey Dick trade remains in limbo. The NBA investigation into the **Clippers** has not resolved, and it is at least possible that Ingram and Dick are still technically **Raptors** when training camp opens in October. The only clean exits are if the investigation finds no wrongdoing or if the Clippers and the league settle on penalties without going through the full process. • **Yaxel Lendeborg** won his second ring in four months, leading the **Golden State Warriors** past the **Memphis Grizzlies** 94-90 in the Summer League championship game Sunday. The Michigan product finished with 21 points and 10 rebounds to outduel **Cameron Boozer**, and became just the second player to win a Division I NCAA title and a Vegas Summer League title in the same year, joining Davion Mitchell. • **Klay Thompson**, entering the final year of his deal with Dallas, said it would be nice to end his career in Golden State when asked by a fan about a return to the Warriors. "Shoot, I don't know. I got one year on my deal. It'd be nice to end it in Golden State." Nothing is imminent, but the sentiment is out there. • **Anthony Edwards** said he doesn't consider himself one of the faces of the league yet. "I think the face of the league comes with a lot of responsibilities and I think one of those is winning a championship. I haven't did that yet," Edwards said. On his incoming partnership with **LaMelo Ball**, Edwards was candid: "First year together maybe a lot of downs. We'll celebrate both of them." • Lakers co-owner **Mark Walter** is facing federal scrutiny. Bloomberg reported that US prosecutors have been investigating potential financial improprieties at two of his insurance companies and at Guggenheim Partners, with the insurers receiving grand jury subpoenas in February. Prosecutors are examining whether the companies failed to disclose that certain private credit investments backed other parts of Walter's business empire. • The **Portland Trail Blazers** and the city of Portland are growing further apart on arena renovation funding. "I think we are very far apart," a team source said. The lease runs through 2030, and while two high-ranking Blazers sources said owner Tom Dundon has never mentioned relocation, the tension is real, with both sides accusing each other of foul play over transparency. • **Mario Hezonja** has informed Real Madrid he is exercising his NBA clause and leaving the club. No team has been announced yet. • Per HoopsHype, the **Mavericks** are waiving two-way guard Taelon Peter and also signed Jett Howard to a two-way contract. The **Pacers** are also waiving two-way guard **Taelon Peter**, who averaged 4.5 points in 38 games last season after being the 54th pick in 2025.
