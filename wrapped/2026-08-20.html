NBA Rumors Wrap: Cavaliers land Peyton Watson in five-team deal, Nuggets pivot to DeRozan, Buss family sale dispute drags on

The Cleveland Cavaliers have landed restricted free agent Peyton Watson in a massive five-team sign-and-trade, per Shams Charania. Watson will sign a four-year, $88 million deal with a player option and a trade kicker to join Cleveland, with Watson's reps at Klutch Sports navigating competing offers from the Bucks, Trail Blazers and Clippers before landing on the guaranteed money. The Denver Nuggets receive an unprotected 2031 Cavaliers first-round pick and a 2032 Kings second-rounder, while the Los Angeles Clippers receive Max Strus. The deal also folds in a pre-existing Dennis Schroder trade, with Schroder heading to the Charlotte Hornets and Tre Mann going to the Washington Wizards in exchange for Cam Whitmore, who gives Cleveland cap flexibility with a smaller salary. Cleveland has been the architect of the whole construction, orchestrating each moving piece of the five-team concept simultaneously.

With Watson out the door, the Nuggets have quickly shifted their focus to the free agent market. Denver is now targeting six-time All-Star DeMar DeRozan, who remains available and has drawn interest from multiple contenders. The Nuggets have a significant cap constraint to work around, though: as a second-apron team, they are currently prohibited from using the trade exception created by Watson's sign-and-trade, and using it at all would hard-cap them to the second apron.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Buss family ownership saga around the Los Angeles Lakers continues to churn, though multiple sources say the dispute is not expected to derail the pending sale. A source familiar with Mark Walter's thinking told The Athletic that Walter does not believe the Buss family conflict threatens his deal with Bob Iger and Josh Kushner, which values the franchise at a record $12.5 billion.
  • Jeanie Buss has no intention of selling her shares before her governorship ends, and a league source says she would be open to a scenario where her siblings sell while she retains at least 15 percent ownership and remains governor. The NBA requires team governors to own at least 15 percent of the franchise, so any resolution would need to thread that needle. Ramona Shelburne said on ESPN that Jeanie's legal position may be strong, but she is effectively on an island with immense external and internal pressure bearing down on her.
  • The broader financial picture around Walter is complicated. Walter has been seeking cash to fund billions in loans for months, and efforts to arrange an early lump-sum exit from his local TV deals with Charter Communications for the Lakers and Dodgers went nowhere. A podcast report also raised questions about GM Rob Pelinka's job security as new ownership takes shape, with one source saying Pelinka is "less safe than he was."
  • Phoenix Suns owner Mat Ishbia is facing financial scrutiny of his own. Ishbia pledged more than 805 million shares of his mortgage company UWM as collateral for JPMorgan loans used to buy the Suns in 2023, and UWM's stock has since fallen roughly 70%, meaning shares once worth $4.6 billion are now valued around $1.15 billion. The question being asked in some corners is whether a margin call from JPMorgan could eventually force Ishbia to sell equity in his sports portfolio.
  • Victor Wembanyama has been officially named captain of the France national team for the upcoming FIBA World Cup qualifying window. "Being captain is first and foremost about setting an example, being a leader, through performance on the court," Wembanyama said. "It's probably earlier than I expected, and likely earlier than everyone else expected. But it's not rushed either."
  • Santi Aldama will sit out Spain's upcoming FIBA qualifying window after his mid-March knee surgery, but the belief in Dallas is that he will be ready for Mavericks training camp when practices begin around September 23. The Mavs and Rockets are able to hold camp before the league-wide September 29 start date because both teams play preseason games abroad.
  • Luka Doncic is going all-out for Lakers team bonding, chartering a private plane for 14 teammates for a trip to Slovenia that includes gift bags of Slovenian items, dinner at one of his favorite restaurants, fishing at Lake Bled and golf. Additional gifts are expected to be waiting at the hotel on arrival.
  • Tyrese Maxey sat down with HoopsHype and addressed the ego question that comes with Philadelphia's star-studded new roster. "We have two guys that have won regular-season MVPs, two guys that have won Finals MVPs, multiple gold medal winners, multiple 25-plus points per game, All-Stars, All-NBA," Maxey told HoopsHype. "Nobody on this team can have an ego." He also said his signature shoe, the Maxey v1, was a dream come true: "Wow. I was surprised, I was thankful. For me, it's a blessing."
  • Ajay Mitchell is joining UC Santa Barbara's men's basketball program as general manager while still under contract with the Thunder. The UCSB alum will serve as a mentorship resource for current players. "UC Santa Barbara changed my life in so many ways," Mitchell said in the announcement.
  • Aaron Holiday has signed with Maxima Roma in Italy after three seasons with the Rockets. His deal includes an NBA out clause throughout the contract, allowing him to return to the States if a team meets buyout conditions.
  • The Nuggets announced a broadcasting overhaul, with Noah Reed joining Altitude as the new play-by-play voice and Katy Winge stepping into the lead color analyst role. Winge becomes the third full-time female analyst on an NBA team's regional network. Former NBA player Jason Richardson also announced he will join Denver as a studio analyst this season.
  • The Miami Heat has been kicking around the idea of signing center Nick Richards, who has been working out at Kaseya Center with Heat players. But there was no offer and no deal as of Wednesday midday. The Heat has its mid-level exception reserved for Klay Thompson, pending a buyout from Dallas; a 14th roster spot could go to a center or DeMar DeRozan, though DeRozan wants exception money the Heat is holding for Thompson.
  • At the Basketball Hall of Fame ceremony, a nearly 30-year feud between Dennis Rodman and Alonzo Mourning came to an end with a handshake and a laugh, all captured on video by Chris Webber. Kevin Garnett was on hand to hype up the moment. "Power Forward Summit," Webber wrote. "What's beef?"
