NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron lands in Philadelphia, Cavs explore Schroeder trades, Dybantsa dismisses trade talk

LeBron James is a Philadelphia 76er, and the man who made it happen is explaining how it came together. Sixers president Mike Gansey said the signing of Jaylen Brown was the unlock, telling reporters: "Without getting Jaylen, I don't think we get LeBron." Gansey also studied Olympic tape of LeBron and Joel Embiid playing together before committing to the pursuit, and he liked what he saw. "Any given night anyone can be the guy," Gansey said of the roster's flexibility. The Sixers then mounted a full-court press, asking everyone in the organization to reach out and sell LeBron on Philly — and it worked.

VJ Edgecombe got a FaceTime call from LeBron during the process and tried to play it cool. "I was in disbelief, but I had to act cool," Edgecombe said. "I'm on the phone all cool and stuff. My headphones on, I'm playing the game. Had to act all cool, but it's really Bron." Gansey summed up what LeBron brings to the locker room beyond basketball: "LeBron's a connector. He fits every role you possibly can. He's gonna elevate everyone in the whole building."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers are exploring trade packages involving Dennis Schroeder, with an eye toward creating financial flexibility to pursue free agents Jonathan Kuminga and Peyton Watson, per cleveland.com. No deal is done, but the organization is actively shopping scenarios.
  • AJ Dybantsa ran into a fan who asked if he planned to request a trade from the Washington Wizards. The No. 1 overall pick wasn't having it. "A trade? No? That's not even allowed. You can't even do that," Dybantsa told the fan.
  • The WNBA is navigating a charged moment around transgender athlete participation. Commissioner Cathy Engelbert sent a memo to teams this week stating that player eligibility rules are collectively bargained and that "preserving the integrity of our game and ensuring fair competition will always remain among the league's highest priorities," per ESPN. The WNBPA also released a statement denouncing transphobia, saying the union "embraces justice, equity, diversity."
  • Former NBA players Enes Kanter Freedom and Royce White both announced plans to enter the 2027 WNBA Draft. White, who is also running in Minnesota's Aug. 11 Republican primary for Senate, told Fox News: "I'm transgender. I'm a woman. I identify as sometimes identifying as a woman for purpose of basketball." Former NBA center Robin Lopez was unimpressed, writing: "I can't imagine anybody wants Enes Kanter or Royce White around them for even 4 minutes."
  • Jabari Smith Jr. says the Houston Rockets will be a more cohesive unit next season. "We added Kevin Durant last year, just gotta adjust to playing with him," Smith said. "Getting Fred back will be huge for us. You'll just see everybody older, obviously a lot better, more mature."
  • Rudy Gobert's coach has his back. Timberwolves coach Chris Finch said Gobert's defensive impact goes underappreciated because his errors are visible while his background work is not. "He's a first-ballot Hall of Famer," Finch said. "I think it's unfortunate how much criticism he receives."
  • OG Anunoby is back in London this week and has already moved past his Finals heroics for the Knicks. Asked if he still feels the same rush watching back his defining Finals moment, he was direct: "No. I'm over it now." Anunoby also credited the team's collective mindset for the title run: "We went on a crazy run. Every game was focused on the next."
  • Russell Westbrook says his free agency isn't weighing on him. "I don't think about it honestly," Westbrook said. "I love playing basketball and if it happens, it works and that's my plan."
  • Shaquille O'Neal isn't framing LeBron's departure from the Lakers as a mistake on the franchise's part. "No, it's what we call the business of basketball," O'Neal told Heavy. "When you get older, and you're still playing at a high level, you're still trying to demand high money."
  • Atlanta Hawks rookie Kingston Flemings chose No. 4 for his jersey with an assist from Derrick Rose. No. 1 was taken by Jalen Johnson, but Flemings had a backup plan: "I always liked being No. 1 because of D-Rose," he said. "But D-Rose also wore No. 4."
  • Coby White says the Hornets don't need to respond to doubters with words. "I think we don't got to say too much on it," White said. "Everybody's going to have an opinion and that's just what comes with being in the NBA." White also praised new teammate Grayson Allen, saying Allen has been "part of a winning culture everywhere he's gone."
  • Nike previewed an unseen Kobe Bryant sneaker silhouette at the Mamba Invitational this weekend. A mixed-media installation called "Sole Obsession" featured 13 historical Kobe outsoles alongside a previously unseen prototype from a shoe Kobe was working on before his death.
  • Isaiah Hartenstein and his foundation hosted a back-to-school bash for more than 300 students in Oklahoma City on Saturday, welcoming families from the Boys & Girls Clubs of Oklahoma County, Positive Tomorrows, and the City Rescue Mission. Meanwhile, Dereck Lively II covered the cost of admission for all 105 campers at a free hoop camp he ran at Beckley-Saner Recreation Center in Dallas's Oak Cliff neighborhood.
  • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson and his staff visited the Seahawks' training camp in Renton, Wash. this week. Johnson, a Seattle native, and Seahawks coach Mike Macdonald exchanged notes on how each runs their program.
