NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade talk heats up before draft, Wizards weigh Peterson at No. 1, KAT extension looms

The Milwaukee Bucks' Giannis Antetokounmpo situation is accelerating toward a resolution before Tuesday's NBA Draft, with multiple contenders still in the mix and the clock ticking. The Miami Heat remain the frontrunner, though Milwaukee has not loved their offer, and Boston Celtics are involved to a degree that remains unclear. Per Zach Lowe, Milwaukee will want the draft pick included, which is part of why a pre-draft deal makes sense for all parties. The Athletic's Sam Amick said that if the Bucks asked for Jaylen Brown straight up for Giannis, he believes that deal gets done, though Boston's position is that Brown alone should be sufficient without additional sweeteners. Brian Windhorst said the whole league will change when Giannis is eventually moved.

Marc Stein reports the Detroit Pistons should be watched as a potential third-team facilitator in a Giannis-to-Miami deal, with Tyler Herro landing in Detroit as part of the framework. That would be a significant haul for the Pistons, who are hunting shooters and playmakers this offseason. Barry Jackson put the cost in perspective: if Detroit offers their No. 21 pick for Herro, Miami would effectively be trading at least six first-rounders for Giannis, including the No. 13 pick, two future firsts, and players like Jaime Jaquez and Kel'el Ware.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Washington Wizards may not take AJ Dybantsa with the No. 1 overall pick. Multiple draft experts told Marc Stein there is "increased consideration" for Kansas guard Darryn Peterson at the top spot. ESPN's big board notes Peterson dealt with cramping issues this season but NBA teams are not concerned about his long-term health, with executives comparing him stylistically to Devin Booker.
  • The Knicks' championship core may not be as locked in as it seems. ESPN's Bobby Marks flagged that Karl-Anthony Towns is eligible for a four-year, $272 million extension, and Brian Windhorst said both Towns and Josh Hart would each need to take a haircut of $7-10 million over multiple seasons for the current group to stay together for the next three to five years.
  • Agent Bernie Lee weighed in on Jalen Brunson's decision to take a pay cut to stay in New York, and his take was more cautionary than celebratory. "Once a player sacrifices money, he is handing a tool back to an organization to make decisions he does not control," Lee wrote, pointing to Dwyane Wade as a cautionary example: Wade sacrificed repeatedly, won championships, got a statue and a street, but the money never came back once his career ended.
  • San Antonio Spurs sources pushed back on any notion that De'Aaron Fox's rough NBA Finals performance changed anything internally. The team views Fox as their franchise point guard, describing him as a calming presence and closer who was essentially playing on one leg. His four-year, $229 million extension kicks in next season, ramping from $49.8 million up to $61.7 million.
  • Multiple big men are already angling to join the Spurs this offseason, according to ESPN. San Antonio is looking for a stretch big who can play alongside Victor Wembanyama in larger lineups, and the combination of the franchise's culture, youth and Wembanyama's presence makes it a preferred destination. Rui Hachimura was specifically mentioned as a type of player that fits what they are looking for.
  • The Celtics are not sitting still if the Giannis deal falls through. League sources told The Athletic that Boston is among the many teams interested in New Orleans Pelicans forward Trey Murphy III as a frontcourt upgrade. Windhorst separately said he believes there is "a very good chance" Brown remains a Celtic at the start of next season.
  • Marc Stein also reported that Zach LaVine is drawing trade interest from the Pistons, along with Charlotte's Coby White and Oklahoma City's Isaiah Joe. LaVine has a $49 million player option he must decide on by June 29, adding urgency to any potential deal. Separately, league sources say Detroit big man Isaiah Stewart is available, with two years and roughly $30 million left on his contract.
  • Pistons president Trajan Langdon said retaining center Jalen Duren is a priority this offseason. "I want him here. We really want JD to be here," Langdon said, adding that owner Tom Gores has never said no to resource requests from the organization.
  • Draft prospect Nate Ament named four pre-draft workouts on SportsCenter — the Bulls, Nets, Thunder and Clippers — and the Mavericks were not among them, despite Dallas holding the No. 9 pick and having been linked to interest in the 6-foot-9 Tennessee forward. Some rival executives view the Bucks at No. 10 as Ament's floor, with the Pelicans also reportedly looking to trade up for him.
  • The Clippers' Kawhi Leonard situation is more complicated than a straightforward extension. Tim MacMahon said any deal between Kawhi and Los Angeles will require genuine negotiations and a pay cut on Leonard's part. Kawhi took the league's reported minimum deal to re-sign last offseason and has leverage, but the Clippers' cap situation limits their flexibility.
  • Kentavious Caldwell-Pope is staying in Memphis. The guard exercised his $21.6 million player option for the 2026-27 season, per Shams Charania.
  • NBA Deputy Commissioner Mark Tatum said the league plans to name winning bidders for 12 permanent European teams within the next 60 to 90 days, with the league on track to debut in October 2027. The 12 cities include Rome, Milan, London, Paris, Madrid, Barcelona, Berlin and Istanbul, among others. NBA Europe teams could play North American franchises in the preseason initially, with deeper integration to follow.
  • Kendrick Perkins is moving into college basketball administration. The 14-year NBA veteran and 2008 champion agreed to become men's basketball GM at HBCU Jackson State, per Shams Charania, joining a growing list that includes John Wall at Howard, Stephen Curry at Davidson and Trae Young at Oklahoma.
  • New Bulls coach Tiago Splitter grew up a Bulls fan in Brazil, watching Michael Jordan games past midnight while hidden from his parents. "Super excited to be here," he said at his introductory news conference. Splitter said he is prioritizing the development of Matas Buzelis, adding that "nobody knows yet what is his ceiling."
