NBA Rumors Wrap: Harden returns to Cleveland on $97M deal, Lakers ownership picture shifts, Kuminga still unsigned

James Harden is staying in Cleveland. The veteran guard agreed to a three-year, $97 million contract to return to the Cavaliers, per Shams Charania, with a player option on the third year and a 15% trade kicker. The deal starts at around $30.5 million in Year 1, with raises to follow. The agreement closes out a long offseason saga: Harden had declined his $42.3 million player option in June, and negotiations dragged as Cleveland worked to structure the deal around apron constraints tied to acquiring Peyton Watson in a five-team trade. With the Harden signing now official, his career earnings will reach $511 million, making him the fourth player in NBA history to eclipse the $500 million mark, joining LeBron James, Kevin Durant and Stephen Curry.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Lakers' ownership picture got a little clearer Thursday, and a little more complicated. Minority owner Dr. Patrick Soon-Shiong is not selling his 4% stake in the team following Mark Walter's sale of his majority share to Bob Iger and Josh Kushner. That leaves Jeanie Buss in a bind: if her siblings sell their share, she would hold just under 3% of the team and would need to acquire at least 12% more to meet the NBA's 15% threshold required to remain acting governor. With Soon-Shiong's slice off the table, the only remaining minority owner she could target would be Ed Roski Jr., who holds roughly 3%.
  • Jonathan Kuminga remains unsigned, and the field around him is narrowing. The Cavaliers effectively removed themselves from contention by completing the Watson trade, and the Timberwolves were the other finalist. That leaves the Lakers as the most logical destination, though the two sides have been unable to reach an agreement all summer. Los Angeles initially offered Kuminga around $10 million annually alongside a starting role next to Luka Doncic.
  • Darius Garland said the transition to the Clippers was easier than expected, even if leaving Cleveland after seven-plus years still stung. "So once I got to LA, I was pretty happy. Got to be in a new city, like a real new revamp," Garland said. He told Ty Lue he wants to model part of his game after how Kyrie Irving played in the 2016 Finals, which Lue coached.
  • The Pistons' cap situation is getting complicated. Detroit needs to know what Jalen Duren's number will be on their books in 2028 and 2029 before they can finalize plans for Ausar Thompson's rookie-scale extension. The two decisions are linked, and the one they make first will signal something publicly about which player they value more.
  • The Knicks and Josh Hart are working toward an extension, with one league source telling The Athletic that a three-year deal in the $54-62 million range would satisfy both sides. The low end would pay Hart roughly $18-20 million annually, in line with what he is currently earning.
  • Shaquille O'Neal has doubts about the new-look 76ers. "It's not going to work in my opinion," Shaq said of Philadelphia's roster, raising concerns about too many lead voices between LeBron, Joel Embiid, Jaylen Brown and Tyrese Maxey. Separately, Shaq also weighed in on the Buss family's reported sale of their Lakers stake, saying: "from a business standpoint, for what they purchased it for to what they're selling it for... you get the golf clap."
  • Nikola Jokic nearly had a triple-double as Serbia beat France 90-87 in front of 20,000 fans in Belgrade, topping Victor Wembanyama's squad in a FIBA tune-up. Jokic finished with 20 points, 9 rebounds and 8 assists in 29 minutes. After the game, he said of Wembanyama: "He has the opportunity to be the most unique basketball player we've ever seen — to be the best ever." Jokic also said he was happy for Peyton Watson following the Watson trade to Cleveland: "He's a really good guy, brings great energy and is a positive presence in the locker room. We'll definitely miss him."
  • George Karl posted a pointed message about his former franchise, writing that "some franchises aren't capable of supporting their legends the right way" and that Jokic "was on a path to becoming one of the Top 10 players to ever play basketball. That wasn't realistic in Denver."
  • Matas Buzelis recalled a trash-talk exchange with Luka Doncic that turned into a genuine back-and-forth: Doncic "baked" him 12 possessions in a row, but Buzelis said he shrugged it off and finished with more points at halftime. "I have 20 at half and he has 22. So I'm chilling." Buzelis also said his ultimate goal with Chicago is simple: "I want to bring them a championship… I can't imagine playing anywhere else, man."
  • Chet Holmgren said he has been in the gym all summer after the Thunder's early playoff exit. "I've been on the court all summer. I'm excited to get back to OKC with the guys and get back to work." When asked to name the player he would most want as a teammate from any era, Holmgren offered a starting five: Michael Jordan, Kobe Bryant, OKC-era Kevin Durant and 2011 Dirk Nowitzki.
  • Jae Crowder has signed a one-year deal with LDLC ASVEL Villeurbanne in France for the 2026-27 season. ASVEL is managed by four-time NBA champion Tony Parker, who will serve as head coach for the first time at the club level.
  • Malique Lewis is nearing a deal with the Cairns Taipans in the NBL, with discussions in advanced stages. Lewis would fill the team's final import roster spot.
  • Portland's Casey Holdahl floated that if Cam Whitmore is waived, he would be shocked if the Blazers don't try to bring him into camp.
  • Anthony Edwards has been ordered to pay $14,000 a month in child support for a son he has acknowledged never meeting in person. A judge also ordered Edwards to take out a $2.6 million life insurance policy naming the child's mother as beneficiary and to cover her legal fees of more than $21,000.
