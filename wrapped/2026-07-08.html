NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's free agency drags toward Summer League, Jalen Brunson needs wrist surgery, massive six-team trade reshapes the East

LeBron James' free agency is showing no signs of a quick resolution, and the league is bracing for the wait to stretch well into next week. Per Shams Charania, Cleveland, Miami and Philadelphia are the current top three destinations, with the Cavaliers generating the strongest vibes. Brian Windhorst said on ESPN that "the vibes are pointing towards Cleveland," while also reporting that LeBron already knows his decision. The 76ers entered the picture more seriously after landing Jaylen Brown, with Charania saying LeBron is "taking their pitch very seriously." According to Marc Stein, the LeBron Watch is now expected to bleed into Las Vegas Summer League, which tips off Thursday and runs through July 19.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Six teams struck an elaborate deal Tuesday involving the Mavericks, Wizards, Clippers, Pistons, Bucks and Grizzlies. The centerpiece is Khris Middleton agreeing to a three-year, $17.6 million deal with Washington via sign-and-trade, returning to D.C. where he spent parts of the last two seasons. Also moving in the deal: Caris LeVert and two second-round picks go from Detroit to Milwaukee for Taurean Prince and Gary Harris, Isaiah Stewart goes to Memphis, Santi Aldama goes to Dallas, and the Wizards ship D'Angelo Russell to Memphis along with two future second-round picks.
  • • The Mavericks come out of the deal with Aldama and Marcus Sasser without touching their $20.3 million trade exception from the Anthony Davis trade or their $15 million MLE. That preservation matters: Dallas can now formally sign Croatian forward Tarik Biberovic to a two-year, $6 million deal once the six-team trade is official.
  • • Biberovic has informed Fenerbahçe he is leaving for the NBA, ending an eight-season run in Turkey. His contract carried a $2 million buyout clause, though under NBA rules Dallas can only contribute up to $900,000 toward it. Per HoopsHype, the two sides have worked through that gap and Fenerbahçe is expected to receive the full buyout amount once the Aldama trade formally closes.
  • • Donovan Mitchell signed a four-year extension with the Cavaliers on the first day he was eligible, locking in $272.8 million rather than waiting a year for a potential five-year, $353 million deal. The contract pays him $60.9 million in Year 1, rising to $75.5 million in Year 4. Mitchell would also welcome LeBron joining Cleveland if he chooses to return.
  • • The Lakers remain in active pursuit of free agent wing Jonathan Kuminga, but have not given him an offer compelling enough to commit. Rob Pelinka has been subtly improving his offer while pitching a starting role next to Luka Doncic, but Kuminga and his agent Aaron Turner believe better deals are still available. The Cavaliers also have interest, with coach Kenny Atkinson having worked with Kuminga in Golden State, though Cleveland's focus on the LeBron sweepstakes has left the Lakers as his most aggressive pursuer.
  • • The Lakers added veteran center Kevon Looney on a one-year, $3.9 million deal. The three-time champion gives Doncic and Austin Reaves an experienced pick-and-roll big after years of running screens for Stephen Curry's Warriors teams.
  • • Jalen Brunson, the Knicks' Finals MVP, is having surgery on his left wrist and is expected to return to basketball activities later this summer. Brunson played through the injury during New York's championship run, and the recovery timeline is at least two months.
  • • The Warriors' pursuit of LeBron may hinge on a move they haven't made yet. Charania reports Golden State believes they are not at the top of LeBron's list unless they can package Anthony Davis. Meanwhile, Draymond Green and LeBron are spending time together this week. Marc Stein reached LeBron's agent Rich Paul, who said: "There's an argument for several teams but ultimately the decision will be LeBron's and whatever he feels he should do."
  • • The Heat's LeBron pitch has an unexpected ally: Giannis Antetokounmpo would want LeBron in Miami, per Charania. Miami is also keeping an eye on other free agents, having had initial conversations with Bradley Beal, though they have not been aggressive in that pursuit and had not contacted DeMar DeRozan as of Monday.
  • • Windhorst also reported that James Harden is "embracing the concept" of teaming with LeBron in Cleveland, and that Harden could sign a three-year deal with the Cavs. Six teams, including the Heat, Warriors, Cavaliers, 76ers, Timberwolves and Nuggets, are all watching every dollar to maintain maximum flexibility for LeBron.
  • • Trae Young has a 7.5% trade kicker in his four-year, $212 million deal with the Wizards, along with a fourth-year player option. The kicker means any future trade would add to his salary, making deals involving him more expensive for acquiring teams.
  • • Shams Charania revealed that Jayson Tatum and Jaylen Brown's personal relationship off the court was "as close to non-existent as you could have" over the last year of their time together in Boston. "It is a tale of two different people," Charania said.
  • • Kyle Lowry signed a one-day contract with the Toronto Raptors and announced his retirement after a 20-year NBA career.
  • • Guerschon Yabusele is heading back to Europe, agreeing to a three-year deal with Panathinaikos that will make him one of the three highest-paid European players. The forward averaged 8.3 points over two NBA seasons split between Philadelphia, Chicago and New York.
  • • Tyler Herro's departure from Miami came with some friction. Before being traded to Milwaukee, Herro posted a graphic on Instagram of the league's worst midrange shooters, listing Giannis as the worst at 26.8% and Bam Adebayo as the 10th worst at 35.9%. A comment attributed to Herro also questioned whether Adebayo deserved $60 million, though the Herald noted Adebayo has never been paid that figure, with his extension topping out at a $57.4 million player option in 2028-29.
  • • Lamar Odom is expected to enter a no-contest plea in his Las Vegas DUI case, which will reduce the charge to reckless driving. Odom, 46, was arrested in January and had pleaded not guilty in March. The court hearing was continued for a plea entry later this month.
  • • The Spurs will be able to play up to four regular-season home games away from the Frost Bank Center in each of the next two seasons, with games in Austin and Europe among the likely options, after Bexar County commissioners signed off on the arrangement.
