NBA Rumors Wrap: Duren signs $200M extension with Pistons, Luka custody battle escalates, Vegas expansion timeline slips

Jalen Duren and the Detroit Pistons agreed to a five-year, $200 million extension after months of acrimony that included a short-lived weight clause, below-market offers and a puzzling lack of communication between Duren's camp and team president Trajan Langdon. The deal declines in year two to $36.95 million before rising each season through 2030-31, a structure that reflects the complicated negotiations. What ultimately helped bridge the gap, per The Athletic, was Pistons owner Tom Gores personally reaching out to Duren on the final day. Gores had already gotten face time with Duren at Cade Cunningham's birthday party in Los Angeles, and the direct communication from ownership carried weight that front-office channels apparently hadn't. "That's our guy and he's been our guy," coach JB Bickerstaff said. "We are happy to have him back."

The road there was bumpy. The Sacramento Kings had made clear in their restricted free agency meeting that they viewed Duren as a max player, and the Milwaukee Bucks were also known to have serious interest, meaning Duren had real leverage to walk on a qualifying offer and test unrestricted free agency next summer. Detroit's decision to include a weight clause in one of its offers only deepened the rift, with league sources telling The Athletic that Duren was caught off guard by the proposal given he had never had weight issues and had played at least 60 games in each of his NBA seasons. The Pistons removed the clause, and Gores' personal outreach on decision day ultimately closed it out.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Luka Doncic has filed a Hague Convention petition seeking access to his daughters, who remain in Slovenia with his fiancée. Doncic had set a Sept. 25 deadline to resolve the situation, but it passed without agreement from her or the Slovenian courts. He returned to the U.S. for Lakers training camp with no guarantees of when he will see either daughter during the NBA season. "Nothing is more important to me than my daughters," Doncic said in a statement. "I will do whatever it takes to make sure they have both parents present in their lives."
  • A possible Las Vegas expansion franchise could debut in 2029-30 rather than 2028-29, sources told The Athletic, though it remains a discussion rather than a decision. Three groups are bidding on Las Vegas: Nancy Walton Laurie and Bill Laurie; Steve Apostolopoulos and Marc Lasry; and Bill Foley and Jerry Colangelo, with a vote on the winner expected soon. Runner-up groups could then pivot to Seattle, where Kraken owner Samantha Holloway's group appears to be the frontrunner at a valuation sources currently estimate around $6 billion.
  • Portland Trail Blazers owner Tom Dundon is using the expansion process as leverage in a standoff with the city of Portland over renovating the Moda Center, according to multiple ownership and industry sources. Commissioner Adam Silver has publicly called relocating the Blazers "a failure," but Dundon's threat is real as long as Seattle and Vegas remain viable NBA markets. That said, expansion is considered far more likely than relocation.
  • The Blazers had a rough media day off the court: the team hired television broadcaster Braiden Bell as its new play-by-play announcer and fired him the same day after past social media posts surfaced. KGW-TV reported the posts were made when Bell was a teenager in 2012 and 2013. Bell issued an apology, saying the tweets "have no reflection of who I am today."
  • The Blazers' starting lineup is also unsettled heading into the season. Coach Micah Nori said before being hired that Damian Lillard would start if healthy, but analytically the better option may be to start Jrue Holiday and bring either Lillard or Ja Morant off the bench, given both stars are undersized and have never excelled defensively. Asking an All-Star to come off the bench is a difficult ask for any coach, let alone a first-year coach on a one-year contract. Morant said winning is what matters: "If I'm starting, if I'm not, it's the same thing."
  • Washington Wizards guard Bub Carrington acknowledged in plain terms that Washington spent the last two seasons tanking, saying he and his teammates had been "held back" during his first two seasons as the team posted records of 18-64 and worse to chase lottery odds. The NBA is not expected to discipline Carrington or the team for the comments, per a league source.
  • Jimmy Butler is working hard on his ACL rehab in Hawaii with the Golden State Warriors, with his personal trainer Armando Rivas putting him through extra workouts between practices. Kerr got a glimpse of vintage Butler when a rookie called Butler's number for a free throw drill and Butler immediately said, "Hell yeah, I'll shoot those free throws!" — then knocked both down. "He loves playing, he loves competing, he loves talking trash," Kerr said. "He's such a gamer."
  • Kerr also said rookie Yaxel Lendeborg has already earned a major rotation role: "Summer league or pickup games or now, he's one of our best players already." Brandin Podziemski was the standout of a recent scrimmage, looking like a player ready to take another step in year four as he awaits his second contract. "Three days in, he's had a great camp," Kerr said.
  • Four New York Knicks are eligible for extensions: Karl-Anthony Towns, Miles McBride, Josh Hart and Tyrese Kolek. Hart said Monday he does not expect a deal to get done, and league sources told The Athletic that McBride is not close to receiving one either.
  • The Memphis Grizzlies and guard Jordan Hawkins have mutually agreed to part ways, per Shams Charania, allowing the 2023 lottery pick to find a new home once he clears waivers. Meanwhile, guard Ty Jerome has generated buzz throughout training camp and positioned himself as a strong candidate to be the team's starting point guard.
  • Zach Edey has looked "really good" at Grizzlies training camp and is moving better than at any point in his first two seasons, coach Tuomas Iisalo said. Edey participated in 5-on-5 drills every day, and whether he plays in the preseason depends on how his body responds. "The regular season is always the goal," Edey said. "Preseason would be cool, but I'm taking it day by day right now."
  • Domantas Sabonis said he tuned out trade rumors this summer by leaning on his relationship with Kings GM Scott Perry. "I can't control any of it," Sabonis said. "The injury helped. I was just focused on getting healthy." Asked specifically about the noise: "It's all made up," he said.
  • LeBron James has made an early impression on Philadelphia 76ers coach Nick Nurse with his training habits. "He's a very early guy, so he's in here very early, and he's got his set, straight to the weight room when he gets in," Nurse said. "I think he's obviously in great condition." Meanwhile, Joel Embiid is drawing rave reviews internally, with teammate Justin Edwards saying Embiid is "really happy" and "one of the most funny people on the team."
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo, now with the Miami Heat, reflected on adapting his game: "I've known how to win a specific way and maybe that way that I had known how to win is old. I might need to do something different but at the end of the day I know that I'm willing to do that."
  • The Lakers named Derrin Horton as their new public address announcer, stepping into the role held by Lawrence Tanter, who died last week. "There's no way you can fill his shoes," Horton said. "You just come here with a passion and a goal to be informative and entertaining and be a part of Showtime."
  • "Inside the NBA" will air on ESPN or ABC on 25 nights in the 2026-27 regular season, five more than its first season with Disney following the split from Turner Sports, including seven appearances before Christmas.
