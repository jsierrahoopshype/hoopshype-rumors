NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard commits to Toronto long-term, Hawks ship Hield and Nembhard for Finney-Smith, Kyrie Irving trade talk heats up

Kawhi Leonard is staying in Toronto for the long haul. The Raptors and their All-NBA forward agreed on a two-year, $115 million contract extension, plus a player option for the 2028-29 season, per Shams Charania. Leonard, 35, now has three years and $165 million total on his Raptors deal after the sides maintained consistent dialogue since July, when he returned to Toronto via trade. To make the deal work, Leonard took $11 million less than his maximum extension number and had already waived a $7.45 million kicker to accommodate his trade from the Clippers, all to give the Raptors flexibility as their salary books rise. His agent Harrison Gaines of SLASH Sports confirmed the deal to ESPN, and Charania reports that Leonard fully expects to retire as a Raptor.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Atlanta Hawks are trading Buddy Hield, Ryan Nembhard and cash to the Charlotte Hornets for Dorian Finney-Smith. Atlanta takes on roughly $2.4 million in salary with the swap and will sit about $1.4 million under the luxury tax with 15 players on standard contracts. Charlotte, meanwhile, will have 18 players on standard deals after the trade, meaning the Hornets will need to cut three players.
  • • With Nembhard now headed to Charlotte as part of the deal, Marc Stein had reported earlier in the day that if the guard were waived, he'd draw multiple two-way suitors, with the Nuggets among the most interested. Nembhard had a 28-point, 10-assist game in Denver as a rookie and a 23-assist game against Chicago in the season finale, so the interest is easy to understand.
  • • People around the league are quietly monitoring the fit between Kyrie Irving and the Dallas Mavericks heading into the trade deadline. The key question being asked is how long Irving remains the team's veteran leader and whether it would make sense for both sides to find him a contender. That said, Irving's relationship with Mavericks president Masai Ujiri is described as strong enough that, if a split came, it would be handled amicably.
  • • The Detroit Pistons signed forward Ausar Thompson to a contract extension. No terms were announced. The Pistons also signed guard Jordan Riley to a training camp deal and waived guard Drake Allen.
  • • Pistons executive vice president of basketball operations Michael Blackstone is speaking publicly about his Stage 4 lung cancer diagnosis, saying he wants to encourage people to take aches and pains seriously rather than push them aside. What started as a pain in his right shoulder turned out to be cancer that had metastasized from his lung to his shoulder and spine. Blackstone, who has never smoked, says his love of basketball and the city of Detroit has carried him through radiation treatments, and he plans to keep working.
  • • Rockets GM Rafael Stone offered an optimistic read on Kevin Durant's fit in Houston heading into his player-option year. "His fit's been really good. It feels like he's really happy to be here, he's been a good teammate. He just wants to hoop, which fits great with our culture," Stone said. "We'll definitely sit down and talk at the end of this year." Durant himself was direct when told that Stone and coach Ime Udoka have discussed reducing his minutes this season: "Why? I'm better on the court. The team is better when I'm on the court."
  • • Marcus Smart had a candid take on what it's like to finally be on Durant's team rather than guarding him. "I get to be on the other side of him taking the shots and not taking the shots over me," Smart said. "His ability to make shots, it doesn't matter who's there. And it also doesn't help that he's close to seven foot, and I'm close to six foot."
  • • Tyrese Maxey said he had multiple conversations with LeBron James before LeBron signed with the 76ers, describing James as "like a big brother, like an OG to me." Maxey said the two worked out together early in the mornings and that, when a kid asked him recently whether he and LeBron were friends, he leaned back and said, "I guess we are."
  • • The Miami Heat officially signed guard Pelle Larsson to a contract extension. The deal includes a mutual option for a fourth year that both Larsson and the Heat would almost certainly pick up, making it effectively a four-year commitment barring an extension at a higher figure. Miami also claimed guard Bez Mbeng off waivers from Charlotte, bringing their training camp roster to 19.
  • • Veteran forward James Johnson attended the Pacers' players-only minicamp in Nashville and is signing an Exhibit 9 deal to join training camp. Indiana's training camp roster is taking shape ahead of the season.
  • • Alperen Sengun is eager to work with new Rockets assistant and shot guru Chip Engelland this season. "I feel like there's nothing wrong with my shot, it's all in my head," Sengun said. "That's what I want to get in and improve with him."
  • • Christian Koloko, who spent time with the Raptors and Pelicans, has shared his medical journey publicly through Cedars-Sinai. A blood clot was found in his lungs during his rookie season, but doctors later discovered the underlying cause was a narrowing of a vein in his left arm, which had grown three centimeters larger in diameter than his right arm. Surgery at Cedars-Sinai addressed the root issue.
  • • Tickets for the NBA's first-ever regular season game in Manchester are sold out. The Pelicans and Spurs will play at the Co-Op Live arena in 2027 as part of the league's European expansion.
  • • Doc Rivers says he is done coaching for good, announcing he will join NBC, Peacock and NBCSN as an analyst this season. "I honestly can say I'm absolutely retired," Rivers told the Associated Press. "I had a long run. It ended up in the Hall of Fame and I am so thankful."
  • • Zion Williamson has filed a legal response to a civil lawsuit in which a woman accuses him of rape, issuing a general denial of all allegations and describing their relationship as a consensual "friends with benefits" arrangement. Williamson says their interactions were "pleasant and consensual" and that there was no assault or sexual battery.
  • • Isaiah Hartenstein and the Hartenstein Foundation donated $22,101.54 to Positive Tomorrows, Oklahoma's only elementary school and social service agency for children and families experiencing homelessness, during a surprise visit to the school's after-school basketball program.
  • • Steven Adams has a warning for the league: GTA VI drops in November. "You guys should anticipate November. The performance of the league, league wide. GTA is dropping. So look at NFL, look at all the leagues and watch them plummet, mate. Just for the month of November, performance is gonna go down."
  • • The passing of Chris Spatola, ESPN analyst and son-in-law of legendary Duke coach Mike Krzyzewski, at age 47 drew a heartfelt statement from the Krzyzewski family: "There are no words to adequately express our heartbreak over the loss of our beloved Chris Spatola." Spatola served on Krzyzewski's coaching staff at Duke from 2007 to 2012 before transitioning to broadcasting.
