NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis lands in Miami, LeBron's camp goes quiet, Jokic pledges Denver loyalty

The Miami Heat officially acquired Giannis Antetokounmpo from the Milwaukee Bucks on Monday, completing one of the most significant trades in recent NBA history per Shams Charania. Miami sent Tyler Herro, Kasparas Jakucionis, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel'el Ware, three first-round picks (2026, 2031, 2033), a 2030 first-round swap, and a 2033 second-round pick to Milwaukee. The Bucks also sent Bobby Portis Jr. to Miami as part of the package. Heat president Pat Riley called it "one of the great trades in HEAT history," saying Giannis is "one of the top five players in the league." Giannis, visibly emotional after learning of the deal, said: "I'm grateful for the Bucks. The fans. This is a tough moment for me right now but we'll get through it."

With Giannis now in South Beach, the Heat's front office has some roster decisions to make. Veterans such as DeMar DeRozan, Bradley Beal, and Khris Middleton could all be on hold until LeBron James makes his free agency decision, per Jake Fischer. Andrew Wiggins' contract extension is now official in Miami, with Riley calling him "the quintessential small forward who fits perfectly with this roster." As for extension options for Giannis himself, he can sign a three-year, $193 million deal as of October 1 if his 2027-28 player option is declined, or a four-year, $275 million deal starting January 6, per the contract structure outlined by Bobby Marks.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • LeBron James is keeping teams in the dark as free agency opens. His agent Rich Paul is handling all background conversations with teams and relaying information back to LeBron, who has no plans to meet with any team directly, per Chris Haynes. ESPN's Brian Windhorst said teams in the sweepstakes haven't heard from LeBron or his camp, and when he asked people in Philadelphia their read on the situation, the recurring answer was: "We're afraid it's Cleveland."
  • • Nikola Jokic is not signing an extension with the Nuggets this summer, and Denver knew it was coming. Jokic wants to wait until next summer so he can add another year of max money, worth approximately $80 million, to his deal. Signing next summer would push his contract through 2032 rather than 2031. Jokic was direct about his intentions: "My idea is to sign next summer and stay with Denver for the rest of my career."
  • • The Celtics' Brad Stevens addressed the Jaylen Brown trade at a press conference Monday. Stevens said the deal was driven by perceived salary sheet constraints and that the draft capital, including a 2028 pick with complicated stipulations, represented "potential premium assets." He admitted he could be wrong about the move, but said it kept the team competitive while adding flexibility. Stevens also said he hopes his relationship with Brown "is either intact or can be mended" after Brown publicly said he felt disrespected by the process.
  • • Celtics owner Bill Chisholm pushed back on the idea that money was behind the Brown trade. "I know people feel like there must be a smoking gun somewhere around the money," Chisholm said. "That is just not what this is about." He added: "I know we have to prove it, and we will."
  • • Paul George waived his $3.9 million trade bonus as part of the deal to Boston. His cap hit remains at $54.1 million this season and $56.6 million next year on a partial guarantee.
  • • The Kings released DeMar DeRozan on Monday after both sides explored trade options without finding a match. The Lakers are not considered a potential landing spot for the six-time All-Star despite DeRozan's Southern California roots. The Warriors have been told he is a player they will look into, particularly if they miss out on LeBron, and the Heat, Clippers, and Raptors have also been mentioned as fits.
  • • Rogers Communications signed a $4.35 billion agreement to become the 100 percent owner of Maple Leaf Sports and Entertainment, which owns the Raptors and the Toronto Maple Leafs. Rogers previously held a 75 percent stake in MLSE after buying out Bell's share in 2024. The deal, which buys out Larry Tanenbaum's remaining 25 percent via his Kilmer Sports Inc., is subject to league approval and expected to close by end of 2026.
  • • The Knicks signed Andre Drummond and re-signed Jose Alvarado, with team president Leon Rose saying Alvarado "brought a toughness, competitiveness, and an unwavering commitment to winning." Landry Shamet's new deal in New York is guaranteed for $14.5 million over four years, with a player option on the final season giving both sides flexibility heading into the 2029 offseason.
  • • The Nets brought back Day'Ron Sharpe on a two-year deal that contains a $10 million team option for the 2027-28 season, per HoopsHype. Both Sharpe and Josh Minott waived their implied no-trade clauses in their new contracts with Brooklyn.
  • • The Celtics signed Ron Harper Jr. to a four-year, $13.7 million deal, with the first two years fully guaranteed and a non-guaranteed team option in Year 4.
  • • Former college basketball player Kerr Kriisa, who played six college seasons at Arizona, Kentucky, West Virginia, and Cincinnati, has been arrested and indicted on a $2.2 million fraud scheme by the Department of Justice. According to the DOJ, Kriisa allegedly ran a four-year scheme that included convincing a victim to send money by falsely claiming his mother had cancer, posing as his mother to solicit funds to "save" a family farm, and impersonating a woman named "Irene" to sign a fraudulent repayment promise.
  • • The Hornets signed forward Michael Ajayi and guard Kylan Boswell to two-way contracts, waiving two-way forward Tosan Evbuomwan in a corresponding move. Summer league coach Blaine Mueller said rookie duo Hannes Steinbach and Christian Anderson Jr. have shown "really mature offensive games," adding that Anderson's ability to create and Steinbach's feel for playmaking have both stood out early.
  • • Memphis extended a three-year, $30 million offer sheet to Quinten Post, but only the first season is guaranteed. The deal contains roughly $1.3 million in unlikely incentives each year tied to All-Defense Team honors, which would count against the Warriors' apron if Post were to earn them.
  • • Jay Triano has joined the coaching staff of the Trail Blazers under head coach Micah Nori, reuniting with Damian Lillard. The Wizards also announced their full coaching staff, with Patrick Ewing joining as an assistant and Steve Clifford coming on as a coaching advisor.
  • • Suns rookie Koa Peat set a specific goal heading into summer league: lead the field in offensive rebounding. "Just trying to be a menace on the glass," Peat said at his first media availability since draft night.
