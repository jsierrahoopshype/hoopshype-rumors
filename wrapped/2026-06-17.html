NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade talks heat up, Jazz torn at No. 2, SGA lands Nike deal

The Milwaukee Bucks are not sold on what the Miami Heat have put on the table for Giannis Antetokounmpo, and are continuing to shop his rights around the league, per Brian Windhorst. Miami's best offer is a package headlined by the No. 13 pick and rotation players including Wisconsin native Tyler Herro, but the Bucks remain unimpressed and are listening to other teams. Boston has also emerged as a suitor, with Windhorst reporting that Giannis would be willing to sign an extension with the Celtics. The catch is that Boston would likely need to include Jaylen Brown in a three-team structure, and multiple reporters are cautioning the deal is far from real. "I think the Celtics are going to be very, very cautious about making any firm offer," Windhorst said. "I will be very careful about saying anything has truly been offered."

The Brown angle carries real risk for Boston. As ESPN's Bobby Marks said on air, "You ought to be awfully careful if something does not materialize" with Brown — meaning if he learns he was offered in a trade that fell apart, the Celtics could face a disgruntled star. On the three-team scenario specifically, Marc Spears poured cold water on any Clippers involvement, saying he has not heard of any conversations between Boston and Los Angeles about routing Brown through a deal. Ramona Shelburne added that most teams around the league view Brown's availability as speculation and leverage, not a real offer: "All this is, is leverage though. All this is, a moment of maximum leverage."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Utah Jazz are "genuinely torn" between AJ Dybantsa, Cameron Boozer and Darryn Peterson with one week until the draft, per The Athletic. Peterson canceled his Utah workout this week, signaling he expects to go No. 1 to the Wizards, though Utah says it was not caught off guard and won't hesitate to take him if he grades out as the best player. There is also behind-the-scenes concern in Peterson's camp that he and Jazz guard Keyonte George share the same agency, though the Jazz say they believe the two can play together fine.
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander has officially signed with Nike as a signature athlete, moving from Converse back to the parent brand after back-to-back MVP seasons. Nike called him "one of the most creative leaders in the game." It is a rare move inside the same corporate family — only a handful of players have shifted between Nike subsidiaries in recent memory, including Tatum going from Nike to Jordan in 2019 and Wade moving from Converse to Jordan in 2009.
  • OG Anunoby is getting his own signature shoe with Skechers. Brand president Michael Greenberg confirmed the shoe is in the works, telling reporters "It's too early to share specific details, but we do have a signature shoe in the works." Anunoby filed a trademark request with the US Patent Office for a personal logo earlier this year.
  • Kyrie Irving appears increasingly likely to stay in Dallas, per Jon Krawczynski. Over the last week to 10 days, more signs have pointed to the Mavericks wanting Kyrie to play alongside Cooper Flagg and to Kyrie genuinely wanting to remain in Dallas, where he has found comfort.
  • Trae Young's market may get complicated. He was initially projected to sign an extension with the Wizards in the $40 million-per-year range after a warm reception from the organization and fanbase, but Marc Stein reports external interest could surface and shake up his market depending on how draft-related trades play out over the next week.
  • Wolves assistant Micah Nori is a top candidate for both open NBA head coaching jobs, per Marc Stein. Nori is a confirmed finalist in Portland alongside Celtics assistant Tyler Lashbrook and is also among Dallas' top targets as the Mavs search for Jason Kidd's replacement. Other names in the Dallas mix include Houston assistant Royal Ivey and veteran coach Terry Stotts. Meanwhile, Tiago Splitter has left Portland to take the Chicago job.
  • LeBron James and the Lakers are actively negotiating a new deal, with Windhorst saying he believes the two sides "are going back and forth" right now. Many rival teams expect LeBron to return at a reduced salary, which would give Los Angeles room to add another piece, though his willingness to lower his number may depend on what the team can deliver with the savings.
  • The Spurs have the financial flexibility to be active in free agency and could pursue a stretch four to complement Victor Wembanyama. ESPN's Bobby Marks singled out Laker Rui Hachimura as a logical target, saying "When you look at a stretch 4, a player like Rui Hachimura from the Lakers, you pair him up with a big who can shoot."
  • The NBA Finals between the Knicks and Spurs averaged 20.6 million viewers per game on ABC and ESPN, the most-watched Finals in 28 years. Game 5's series-clinching win peaked at 33 million viewers. The league also had its most-watched postseason in 28 years across all broadcast partners.
  • New York City will hold a ticker-tape parade for the champion Knicks on Thursday, with Mayor Zohran Mamdani making 600 free tickets available for fans to attend a ceremony at City Hall plaza following the Canyon of Heroes route. Jalen Brunson, meanwhile, delivered on a promise he made to Jimmy Fallon in 2024, bringing the trophy to The Tonight Show after telling the host he would return with it once the Knicks won a championship.
  • Mikal Bridges said on Instagram Live that Becky Hammon's past comments about Brunson's limitations as a No. 1 only motivated the point guard further. "I appreciate (Becky Hammon), no cap," Bridges said. "All that did is fuel that n****, bro. He know what she said." On that note, Isiah Thomas told FanDuel's Run It Back that Brunson's title run stands apart from those of Stephen Curry because of his two-way offensive impact. "Brunson and I, we did it, scoring and assisting. That is something totally different," Thomas said.
  • Luka Doncic, speaking in Madrid, said the expectations at the Lakers feel exactly like what he experienced at Real Madrid. "If you don't win the championship, it's a bad season," he said. Doncic also congratulated Brunson on the title and said the two have "both improved a lot" since their time as teammates in Dallas.
  • The NBA and the Hornets told federal prosecutors they want Terry Rozier to remain prohibited from contacting current and former Charlotte players while he is out on bond awaiting trial on four charges tied to an alleged NBA gambling and insider information investigation.
  • Paul Pierce reached a plea deal on his DUI case in Los Angeles, pleading nolo contendere to one count of wet reckless driving. He was sentenced to 12 months of probation and a $375 fine.
  • A tragedy has struck the personal life of Tyrese Haliburton. A 26-year-old woman named Makenzi Kern died unexpectedly from health complications during a bachelorette party in St. Barts hosted by Haliburton's fiancée, Jade Jones. The family does not suspect foul play, drugs, or alcohol contributed to her death.
