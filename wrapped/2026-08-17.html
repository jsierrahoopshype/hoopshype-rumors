NBA Rumors Wrap: Celtics add Paul George, Mitchell Robinson, Timberwolves' Edwards backs new-look backcourt, Lakers sale update

Brad Stevens addressed the Jaylen Brown trade this weekend, offering the clearest public window yet into how the Boston Celtics front office weighed one of the most significant roster moves in franchise history. "It's something that you have to just say, do you think where we currently are…will get us to where we ultimately want to go?" Stevens said. "If not, does it make sense to explore other pathways, even if that could be very tough in the near term?" The comments came as Boston welcomed two of the players who arrived in the retooled roster. Paul George visited the Celtics facility for the first time and told the crowd, "I'm gonna give y'all everything… It's surreal. I'm ecstatic. I'm looking forward to the journey. I'm looking forward to wearing that green and representing for Boston." Mitchell Robinson also checked in with Boston fans, saying he's long enjoyed visiting the city and has one priority on arrival: "I heard the lobster here is A1, so I definitely gotta try that."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The ongoing legal pressure around Lakers owner Mark Walter is coming into sharper focus. Federal prosecutors and the SEC are zeroing in on four entities they believe served as intermediaries between insurance companies Walter controlled and businesses he also controlled, including ABS Capital, Amistad Financial, Bradford Allen, and Hudson Trading. The Los Angeles Times reported that neither Walter nor his companies have been charged with any crimes, and a spokesperson previously said they are cooperating with authorities and expect the matter to be resolved "favorably." Separately, the Wall Street Journal noted that selling the Lakers freed up collateral Walter had pledged when financing the original team purchase, which could accelerate an insurance remediation effort that was not originally part of the compliance plan.
  • With the sale in limbo, ESPN's Ramona Shelburne told Stephen A. Smith that the "Dodgerizing" of the Lakers is still underway. "All of the brains and all of the apparatus that had been installed with the Dodgers to make them the most successful franchise in baseball the last few years was being implemented with the Lakers… Until this goes through, that's still going on, right?"
  • Anthony Edwards did not hold back when asked about the Timberwolves' chances against their biggest Western Conference obstacles. "Against those two teams, I think we're going to beat them," Edwards told ESPN during a visit to Manila, referring to the Thunder and Spurs. He tempered the championship talk for now, though: "Right now, I don't think we're thinking about winning the championship. It's step by step. We got LaMelo, a new player, and a bunch of new pieces. We are just trying to build it together."
  • Isaiah Hartenstein opened up about re-signing with Oklahoma City despite being worth more on the open market. "I really wanted to stay with this team for the long term and continue playing for a title contender," he said, adding that the second apron made the negotiation complicated: "It's not like the team doesn't want to pay you. But when you're in the second apron, it's no longer just a financial question; you're also severely restricted in trades." He also addressed the Game 7 loss to the Spurs, saying Oklahoma City played without two of its top scorers and declined to make excuses: "They deserved to win."
  • Hartenstein also returned to Germany's national team after more than eight years away from the program. The 28-year-old last wore the national team jersey in February 2018 and said he had been trying to return for the past two years.
  • Tyrese Haliburton recalled the exact moment he tore his Achilles in Game 7 against Oklahoma City: "I don't know if I'll ever get over it." He said the team had privately known the calf strain he was playing through made a rupture possible, which is why criticism from outside voices telling him to sit out bothered him. "I knew it was a possibility," he said. "In that moment, everyone talks about how it feels like you got kicked. I knew right away."
  • On a lighter note, Haliburton also revisited the night he was traded to Indiana. He had both agents on the phone, and then somehow Adrian Wojnarowski was also on the call. "I said, Indiana for who? Cause I didn't even know. And they were like, Sabonis. And I was like, oh, word."
  • The 76ers have hired Tommy Balcetis as Head of Basketball Intelligence, Analytics and Innovation, per HoopsHype. Balcetis spent 12 years with the Nuggets, most recently as an assistant general manager.
  • Cade Cunningham was asked whether he tried to recruit LeBron James to Detroit before LeBron signed with Philadelphia. "I didn't give LeBron a call," Cunningham said. "I don't think he would have cared to hear what I had to say about it. He's doing what he wants to do at this point. I'm not mad at that."
  • Rajon Rondo was bullish on LeBron's fit in Philadelphia, saying other suitors like Golden State were too old and Miami lacked enough shooting. "If you give him these type of pieces to play with, I am an early bet with them being the favorites to win in my book."
  • Dillon Brooks said he knew he wanted to stay in Phoenix long-term before a hand injury interrupted his season. "I don't want to be anywhere else," he said. Warriors rookie Yaxel Lendeborg already has an eye on one matchup this season. When asked about facing Brooks, the 23-year-old said: "I'm finna give Dillon Brooks belt, man. Stop playing, bruh."
  • Knicks star Karl-Anthony Towns married Jordyn Woods on Saturday in a black-and-white themed ceremony in Malibu. The ceremony was attended by Kylie Jenner, Timothée Chalamet, and Justin and Hailey Bieber, among others.
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo, currently in Paris for the Esports World Cup, said he wants to play chess against Victor Wembanyama, who shares the interest. "I would love to, actually," Giannis said. "Maybe after this, I'm going to reach out to him."
  • Partizan Belgrade have officially signed former NBA guard Ethan Thompson to a two-year deal, making him the eighth addition to the club this summer.
  • Dallas Mavericks GM Mike Schmitz gave an encouraging update on Dereck Lively II's recovery, saying the young center is "in the facility constantly, completely locked into everything we're doing." Schmitz added: "He's doing everything in his power, and we're just really proud of the progress he's made and continuing to chip away at everything as we get closer to the start of camp."
