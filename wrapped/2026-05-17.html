NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's uncertain future, Wolves frontcourt questions loom, Spurs-Wolves sportsmanship debate

LeBron James' future remains the league's biggest offseason question, and Marc Stein laid out the landscape Sunday in a piece examining who can realistically land him. Per Stein, the Golden State Warriors have maintained a longstanding interest in bringing James to Northern California, but will be severely limited in what they can offer financially. Paul Pierce went further on his podcast, saying flat out that James should retire, arguing that no all-time great has ever been asked to accept a third-option role the way LeBron would be if he returns. Pierce noted that Jordan never took a secondary role with the Wizards, Kobe never deferred, and that asking LeBron back under those conditions is something different entirely. Chris Webber, for his part, said he hopes the Lakers honor James with the contract he deserves, but added that if that doesn't happen, a contending team that lands him could see something special happen.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • The Minnesota Timberwolves' frontcourt is the most pressing offseason question for the franchise. Julius Randle ($33M), Rudy Gobert ($36.5M) and Naz Reid ($23.3M) are set to cost almost $95 million combined next season, and both Gobert and Randle have two years remaining on their deals. Anthony Edwards is 24; the clock on whether that group can compete around him is becoming harder to ignore.
  • Tim Connelly is entering the final year of his contract with the Wolves, and team ownership wants to get an extension done with him. There were never any discussions between Connelly and the Dallas Mavericks before Masai Ujiri was hired there, per team sources, so the front office appears set to return intact next season.
  • Minnesota was also involved in the Kevin Durant sweepstakes at the 2025 trade deadline. Team and league sources told The Athletic that the Wolves believed they were in position to execute a deal in the hours before the deadline, but the Phoenix Suns ultimately decided not to pull the trigger.
  • Reid, reflecting on consecutive playoff exits to Oklahoma City and San Antonio, pointed to culture as the gap between Minnesota and those teams. "Probably just the moodiness," Reid said. "You look at both of those teams and they're playing for one another, they're excited to be on the floor with one another, they're a team where they're selfless." He added that the Wolves have more than enough talent but need to close that gap in mindset.
  • Mike Conley will weigh retirement this offseason but could return on a cheaper deal. Bones Hyland will also be a free agent.
  • Anthony Edwards walked over to congratulate the Spurs bench with eight minutes left in a blowout loss, and the reaction was split. Stephon Castle said he had never seen that before, and Mitch Johnson appreciated the gesture. Dirk Nowitzki and Udonis Haslem were less impressed: "As a leader, I would not have walked down there and shook their hands," Haslem said. "The game is not over. I've got eight minutes left. I still got smoke coming." Nowitzki said he's watched the NBA for a long time and has never seen a player dap up the opposing huddle with that much time remaining.
  • Jaden McDaniels was blunt about what made this series so difficult: "Wemby is probably the greatest defender I ever played against," McDaniels said of Victor Wembanyama. "Him being so tall and so long, it was hard to get to the rim."
  • Castle had 32 points, 11 rebounds and six assists in the series-clinching win, and his confidence heading into the conference finals was straightforward. "They're the defending champs. We know it's gonna be tough to knock them off," Castle said of OKC, "but I think we're pretty confident we could do it."
  • In the Detroit Pistons-Cleveland Cavaliers series, Paul Reed has been Jalen Duren's loudest supporter through an underwhelming playoff run. "I don't think anybody in here is as happy for JD as I am," Reed said after one game. "I was talking to him all night, telling him, 'That's the energy we need. Go harder. Make sure there's nothing left in the tank.'" The flagrant foul situation involving Ausar Thompson and Sam Merrill also drew scrutiny; lead referee Zach Zarba said Thompson's hand did contact Merrill's neck but no ejection was warranted because there was impact and follow-through but no windup, one of three required criteria for a flagrant 2.
  • Fred VanVleet put the Rockets' season in perspective after their first-round exit. "If you told me in September that I would miss the whole season, Stephen would miss 75% of the season, and we would win 52 games and play the Lakers in a four-five matchup, take them to six without KD, I would probably say, 'Okay, that doesn't sound crazy to me,'" VanVleet said. He also noted that adding Durant meant essentially a brand-new team with no continuity and no preseason practices together.
  • Jrue Holiday made clear he wants to return to Portland for 2026-27. "I hope so. I don't like being traded and moved and stuff," Holiday said. An anonymous scout offered a different view, questioning whether his roughly $35 million salary is justified given his current level of play and the pending return of Damian Lillard.
  • Terry Stotts has told the Golden State Warriors he will not be back as Steve Kerr's top assistant next season. He's open to a return to Portland, telling one reporter, "I would love to come back to the Blazers and Portland." The Warriors have also expressed interest in re-signing Kristaps Porzingis and Al Horford, with Mike Dunleavy Jr. saying they would "definitely love to have both back."
  • The NBA's MVP for 2025-26 will be announced Sunday night on NBA on Prime. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander is the heavy favorite.
  • Matisse Thybulle said he has no expectations heading into unrestricted free agency this summer. "I don't really know how the whole proper free agency thing goes," Thybulle said, though he added he would love to stay in Portland.
