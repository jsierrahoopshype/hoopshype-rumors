NBA Rumors Wrap: Naji Marshall locks up $52M Dallas extension, tech billionaire eyes London NBA franchise, Wembanyama hosts Spurs in Paris

Naji Marshall is staying in Dallas for the foreseeable future. The Mavericks forward signed a three-year, $52.2 million fully guaranteed extension that runs through the 2029-30 season, per Shams Charania. The deal starts at $18.2 million in 2027-28 and declines slightly in each of the following two years, per Marc Stein. Marshall, 27, averaged career-bests across the board last season, putting up 15.2 points, 4.7 rebounds, 3.3 assists, and 1.1 steals in 74 games. The structure also keeps him trade-eligible, with $18.195 million sitting right at the extend-and-trade ceiling of 120 percent of the estimated average salary. As a side note, Marshall will switch from No. 13 to No. 3 this coming season.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • American technology billionaire Nikesh Arora is in talks to buy the new London team in the NBA's European expansion. Arora, who previously led the consortium that purchased English cricket's most lucrative franchise in the Hundred competition, has assembled a group of investors prepared to offer more than $1 billion for the London side, which is among the most coveted of the new franchises.
  • Victor Wembanyama is bringing a group of Spurs teammates and coaches to Paris ahead of training camp. Spurs CEO R.C. Buford said roughly eight to ten people will travel to France next week to work with Wembanyama before the 2026-27 season tips off.
  • VICI Properties and Caesars Entertainment are the latest group exploring a partnership to build an NBA arena in Las Vegas. The list of groups angling for a Vegas facility continues to grow as the league's expansion process moves forward. The Trail Blazers' arena situation in Portland is also drawing relocation speculation, with Raleigh mentioned as a potential destination if the team were to leave — though the NBA's preferred path to both Las Vegas and Seattle remains through expansion, not relocation.
  • Donovan Mitchell and Grammy-winning R&B singer Coco Jones got married Saturday in front of family and friends. Bam Adebayo and Jaylen Brown were among the NBA stars in attendance, along with Ciara and Russell Wilson.
  • Bam Adebayo was asked who his favorite Heat teammate was and didn't hesitate. "I'm gonna have to go with Giannis," Adebayo told The Athletic. On LeBron James choosing the 76ers over a return to Miami, Adebayo kept it warm: "I consider LeBron like a brother. I'm happy for him. You want the best for somebody."
  • Moses Moody is targeting September to resume running and return to on-court work as he continues rehabbing in the Bay Area. Stephen Curry, who flew in for a community event Moody organized in Arkansas, said of his teammate: "He's committed to the work. He's not fighting it."
  • Khris Middleton opened up about his emotions after being traded from the Bucks to Washington. "Of course a lot of emotions, man. Pissed, angry, happy, sad, all the above," Middleton said. He added that he drove himself home after learning of the deal, spending a day or two there before heading to D.C. once everything was finalized.
  • Guerschon Yabusele explained why he chose to leave the NBA for Panathinaikos after a difficult stint with the Knicks, where he averaged just nine minutes in 41 games without a single start. "It was an important decision. I didn't make it easily," Yabusele said. "It took a long time, and there were many questions."
  • Nikola Vucevic had conversations with Crvena Zvezda about a potential move to Europe this summer before ultimately deciding to stay in the NBA. Zvezda president Zeljko Drcelic said money was not a factor, and that Vucevic's 20-plus years in the United States made a return complicated: "Such moves are not easy."
  • Simone Fontecchio told Erik Spoelstra the truth when asked if Miami's training camp was the hardest he had ever experienced. "I said that, if it made him happy, I could say it was," Fontecchio told the YouTube channel La Giornata Tipo. "In the NBA, yes. But not in my entire life." He credited Dusko Ivanovic's preseason at Baskonia as the true benchmark, noting that Ivanovic gave him just two extra days to recover after the Tokyo Olympics before expecting him on the practice floor.
  • Adebayo also had some fun at teammate Davion Mitchell's expense, flashing a wide grin while telling reporters: "Man, Davion's not fat anymore. You know, he's not fat anymore. He got himself in shape."
  • AJ Dybantsa says he wants a one-on-one shot at Luka Doncic. "I just want to be on like an ISO island. This sounds crazy, but I want to be on an ISO island and just really see if he's moving that slow," Dybantsa said on the Road Trippin Show.
  • The Earl Monroe New Renaissance Basketball Charter School in the Bronx — the country's first hoops-themed charter school — saw all 97 students in its second graduating class accepted into college and opens new facilities this fall. Knicks guard Jose Alvarado will be among the players attending a ceremony on Oct. 15, alongside legends Earl Monroe, Walt Frazier, and Bill Bradley, plus NBA Commissioner Adam Silver.
  • Charles Oakley has a message for Cavaliers fans upset about LeBron James signing with the 76ers: get over it. The Knicks legend told TMZ that LeBron's pursuit of more rings at 41 should be admired, not criticized.
