NBA Rumors Wrap: Timberwolves land Kuminga, Curry extension window opens, Jalen Duren off the market

The Minnesota Timberwolves have completed a flurry of moves to sign Jonathan Kuminga using the taxpayer midlevel exception, reshaping their roster heading into next season. To create the necessary cap room, Minnesota traded Josh Green and cash to the Utah Jazz for Cody Williams and John Konchar, then plans to waive and stretch Konchar. The financial math was tight: per Shams Charania, the Wolves needed to shed just $2.1 million to get under the second apron, and the Green trade cleared $2.5 million. Kuminga's agent, Aaron Turner, said the decision came down to fit over money, choosing Minnesota over a more lucrative offer from Portland and a competitive bid from Los Angeles. "The conversations with Ant and Chris Finch," Turner explained, "owner, GM, head coach, star player all in complete alignment that this is the guy that they want."

Turner also went on record to dispute the widely circulated narrative around Kuminga's departure from Golden State, saying "there was never an offer from the Warriors for 5 years, $150M... to take it a step further, the Warriors never made an offer on an extension." On a related note, Kuminga's previous agent told Run It Back that Sacramento and Chicago, two teams that had been pursuing a sign-and-trade, will have significant money available next summer, framing next year's free agency as a better opportunity for Kuminga than this one. Turner said Kuminga's motivation in Minnesota is the team's defensive identity: "Offense is secondary to what he's thinking about. It's going to be whatever we gotta do to win that night."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Draymond Green offered a warning about Kuminga's situation in Minnesota despite welcoming the move. "A $6.5 million salary in the NBA is a bench role salary," Green said. "Your salary determines ultimately how a team treats you. So my only worry for J.K. is that if it's not working, you're the first plug that gets pulled."
  • Jon Krawczynski expects the Wolves to continue adding, looking for a veteran who can provide locker room presence and spot minutes. Minnesota is now $415,566 under the second apron after the Green trade, leaving very little room to maneuver.
  • Saturday marks the first day Stephen Curry can sign a max extension with the Warriors, entering the final year of his current deal worth nearly $63 million. The expectation has always been that he signs, but if he waits, it would create a distraction that hangs over Golden State all season.
  • The Detroit Pistons are not budging on Jalen Duren. "The Pistons absolutely positively 100% intend to keep" him, per Tim MacMahon, who added that Detroit has told teams pursuing a sign-and-trade they are wasting their time. Duren remains the only significant restricted free agent still unsigned this offseason.
  • Brian Windhorst said he has heard nothing on rookie extensions across the league this summer, with Victor Wembanyama the lone player on a rookie contract to have already gotten one done.
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers have waived Cam Whitmore, per HoopsHype. Whitmore, the 20th pick in 2023, averaged 10.5 points in 17.3 minutes over three seasons with Houston and Washington.
  • The Clippers investigation into possible salary cap circumvention involving Kawhi Leonard is producing some clarity. Ramona Shelburne said conversations between the NBA and Steve Ballmer's lawyers have focused on "failure to supervise and the nature of the introductions" between the Clippers and Leonard's representatives, rather than direct evidence of cap circumvention by Ballmer himself. Shelburne added that if the pending trade to Toronto falls apart over the investigation, the Clippers are comfortable having Leonard back.
  • The Lakers ownership sale is moving. Shelburne believes the transaction closes this season, likely before year's end. The open question is whether Jeanie Buss can retain her role as controlling owner, since her agreement was with Mark Walter specifically and it is unclear how that arrangement survives the sale.
  • The NBA's expansion process has a pricing framework. A Las Vegas franchise is expected to cost around $9 billion, on the high end of the league's stated $7-10 billion range, while Seattle is expected to draw a lower fee. The NBA plans to select a Las Vegas owner before moving on to Seattle.
  • Kevin Durant, who is 38 and holds a $46 million player option for 2027-28, said Russell Westbrook's retirement made him think, but he is not close to walking away. "I put everything into the game and I'm cool with whatever happens," Durant said. "I'm going to continue to put everything into the game until I can't no more." Durant, who averaged 26 points last season and earned second-team All-NBA, also said one of his long-term goals is to serve as GM of Team USA at an Olympics.
  • Durant was asked whether he would have taken a below-market deal like Jalen Brunson did with the Knicks. "I would have sat down and talked to all my people like I usually do," he said, "and I'd be like, why, are you serious? Why do you need to take that risk when these dudes are billionaires?"
  • An ESPN survey of scouts, executives and coaches found Cameron Boozer as an early Rookie of the Year favorite. "Boozer is a winner and he's won things his entire young life," one Eastern Conference executive said. "Memphis isn't going to have a good year, but he's going to be the bright spot and he's going to win the award."
  • Gregg Winik, the longtime NBA content executive who produced "The Last Dance" and "Inside Stuff," will step down after the 2026-27 season following more than 35 years with the league. The NBA has begun searching for his successor, and Winik will remain in his role this season to assist with the transition.
  • An ESPN Films documentary on Stephen Curry, "Portrait of an Artist: Stephen Curry," opens exclusively on IMAX screens Oct. 9. Directed by Gotham Chopra, the film covers Curry's path to 4,000 career three-pointers.
