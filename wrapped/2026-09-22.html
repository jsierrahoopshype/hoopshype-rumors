NBA Rumors Wrap: Jalen Duren extension impasse deepens, Clippers name interim CEO after Ballmer punishment, Nicolas Batum retires

Jalen Duren and the Detroit Pistons appear headed toward a genuine crisis, with Shams Charania reporting that it is "highly up in the air and questionable" whether Duren will show up for media day in seven days. The Pistons upped their offer by $10 million in recent days, moving from a five-year, $190 million proposal to a five-year, $200 million fully guaranteed deal with no options, but Duren, who is eligible for a five-year, $287 million max, has rebuffed it. Charania added that Duren feels disrespected by the process, while the team views the offer as fair for a player coming off an inconsistent stretch. The Oct. 1 deadline for Duren to accept or decline a $9.6 million qualifying offer looms over everything.

Duren's leverage is real. The Sacramento Kings have signaled willingness to offer him a max deal next summer, and the Milwaukee Bucks are among at least two other teams with serious interest. If Duren walks into restricted free agency, Detroit will have the right to match any offer sheet, but the Pistons would be in an uncomfortable position regardless.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Clippers have named Los Angeles-based lawyer John Gibson as the franchise's interim Governor and CEO, with Steve Ballmer nominating him and the NBA approving the appointment. The move comes after the league's yearlong investigation into alleged salary cap circumvention, which Marc Stein reports was also the backdrop for the Clippers declining to offer Kawhi Leonard an extension beyond his $50.3 million contract for 2026-27, sparking trade talks with the Raptors. Jazz owner Ryan Smith said of the situation: "Steve is a standup individual first and foremost. These rules have to mean anything."
  • Nicolas Batum has announced his retirement after 21 years in basketball. The former Clipper and Blazer posted a tribute to Portland on social media: "Portland was my introduction to this league and this country. I couldn't have asked for a better franchise to start my career with."
  • The Atlanta Hawks and Charlotte Hornets have had on-and-off trade discussions this month centered on Dorian Finney-Smith and Buddy Hield, per Marc Stein. One proposed framework could have included former lottery pick Devin Carter, but Atlanta waived Carter on Sept. 11, and he subsequently signed with Boston.
  • Per HoopsHype, the Toronto Raptors converted rookie two-way guard Jaden Bradley to a four-year, $9.3 million deal, with the first year guaranteed. Bradley was the 50th pick of the 2026 draft out of Arizona, where he won Big 12 Player of the Year. The Raptors are also converting Malachi Smith to a two-way contract. Smith shot 48.5 percent from the field and 43.5 percent from three in 15 games for Brooklyn last season.
  • The NBA continues to advance its expansion process, with Las Vegas bidders facing a second submission deadline of Sept. 17. A group led by Nancy Walton Laurie is considered a leading Las Vegas contender, with Golden Knights owner Bill Foley also in the mix. In Seattle, Kraken owner Samantha Holloway remains the only known bidder, and the league could name a Las Vegas winner before pivoting to Seattle.
  • The Warriors are signing big man Graham Ike to a two-year, two-way contract after a strong summer league showing. Gary Payton II said the low expectations around Golden State this season give him a 2022 feeling, adding: "When we get Jimmy [Butler] and we get Moses [Moody] back, you never know."
  • Tyrese Haliburton opened up about a difficult stretch last season, saying he contemplated taking a mental health break during the first half of the year before turning things around. He also addressed the awkward experience of watching the Knicks, a team full of his friends, win the championship in a year he couldn't play.
  • Haliburton also recalled a Team USA meeting where Kevin Durant set the tone: "KD said, 'At the end of the day, we 12 gods.' Which is a crazy thing to say. Shout out to KD for thinking like that."
  • Doc Rivers is joining NBC Sports as an NBA game analyst when the network's coverage begins next month across NBC, Peacock, and NBCSN. "I have great memories working with NBC, and I'm excited to be back," Rivers said.
  • The Raptors clarified that the Kyle Lowry sculpture displayed outside Scotiabank Arena over the weekend was a temporary art installation tied to a charity weekend, not a permanent monument. The Raptors-mascot sculpture will remain in the concourse all season. Alumni including DeMar DeRozan, Pascal Siakam, OG Anunoby, Fred VanVleet and Nick Nurse attended the gala dinner.
  • Gabe Vincent turned down the chance to sign with the Knicks to compete for a wing spot, electing to wait for a more secure opportunity, per Marc Stein.
  • The Mavericks are expected to hire former Canadian soccer trainer Nico Berg to lead their medical department, per Stein. Berg studied under Alex McKechnie in Toronto, which would naturally appeal to new Dallas president of basketball operations Masai Ujiri.
  • The NBA is meeting with African government leaders during the UN General Assembly this week as it advances plans to sell up to 10 permanent Basketball Africa League franchises. Initial bids came from markets including Morocco, Nigeria, Senegal, Kenya, Rwanda and South Africa, with multiple offers exceeding $50 million.
  • Jefferson City High School dedicated its basketball court to OG Anunoby, the two-time NBA champion who grew up in the area. Anunoby attended the all-school assembly along with former coaches, friends and city officials.
  • Shaquille O'Neal's trading cards have surged dramatically in value, with his 1992-93 Upper Deck #1 base card in PSA 10 selling for $7,500 this week after going for $2,880 last October. O'Neal ended August as the fifth most-searched athlete on eBay's collectibles marketplace, trailing only Michael Jordan, Shohei Ohtani, Kobe Bryant and Cooper Flagg.
