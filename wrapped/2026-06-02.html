NBA Rumors Wrap: Curry signs with Li-Ning, Spurs youngest Finals team in 50 years, Giannis pursuit heats up

Stephen Curry has signed a landmark 10-year shoe endorsement deal with Chinese company Li-Ning, per Shams Charania. The agreement extends Curry's existing Curry Brand venture globally and covers basketball products, athleisure, a full golf line, and gives Curry the ability to sign athletes under his own brand. Curry explained the decision was shaped during his sneaker free agency, when he wore shoes from Dwyane Wade and Jimmy Butler's Li-Ning lines. "It was during that time playing in their sneakers that I knew Li-Ning could be the right partner to deliver on the innovation and design that I want Curry Brand to stand for," Curry said. The deal is one of the more significant sneaker signings in recent NBA history and sets up Curry Brand as a true global operation.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Spurs are the youngest team to reach the NBA Finals since the 1976-77 Portland Trail Blazers, per HoopsHype research. Their weighted average age is 25.15 years, and four players under 23, including Victor Wembanyama, Stephon Castle, Dylan Harper and Carter Bryant, account for 39.4 percent of San Antonio's playoff minutes. No Finals team in that database has leaned this heavily on players under 23.
  • The Spurs are eligible to sign Wembanyama to a max rookie extension this summer that projects at five years and $251 million, with super-max language that could push it to $301 million based on a projected $173.25 million salary cap for 2027-28.
  • The Giannis Antetokounmpo trade situation is moving toward a resolution sooner rather than later. Sources told Marc Stein that the Heat and Trail Blazers are already in pursuit, and Antetokounmpo's future is increasingly expected to be resolved over the next few weeks around the NBA Draft. Meanwhile, the Cavaliers have real internal resistance to a Giannis-for-Evan Mobley swap, with multiple members of Cleveland's front office strongly against trading the 2025 Defensive Player of the Year, who is viewed as both a future star and a long-term safety net in the event of another rebuild.
  • The Nuggets are increasingly expected to explore trades involving Christian Braun and Cam Johnson to create flexibility to match a restricted free agency offer to Peyton Watson and potentially retool around Nikola Jokic.
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander will let his voice be heard with Thunder management this offseason despite earlier comments suggesting otherwise, according to ESPN's Tim MacMahon. MacMahon said SGA was "playing a little bit coy" and will remind Sam Presti how much he values having Luguentz Dort as a teammate.
  • On the broader OKC roster picture: analysts on Run It Back said the Thunder should not give up on Chet Holmgren, with Lou Williams arguing "Lu Dort will be the adjustment." Chandler Parsons added that OKC's free agency situation is relatively clean, with Hartenstein and Dort as their only significant free agents. Separately, Parsons said he believes Holmgren has some kind of mental block against Wembanyama, pointing to his two-shot Game 7 as evidence.
  • Hall of Fame coach Rick Adelman has died at 79. Adelman coached 29 NBA seasons and won 1,042 games, the 10th-most in league history, before being inducted into the Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame in 2021. Clyde Drexler, who developed into an All-Star under Adelman in Portland, called him "just an absolute gentleman, a prince of a guy." The Warriors released a statement remembering Adelman, who also coached Golden State from 1995 to 1997.
  • The 76ers' VP of player personnel Prosper Karangwa, who drew interest from the Lakers and Mavericks, is increasingly expected to stay in Philadelphia following the hiring of Mike Gansey as the team's new president of basketball operations. Assistant GM Jameer Nelson is widely expected to be promoted to GM under Gansey, and Elton Brand is expected to be offered another role within the organization.
  • Aspiration co-founder Joseph Sanberg, whose company is at the center of an NBA investigation into the Clippers, was sentenced to 14 years in federal prison Monday. The judge said Sanberg's actions were "among the worst I've ever seen" and that the case "touched almost every badge of fraud."
  • Jeremy Sochan, who signed with the Knicks after clearing waivers, is ready to share everything he knows about his former Spurs teammates ahead of the Finals. "Whether I'm playing or not, it's important to feed all the information I have," Sochan said. He specifically noted that Wembanyama gets tired when made to run and suggested the Knicks be "very physical" and give him different looks.
  • Spurs forward Julian Champagnie reflected on the road that brought him to the NBA Finals. He went undrafted in 2022 and watched the Brooklyn draft from his nearby family home. "I was literally in the back of the crib in the corner crying," he told Andscape. He was later waived from the G League with no warning before eventually finding his footing in San Antonio.
  • Giannis opened up at length this week about financial literacy for athletes, sneaker history and his path to becoming a billionaire. On Adidas: he said he left the company because they refused to give his brother the same €5,000 deal they offered him. He also told the story of how Kobe Bryant challenged him to win MVP, leading Giannis to write "MVP" inside the cap of his water jug and carry it daily until he won it the following year.
  • Jusuf Nurkic shared stories from his Portland years in a new interview, describing Carmelo Anthony as someone who "stepped outside the system" by consistently showing up late to every team meeting. Carmelo's defense, according to Nurkic: "nobody told me." Nurkic contrasted that with Damian Lillard, who he said was never late for anything and "led by example" in everything he did.
  • NBA Finals ticket prices are showing a split: get-in costs for Games 1 and 2 in San Antonio are dropping while seats at Madison Square Garden are getting increasingly expensive, according to Gametime data. Game 6 of the Western Conference Finals drew 11.6 million viewers on NBC and Peacock, the most-watched game of the season.
  • Draft prospect AJ Dybantsa is already eyeing the Wizards' jersey number situation. If Washington drafts him, he says he needs No. 3 from Trae Young: "If they draft me, I do need 3, Trae. We're gonna see in like 5 weeks."
