NBA Rumors Wrap: SGA wins back-to-back MVP, Cavs advance to ECF, Donovan Mitchell faces contract decision

Shai Gilgeous-Alexander has won his second consecutive NBA Most Valuable Player award, becoming the 14th player in league history to win back-to-back MVPs, per Shams Charania. The Thunder guard is the first player to win consecutive MVPs since Nikola Jokic in 2020-21 and 2021-22, and the first guard to do it since Stephen Curry in 2014-15 and 2015-16. He also became the first guard in NBA history to average 30 points and shoot 55 percent in a season, and joined Michael Jordan as the only players to average 30 points, five assists and 50 percent shooting in four consecutive seasons. When Steve Nash announced him as the winner, SGA called it "a full circle moment," saying Nash was "the first person to really tell me I was going to the NBA other than my family."

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • The Cavaliers dominated the Pistons 125-94 in Game 7 to advance to the Eastern Conference Finals, the first time Cleveland has reached the ECF without LeBron James since 1992. Donovan Mitchell led the way with 26 points and eight assists and zero turnovers, while Jarrett Allen (23), Sam Merrill (23) and Evan Mobley (21 points, 12 rebounds) all topped 20. The Cavs become just the second team ever to win a Game 7 by 20-plus points after losing Game 6 by 20-plus points, joining the 1995 Magic.
  • Cade Cunningham was blunt after the game: "That game sucked." He added that the sting of falling short "we're going to feel for a while," though he also said the loss would fuel Detroit going forward. Duncan Robinson said the Pistons "didn't come out with the right disposition for a Game 7" across the areas they had prided themselves on all season.
  • Coach J.B. Bickerstaff refused to use the word "disappointment" for his group after the loss, saying "not ever will I be disappointed in these guys." Detroit's run ended as the team's deepest playoff run since 2008. Meanwhile, Jalen Duren averaged 9.3 fewer points in the playoffs than he did in the regular season, the biggest such drop in an All-Star season since Elgin Baylor in 1969.
  • Cavs coach Kenny Atkinson revealed one of Cleveland's key tactical wrinkles in the series: "The tactical adjustment we made was avoiding [Ausar] Thompson. We were just like, if he's near the ball, throw it to someone else." The Cavs now head to New York for Game 1 of the ECF on Tuesday. Allen said he knows MSG will be loud, but added, "We came into an incredible arena like this and took over the game. We just have to do it again."
  • The bigger question hovering over Cleveland is what happens with Mitchell this summer. The Cavs will likely want to lock Mitchell into a new deal in July, adding four years and up to $272 million. But Mitchell can, and probably should, wait until 2027, when 10 years of service time would allow him to sign for five years and roughly $350 million, plus a no-trade clause. That is a massive difference in guaranteed money for a player entering his prime.
  • Cleveland also has an informal understanding with James Harden that the team plans to work out a new deal to keep him, according to ESPN. The Clippers had declined to commit to Harden's $42 million team option for next season, and once both sides made their positions clear, Harden's representation sought a team willing to make a commitment. The Cavs were willing. A formal deal cannot be completed until the offseason.
  • The Timberwolves are expected to make significant changes around a core of Anthony Edwards, Jaden McDaniels, Naz Reid and center Joan Beringer after being eliminated by San Antonio. The organization does feel good about that group going forward, but the Spurs showed them where the bar is set in the West. Ayo Dosunmu, who came over at the trade deadline and becomes an unrestricted free agent, said his time in Minnesota "has been great" and he is "excited for the future," though he added it is his first time going through unrestricted free agency and he is looking forward to exploring his options.
  • Portland's head coach search is expanding. The Blazers have expressed interest in Minnesota assistant Micah Nori alongside previously reported candidates Jared Dudley of Denver and Steve Hetzel, while Lakers assistant Greg St. Jean is also on the list. Owner Tom Dundon pushed back on the notion that he runs a frugal operation, saying "I want to win more than I want to make money" and that winning is the only reason to own a team. On relocation fears, Dundon said the team had an agreement in principle with the city and state before he even purchased the franchise: "Assuming that all gets done, then this is a non-story."
  • The Pelicans' lengthy head coaching search appears to be nearing its end. A decision is expected early this week. The reason for the delay has become clearer at the draft combine: New Orleans has a longstanding interest in former Magic coach Jamahl Mosley and has been taking its time to allow his dismissal in Orlando to make him a fully eligible candidate for the role.
  • AJ Dybantsa is the overwhelming choice among scouts and executives at the draft combine for the Wizards' No. 1 pick. Seven of 13 respondents polled by The Athletic voted for him, with one saying, "Take AJ Dybantsa and sleep like a baby." One executive who voted for Dybantsa also raised the possibility of trading down with the Jazz, who own the No. 2 pick: "Utah has got assets and good players. The question is: How good of a player could you get from the Jazz?"
  • The NBA is moving toward its most significant draft lottery overhaul in decades, with a vote on the "3-2-1 proposal" set for the Board of Governors on May 28. But there is resistance inside front offices, with executives worried the new system will create unintended consequences rather than solve tanking. One assistant GM summed up the mood: "They're hellbent on doing this." At least 23 of 30 governors must approve the changes for them to pass; if they do, the new rules would begin with the 2027 draft.
  • Victor Wembanyama has been ruled out of the July FIBA window for France's World Cup qualifiers now that the Spurs are in the Western Conference Finals. France's national team coach confirmed Wemby was extremely unlikely to be available if San Antonio continued to advance, and that decision is now official.
  • Luka Doncic will skip representing Slovenia this offseason and instead focus on rehabbing his hamstring before rebuilding his conditioning for the 2026-27 season. Before heading home to Ljubljana, he met with Lakers management to discuss both the team's summer personnel plans and his own offseason program. Doncic appeared in 64 games this season, his third-lowest total since entering the league in 2018.
  • Jaylen Brown turned down the NBA's Social Justice Champion nomination on Twitch, saying "I don't really feel like you need to be rewarded for your responsibility." Brown said the league has asked for his participation in the award over the past five to seven years and he has declined every time, saying community work is simply "a responsibility" to him, not something that requires recognition.
  • Fred VanVleet addressed Kevin Durant's alleged burner accounts on his "Unguarded" podcast after Houston's playoff exit, saying Durant's openness is both a gift and a burden. "He's so authentic and so approachable," VanVleet said, arguing that authenticity invites others to add false narratives, and Durant's reluctance to deny them only makes it worse.
