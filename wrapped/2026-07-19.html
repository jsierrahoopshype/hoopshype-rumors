NBA Rumors Wrap: Mavs match Knicks' Cisse offer sheet, Wembanyama returns to France squad, LeBron free agency heats up

The Dallas Mavericks moved quickly to protect their roster, matching the New York Knicks' two-year, $4.7 million offer sheet on restricted free agent center Moussa Cisse, per Shams Charania. The Mavericks had until Monday at 11:59 p.m. ET to decide, but matched well ahead of the deadline. The Knicks had signed Cisse to the sheet earlier Saturday, with the contract starting at the veterans minimum of $2.5 million, half-guaranteed in year one and non-guaranteed in 2027-28. Had Dallas declined to match, New York would have added a young center to fill depth after losing Mitchell Robinson and Ariel Hukporti elsewhere in free agency, though landing Cisse would have cost the Knicks the ability to sign their second-round draft picks at the second-round minimum while remaining under the second apron.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Victor Wembanyama will return to the French national team for the first time since the 2024 Olympics, the French Basketball Federation announced Saturday. He's been named to the squad for two 2027 World Cup qualifying matches in late August against Slovenia and Sweden, along with warm-up games against Serbia. Wembanyama had missed Euro 2025 while recovering from venous thrombosis in his right shoulder that ended his 2024-25 season early. French national team coach Frederic Fauthoux called the return "extremely important," adding: "Victor is the undisputed leader of this generation."
  • Spurs CEO RC Buford reflected on last season's run to the NBA Finals in an interview with L'Equipe, crediting the team's chemistry and ball movement as keys to the unexpected run. "Our style of play, our ball movement, and the genuine emotion we saw on the court after winning the Conference Finals against Oklahoma City — it was all authentic. And that's very rare in elite sports." San Antonio will also return to Paris in January for two regular-season games, with Buford noting the French market has been "incredibly well served" since Wembanyama arrived.
  • LeBron James' free agency continued to generate chatter at Fanatics Fest in New York, with multiple teams making their pitches known. Sixers owner Josh Harris had a brief conversation with LeBron at a VIP event, and sources say Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid, and Jaylen Brown have all let LeBron know they'd love to have him in Philadelphia. On the ESPN podcast circuit, Dave McMenamin said there was "some sort of dialogue" between LeBron's camp and the Spurs, while Brian Windhorst said he believes Boston would rank last on LeBron's list of preferred destinations.
  • Anthony Edwards made his own LeBron pitch, recording a message for James on behalf of the Timberwolves: "Bron Bron... come to Minnesota. We got your card, man." Meanwhile, Giannis Antetokounmpo, now with the Heat, was asked whether he's in the GOAT conversation. His answer: "No, no, not yet. But if we get the job done here, maybe in a few years I'll be."
  • Michael Porter Jr. is making it known he wants Peyton Watson in Brooklyn. A fan captured an impromptu conversation between the two at Fanatics Fest where MPJ said, "He boutta sign for $130 million. I'm tryna get him in Brooklyn, though." Porter and Watson were teammates in Denver before MPJ was traded to the Nets, where he finished the season as the leader of the youngest roster in the NBA. "We had five rookies, the youngest team in the NBA," Porter said. "It was a completely different role that I had, but it was something that I cherished."
  • The Las Vegas Jacks ownership group has been working toward an NBA expansion bid since January 2024, well before their public announcement this month, according to the Las Vegas Review-Journal. The group includes former NBA owner Jerry Colangelo and former NBA player and coach Vinny Del Negro. The "Jacks" name is not guaranteed to stick — the group has trademarked it but is open to fan input on what the team should ultimately be called.
  • Charles Barkley addressed the viral confrontation with Draymond Green on Inside the NBA, and his reaction was vintage Barkley. Speaking on Pardon My Take at the Lake Tahoe celebrity golf tournament, he said he wasn't paying attention to the internet firestorm because he and Green went to a dive bar together after the show: "So we go to a little dive bar after work all the time and Dray went with me that night. And so I'm not even paying attention because I don't do the internet."
  • Trae Young, now with the Wizards, showed up to Fanatics Fest in New York and took the stage with a Jalen Brunson jersey and tried to rip it apart in front of the crowd. The jersey mostly just stretched. Young was booed as he walked through the event. He later announced on social media that a second Young Family Athletic Center will be built near Yukon High School in Oklahoma, funded through a tax increment financing district.
  • Jose Alvarado, the only New York City native on the championship Knicks roster, said being home after winning a title is something he'll never take for granted. "Especially when you win a championship," he told the New York Post from the ESPYs red carpet. "It's cool being home." After the Knicks clinched in San Antonio, Alvarado gave away his jersey, shorts, sneakers, headband and championship shirt to fans at the arena.
  • Caitlin Clark put on one of the great individual performances in WNBA history Friday night, finishing with 45 points and 10 assists as the Fever won 110-107. Clark became the first player in league history to record 45 points and 10 assists in a game, and she did it in fewer than 30 minutes. She also became the fastest player to reach 200 three-pointers in WNBA history, hitting the mark in 74 games. It was her fourth career game with 30-plus points and 10-plus assists, a feat that has happened only four other times in league history.
  • Warriors rookie Yaxel Lendeborg said he's already leaned on Stephen Curry and Draymond Green for advice on navigating his first year in the league. "What are the things that I should do to try to stay in the league as long as they did," Lendeborg said of the conversations. The first-year forward is joining a Golden State core that includes two future Hall of Famers.
  • Former NBA first-round pick Patrick Baldwin Jr. is heading overseas after signing with Crvena Zvezda Meridianbet Belgrade. Baldwin spent last season split between the G League and NBA stints with the Clippers, 76ers, and Kings.
  • Baron Davis is being sued by his sister Lissa Davis for fraud and intentional infliction of emotional distress. According to the lawsuit obtained by TMZ, Lissa claims Davis helped her buy a house nearly 20 years ago, kept the title in his name for "tax purposes," and then sold the property this year without warning her.
  • Bam Adebayo turned 29 on Saturday and marked the occasion in typical fashion: "Dang, am I really Unc fr?" He was 19 when the Heat drafted him a decade ago.
