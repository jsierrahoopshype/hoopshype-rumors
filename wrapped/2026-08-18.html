NBA Rumors Wrap: Buss family sells Lakers stake, Kawhi investigation nears resolution, Klay Thompson trade saga continues

The Los Angeles Lakers ownership situation reached a dramatic turning point Monday when the Buss family voted to sell its remaining 17.8% stake to new majority owners Josh Kushner and Bob Iger, per Shams Charania. The sale required four of six sibling votes to trigger a tag-along provision from Mark Walter's earlier transaction, and five siblings ultimately voted yes, with Jeanie Buss the lone holdout. Once the deal closes, Jeanie will fall below the 15% ownership threshold required to remain governor, effectively ending her tenure leading the franchise. She is already contesting the sale, with her attorney Adam Streisand arguing in a letter to her siblings' legal representatives that no transaction can be executed without her consent as a co-trustee, per a 2017 court order. The family released a statement saying, "We love the Lakers, Laker fans and will continue to support Los Angeles; but it is time to use this opportunity to move on and exit gracefully while we still can."

The vote itself had a chaotic lead-up. Lakers senior VP of finance Joe McCormack called an emergency meeting of the siblings last Tuesday to discuss dissolving the family trust and locking down shares under a new structure. Sources told ESPN that during that meeting, no one mentioned Walter's pending sale to Kushner and Iger, which was reported by ESPN the very next day. That revelation apparently rattled the family and prompted the fresh push to sell. A previous vote taken months earlier had produced only three votes in favor; this time, after Walter's sale became public, two more siblings joined in.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Clippers' cap circumvention investigation is moving toward a resolution, and the latest details suggest Steve Ballmer is in better shape than the case's origins implied. The NBA has found no evidence that Ballmer funneled money through team sponsors to pay Kawhi Leonard, and the league's current focus is on whether introducing Leonard to team partners constitutes a cap violation on its own. One league insider called that theory "an outrageous overreach," and two sources familiar with NBPA thinking said that argument is "dead on arrival" in arbitration.
  • The investigation, led by law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, has expanded beyond Aspiration to at least three other companies, including Daktronics and Boingo Wireless, but sources say the league has presented no evidence of Ballmer directing any of them to compensate Leonard. The Clippers responded with a statement saying that introducing players to business partners "is both an ordinary practice by NBA teams and a common request of players and representatives." The NBA pushed back hard on ESPN's reporting, with spokesman Mike Bass saying the story "contains numerous and significant inaccuracies."
  • The Raptors are not waiting for the investigation to fully conclude before moving forward on the Clippers trade that would bring Leonard to Toronto along with Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick. According to Raptors reporter Josh Lewenberg, Toronto just needs assurance that Leonard won't face a voided contract, and those assurances could be coming soon.
  • The Dallas Mavericks still want to trade Klay Thompson, and the Miami Heat remain the most aggressive suitor. Marc Stein reported that Miami is "desperate" for Thompson but wants to sign him outright as a free agent rather than acquire him in a trade, a position that does not align with Dallas's preference. Thompson would not be the only team in play, Stein said.
  • Russell Westbrook has apparently retired. The Washington Wizards made him a two-year offer to return to the city where he played earlier in his career, but sources told Marc Stein that Westbrook, not Washington, ended the talks, balking at the terms and the role presented. He is 37.
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers are shopping Max Strus. After agreeing to send Dennis Schröder to Charlotte, Cleveland is actively seeking a trade for Strus, who is entering the final year of his deal at $16.7 million. Schröder, for his part, said he had known about the trade for a month and called Charlotte "a better situation for me from a sporting perspective," describing his role behind Donovan Mitchell and James Harden in Cleveland as "an extremely difficult role."
  • Bennedict Mathurin's restricted free agency remains unresolved. His representatives are still in talks with the Pelicans, Bulls and Bucks, and the door on a return to the Clippers has not fully closed, even after Los Angeles re-signed Bradley Beal earlier this week.
  • Per HoopsHype, the Houston Rockets signed guard Sean Pedulla to a two-way deal. Pedulla was the NBA G League Rookie of the Year last season, averaging 23.6 points, 6.8 assists and 5.1 rebounds per game.
  • Haywood Highsmith is returning to the Phoenix Suns on a one-year deal, per Shams Charania. Trendon Watford also found a home, agreeing to a one-year, $2.9 million deal with the New Orleans Pelicans after a year in Philadelphia that included a 20-point, 17-rebound, 10-assist triple-double in November.
  • The San Antonio city council voted 6-5 Monday to block a public referendum on the city's $489 million contribution to a new Spurs arena. The project moves forward as planned, though a separate public vote to authorize the bonds needed for that funding is still expected to be scheduled next month. The total arena price tag is $1.3 billion, with the Spurs committed to at least $500 million of that.
  • Kyle Korver is leaving the Atlanta Hawks after four years as an assistant GM. Sources say he parts on good terms and is looking forward to spending more time with his family. Korver also had an All-Star season as a player in Atlanta.
  • Former Michigan coach John Beilein is joining the Orlando Magic as a senior coaching consultant for the upcoming season, working alongside head coach Sean Sweeney.
  • The Charlotte Hornets hired John Bryant as an assistant coach. Bryant previously worked on Billy Donovan's staff in Chicago and before that with Brett Brown in Philadelphia.
  • The Denver Nuggets are hiring Austin Dufault as assistant coach and VP of player development. Dufault was most recently director of player development with the Bulls and previously an assistant with the Pistons.
  • Michael Jordan's jersey from Game 3 of the 1998 NBA Finals is heading to auction through Joopiter this fall. The current record for a Jordan Finals jersey is $10.1 million, paid in 2022 for his Game 1 jersey from that same series.
