NBA Rumors Wrap: Daryl Morey fired by 76ers, Wizards land No. 1 pick, Jason Collins and Brandon Clarke remembered

Daryl Morey is out as president of basketball operations with the Philadelphia 76ers after six seasons, per Shams Charania. Managing Partner Josh Harris met with Morey on Tuesday evening in Philadelphia and the two sides agreed to part ways. Harris said in a statement that the franchise has "fallen well short of our own expectations and failed to deliver in the way this city deserves." Bob Myers, the president of sports for Harris Blitzer Sports & Entertainment and a four-time NBA championship architect, will oversee the basketball department in the interim and lead the search for a new head executive. Coach Nick Nurse will return for a fourth season, according to Charania.

Myers offered his own statement: "I know how much the 76ers mean to the City of Philadelphia, and it's important we find the right leader to shape the future of this team. The process will start immediately, and we will be thorough and deliberate in our evaluations."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The NBA closed its investigation into the Milwaukee Bucks over the late-season shutdown of Giannis Antetokounmpo with no further action warranted. The league launched the probe after Antetokounmpo and the Bucks told conflicting stories about his readiness to return from a hyperextended left knee. The Bucks told investigators that Antetokounmpo declined to participate in 3-on-3 scrimmages as part of the return protocol; Antetokounmpo maintained he was healthy enough to play.
  • • The Washington Wizards won the No. 1 overall pick in the 2026 NBA Draft, with ESPN draft analyst Jeremy Woo projecting BYU's AJ Dybantsa as Washington's likely selection. John Wall represented the franchise at the lottery, arriving with a backup good-luck charm after the necklace from his late mother broke, and owner Ted Leonsis admitted he spent the night dreading a "Final Destination"-style collapse to the fifth pick.
  • • The Chicago Bulls will begin a head coaching search this week, with new top executive Bryson Graham requesting permission to interview candidates. Graham has previously expressed openness to hiring an up-and-coming coach rather than a veteran, meaning current assistants on prominent staffs could be in play. Graham is also expected to continue reshaping the front office in the coming weeks, potentially including new scouts and support staff.
  • • Victor Wembanyama dropped 27 points, 17 rebounds and five assists as the San Antonio Spurs crushed the Minnesota Timberwolves 126-97 in Game 5. The Spurs lead the series 3-2 and can close it out in Game 6 Friday in Minnesota. The Timberwolves have lost the last two games in San Antonio by a combined 67 points, with Julius Randle shooting just 36 percent in the series.
  • • Spurs-Timberwolves Game 4 averaged a combined 7.9 million viewers across Nielsen and Adobe Analytics on Sunday, the most-watched game of the playoffs aside from Sixers-Celtics Game 7 on NBC. Game 3 on Prime Video drew 5.25 million, a 24 percent increase from Thunder-Nuggets on ESPN a year ago and the streamer's largest NBA audience to date.
  • • Gregg Popovich has been an active presence for the Spurs during this playoff run despite stepping down as coach last May following his stroke. Devin Vassell said Popovich has been involved in film sessions and offering individual advice throughout the series. "He has so much wisdom, and that's just stuff you can't take for granted," Vassell said. "You got to be able to ask questions and he'll be able to help you out."
  • • De'Aaron Fox (sore ankle) and Dylan Harper (sore knee) were game-time decisions for Game 5. Coach Mitch Johnson said both were fighting to play, but added the staff had to make sure they were "capable of being out there." Harper was spotted going through his usual pregame warmup before tip, a positive sign.
  • • The Lakers face a complicated offseason with both LeBron James and Austin Reaves set to be unrestricted free agents. Team and league sources say running back a similar roster is not a palatable option, with the organization wanting better center play, two-way wings, and a long-term secondary playmaker alongside Luka Doncic. Coach JJ Redick said the group was just starting to look like a championship-level team before injuries hit: "I wish we had more time with those guys all in the lineup."
  • • Steve Kerr agreed to a multi-year contract extension with the Golden State Warriors.
  • • Jason Collins, the first active openly gay player in NBA history, died Tuesday at age 47 after a battle with stage 4 glioblastoma. Collins had traveled to Singapore for experimental treatments last winter and was able to attend NBA All-Star Weekend in Los Angeles before the cancer returned. He played 13 NBA seasons, spending eight with the Nets, including their back-to-back Eastern Conference Finals teams in 2002 and 2003. Commissioner Adam Silver said Collins' impact "extended far beyond basketball" in making the league and sports community more inclusive. Jason Kidd wrote: "He had courage like you've never seen. He was an incredible teammate."
  • • Brandon Clarke of the Memphis Grizzlies died Monday at 29 years old. The Los Angeles Fire Department responded to a medical emergency at the home where Clarke was staying, and his death is being investigated as a possible overdose after authorities found drug paraphernalia at the scene. Clarke was a first-round pick in 2019, won Summer League MVP, and was an All-Rookie First Team selection. The Spurs held a moment of silence before Game 5 for both Clarke and Collins. Keldon Johnson, who entered the league the same year as Clarke, said simply: "It sucks. Sending condolences to his family."
  • • Luka Doncic is among the investors in a consortium led by former Mavericks GM Donnie Nelson to move Italian club Vanoli Cremona to Rome, bringing top-level European basketball back to the Italian capital. Separately, European football clubs remain skeptical of the NBA's European league project, with one report describing ongoing tension over the gap between the NBA's financial expectations and what clubs are willing to commit.
  • • The Pistons downgraded Caris LeVert (right heel contusion) and Duncan Robinson (low back soreness) to questionable for Wednesday's Game 5 against Cleveland, per HoopsHype. Kevin Huerter (left adductor strain) was also listed as questionable.
