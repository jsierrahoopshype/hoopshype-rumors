NBA Rumors Wrap: Daryl Morey out in Philly, Jason Collins dies at 47, Wizards land No. 1 pick

Daryl Morey is out as president of basketball operations with the Philadelphia 76ers after six seasons, per Shams Charania. Morey and 76ers owners Josh Harris and David Blitzer met Tuesday evening in Philadelphia and parted ways. Harris said in a statement that "we have fallen well short of our own expectations and failed to deliver in the way this city deserves." Four-time NBA championship architect Bob Myers, the president of sports for Harris Blitzer Sports and Entertainment, will lead the search for a new head of basketball operations and oversee the franchise's basketball department in the interim. Myers said the process will "start immediately" and called Philadelphia "a destination for top talent across the league." Nick Nurse will continue as head coach into a fourth season with the team.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The basketball world lost two figures Tuesday. Jason Collins, the first active openly gay player in NBA history, died at 47 after a battle with stage 4 glioblastoma. Collins had traveled to Singapore for experimental treatments not yet authorized in the United States, which were effective enough to allow him to return home and attend NBA All-Star Weekend events in Los Angeles, but the cancer returned. The Brooklyn Nets, for whom Collins played eight seasons and won back-to-back Eastern Conference titles in 2002 and 2003, called him "selfless, tough, and deeply respected." NBA commissioner Adam Silver said Collins' impact "extended far beyond basketball."
  • Brandon Clarke of the Memphis Grizzlies also died Tuesday at 29 years old. The Los Angeles Fire Department responded to a medical emergency call around 5 PM Monday, and Clarke was declared deceased on the scene. His death is being investigated as a possible overdose, with drug paraphernalia found at the home. Clarke, a first-round pick out of Gonzaga in 2019, was an All-Rookie First Team selection and averaged 10.2 points and 5.5 rebounds over seven seasons. Former coach Tony Darden's son AJ, whom Clarke treated like a little brother, spoke about how close they were. The Spurs held a moment of silence for both Collins and Clarke before Game 5.
  • The NBA closed its investigation into the Milwaukee Bucks over the late-season shutdown of Giannis Antetokounmpo with no further action warranted. The league launched the probe after Antetokounmpo and the Bucks told conflicting stories about his readiness to return from a hyperextended left knee. The Bucks told investigators that Antetokounmpo declined 3-on-3 scrimmages as part of his return protocol; Antetokounmpo maintained he was healthy enough to play.
  • Victor Wembanyama put up 27 points, 17 rebounds and five assists as the San Antonio Spurs rolled the Minnesota Timberwolves 126-97 in Game 5, putting San Antonio one win from their first Western Conference finals appearance in nine years. The Timberwolves have lost the last two games in San Antonio by a combined 67 points and shot below 40 percent Tuesday. Julius Randle is shooting 36 percent from the field for the series. Game 6 is Friday in Minnesota.
  • Ahead of Game 5, De'Aaron Fox (sore ankle) and Dylan Harper (sore knee) were listed as game-time decisions for San Antonio. Coach Mitch Johnson said he wanted to "save them from themselves." Harper was spotted going through his usual pregame warmup at the Frost Bank Center before tip, a positive sign ahead of the game.
  • Gregg Popovich, who retired from coaching last May after suffering a stroke, has remained a resource for the Spurs throughout this playoff run. Devin Vassell said Popovich has been involved in film sessions and individual conversations with players. "He helps out a lot," Vassell said. "He just has so much wisdom, and that's just stuff you can't take for granted."
  • The Wembanyama-era Spurs are a TV ratings story. Sunday's Game 4 averaged 7.9 million viewers across Nielsen and Adobe Analytics, the second-most-watched game of the playoffs behind only Sixers-Celtics Game 7. Game 3 Friday on Prime Video drew 5.25 million, the streamer's largest NBA audience of the season and a 24 percent increase over last year's Thunder-Nuggets on ESPN. Before the playoffs, the most-watched regular season game was Spurs-Thunder on Christmas Day.
  • The Washington Wizards won the lottery and the right to draft AJ Dybantsa with the No. 1 pick. John Wall, who represented the franchise at the lottery, said the pressure from everyone around him was real: "I need it. That's what everybody keeps telling me." Owner Ted Leonsis said before the envelope was opened, all he could think about was ending up in the hospital and sliding to fifth. ESPN draft analyst Jeremy Woo projects BYU's Dybantsa as the pick.
  • The Lakers face a significant offseason. According to team and league sources, running it back with a mostly similar roster is not a palatable option. LeBron James is an unrestricted free agent and Austin Reaves will decline his player option. Both have played their way into large contracts, and retaining both could limit the team's ability to build fully around Luka Doncic. Coach JJ Redick said the team needs to build depth beyond an eight or nine-man rotation, whether through development, the draft, trades or free agency.
  • Redick said he wished the big three had more time together: "We were just beginning to see what that could look like when Luka and AR got hurt and that's sort of the disappointing part." GM Rob Pelinka praised the sacrifice of the on-court leaders, saying Redick dealt with having "three quarterbacks."
  • Luka Doncic is among the investors in a group led by former Dallas GM Donnie Nelson that is relocating Italian club Vanoli Cremona to Rome, per EuroHoops. The group has interest in securing a wildcard for the EuroLeague or EuroCup. On the broader NBA Europe project, football clubs remain skeptical, with L'Equipe reporting tension over "the gap between the NBA's financial expectations" and what soccer clubs are willing to commit.
  • The Chicago Bulls will begin their coaching search this week, with new head of basketball operations Bryson Graham requesting permission to interview candidates. Graham has signaled he is open to hiring an up-and-coming coach rather than a veteran with championship experience, meaning current NBA assistants are in the mix. Graham is also expected to continue reshaping the front office in the coming weeks, potentially including new scouts and support staff.
  • Steve Kerr and the Golden State Warriors agreed to a multi-year contract extension, keeping him as head coach.
  • The Pistons downgraded Caris LeVert (right heel contusion) and Duncan Robinson (low back soreness) to questionable for Game 5 against Cleveland, per HoopsHype. Kevin Huerter (left adductor strain) is also questionable for the contest.
