NBA Rumors Wrap: Podziemski locks up $110M Warriors extension, Kings' Ranadive roast controversy, Maxime Raynaud exits in wheelchair

Brandin Podziemski and the Golden State Warriors agreed to a five-year, $110 million fully guaranteed rookie-scale extension, with agent Bill Duffy of WME Basketball confirming the deal, per Shams Charania. The contract includes a mutual option in the fifth year (2031-32) and a trade kicker. Coach Steve Kerr said getting the deal done before the season was a "good relief," adding that Podziemski can now just focus on basketball. Kerr praised him as a player who does "all those things" in the dribble-pass-shoot process and said his fit alongside Stephen Curry is natural. "He's there for you every night," Kerr said.

Curry, for his part, is being managed carefully this preseason. Kerr confirmed Curry will play only the first half of preseason games, saying the Warriors are "ramping him up slower than normal" and plan to lighten his practice load during the season to protect his knees at this stage of his career. Curry himself addressed the criticism of the front office's quiet offseasons, acknowledging injuries derailed recent years but stopping short of any public complaint: "All the other chatter doesn't necessarily matter to me in terms of how I grade our seasons."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Sacramento Kings are dealing with a significant preseason injury scare. Second-year center Maxime Raynaud had to be led off the court in a wheelchair after appearing to injure his right knee while setting a screen, with GM Scott Perry following him to the locker room. The Kings were already shorthanded, with Domantas Sabonis, Malik Monk and Dylan Cardwell all out for the game, though Ben Simmons made his Kings debut in San Francisco.
  • • The Kings' offseason has been eventful off the court too. Owner Vivek Ranadive reportedly turned a preseason team dinner into what some players and staff found alarming, going around the room and calling out individuals for their perceived failures, per Zach Lowe's podcast. GM Scott Perry pushed back hard, saying Ranadive first spoke about his own portrayal in the media before addressing players, and insisted the whole thing was "lighthearted" and "clearly not meant" as a roast. Some in the room saw it as motivational; others did not.
  • • Trae Young and Washington Wizards guard Daniss Jenkins were both ejected from their preseason game against the Pistons after each received two technical fouls following a confrontation away from the ball. Young, who has not played in over a year due to injury and a trade, said he has been training like he's back in high school, getting up at 6 a.m. and doing whatever it takes to change the perception that his best basketball is behind him.
  • • The relationship between Jaylen Brown and Jayson Tatum is drawing plenty of attention with the Philadelphia 76ers visiting Boston for a preseason game. Tatum said he'll miss Brown's personality and that "his presence will be missed in the city," while Brown compared the situation to running into an ex in public: "You just look at them and just like dang, and you just got to go about your business." The big three of Brown, LeBron James and Joel Embiid were all out for the preseason matchup. The Celtics plan to show a tribute video for Brown when Philadelphia visits for their first regular-season game in Boston in January.
  • • Brown also found himself in an Adidas ad spat with Anthony Edwards. After Edwards clowned LeBron and Brown in a commercial, Brown went live on Twitch with teammate VJ Edgecombe to watch it and fired back. Edwards, playing a late-night talk show host, had jabbed LeBron for choosing Philadelphia over Minnesota.
  • • Philadelphia 76ers president Bob Myers addressed trade rumors swirling around the roster with characteristic candor: "I can say what you guys said, which is I haven't traded you yet, nothing I've said yes to, you're on this team." He noted he would give every one of the 14 other players the same honest answer if they asked the same question.
  • • Tyrese Haliburton won't play against the Hawks after tweaking his right ankle in Ames. Coach Rick Carlisle said an MRI looked good and there is no concern about the Achilles. Haliburton could return to practice as soon as Monday.
  • • Los Angeles Lakers guard Collin Sexton is day-to-day with a lower leg injury, limited to modified practice work with no scrimmaging. Separately, coach JJ Redick said it is uncertain whether new center Walker Kessler will make his debut against the Warriors on Tuesday. Kessler practiced but did not participate in live action and could return to scrimmaging Sunday.
  • • Laker Kevon Looney described what Luka Doncic means to the team's confidence: "When you got a guy like Luka, going into the game you feel like you can beat anybody. We feel like if we do our job, he's going to do his job and he's going to clinch us the game."
  • • Scoot Henderson is getting rave reviews from his Portland Trail Blazers teammates this preseason. Toumani Camara said Henderson has put it all together mentally: "I think there's no one time this summer where I'm like, 'You should have done this, Scoot.'" Coach Micah Nori added that Hansen Yang has shown well, though his rotation spot this season may come down to foul trouble and matchups.
  • • Toronto Raptors guard Chucky Hepburn, who missed most of last season with a torn meniscus, left a preseason game grabbing at his left hamstring. Kawhi Leonard remains out for reconditioning, while Allen Graves, Trayce Jackson-Davis, Alijah Martin and Nate Bittle are also sidelined.
  • • Phoenix Suns coach Jordan Ott said Koa Peat's basketball IQ stands out every time he plays, adding that Peat "thinks the game in a different way" and they want to use him accordingly, including at center in small-ball lineups. Meanwhile, Devin Booker and Miles Bridges were both out for rest against San Antonio, with Bridges' ankle described as fine.
  • • De'Aaron Fox sat out for San Antonio in Phoenix with tightness, though the Spurs have no long-term concern. Alex Sarr made his preseason debut for Washington after mid-June foot surgery. Oklahoma City sent only its rookies and two-way players to Atlanta, with Mark Daigneault keeping veterans home.
  • • A Cooper Flagg NBA Debut Patch card sold for more than $8 million at public auction through Fanatics Collect, making it the fifth-most expensive sports card sale on record. The card came to its owner, South Carolina native Jason Williams, through an online box break run by a small Rhode Island card shop.
  • • Dwight Howard made his European debut in Georgia's top league, finishing with 12 points, 7 rebounds and 3 blocks in 17 minutes for Black Fortress Tbilisi despite a 70-75 loss to Iverioni.
  • • NBA Europe continues to generate debate on the continent. Galatasaray president Dursun Ozbek confirmed ongoing discussions with NBA officials about the new league. Former NBA coach Ettore Messina warned that launching without an agreement with EuroLeague risks worsening fragmentation: "We need to find a balance."
