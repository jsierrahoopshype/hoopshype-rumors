NBA Rumors Wrap: Klay Thompson opens up on Heat move, Mavs front office overhaul continues, Warriors round out roster

Klay Thompson held court with South Florida reporters Tuesday, giving his most detailed account yet of why he wanted out of Dallas and why Miami was always the destination. Thompson said the Mavericks were understanding when he told them where he wanted to be: "Miami was always on my mind. They understood that I really wanted a chance to make a deep postseason run." The five-time champion, who missed the playoffs entirely last season in Dallas, described Miami's culture as "very militaristic" with "really demanding" fitness programs, and said that environment was a feature, not a warning. He'll wear No. 11 again, a number he called "super special," and said playing within proximity of the Bahamas, where his family is from, added another layer of meaning to the move. Thompson also addressed his fit alongside Giannis Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo, saying the spacing opportunities are "almost limitless."

Thompson described the move explicitly as a reset: "It's kind of a redemption season for me to show I can still make an impact on winning and do it consistently." He said he had little prior relationship with coach Erik Spoelstra but trusted the recommendation from former Warriors coach Steve Kerr, who worked with Spoelstra through Team USA. Thompson also paid tribute to Ray Allen after being compared to Allen's famous Game 6 corner three, saying Allen and Reggie Miller "were the blueprint for my whole off-ball formula" and that being mentioned in the same sentence still gives him chills.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Dallas Mavericks' front office continues to shrink quietly. Vice president of strategy and analytics Andrew Baker and director of pro player personnel Makhtar Ndiaye parted ways with the franchise earlier this month, per Marc Stein. Both were hired by former GM Nico Harrison and continued under co-interim GMs Matt Riccardi and Michael Finley after Harrison was dismissed last November.
  • Meanwhile, rival teams continue to test Dallas's resolve on Kyrie Irving. The Mavericks have consistently rejected trade interest in the nine-time All-Star all summer, but teams around the league are quietly wondering how firm that stance will remain once the season starts and the February trade deadline draws closer.
  • The Golden State Warriors filled their final two roster spots Tuesday, signing guard Brandon Williams to a one-year, $2.6 million deal and forward Georges Niang to a one-year, $3.9 million deal. Both had been targets for over a month. Niang, a career 40% three-point shooter, missed all of last season with an injury but had appeared in at least 72 games in each of the five seasons prior.
  • The Williams signing has a nice backstory. Earlier this summer, Stephen Curry personally called Williams to recruit him, catching the guard mid-workout. Williams's trainer recalled the moment: "When he first came in, he was like, 'Oh, I gotta go home. I got a Zoom call with Wardell.'"
  • The Sacramento Kings are stretching DeMar DeRozan's remaining $10 million salary for 2026-27 over three years at $3.33 million per season, per HoopsHype. The move gets Sacramento under the luxury tax line with enough room to fill their last two roster spots on minimum deals before Saturday's stretch deadline.
  • The Kings may have another addition in the works. Sacramento GM Scott Perry traveled to Florida to watch free agent Ben Simmons work out, per Carmichael Dave.
  • Bennedict Mathurin is expected to either be sign-and-traded to the New Orleans Pelicans or opt into his qualifying offer to remain with the Clippers, per Jake Fischer via B/R. New Orleans has been the only team aggressively pursuing him this summer.
  • Jalen Duren is still on track to re-sign with the Detroit Pistons on a deal expected to reach four years and $160 million or more, per Fischer.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves' incoming operating owner Marc Stad is expected to have a strong working relationship with team president Tim Connelly, which matters given Connelly is entering the final year of his contract and needs a new deal. The formal league approval process to install Stad as operating owner could take until January.
  • The Kawhi Leonard investigation is not resolving anytime soon. Despite commissioner Adam Silver saying in July that it "needs to be wrapped up before the beginning of next season," sources told ESPN the probe could drag into 2027 if the parties disagree on findings or go to arbitration.
  • Bloomberg reported Tuesday that an LLC controlled by LeBron James borrowed nearly $300 million from two Midwestern life insurers arranged through Guggenheim Partners in 2018, around the time he signed with the Lakers. A spokesperson for James said the transactions were independently credit rated and the 2022 deal was fully approved by the NBA, and stressed James has no affiliation with Guggenheim or the insurers involved beyond the transactions themselves. The report notes there is no indication the loans are connected to the federal probe into Mark Walter, who agreed to sell the Lakers this month.
  • The Chicago Sky have banned Enes Kanter Freedom from Wintrust Arena indefinitely after an incident at a recent game. Sky owner Michael Alter said Freedom bought his ticket under a different name, and multiple witnesses told the team that Freedom initiated the confrontation with player Natasha Cloud. The WNBA does not plan to impose a league-wide ban, but Freedom maintains he was there to support a friend and did nothing wrong. Freedom also disputed reports that he is ineligible for the WNBA draft, saying no one from the league has contacted him about any ineligibility.
  • Michael Porter Jr. said in a new interview that releasing Kentavious Caldwell-Pope was the turning point for Denver: "I don't know why they let KCP go. That was the beginning of our downfall." He also spoke about working out with Kyrie Irving recently, saying Irving was moving freely coming off his ACL rehab: "I was hooping with him yesterday and this is off of his ACL. That dude was so cold, bro."
  • Lonzo Ball opened up on his shoe regrets in a new interview, saying he never heard offers from Nike or Adidas because he was just doing what he was told. "I guess that would be my one regret, like taking control of my career earlier," he said, adding that when he raised concerns about the Big Baller Brand shoes, it was framed as going against his family.
