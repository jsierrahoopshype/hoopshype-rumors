NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade buzz grows, Knicks go up 2-0, Mitchell Robinson tech rescinded

Giannis Antetokounmpo's future in Milwaukee is generating more serious chatter around the league. Chris Haynes said Giannis has come as close as he ever has to signaling he wants out without explicitly asking for a trade, and added that the Knicks could become players for him if they stumble against the Spurs in the Finals. On the team most often connected to Giannis in trade speculation, league sources with knowledge of the Thunder's thinking continue to dismiss Oklahoma City as a legitimate suitor, per Marc Stein, with Sam Presti's history of avoiding massive financial commitments cited as the reason. The Heat are also in the mix in an indirect way: Miami is weighing whether to keep or trade the No. 13 pick, and the team has already announced a draft party with language suggesting they expect to use it. What happens in these Finals matters too — a Knicks title could close that window for Milwaukee to find a New York landing spot.

On the trade market more broadly, Domantas Sabonis is expected to be made available by Sacramento again this summer, behind Giannis and Ja Morant on the list of names league executives are projecting to move. The Kings are known to be looking to shed salary and are likely to field more interest in Sabonis than in other veterans on the roster.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The NBA rescinded the technical foul assessed to Mitchell Robinson during his second-quarter shoving match with Victor Wembanyama in Game 2. Wembanyama did not receive a tech on the play. Robinson held Wembanyama scoreless on two possessions in the final 30 seconds of the game, and Karl-Anthony Towns called it "the biggest play in the game." Robinson, who has spent eight years in New York, said afterward: "Been here for 8 years, now here we are in the Finals. I can truly say I done seen it all."
  • • The Knicks are heading into Game 3 on the second-longest single-season win streak in NBA playoff history and have won eight straight road playoff games. They are just the third team to win Games 1 and 2 of the Finals on the road. Jalen Brunson, whose steal and free throws sealed the Game 2 win, said he noticed Wembanyama wasn't looking and went for it. "I saw he wasn't looking so I just tried to go get it."
  • • The NBA's official two-minute report found no errors by referees in the final two minutes of Game 2. On a late play involving Luke Kornet, the league ruled: "Although Kornet lifts his foot, he does not intentionally kick the loose ball."
  • • Mikal Bridges credited his prior Finals experience for helping him stay composed in the series. "I think just being here before, knowing how it's going to be. Just a lot of experience, knowing how much effort you need to give every single moment," he said. Coach Mike Brown trusted Bridges to anchor the lineup to close out the third quarter and start the fourth, and Bridges said it all started with getting defensive stops.
  • • The celebration outside Madison Square Garden after Game 2 turned ugly. A watch party of several thousand fans erupted into chaos after the Knicks won, with police taking 26 people into custody — 17 arrests and nine summonses for disorderly conduct. One person punched a 23-year-old female officer in the face. Mayor Zohran Kwame Mamdani called the assault unacceptable and said there is "no tolerance for attacks on police officers." The Knicks have announced a no-bag policy for Game 3 and are asking fans to arrive two hours early for screening.
  • • In a different corner of New York, roughly 2,000 people incarcerated at Rikers Island across about 44 housing units gathered to watch Game 1 of the Finals. Those in an "honors house" — reserved for people who have gone at least 120 days without a disciplinary incident — received snacks and were allowed to stay up past the normal 9pm lock-in. "It's the chemistry and the teamwork that makes them great," said one man watching from the Beacon Center. "This is the year they finally might get it done."
  • • The Spurs' Luke Kornet went viral during the series for a moment before Game 5 against the Thunder when a Catholic nun placed her hand on his head and prayed for him. The Spurs won that night. Before Game 1 of the Finals, Wembanyama was standing courtside when he turned to the sisters and asked: "Sisters, could you please pray for me?" After the Game 2 loss, Kornet said the sisters need to be on the plane to Oklahoma City. "We've got to get 'em on the plane," he said.
  • • Sources insist that trading Chet Holmgren is not part of the Thunder's plans this offseason, despite Oklahoma City facing difficult roster decisions on their team options for Hartenstein, Dort and Williams. One source close to the team's playoff exit described Holmgren as "the best defensive player in the league ... aside from one."
  • • Cleveland has a pressing roster decision beyond its future agreement with James Harden. Team officials are already conceding behind the scenes that it will be extremely difficult to keep both Dean Wade and Keon Ellis as they try to move out of the second apron.
  • • The Knicks' Mitchell Robinson is expected to draw significant free agent interest this summer, even after playing just 14 minutes in Game 2. Marc Stein reports Robinson is seen as Hartenstein's heir at backup center in New York, with coach Mike Brown and Karl-Anthony Towns both praising his defense on Wembanyama postgame.
  • • Jeremy Lin and Carmelo Anthony are in contact about a possible appearance on Anthony's podcast, but Lin wants a private conversation first. "My desire is still the same. I'd like to have a private conversation before having a public recorded conversation," Lin said. Anthony has said he wants to "clear the air" on stories that have followed them since their time together on the Knicks.
  • • Marc Stein reports the Timberwolves' future is in question following their playoff exit. Brian Windhorst said the uncertainty stems partly from what Anthony Edwards feels about the roster, adding: "I wonder if KAT was still there, if he did."
  • • Draft prospect Cameron Boozer met with the Wizards at the combine and came away encouraged. "I think I'd fit well there with their vision and their plan for the future," he said. The son of former All-NBA big man Carlos Boozer has leaned on his winning pedigree as his calling card heading into the draft. "My competitiveness and my will to win, I think those are things that will translate," he said.
  • • The Thunder have held individual pre-draft workouts with Michigan big men Yaxel Lendeborg and Morez Johnson Jr. The Magic hosted a workout that included Kylan Boswell, BJ Edwards, Nate Johnson and Tobi Lawal.
  • • The Marc Stein piece also revisited the origin of the Jalen Brunson-Dallas split. When the Mavericks offered Brunson a 4-year, $55M extension near the 2022 trade deadline, his camp essentially laughed at the offer. "The Brunson camp basically laughed at them and said, 'Uh, we'll take our chances in free agency now, you guys had your chance,'" per The Stein Line. Dallas had the option to go as high as $175 million over five years but didn't make the call.
