NBA Rumors Wrap: Mitchell Robinson reveals injury backstory, LeBron James spotted in Akron, Nuggets' Watson decision

Mitchell Robinson broke his silence on the hand injury that clouded the early days of the New York Knicks' NBA Finals run, revealing in a Facebook post Sunday that he hurt himself after learning his youngest brother had been in a car crash with potentially life-threatening injuries. Robinson wrote that despite "personal issues, relationship problems, and internal struggles" throughout the season, he ultimately felt confident enough to play and help New York claim the title. The post came shortly after Robinson agreed to a three-year, $47.4 million deal to join the Boston Celtics, per Shams Charania. Deals cannot be made official until Monday when the moratorium lifts. "After consulting with doctors, I was able to gain the confidence in myself to go in and get the job done and WE DID 2026 NBA CHAMPS," Robinson wrote.

In New York, artists and fans are making sure the championship run isn't forgotten. A gallery's worth of murals remains on city walls, including a painting of Jalen Brunson on the Lower East Side and one of Robinson dunking on 30th Street in Manhattan. "This is how I can keep that moment alive," said the artist who painted Robinson. "We're documenting history, for real."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The LeBron James free agency watch got more interesting Sunday. James was spotted in Akron with his St. Vincent-St. Mary teammates, including Cleveland Cavaliers assistant GM Brandon Weems. Around the same time, LeBron's odds of landing with Cleveland on prediction market Polymarket surged from 39.9% to 51.7% over a six-hour window.
  • Draymond Green, himself a free agent this summer, weighed in on LeBron's decision on his podcast. "People don't understand the weight that LeBron James carries," Green said. "Still, after 24 years, I still think people don't understand the force, the weight that this guy carries. From an overall standpoint, the economics that he brings to a team, the economic impact that he may have on a city."
  • The Denver Nuggets intend to match any offer sheet for restricted free agent Peyton Watson once the moratorium lifts Monday, per league sources. The Nuggets and Watson's agent, Rich Paul, remain apart in contract talks, and Denver is also open to a sign-and-trade for the 23-year-old forward. The Los Angeles Clippers have shown genuine interest in Watson but are also sorting out their own restricted free agency situation with Bennedict Mathurin, which complicates any pursuit.
  • Chris Mannix reported that at least one team made a legitimate offer to Boston for Jaylen Brown before the Philadelphia 76ers deal was done, and that team's front office was stunned the Celtics agreed to the Philly package. "Mostly because they knew their offer wasn't very good," Mannix said, "but they told me, this person told me, that the offer would have gotten better. They were probing to see how low can they go." Separately, analyst Rich Bucher argued the core reason Boston moved Brown was financial: "a franchise can't possibly compete for a title with two players signed to super max contracts."
  • Steve Clifford is joining the Washington Wizards as a coaching advisor, per Shams Charania. Clifford previously held similar advisor roles with the Suns and Nets and has a longstanding connection to Wizards head coach Patrick Ewing, having worked together in Orlando and Charlotte over a decade. On a related note, lead assistant David Vanterpool is moving into a front-office role with Washington, with Adam Caporn stepping up as lead assistant.
  • HoopsHype's trade machine data shows that over one-quarter of all trades submitted recently have included Jarred Vanderbilt, the Lakers' defensive forward with two years and $25.7 million left on his deal. Separately, the most common trade involving New Orleans Pelicans forward Trey Murphy sends him to the Atlanta Hawks in exchange for a package built around reigning Most Improved Player Nickeil Alexander-Walker and big man Onyeka Okongwu, per HoopsHype.
  • On the Toronto Raptors trade front, 84.4 percent of theoretical Jakob Poeltl trades in the HoopsHype trade machine have included teammate RJ Barrett in the deal, as Toronto fans use their combined $49.1 million salary to target stars in the machine.
  • The Lakers signed AK Okereke to a two-way contract, filling out their two-way roster alongside Chris Mañon and Peter Suder. On the summer league floor Sunday, rookie Cameron Carr put up 26 points on 7-of-16 shooting with four threes and eight rebounds as L.A. beat Miami 93-91 in overtime to go 1-1 at the California Classic.
  • The Celtics signed rookie Chris Cenac Jr. to fill out their 15-man roster. Cenac's rookie scale runs from $3.0 million this season through a $5.9 million team option in 2029-30.
  • The Utah Jazz have been busy on the two-way front. Trey Alexander, who played for Denver and New Orleans over the past two seasons, is signing a two-way deal with Utah, joining Tamar Bates, who also signed a two-year, two-way contract with the Jazz. Jazz rookie Darryn Peterson summed up his summer league experience simply: "I got Jazz across my chest now, so it's a huge honor and privilege."
  • The Detroit Pistons signed second-round pick Ugonna Onyenso to a two-year, two-way contract.
  • Nikola Vucevic received a standing ovation from a tearful arena after retiring from Montenegro's national team. Meanwhile, Utah Jazz forward Svi Mykhailiuk dropped 30 points on 9-of-16 shooting, including 6-of-10 from three, to lead Ukraine to a 99-69 win over Denmark in FIBA World Cup qualifying.
  • Kostas Antetokounmpo has signed with Aris Thessaloniki through 2029. The 28-year-old center, who won an NBA title with the Lakers in 2020, had been on loan at Aris from Olympiacos during the 2025-26 season.
  • Isaac Bonga is finalizing a multi-year deal with Panathinaikos after his preferred NBA opportunity didn't materialize this offseason. The agreement will include NBA exit clauses each summer.
  • Oklahoma City Thunder rookie Otega Oweh described himself as a "junkyard dog" at his introductory press conference, tracing it back to growing up as the youngest of many siblings. "Being the youngest, you've got to have that chip on your shoulder," Oweh said. He also praised teammate Aday Mara's impact on both ends: "On defense, you can be a little more handsy and a little more aggressive, knowing he'll be in the paint to clean it up."
