NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron officially a Sixer, Heat target Klay Thompson, Kuminga pursuit heats up

LeBron James has officially signed with the Philadelphia 76ers, ending one of the more surprising free agency decisions of the modern era. He will wear No. 23 in Philadelphia, joining Tyrese Maxey, Jaylen Brown, Joel Embiid and VJ Edgecombe on a roster that now has championship ambitions after years of stagnation. Those close to James say they believe him when he said in his statement that he thought he "was done when the season ended," but that leading the Lakers to a first-round upset of the Rockets while Luka Doncic and Austin Reaves were injured rekindled something in him. One source told Marc Stein that Philadelphia's proximity to New York City emerged as a meaningful bonus on top of the chance to chase a fifth ring with a fourth different franchise. Per Shams Charania, Brown was fully on board from the start, having reached out to Rich Paul directly during the process to say he wanted LeBron on the team.

The Sixers' roster construction is already drawing scrutiny from around the league. One Eastern Conference executive told ESPN the moves were understandable for a franchise that needed to break through, calling the Brown acquisition a good risk and the James signing on a minimum "outstanding," but adding: "You've got a lot of guys who want the ball in their hands. Who's setting screens? Who's doing the dirty work? Who's defending? A lot of that is going to fall to LeBron." Charania said Philadelphia expects James to play significant time at point guard, setting the table for the team's collection of scorers. One NBA coach offered the bluntest assessment yet of where the franchise stands: "The Process is over. This needs to be the product."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Miami Heat have emerged as one of the most aggressive teams this offseason on multiple fronts. Charania reported that Miami is among several championship-contending teams monitoring Klay Thompson's situation, with interest in acquiring him via trade or buyout. Shooting is the Heat's stated priority, and Thompson sits at the top of their list, ahead of even the Pat Riley playmaking rhetoric. DeMar DeRozan is also drawing Miami's attention, with Denver and Cleveland among the other teams in that mix as well.
  • Thompson, for his part, has been working out at the NBPA's offseason retreat in Andalucia, Spain, with the NBPA's social accounts tagging him as a Maverick heading into next season. He is in the final year of his deal in Dallas. A lot has changed there since he signed on to play with Luka Doncic and Kyrie Irving, and teams are clearly watching.
  • On Kyrie: Dallas has continued to rebuff trade feelers for the 34-year-old, and the working expectation is that he will open next season alongside Rookie of the Year Cooper Flagg. Charania added that Irving's ACL rehab has gone well and that he is fully recovered and entrenched in the Mavericks' offseason plans.
  • Dallas also held early-stage extension talks with Dereck Lively II, per Marc Stein. Lively is eligible for a new deal through Opening Night Eve but played just seven games in 2025-26 after foot surgery. Separately, veteran center Dwight Powell and his representatives have maintained dialogue with the Mavericks about a potential roster spot, though Dallas currently sits one player above the in-season maximum at 16.
  • The Lakers and Cavaliers are among the teams seriously pursuing Jonathan Kuminga via sign-and-trade from the Atlanta Hawks, who declined his team option this summer. Brian Windhorst said Cleveland's agent communication has been positive but that the financial pathways for the Cavs to make it work don't look realistic from where he sits. Cleveland also has James Harden still unsigned, though Charania said full expectation is that Harden returns and the two sides will reach an agreement soon.
  • Jalen Duren's contract standoff with the Detroit Pistons is nowhere close to resolution. Charania said both sides are dug in and this could drag deep into September. Meanwhile, Denver's Peyton Watson is in a similar spot, weighing the prospect of riding out a qualifying offer to become an unrestricted free agent next summer, knowing that doing so means leaving significant money on the table.
  • Stephen Curry's future in Golden State appears secure. Charania said he fully expects extension talks to begin in late August when Curry becomes eligible, with mutual interest on both sides for either a one- or two-year deal.
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo, now with the Miami Heat, opened up about his time in Milwaukee. "I feel like they were my family," he said. "You never know in life, maybe our roads meet again. But for now, let's go win a championship." He also explained his number change from 34 to 7: his parents were born in 1963 and 1964, so he combined their numbers to get 34 for years, then realized 3+4 equals 7, and his mother was born on April 7th.
  • Kentavious Caldwell-Pope gave back $3.9 million in his buyout from the Memphis Grizzlies, per HoopsHype, matching the salary he will earn on his new deal with the Philadelphia 76ers. The Sixers still need to create first-apron room before he can sign, with options including waiving or trading Johni Broome or Jabari Walker.
  • Dave Joerger has agreed to join the Denver Nuggets as lead assistant coach under head coach David Adelman.
  • The Portland Trail Blazers' arena lease situation is deteriorating. The team declined to attend a City Council work session and has not responded to the council's written invitation or provided requested documentation. Blazers president Dewayne Hankins said he wants to avoid negotiating through the media, but City Council president Jamie Dunphy called the team's non-participation "very disappointing."
  • The Sixers are expected to receive the maximum 34 national game telecasts across ABC/ESPN, NBC Sports/Peacock, and Amazon Prime Video this season. That would more than double the 14 national appearances the Embiid-led Sixers had in 2025-26.
  • Mikal Bridges addressed his struggles with the Knicks directly: "When they feel like I'm struggling, trust me, I know I am, and I'm trying to do whatever it takes to get over that hump."
  • Former Blazers guard Isaiah Rider was taken into custody in Arizona for failing to comply with a court order, per TMZ.
