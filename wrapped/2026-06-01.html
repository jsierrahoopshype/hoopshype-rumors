NBA Rumors Wrap: Lakers weigh LeBron vs. Reaves priority, Donovan Mitchell extension talks, Thunder face costly offseason

The Los Angeles Lakers are preparing for a pivotal free agency period, and the order of business may surprise some: according to reporter Jovan Buha, the franchise is leaning toward prioritizing Austin Reaves over LeBron James when it comes to handing out big money this summer. "So if it comes down to, would you rather pay Reaves $40 million for the next five years, or LeBron $40 million for one year, they're going to prioritize the long-term contract here," Buha said. LeBron, meanwhile, is waiting for the Lakers to make the first move. Per ESPN's Brian Windhorst, James' camp wants the team to come forward with a specific offer and plan before any decision is made. "The LeBron side, from what I am told, would like the Lakers to come and say, 'LeBron, here's our plan for you. Here's what we're offering,'" Windhorst said on "The Hoop Collective." The Lakers are also monitoring the market for center Jalen Duren, and Buha says retaining Reaves and signing Duren are not mutually exclusive, since Reaves' contract situation operates separately from the team's cap space.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers are optimistic about locking up Donovan Mitchell long-term. Mitchell becomes eligible for an extension on July 7, and Cavs president Koby Altman offered no ambiguity about where things stand: "Donovan has been steadfast in how much he loves it here, and so there hasn't been any question of will he be here and does he want to be here."
  • The Oklahoma City Thunder head into the offseason with real financial pressure after their Game 7 loss to the Spurs. The team is projected to sit roughly $39 million above the second apron next season, and they hold team options on Isaiah Hartenstein ($28.5 million) and Lu Dort ($18.2 million) that will define their roster construction. League sources have long maintained OKC would not pursue Giannis Antetokounmpo in trade talks, though the stunning series loss is enough to revive that conversation.
  • Both Hartenstein and Dort addressed their futures during OKC's exit availability. Hartenstein, who has a team option coming, said "a lot of it's in their hands" but expressed genuine gratitude for his time in Oklahoma City. Dort was equally direct: "I want to stay here. This organization, this city shaped me as a player. My main goal is to stay here."
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander called the season "a failure" by his own standards. He also addressed the roster decisions ahead, saying he trusts Sam Presti to handle the front office work while he focuses on playing: "I wear the Shai hat. Sam wears the Sam hat."
  • Jalen Williams acknowledged he was nowhere near full strength during the playoffs. "Everybody in the world knows I wasn't myself," he said, adding that he played nine minutes in Game 6 without even attempting a shot. Coach Mark Daigneault said getting Williams healthy is the summer's top priority: "I'm excited about a healthy Jalen Williams. I'm excited about a healthy team."
  • Chet Holmgren struggled all series against the Spurs, finishing Game 7 with four points after posting eight, 13, 14, 10, 16 and 10 in the previous games. Daigneault backed him publicly, saying "every minute Chet Holmgren's been on the team, we've been the 1 seed in the Western Conference." Holmgren, for his part, said losses "don't suck any less" but that he's gotten better at processing them objectively rather than emotionally.
  • Cason Wallace is eligible for an extension this summer and said he's not focused on the money: "I'm not money driven. I want to compete, that's the main focus of me." Kenrich Williams was equally open about wanting to stay, saying "it's no secret that I want to be here until I'm done playing."
  • Julian Champagnie's path to the NBA Finals started with getting cut by Philadelphia on Valentine's Day 2023 as an undrafted 21-year-old out of St. John's. "I thought it was over, I ain't going to lie to you," he said of that moment. He finished the Western Conference Finals as one of the few players in NBA history with more 3-pointers than Stephen Curry in a conference finals Game 7. The Spurs hold a team option on him, and the expectation is they will decline it and re-sign him to a better deal.
  • Victor Wembanyama said winning the Larry O'Brien Trophy is "almost like the meaning of my life," describing a championship as a childhood dream that now has a tangible chance of becoming real. He also said he needs to call Gregg Popovich immediately: "There's no way I can understand right now how he feels."
  • Josh Hart was asked at Knicks practice what New York can take from its NBA Cup loss to San Antonio back in December. His answer: nothing, technically. "Technically, that game didn't happen," Hart said. He also had a response ready for Wembanyama guarding him at the perimeter: "The only unanimous Defensive Player of the Year is guarding me, so that's a good sign for me, right?"
  • The Sacramento Kings met with the family of Houston point guard Kingston Flemings this weekend in San Antonio, per a league source. Flemings is among the guards the Kings are considering with the seventh pick in the NBA Draft. Sacramento also announced a pre-draft workout for Monday featuring Quadir Copeland, Melvin Council, Tre White, Nick Martinelli, KeShawn Murphy and Caden Powell.
  • Concerns about the Portland Trail Blazers' future in the city are growing. One former team employee and local media figure said owner Tom Dundon "is going to do everything in his power to move them," comparing the situation to the Seattle SuperSonics relocation and saying Dundon is running the franchise the way he runs his NHL team, the Carolina Hurricanes.
  • Bill Simmons floated the Celtics as a possible landing spot for Trey Murphy if New Orleans trades the forward in the next few weeks, adding that whichever team lost the Spurs-Thunder series would also be in the mix.
  • Jeremy Sochan is set to receive a 2026 NBA championship ring regardless of who wins the Finals. He began the season with the Knicks before moving to San Antonio, making him eligible for a ring from New York's championship if they win the title.
