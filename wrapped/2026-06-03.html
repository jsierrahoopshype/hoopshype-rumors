NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks ruled out as Giannis destination, Kyrie trade calls expected, Curry signs $400M Li-Ning deal **Shams Charania** confirmed Tuesday that the **New York Knicks** are no longer in play for **Giannis Antetokounmpo**, removing the most-watched potential suitor from the board now that New York has reached the Finals. Per Shams Charania, the two sides never got close to a deal, with the Knicks feeling the **Bucks** had no real interest in moving him and Milwaukee feeling New York's offers weren't strong enough. "We can all safely say the Knicks are not going to be one of the teams Giannis will be on next season," Charania said on Tracy McGrady's Cousins podcast. "There are going to be other destinations for that." The Knicks' path to the Finals closes off what would have been a blockbuster offseason trade and forces the rest of the league to recalibrate around who can actually put together a convincing package for Milwaukee. Here are more notes from around the league: • **Kyrie Irving** is not expected to remain with the Mavericks into next season, according to Jake Fischer. "I don't necessarily believe that Kyrie Irving will be on the Mavericks roster come opening night," Fischer said on his Bleacher Report livestream. "There will be contending teams that would make trade calls to Dallas." • **Stephen Curry** has signed a 10-year endorsement deal with Li-Ning worth over $400 million, per Shams Charania. Curry had comparable or larger offers from other brands but chose Li-Ning to build out Curry Brand. • **Jaylen Brown**'s hometown of Atlanta has emerged as a team keeping close tabs on the **Celtics** star, according to Fischer, though Boston has shown no indication it is actively shopping him. "Atlanta is Jaylen Brown's hometown," Fischer said. "I think that's a spot that he would potentially have interest in going to." • The **Spurs-Knicks** NBA Finals tips off Wednesday, and scouts and coaches around the league are more skeptical of San Antonio's advantage than the betting market suggests. "They're really surprised that the Spurs are favored by as much as they are," Brian Windhorst said. "They really believe that the Knicks have some advantages in the series and that this thing could absolutely go New York's way." • New York enters on a historic run, posting the greatest 11-game stretch by any team in the NBA's 80-year history, with a plus-262 point differential across their playoff win streak. Their 123.3 offensive rating through the East bracket was the highest by an East champion since the current format began in 2003, topping LeBron James' 2017 Cavs. • **Jalen Brunson** came away from media day with clear respect for **Stephon Castle**. "He plays with a chip on his shoulder," Brunson said. "He's had that since I've seen him at UConn." Castle, for his part, described his approach to guarding Brunson: "Trying to be as disciplined as I can, crowd his space, but not give him the angles that he's looking for." • **Victor Wembanyama** joins Kobe Bryant (2001) and LeBron James (2007) as the only players this century to lead their team in scoring entering the Finals at age 22 or younger. When asked who he's playing for, Wemby kept it direct: "My first answer would be myself. I think it doesn't get more complicated than that." • After the emotional Game 7 win over OKC, Wembanyama treated the Spurs to a team screening of the new horror movie *Obsession* as the team tried to wind down from the weight of the moment. • **Kevin Garnett** had sharp words for two players on opposite ends of the series. On Wemby crying after the Western Conference Finals, Garnett said: "He's crying in the motherf---g Western Conference Finals? That was too emotional for me. He's got four more games to try to get." On **Chet Holmgren**, Garnett was equally blunt: "Chet was the biggest letdown in the playoffs." • **Mitchell Robinson** remains a question mark for the Knicks in Game 1. Coach **Mike Brown** said he was unsure how New York would list Robinson on the injury report and that he needed to consult the medical staff about Robinson's availability. Robinson has done only individual work since his hand injury, with no contact in practice yet. • **Mitchell Robinson**'s status is part of a broader Finals build-up concern for New York, with anonymous coaches flagging whether **Karl-Anthony Towns** is in shape to defend in Hartenstein-style pick-and-roll coverages against Wembanyama. "Karl has to be really disciplined," one coach said. • Knicks owner **James Dolan** said the organization knew it was in a pressure spot the moment it moved on from **Tom Thibodeau** and hired **Mike Brown**. "We knew it from the moment that we said goodbye to Thibs and we hired Mike, that we were gonna be in a hot spot," Dolan said. Brown described Dolan as someone who challenges the front office and players "in the right way" and backs up his demands with resources. • The Spurs-Thunder Western Conference Finals averaged 10.8 million viewers per game on NBC/Peacock, making it the most-watched Conference Finals in 24 years. Game 7 averaged 15.9 million viewers and peaked at 17.7 million. • The **Indiana Fever** have revoked the credential of longtime Pacers and Fever beat reporter Scott Agness after a social media report they said spread "inaccurate and unsubstantiated" information about **Caitlin Clark**'s health status. Agness stood by his reporting and said he believed his post was actually helping the Fever by clarifying Clark had no new injury. The day after Agness published, the Fever received a warning from the WNBA regarding their injury reporting procedures, and Clark has since appeared on the injury report for three straight games listed as probable with a back issue. • The **OKC Thunder** are expected to be aggressive pursuing a contract extension with **Cason Wallace**, per ESPN's Bobby Marks. Separately, Marks said anyone expecting the Thunder to simply decline **Isaiah Hartenstein**'s option and lose him for nothing is misreading the situation: if the option is declined, it likely means a longer-term deal at reduced money is already agreed upon. • **Darius Garland** and **Lonzo Ball** both poured cold water on the idea of LeBron James returning to Cleveland. "Sunny days, 75 every day," Garland said of LeBron's life in LA. Ball added: "He's got a family and he's in LA. He's been here for the last X amount of years. It's hard to just pack up and then go back to Cleveland."
