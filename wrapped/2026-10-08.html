NBA Rumors Wrap: Buss siblings file formal opposition to Jeanie's lawsuit, EuroLeague rejects NBA merger offer, LeBron-Embiid story put to rest

The legal battle over the Los Angeles Lakers escalated Wednesday when five of Jeanie Buss's siblings filed formal court opposition to her petition seeking to block the sale of the family trust's ownership stake, per Shams Charania. Jim, Johnny, Janie, Joey and Jesse Buss called Jeanie's petition "legally and factually baseless" in their statement. The siblings' filing argues that Jeanie is acting in her own self-interest to preserve her role as controlling owner, pointing to a side agreement she made with Mark Walter in October 2025 guaranteeing her position for five years, subject to NBA approval. Their goal, according to a source familiar with their thinking, is for the sale to close without legal delays, with the siblings also insisting that any arrangement allowing Jeanie to retain shares be offered equally to all of them. The filing highlights the $2.2 billion the trust stands to earn through the tag-along provision by selling to the Josh Kushner and Bob Iger group at the reported $12.5 billion valuation.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The EuroLeague has rejected the NBA's merger proposal, but the league's plans for NBA Europe are pushing forward regardless. The intent is a 16-team league that could launch as early as the 2027-28 season, though a senior sports executive told Reuters any collaboration is unlikely before 2028, given the timeline required to organize a new competition.
  • Several EuroLeague clubs from cities on the NBA's expansion target list want to keep the conversation going. Real Madrid, Fenerbahce, Panathinaikos, ASVEL Lyon and Bayern Munich have signaled openness to dialogue with the NBA, while Barcelona and Olimpia Milan were far more reserved. The sticking point for most shareholders is control: the NBA wants full authority over the project, and that is a difficult concession for clubs that have invested in European basketball for decades.
  • Luka Doncic-backed BC Roma SPQR has submitted a binding bid for one of the eight new EuroLeague expansion franchises, bringing the total number of candidates to 14. The EuroLeague's next board meeting will determine which clubs are awarded spots.
  • Broadcaster Mike Breen apologized Wednesday for a story he shared during Monday's preseason broadcast that suggested LeBron James called out Joel Embiid for arriving late to practice. "I'm so disappointed how the story was interpreted," Breen told ESPN. "The whole intent was praising LeBron for already having an impact on the team's professionalism." After James denied the report on social media, Breen accepted James' account: "If LeBron says it never happened then I believe him and apologize for putting it out there."
  • Nick Nurse backed his star center in no uncertain terms. "He's been excellent," Nurse said of Embiid. "He's in great shape, he's been on the practice floor, he's worked very hard." Nurse added that he and the organization had already discussed how to handle outside noise that comes with a roster of this profile.
  • Jusuf Nurkic will miss at least four weeks after suffering a partial plantar plate tear in his right foot, per Chris Haynes. The Jazz center's injury is a significant blow to Utah's frontcourt depth to start the season.
  • Brandon Ingram is ahead of schedule in his recovery from injury, according to Jake Fischer. "I heard optimism that Ingram is gonna be back on the floor sooner than really anticipated," Fischer said. The news matters for the Clippers, who are counting on Ingram as a franchise cornerstone.
  • Karl-Anthony Towns has yet to reach an extension with the Knicks, and the clock is ticking. Insider Ian Begley said he does not expect talks to be tabled once the season starts, warning that letting it drag to mid-January puts both sides in a difficult position. Meanwhile, former NBA player Shannon Johnson argued Towns should be seeking more than $200 million, the figure Jalen Duren just signed for in Cleveland. Towns called his favorite memory from the Knicks' title run OG Anunoby's Game 4 tip-in against the Spurs: "For 53 years Knicks fans thinking something had to go wrong, and something went right."
  • Klay Thompson nearly got emotional recounting a moment from his Warriors days that reframed how he sees new teammate Giannis Antetokounmpo. After Thompson went off for 38 points against Milwaukee in 2023, Giannis walked the length of the court to congratulate him. "To be one of the 75 best players ever and not to be bitter in a moment where your team loses, and I have a great game," Thompson told The Athletic, "that made me look at him in a different light." Now teammates in Miami, Thompson said he was "so impressed" with Giannis's passing in their preseason opener and is looking forward to being on the receiving end of his playmaking all season.
  • Kawhi Leonard is easing into the Raptors' system after going through his second full practice following a week of lighter work at training camp in Quebec City. "I feel good but being smart, taking it slow," Leonard said, adding that he is still learning the plays and getting names right with coaches and support staff. Guard Jamal Shead said Leonard has been clear he wants to fit into the offense, not dominate the ball: "He wants to be in the flow and play off every single person."
  • Donovan Mitchell made his commitment to Cleveland official by signing his extension at the first opportunity. "Actions speak louder than words," Mitchell said. "Showing is better than telling. I'm here." He also said he believes this Cavaliers team can be a top-five defense, citing the addition of Peyton Watson and a full camp to install new wrinkles.
  • Shaquille O'Neal is skeptical Charles Barkley is actually done. "Once somebody taught Charles how to go viral, it was over," O'Neal told Front Office Sports. "Do I believe it? No, I don't believe it." When asked whether Inside the NBA could survive without Barkley, O'Neal gave a one-word answer: "No."
  • Seattle SuperSonics artifacts, including the 1995-96 Western Conference championship banner and locker name plates from former players, are going on public display at the Museum of History and Industry as the prospect of an NBA return to the city grows closer. Separately, BlackSun Private Equity said it is finalizing an investment group and architect for a potential arena project that could require at least $6 billion, with transportation impact studies underway.
  • Trail Blazers coach Micah Nori said he is eager to see how Portland integrates Damian Lillard and Ja Morant into a group that already made the playoffs last season. "Two guys that are dynamic pieces," Nori said, adding that Deni Avdija will be asked to take on more of a two-way role this year with more offensive firepower around him.
