NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks end 53-year drought, Brunson wins Finals MVP, Wemby vows return

The New York Knicks are NBA champions for the first time since 1973, ending a 53-year drought with a 94-90 win over the San Antonio Spurs in Game 5 of the NBA Finals on Saturday night in San Antonio. Jalen Brunson scored 45 points in the clincher and was named Finals MVP, becoming the fourth player listed at 6-foot-2 or shorter to win the award, joining Stephen Curry, Tony Parker and Isiah Thomas. The Knicks trailed for 69% of the series by game time, yet rallied late in every contest, a pattern that defined the entire run. Brunson arrived at the podium holding both trophies and, grinning, asked the room: "Do I be myself or do I talk my shit?"

The Knicks trailed in the fourth quarter of all five games yet found a way each time. One statistical footnote captures just how close it was: if the games had ended at the 46-minute mark, the Spurs would have won the series in five, and San Antonio actually won clutch-time minutes across the series 39-38, shooting 38% in those moments to New York's 32%. None of that mattered when the final buzzer sounded. Mitchell Robinson sprinted through the corridor yelling "Eight years!" as the Knicks celebrated on the Frost Bank Center court, cigars lit, in enemy territory.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Victor Wembanyama was measured and direct after the loss. "What I'm pissed about is that there's probably 100 games before we can get back to the Finals," he said. "I'm going to have to hold that inside of me and slow down and wait." He also called it the biggest learning moment of his life: "This doesn't compare to anything before. I can't tell exactly what the lesson is, but we're learning from that for sure." The Spurs can now begin negotiating Wembanyama's rookie max extension.
  • • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson did not flinch when asked what he wished his team had done differently against Brunson's 45-point night. "Make him score less points," he said. Johnson also acknowledged the bigger picture: "We weren't ready to win an NBA championship. The better team won."
  • • De'Aaron Fox said the most frustrating part of the series was the Spurs building double-digit leads in every game and watching them disappear. "Don't want to sit here and be like you were the better team," Fox said, "but every game we were able to build up this lead and kind of just watch it wither away."
  • • Devin Vassell thanked Spurs fans for their loyalty across six seasons, from 20-win campaigns to the Finals. On a related note, Vassell said rookie Dylan Harper was frustrated with his role and playing time during the season, but "when we needed him most he stepped up." Harper, for his part, fell 27 points short of Kareem Abdul-Jabbar's record for most playoff points by a rookie in a single season. Kareem set that record of 352 in just 10 playoff games.
  • • Karl-Anthony Towns stopped on the walkway leaving the court to lean into the stands and let Knicks fans touch the trophy. "They reacted like it was the best moment of their lives," Fred Katz wrote. Towns also shouted out former Timberwolves teammate Anthony Edwards: "Those guys made me better, they made me a better leader, they made me a better player."
  • • Josh Hart had two quotes worth preserving from Saturday night. On the Knicks' championship run: "People don't understand the weight of that jersey, the expectations, the pressure of that jersey. Today, right now, it's the lightest it's ever felt." On winning alongside Jalen Brunson and Mikal Bridges: "Those are my brothers for life. We obviously won the NCAA championship, but this one takes the cake." Hart also acknowledged the trade that brought Bridges to New York with a simple "Forget them picks, dawg." Bridges walked off the Frost Bank Center floor to chants to the same effect from traveling Knicks fans.
  • • Knicks coach Mike Brown used his press conference to campaign for associate head coach Chris Jent: "He will help your team at the highest level." Brown also had a clear message on Brunson's place in the NBA hierarchy: "He's a top-3 MVP candidate. People say he's too small. People say he's a 1B or a 2B. He's a freaking 1A."
  • • Patrick Ewing, who spent his career chasing the title that eluded him, said he could "finally say" he's part of a championship team. His son Rick Brunson, Jalen's father, tried to settle the debate about the greatest Knick: "Patrick Ewing is the greatest Knick I've ever witnessed." Kenny Smith shot back: "Well, I'm gonna say it, it's gonna be debatable now."
  • • Landry Shamet, who made the roster on a training camp deal two years in a row, got emotional after the win. "On the verge of being out of the league and injuries, ups and downs man. It's crazy. I'm just really, really grateful and proud of myself for not giving up."
  • • Jordan Clarkson became the first NBA player of Filipino descent to win a championship with Saturday's win.
  • • With the Knicks' title in the books, oddsmakers have already posted 2027 futures. The Thunder and Spurs open as co-favorites at +250, followed by the Celtics at +550. The defending champion Knicks sit fourth at +700, with the Pacers and Nuggets next at +2800.
  • • On the Celtics and trade rumors: Brian Windhorst of ESPN pushed back on speculation that Boston will move Jaylen Brown this offseason. "Boston doesn't deal with player agents. Boston does deals with teams. Boston keeps all of their business quiet," Windhorst said. "Whatever Boston is doing, I promise you, we don't know."
  • • Four franchises have now gone 54 or more consecutive seasons without a title heading into 2026-27. The Kings lead the list at 75 seasons, followed by the Hawks (68), Suns (58) and Clippers (56).
