NBA Rumors Wrap: Blazers lead Jaylen Brown suitors, LeBron future murky, Jokic extension talks stall again

Portland has emerged as the most active pursuer of Boston Celtics wing Jaylen Brown, with league sources describing the Blazers as the one team leaguewide most frequently mentioned as actively chasing him, per Marc Stein. Portland's interest dates back to Damian Lillard's first tenure with the franchise. The Blazers had already attempted to trade for Giannis Antetokounmpo this offseason before he signaled he had no interest in signing long-term there. Any Brown deal would be complicated by Portland's desire to retain most of its young core, and All-Star Deni Avdija would not be on the table in any trade talks, league sources told MassLive. The Nuggets have at least internally discussed whether they have the assets to join the chase, but have yet to emerge as a real landing spot.

Brown has made his own feelings known publicly. He shared a video on Instagram that touches on how he was "the best player" on a championship team, how he was "blackballed" from the Olympic team, and how the Celtics have tried to trade him multiple times. The moment arrived after ESPN's Bobby Marks quoted an analytics executive as saying Brown ranked as the "seventh-best player on a team," a clip that spread widely before the fuller context emerged. Marks later clarified he personally disagreed with that assessment, telling Brown directly he considered him a top-10 player who impacts winning, noting Boston went 191-80 over the past four years with Brown in the lineup.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The LeBron James free agency picture remains deliberately opaque. Brian Windhorst said on ESPN Cleveland that LeBron's teammates asked him directly about his future plans and he told them nothing. Analyst Jake Fischer went further, saying it is more likely than not that LeBron leaves the Lakers. The Clippers have also been flagged as a potential cap-space suitor for LeBron, with Zach Lowe noting they could open roughly $20 million this summer.
  • Former teammate Channing Frye threw cold water on a LeBron return to Cleveland, saying "They got soul issues. They got I don't like you issues and I don't want to be here with you issues."
  • Nikola Jokic is again considering delaying contract extension talks with the Nuggets for another year, per Stein, the same move he made at this point last summer. Denver is simultaneously navigating restricted free agency for Peyton Watson, with sources saying it would take an offer sheet in the $30 million range for the Nuggets to balk at matching.
  • Isaiah Hartenstein is returning to the Thunder on a deal worth roughly $75 million over three years with a mutual option on the final season. With Hartenstein back, Oklahoma City projects $10.2 million over the luxury tax and $10.8 million under the second apron, not yet counting Lu Dort or Kenrich Williams.
  • Jimmy Butler said he is "right back to who I've always been" nearly four months after ACL surgery on his right knee, telling ESPN he still expects to be a top player on his return. He also addressed his future directly: "End is a bad word, but this is where I want to be done. Retire here." His expiring contract could still factor into a larger Warriors trade this offseason.
  • Marcus Smart is seriously considering declining his $5.4 million player option with the Lakers before Monday's deadline, with the Rockets viewed as a likely destination. Houston has prior ties to Smart through coach Ime Udoka's time in Boston.
  • Coaching staff news around the league: Former Pelicans coach Willie Green has interest from both the Warriors as a returning assistant under Steve Kerr and from the Mavericks as new Dallas coach Dusty May builds out his staff. Former Bucks coach Adrian Griffin and Heat consultant Noah LaRoche are also candidates for May's bench, per Stein. The NBA coaching community has expressed frustration over the structure of Micah Nori's deal in Portland, a one-plus-one-plus-one contract with team options on the final two years. JB Bickerstaff, as president of the coaches association, publicly objected.
  • The Cavaliers face some roster decisions before free agency opens. Windhorst flagged Max Strus as a player to watch, saying the team may need to trade him to afford Dean Wade, and that Keon Ellis could depart unless Wade leaves first.
  • Per HoopsHype, the Magic have waived Jonathan Isaac, making him an unrestricted free agent. Isaac had $8 million guaranteed remaining on his deal for next season. The move drops Orlando's projected luxury payment from roughly $16.5 million to around $10 million, though the franchise is still expected to pay tax for the first time in over a decade.
  • The Knicks are unlikely to bring back Mitchell Robinson, their longest-tenured player, with league sources citing his injury history and free throw issues as reasons to avoid going deeper into the second apron. Robinson is an unrestricted free agent after finishing a four-year, $60 million deal.
  • Moritz Wagner is expected to draw multiple suitors in free agency and is open to leaving Orlando, even with brother Franz remaining a Magic cornerstone. Meanwhile, the Pelicans are expected to decline the $8 million team option on Kevon Looney, making him an unrestricted free agent.
  • The Celtics are bringing back Ron Harper Jr. on a new three-year, $9 million deal after declining his $2.6 million team option, per Shams Charania. On the Bulls' side, Mouhamadou Gueye's $2.4 million team option has been picked up and his contract remains fully non-guaranteed until January, with Gueye set to be included in the Bulls-Wolves-Nets-Hornets trade.
  • Lonnie Walker IV is a candidate to return to the NBA this summer after a year in the EuroLeague with Maccabi Tel Aviv. He has an NBA out in his contract until July 15 and is expected to draw interest.
  • Kevin Durant had a message for the Knicks and Jalen Brunson at the Wall Street Journal Live conference in Cannes. "This is a time that I don't think New Yorkers or the Knicks are gonna get back," Durant said. "It's been 53 years. It could be another 53-year wait."
