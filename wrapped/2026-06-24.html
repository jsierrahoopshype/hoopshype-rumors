NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade looming, Bucks GM speaks after Giannis deal, draft day recap

Kawhi Leonard's time with the Los Angeles Clippers may be running out. Chris Haynes said Tuesday night that if an extension cannot be worked out, he expects the Clippers to move Kawhi rather than let him play out an expiring deal. Clippers president Lawrence Frank was candid about the team's current standing, saying multiple times during draft night that the organization is "not a contender right now" and that the franchise is building toward that status again. Frank acknowledged there is ongoing communication with Leonard's camp but didn't go further than that. The Clippers used the No. 6 pick on Keaton Wagler, with Frank framing the selection around who Wagler will be once the team returns to contention, not where they are today.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Bucks general manager Jon Horst addressed the team's direction after Giannis Antetokounmpo was traded, stopping short of discussing the deal directly but leaning into the rebuild. "I think this has been a great opportunity for us to have the opportunity in a lottery for the first time in over a decade," Horst said after Milwaukee made two lottery picks. The Bucks used the No. 13 pick, acquired from Miami in the Giannis trade, to select Nate Ament.
  • • Celtics president Brad Stevens was asked Tuesday night about Jaylen Brown's future in Boston. "Every indication, everything that I think about over the past few years has been building around those guys," Stevens said, adding that Brown "has been amazing" and that "there's a lot to celebrate." He stopped short of any commitment about what comes next.
  • • Spurs GM Brian Wright was direct about De'Aaron Fox after a disappointing first season in San Antonio. "The confidence level is very high," Wright said, adding there is "no wavering" in the organization's faith in Fox. Wright also addressed the pairing of Fox and rookie Dylan Harper, saying the team will continue putting both players in situations to succeed and that they will grow together from what they learned this year.
  • • AJ Dybantsa, the No. 1 overall pick to the Washington Wizards, did more than just visit Washington earlier this month. Shams Charania reported that Dybantsa got in "a pretty hard workout" during the visit, had dinner with the organization and spent time with the staff, despite publicly saying he did not work out for any team. Dybantsa spoke emotionally about his father after the pick, saying his dad quit his job and moved in with him at Utah Prep just to keep him safe during his development.
  • • The Timberwolves' Tim Connelly was asked what ownership has said about potentially paying the luxury tax for a third straight year. "If the moves can exponentially increase our chance to win at the highest level, then we're all in," Connelly said, while also acknowledging the team "can't be irresponsible fiscally" as the penalties deepen.
  • • Minny assistant Micah Nori became head coach of the Portland Trail Blazers, and his former boss Chris Finch had a clear message about how the story was covered. "I thought it was a shame that a lot of the story about Micah's great opportunity was overshadowed by the nature of the deal that he signed," Finch told reporters before the draft. Finch described Nori as someone who sees the game "through a modern lens" with a great balance between analytics and simplicity, and said he sees the game the same way Finch does.
  • • The Thunder drafted Spanish center Aday Mara at No. 12, making him the highest-drafted Spanish player since Ricky Rubio went fifth in 2009. Mara was asked about being labeled a potential Victor Wembanyama stopper. "I don't know how yet. But I will do it," he said. OKC now has Mara at 7-foot-3, Chet Holmgren at 7-1, Isaiah Hartenstein at 7-0, and Thomas Sorber with a 7-6 wingspan.
  • • HoopsHype's Cyro Asseo noted that all three Michigan prospects were off the board by the end of the lottery, consistent with what sources had told HoopsHype a week earlier in their aggregate mock draft. The Mavs took Morez Johnson Jr. at No. 9, reuniting him with new Dallas coach Dusty May, who coached Johnson at Michigan. Comps being floated around the league: Bam Adebayo and Al Horford.
  • • The Grizzlies moved back five spots while accumulating five second-round picks in the process: Memphis slid from No. 16 to No. 17 and then to No. 21, eventually selecting Karim Lopez. Along the way, the team traded No. 16 pick Bennett Stirtz to OKC for two second-rounders and dealt No. 17 to the Pistons for three more.
  • • The Knicks had a busy draft night in the second round, entering Wednesday holding picks 31, 47 and 55, with four more future second-rounders still to be made official, per Marc Stein. New York also received Sergio De Larrea and cash considerations from the Lakers for pick No. 24. The Knicks do not expect De Larrea, a draft-and-stash prospect, to come to the NBA this season.
  • • The Spurs added Tarris Reed Jr. by acquiring No. 26 from Denver for pick No. 35 and two future second-rounders. San Antonio also took Jayden Quaintance at No. 20 earlier in the night, giving the franchise two young big men in the same draft.
  • • Utah Jazz draftee Darryn Peterson set the tone right away after being selected, saying the celebration stops tonight. "Tomorrow, I'm now an NBA player," Peterson said. He cited Kobe Bryant as his primary inspiration and said his rookie-year goal is simple: play all 82 games and make the playoffs.
  • • The 76ers selected Labaron Philon Jr. at No. 22. Philon said he connected with the Sixers at the draft combine through former NBA point guard Jameer Nelson and said he is excited about playing alongside Tyrese Maxey and Bub Carrington Edgecombe.
  • • Jorge Sierra of HoopsHype flagged a notable draft-history milestone: the top 20 picks were all college basketball players, the first time that has happened since 1994.
