NBA Rumors Wrap: Jalen Williams day-to-day, Blazers coach finalists emerge, Sixers president search heats up

Jalen Williams is being evaluated day to day with a left hamstring injury, per Shams Charania, and his availability for Thunder-Spurs Game 3 is uncertain. The Thunder downgraded Williams to questionable for Friday's game after he suffered a second injury to the same hamstring in under a month. Oklahoma City already leads the series and is favored to advance, but losing their second-best player for any stretch changes the calculus considerably. Williams will undergo treatment and return based on how he responds.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Spurs have their own injury questions heading into Game 3. De'Aaron Fox (right ankle sprain) has been upgraded to questionable, while Dylan Harper (right adductor soreness) is also questionable for the game.
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander said the Thunder simply played better in Game 2 after a rough Game 1. "I just have sucked when I get too long of a break," he said. He finished with nine assists alongside his scoring and described the team's adjustment simply: "Every team has their identity and when they impose their identity more than the other team, good things happen."
  • Alex Caruso has been one of the most impactful players in the playoffs so far. The Thunder are posting a +23.3 net rating with him on the floor and just +2.2 with him off, with a defensive rating of 101.3 when he's on and 115.1 when he's off. Caruso scored 17 points off the bench with a game-high +18 in Game 2. "If you're not fearless, you're probably gonna lose and go home," Caruso said.
  • Jared McCain said he started crying when he learned he was being traded to Oklahoma City, and his teammates initially thought he was joking. He's since settled in quickly. "They welcomed me immediately when I got traded," McCain said. "The staff, the team, the city in general. It just felt right when I got here." Luguentz Dort said it felt like McCain had been with the team the whole season within his first two to three days.
  • When Isaiah Hartenstein was asked about signing with OKC, he recalled what Sam Presti told him during his recruiting visit: "I can't promise you minutes, I can't promise you your role, but I can promise you a culture." SGA, for his part, said he barely heard the question about Hartenstein during his postgame interview. "When I realized what she said, I gave her the right answer," he said.
  • The Portland Trail Blazers coaching search has three known finalists. The Blazers have requested permission to meet with Clippers assistant Jeff Van Gundy, while interim coach Tiago Splitter is also a finalist for the full-time job despite interest from Chicago. Minnesota assistant Micah Nori rounds out the known candidates, per Marc Stein.
  • LeBron James says a free agency decision could stretch into August. "I think at some point in June, late June, as July rolls around and maybe into August, we'll start to kind of get a feel of what my future may look like," James said. He also praised Shai Gilgeous-Alexander on winning back-to-back MVPs, saying "nobody can sit here and say" he didn't deserve it after going against him for four playoff games.
  • LeBron will likely address his future publicly on July 16 when he records a live episode of the "Mind the Game" podcast with Tyrese Haliburton at Fanatics Fest NYC. The taping comes roughly two and a half weeks after free agency opens.
  • The 76ers president of basketball operations search is taking shape. Jameer Nelson, who served as assistant GM this season, is the strongest internal candidate and is considered a front-runner for a promotion even if he doesn't land the top job. Cavaliers GM Mike Gansey has also emerged as a strong external candidate, while Timberwolves GM Matt Lloyd is expected to interview and has backing from within the organization.
  • James Harden has a $42.3 million player option for next season, but both he and the Cavaliers entered the trade interested in an extension. How he performs the rest of the series will likely factor into those conversations.
  • Donovan Mitchell spoke about his attachment to Cleveland, saying it "feels peaceful" and "connected even through stressful times." A team source put his impact in concrete terms: "Four years without him, in the rebuild: one play-in game. Four years with him: four playoff appearances, three second-round appearances, a conference finals appearance."
  • Thursday marked the one-year anniversary of Haliburton's game-tying shot at Madison Square Garden. Haliburton, still rehabbing a torn Achilles suffered in the 2025 Finals, reflected on that moment in a feature with The Athletic. "I welcome it," he said of the boos. "I think it's cool when people pay to watch me win and people pay to watch me lose."
  • Mitchell Robinson posted a farewell message on Facebook before Game 2, saying he was deleting the app and changing his number to sharpen his focus during the Knicks' playoff run. "This is the start of a new chapter in my life," he wrote.
  • Luka Doncic will not play for Slovenia in FIBA World Cup qualifying this summer. Sources close to the Lakers star have reiterated to Marc Stein that his position has not changed: he wants to spend as much of his offseason as possible with his daughters.
  • Grizzlies forward Cedric Coward spoke about the passing of teammate Brandon Clarke in an interview with R.Org Sports. "It's been rough. But at the same time, I'm grateful for the time that I had with him," Coward said. "Now you honor his memory by winning games and doing what he was doing with being a great teammate."
