NBA Rumors Wrap: KAT extension talks stall, Kyrie makes his return, Kings fear Raynaud knee injury

Karl-Anthony Towns and the New York Knicks have hit a wall on his contract extension, and the situation turned public fast. The Knicks' front office tabled extension talks until later in the season, a move that clearly caught Towns off guard. "It was news to me. Very disappointed to say the least," he said. "Let's state the obvious. It's business, but there is business on the court for us to handle." Towns is eligible for a four-year, $276 million max extension, though the Knicks' offer has centered on a figure closer to $200 million. The clock is ticking: Towns must decline his $61 million player option by Oct. 19 if he wants to keep any extension talks alive during the season, otherwise the two sides are legally barred from negotiating until after the year.

Jalen Brunson, for his part, isn't sweating the drama. "He's gonna be a professional," Brunson said Saturday. "Not just him — everybody. Everyone's gonna be professional and continue to do their jobs. No matter what the situation is, things are gonna happen."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Kyrie Irving made his return to game action Sunday night after 586 days away, and he did not disappoint. The Dallas Mavericks called the first play for him, he drove past Jabari Smith Jr. for a reverse layup, and the sellout crowd in China erupted. Irving finished with 17 points on 6-of-13 shooting in 22 minutes of a 132-115 preseason win over the Houston Rockets. "A lot of emotions leading into it," Irving said. "Grateful that Dusty called the first play for me. Had my mind made up just to attack." It was his first action since tearing the ACL in his left knee on March 3, 2025.
  • The Sacramento Kings are bracing for bad news on center Maxime Raynaud. The team confirmed he suffered a right patellar subluxation and will be reevaluated in approximately two weeks. After the game, a dispirited Raynaud was visited in the hallway outside the visitors locker room by Kings GM Scott Perry and owner Vivek Ranadive, who gave him a hug. Team sources told ESPN there was a growing fear they've lost their young center for the foreseeable future.
  • Walker Kessler says his jammed left pinky "feels good" and that he wants to play "really bad," but his status for Tuesday's Lakers preseason game against Golden State in Las Vegas is still TBD. Lakers coach JJ Redick was not committing to Kessler's debut on Saturday, and on Sunday Kessler confirmed his absences have been precautionary.
  • Redick also said Sunday the Lakers have "seen glimpses" of things they like heading into the regular season, but that nailing down a starting lineup for the Oct. 21 opener against the Warriors has been difficult because the team has been "a little disjointed with the guys being banged up."
  • Austin Reaves showed up to Lakers practice Sunday in a pair of Nike Kobe 5 Protros, which raised eyebrows given that he signed with Chinese sportswear brand Rigorer back in 2023. Asked about it, Reaves kept it brief: "I'm a big Kobe fan." Reaves also had high praise for teammate Sandro Mamukelashvili, saying his basketball IQ is so high that "if he gets the rebound, he should basically play the 1. It doesn't matter if I'm on the floor, it doesn't matter if Luka's on the floor. We trust whatever he has planned."
  • Dejounte Murray is still dealing with the toe contusion that kept him out of the New Orleans Pelicans' preseason loss to Miami last week. Coach Jamahl Mosley said the team is being precautionary and will keep evaluating Murray's response to treatment. New Orleans' next preseason game is Wednesday at Dallas.
  • Desmond Bane joined ESPN's broadcast during a preseason game and said he's "much more comfortable" in his second year with the Orlando Magic, adding that the team's work habits have shifted since last season. On the perks of relocating: his kids have been to Disney "a lot."
  • Deni Avdija credited Chauncey Billups with a simple but transformative piece of advice early in his time with the Portland Trail Blazers. "Chauncey came to me, like, 'Listen, you're a great rebounder. Every time you take the rebound, you push it. I want you to push it,'" Avdija recalled. "So I just started doing that. That was the only thing I was waiting in the game."
  • Cormac Ryan is not losing sleep over his contract situation in Milwaukee. "For me, the hunger and the effort doesn't waver," Ryan said. "I was unsigned last year, I was playing just as hard as I am now. And when I sign a $50 million deal, I'll play just as hard as I do now."
  • Golden State has signed forward Tyler Burton and waived guard Miles Kelly as the Warriors continue trimming their preseason roster.
  • The Brooklyn Nets have requested waivers on guard Dion Brown and center Dain Dainja. Dainja had been competing for a roster spot since camp opened; Brown was added via an Exhibit 10 deal.
  • The Washington Wizards have signed guard Alondes Williams to an Exhibit 10 contract and waived Skal Labissiere.
  • The Resorts World Las Vegas president and CFO Carlos Castro made a public pitch for his property as the future home of an NBA arena, saying "the North Strip is where the next chapter of Las Vegas is being written." Resorts World sits alongside Wynn and Fontainebleau on what Castro called a luxury corridor that he believes is the right home for the league's expansion.
  • Tony Parker picked up his first home win as ASVEL head coach, beating brother TJ's Roanne squad 96-77 in the French Betclic Elite. Roanne had beaten ASVEL in Parker's debut as coach; this time, Tremont Waters led the way with 18 points, six assists and two steals.
  • Celtics president Brad Stevens returned to Indianapolis to watch Ronald Nored's first game as Butler head coach, an exhibition against Indiana.
