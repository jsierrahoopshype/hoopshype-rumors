NBA Rumors Wrap: Austin Reaves faces max decision, Giannis trade timeline slips, Bulls ready to move up

Austin Reaves will decline his $14.9 million player option and enter unrestricted free agency this summer, and the central question is whether the Los Angeles Lakers will pay him. There is a strong belief in league circles that if the Lakers offer Reaves close to or the full max, he will stay in Los Angeles. But if the Lakers low-ball him, Reaves will consider outside offers, and a sign-and-trade becomes a real option. The Brooklyn Nets have Reaves on their radar and hold the clearest path to offering a max contract outright, per HoopsHype. Reaves can earn up to a five-year, projected $239 million deal to stay with the Lakers or a four-year, $177 million deal elsewhere.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Giannis Antetokounmpo trade picture is murkier than it was a week ago. Chris Haynes said the talks could drag into July and that Boston is not looking like a promising destination from what he is hearing. Marc Stein reported that other teams are convinced a deal happens before Tuesday's draft, though Stein himself noted he is not as certain as he once was that a trade gets done before that deadline.
  • The Milwaukee Bucks are also looking to offload Kyle Kuzma and Bobby Portis as part of any Giannis deal, according to Jake Fischer. Separately, the Bucks are searching for a second first-round pick in any package, and Miami's No. 13 overall selection would be Milwaukee-bound if the Heat can finally close a deal.
  • Jake Fischer also shot down the idea that the Clippers would pursue Jaylen Brown in a three-team deal with Boston and Milwaukee. Los Angeles plans to stay put at No. 5 in next week's draft. On the Celtics' side, Gary Washburn said Boston is not actively shopping Brown, and the team's preference is to land Giannis without including him.
  • The Washington Wizards acquired Trae Young viewing him as the most valuable free agent on the market, and Young is excited about a fresh start in Washington, HoopsHype has learned. Young hopes to remain a Wizard long-term. Despite some renewed interest from outside teams, the overwhelming expectation is that he stays put, with Young said to be enthusiastic about the team's No. 1 pick, a healthy Anthony Davis and Alex Sarr returning from surgery.
  • The Chicago Bulls have signaled a willingness to move up from the No. 15 pick, according to executives who spoke with HoopsHype. Chicago also has significant cap space but is not necessarily looking to spend big in free agency. As the team rebuilds, the Bulls are open to absorbing contracts in exchange for draft pick compensation.
  • A Walker Kessler extension with the Utah Jazz remains far from done. Utah put an offer worth roughly $140 million over five years on the table, which would be the largest contract ever given to a center who had yet to make an All-Star team, but a significant gap remains between the two sides. Separately, sources also expressed doubt that the Jazz will get a rookie extension done with guard Keyonte George despite the franchise's confidence in his future.
  • The Golden State Warriors have growing momentum toward bringing back Kristaps Porzingis, but they want him on a short-term deal at a reduced rate from the $30.7 million he made last season. On Draymond Green, the Warriors are open to a decline-and-extend but only if the first-year salary drops from $27.6 million into the $20 million range, giving them flexibility to upgrade elsewhere.
  • The Warriors are also monitoring Trey Murphy III of the New Orleans Pelicans, who league sources say could be more obtainable this cycle. Golden State has had Murphy circled for years as a wing who fits both a roster need and Steve Kerr's system. The Pelicans have also been signaling interest in acquiring a first-round pick at next week's draft, which could facilitate a deal.
  • If LeBron James and the Lakers cannot agree on a new deal, the Warriors are a willing destination. That said, all the intel Golden State has gathered points toward LeBron returning to Los Angeles, and the Warriors are planning their summer on that assumption. ESPN's Marc Spears separately reported he is hearing LeBron is likely coming back to the Lakers, and that Kevin Love could be joining him.
  • The Atlanta Hawks have gauged Jonathan Kuminga's trade value ahead of the draft, but his return to Atlanta on his $24.3 million team option remains a real possibility, league sources told HoopsHype. The Hawks also hope to re-sign CJ McCollum at around $20 million per year and are hoping to retain center Jock Landale, who averaged career-highs across the board last season but figures to draw interest from multiple teams.
  • The Detroit Pistons want to bring back Tobias Harris, who was their second-leading scorer in the playoffs, and will also work to extend Jalen Duren amid his restricted free agency. A five-year, $200 million deal for Duren has been floated as a potential endpoint, though Brooklyn and Chicago are ready to circle with offer sheets if Detroit hesitates.
  • Donovan Mitchell may not sign a contract extension with the Cleveland Cavaliers this offseason, and Brian Windhorst suggested people should be prepared for that outcome. "He is in maximum control, maximum benefit, maximum choice, maximum leverage situation," Windhorst said on ESPN Cleveland. Separately, Windhorst said the Cavs are looking to move Dennis Schroeder as they explore ways to trim salary.
  • Devin Booker is changing his jersey number from No. 1 to No. 15, honoring his USA Basketball uniform and the college number of his father, Melvin, per Shams Charania.
  • Jalen Brunson explained why he went to hug San Antonio Spurs head coach Mitch Johnson before his own father after the Knicks won the championship. "It was just kind of instinct, like how I was raised," Brunson told CBS Mornings. "I think win or loss, you show respect regardless of the outcome."
  • Former Knicks coach Tom Thibodeau is still processing his complicated feelings about New York's title run. A friend said Thibodeau is "genuinely very happy for the players and his guys" but that "Tom is still hurt that the decision-makers made it appear he needed to be replaced." Owner James Dolan said in a pre-playoff video address to the team that Thibodeau was "brilliant" but that the players needed a coach who would "pull you together" rather than simply lead or dictate.
