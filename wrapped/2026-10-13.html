NBA Rumors Wrap: Kyrie Irving trade buzz in Dallas, Knicks prioritize Towns extension, Anthony Black unlikely to get Magic deal

The Dallas Mavericks are generating genuine trade interest around Kyrie Irving as the franchise commits to building around Cooper Flagg. Tim MacMahon reported that there is "a lot of intrigue around the league" as to whether Dallas will entertain trade discussions for Irving, not because the organization has soured on him, but because new team president Masai Ujiri has tasked the front office with constructing a sustainable contender around Flagg long-term. The complication is real: the two stars are 15 years apart in age, and rival front offices are openly questioning whether the Mavericks can maintain that pairing while also addressing roster holes elsewhere. For now, the team's public stance is that Irving stays, and Irving himself has been effusive about Flagg in training camp, saying the 19-year-old "deserves a lot of the praise he's gotten because he's carried a lot" and will "lead this franchise into the future." Whether Ujiri holds that line once the regular season begins will be one of the more closely watched storylines of the early season.

The Irving trade chatter comes alongside a separate report from The Athletic that Irving's deal with Anta includes less guaranteed money than he had at Nike but gives him a double-digit percentage cut of his signature line sales, more than twice the typical 5 percent rate for signature athletes. Irving has two years remaining on that Anta deal, and while the sides have discussed an extension, they have not yet reached an agreement.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The New York Knicks have prioritized extension talks with Karl-Anthony Towns over Josh Hart to this point, per Marc Stein. The Knicks do hold a $22.4 million team option on Hart for next season, which gives them some flexibility, but Hart has publicly downplayed any concern about the standoff.
  • An extension for Orlando Magic swingman Anthony Black looks unlikely at this stage, per Stein. The two sides have not engaged in any meaningful extension dialogue, with Orlando weighing the long-term financial obligations it already stacked up by signing Jalen Suggs to a lucrative deal.
  • Charlotte Hornets forward Brandon Miller is still projected to sign an extension that could exceed Amen Thompson's five-year, $200 million deal with the Rockets, with rival front office executives citing Miller's higher offensive ceiling as the reason his pact could land richer. Meanwhile, extension talks between the Oklahoma City Thunder and the camp of an unnamed player have begun in the $25 million annual average value range, inspired by recent deals for Dyson Daniels in Atlanta and Christian Braun in Denver.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves checked in with Charlotte a few times before actually landing LaMelo Ball in a trade. Coach Chris Finch said the attitude Ball has brought to the team has been a welcome change: "We've traded moodiness for giddiness." Finch on Ball and Anthony Edwards together: "They're really hard to stay mad at. And they do some things that you want to get mad at."
  • The Denver Nuggets have waived Lonnie Walker, per HoopsHype. Walker subsequently hired agent Misko Raznatovic, who is expected to find him a job abroad.
  • Philadelphia 76ers forward Kentavious Caldwell-Pope said the team's chemistry will be its defining factor this season. "We have so much talent from one through 12, or 15, however many guys we've got, and it's talent everywhere," he told ESPN. "So I think our biggest thing is just communicating with each other, learning each other's tendencies." Teammate Dean Wade was similarly upbeat after practice, saying of the starting group: "They're just creating good shots every single time. When they're all super engaged and flowing like they were today, they are a tough team to deal with."
  • LeBron James signing with the Sixers sent the NBA's broadcast partners scrambling. Prime Video told Sports Business Journal it immediately bumped Philadelphia's schedule appearances from six or seven planned games up to 10 once James announced his decision. The league's scheduling process now runs through an AI system called Fastbreak AI, which can generate a full 1,200-game schedule in under half an hour.
  • Ben Simmons was so confident Sacramento would sign him after a workout for Kings GM Scott Perry that he began researching homes in Sacramento before the deal was official, according to an E:60 documentary. The Knicks, Timberwolves and Warriors had interest but none made an offer. Simmons signed a one-year, $3.5 million contract with the Kings, who went 22-60 last season.
  • Toronto Raptors coach Darko Rajakovic said Kawhi Leonard is available for their preseason game against the Knicks after going through a full practice. "He's feeling great. So, yes, he's gonna be available for game tomorrow," Rajakovic said.
  • Phoenix Suns coach Jordan Ott said Miles Bridges is recovering from a right ankle injury but the team will not rush him back. "That's one we're not going to press on if it's not perfect," Ott said. Phoenix has two preseason games remaining.
  • Ja Morant's father Tee Morant had a sideline conversation with Trail Blazers owner Tom Dundon during a preseason game. Tee said Dundon told him his vision is championships in Portland and that nobody around the franchise has anything bad to say about Ja. Tee added that his son knows he has to "pull back" in certain situations because "the narrative is going to shift" and he is "always under a microscope."
  • New York Knicks center Andre Drummond said he has simplified his free throw routine to fix a years-long problem. "I just simplified it, did a routine I've done since a child, and shoot the same shot," Drummond said, adding that overthinking during games had been the core issue throughout his career.
  • New Orleans Pelicans guard Jordan Poole said coach Jamahl Mosley has given him an open line of communication and a clear role. "They want me to be me," Poole told Clutch Points. "They want me to play-make, bring my championship-level experience, score the ball, and put my teammates in a position to be successful."
  • The Brooklyn Nets unveiled ABA-inspired Classic Edition uniforms for the 2026-27 season, marking the franchise's 60th anniversary. The San Antonio Spurs also revealed throwback uniforms and a matching court honoring the 50th anniversary of the ABA-NBA merger, which they will debut Nov. 4 against Dallas.
  • Zach Lowe launched a Substack that will complement his podcast, offering detailed X's-and-O's breakdowns and video content to supplement longer-form analysis that does not translate as well to audio.
