NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi trade in limbo, LeBron free agency heats up, Bam-Herro altercation fallout

The NBA has put a halt on the blockbuster Kawhi Leonard trade between the Los Angeles Clippers and Toronto Raptors, and both sides are still waiting for the league to resolve the hold, per Chris Haynes. The Raptors were comfortable making the deal and did not anticipate the league stepping in to block it. Meanwhile, Brandon Ingram, Gradey Dick and others involved in the trade are already in the Clippers' facility as if the deal is done. Both organizations still expect a trade to happen once the league works through whatever is holding things up.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • LeBron James remains the most closely watched name in free agency, with five teams still engaged. Shams Charania said Cleveland, Miami and Philadelphia are the three teams most others view as the frontrunners, with Golden State and Minnesota also in the mix. Draymond Green offered his own pitch to LeBron, saying "I don't think there's a decision that's been made but say if there was, it'll make you think twice about it." On the subject of LeBron's longevity, Dwyane Wade said it has to be difficult for James to retire precisely because he is still playing at an elite level, something Wade said made his own retirement an easier call.
  • One wrinkle in the Miami picture: Russell Westbrook is a candidate to sign with the Heat, but only if LeBron does not. Westbrook, 37, averaged 15.2 points over 64 games last season with the Sacramento Kings. Jake Fischer also said he would not be surprised if Kevin Love is considered as an add-on for whatever team lands LeBron.
  • The Bam Adebayo-Tyler Herro altercation continues to generate fallout. According to Charania, the incident started when leaked DMs surfaced showing Herro questioning Adebayo's viability as a $60 million player. Adebayo confronted Herro, and the exchange ended with Adebayo striking Herro in front of other players at a practice facility. Both are now on the Milwaukee Bucks after being traded as part of the Giannis Antetokounmpo deal.
  • Udonis Haslem weighed in publicly on both the Adebayo-Herro incident and Draymond Green. On Bam and Tyler: "Neither one of them got it all figured out yet," Haslem wrote, while also noting that if Adebayo had hit Herro at full force, "it's over." On Draymond, Haslem was sharper: "You was on sucka shit four years ago when you swung on Jordan Poole and you on sucka shit now."
  • Draymond fired back at Haslem, saying the person who bothered him most after the Jordan Poole incident was Haslem himself. "I saw Udonis Haslem get into it with guys on teams that was younger than him before. Ala Jimmy Butler," Green said. Haslem responded by telling Green to "keep it pushing" and said he is not handing out any more hall passes.
  • Joe Mazzulla spoke at length about Jaylen Brown's departure from the Boston Celtics, saying "there needs to be a grieving process for losing not just a player in Jaylen, but a person in Jaylen." Mazzulla also said other players on Boston's roster were emotionally affected during the trade process because their own names were involved in discussions.
  • Nick Nurse said acquiring Brown reminded him of his prep work for Kawhi Leonard ahead of the 2019 title run. "You watch him get a lot better from year to year and how much harder it became to game plan for him," Nurse said of Brown. The Philadelphia 76ers had 45 wins and the East's No. 7 seed last season.
  • Kel'el Ware, who was included in Miami's trade package for Giannis Antetokounmpo, said at summer league he had a feeling the trade was coming and was already preparing himself. Ware said being traded alongside Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Kasparas Jakucionis made the transition easier: "Some guys, they get traded by themselves."
  • Per HoopsHype, the Golden State Warriors are hiring Frank Vogel as their lead assistant coach under Steve Kerr. Vogel spent last season as an assistant with the Dallas Mavericks after winning the 2020 NBA title with the Los Angeles Lakers.
  • EuroLeague Defender of the Year Alpha Diallo has agreed to a one-year, $1.4 million deal with the Denver Nuggets, per Shams Charania. The 29-year-old former Providence standout makes his NBA jump on a fully guaranteed contract.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves signed second-round pick Isaiah Evans to a four-year, $9.3 million deal that includes three fully guaranteed years and a fourth-year team option, per HoopsHype. Evans was the 33rd pick and earned All-ACC honors at Duke. The team will formally introduce LaMelo Ball and Josh Green on Tuesday.
  • Arthur Kaluma took advantage of Cameron Carr's absence due to a right thumb contusion and dropped 34 points on 11-of-16 shooting, including six threes, in the Lakers' 91-70 summer league win over the Mavericks. The Lakers also officially announced the re-signing of Austin Reaves and the signing of Collin Sexton.
  • Paolo Banchero set his targets for next season, saying he believes 25 points, 8 rebounds and 7 assists per game is attainable and that he wants to improve across all shooting percentages. The Orlando Magic rookie Izaiyah Nelson sprained his left ankle in summer league and is considered day-to-day.
  • Bill Foley, who built the NHL's Vegas Golden Knights into a Stanley Cup winner, is assembling a group to bid on an NBA expansion team in Las Vegas. Foley said demand to join his investment group has been strong enough that he expects to have to turn people away.
  • DeAndre Jordan is returning to New Orleans on a two-year, fully guaranteed deal worth $7.9 million. Carter Bryant, speaking on Victor Wembanyama's decision to leave $50 million on the table with the San Antonio Spurs, said: "I don't think anybody was surprised... he's a winner."
  • HoopsHype's Bryan Kalbrosky shared that he is facing a serious heart condition called hypertrophic cardiomyopathy after previously battling an autoimmune kidney disease. He has a surgeon specializing in treating it and is accepting support from those who wish to reach out directly.
