NBA Rumors Wrap: Ausar Thompson locks up $155M extension, Keyonte George gets $157.5M, LeBron's Philly commute details emerge

Ausar Thompson and the Detroit Pistons have agreed on a five-year, $155 million rookie-scale contract extension, per Shams Charania. The deal makes Thompson and his twin brother Amen Thompson, who signed with the Houston Rockets this offseason, the first pair of brothers in NBA history to each receive a $100 million contract, with a combined $363 million between them this offseason. Charania had flagged earlier in the day that there was "growing optimism" around both Thompson and Charlotte Hornets wing Brandon Miller reaching extensions with their respective teams, with both sides expected to continue talks in the coming days and weeks. The Pistons locked up one of the league's premier perimeter defenders at a figure that will account for a sizable chunk of their cap going forward.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Keyonte George and the Utah Jazz finalized a five-year, $157.5 million rookie-scale extension after months of negotiations between George's agent Jason Ranne and Jazz president Austin Ainge. The deal includes no team or player option and will account for roughly 16 percent of Utah's salary cap. Jake Fischer adds that $2.5 million of that total is performance bonuses, spread at $500K per season. Ainge described George as someone who leads through work ethic and showing up for teammates rather than being "a screamer and a yeller," and said that approach is worth building around.
  • • Brian Windhorst offered new color on LeBron James' move to the Philadelphia 76ers, saying the first sign the Sixers sensed they were in the running was when LeBron began asking people on the team about commuting options from New York. As it happens, the Sixers' practice facility in South Jersey has a premium helipad, built for owner Josh Harris, who routinely commutes from New York by helicopter. Windhorst was careful to say he doesn't know for certain that's how LeBron will commute, only that he had looked into it.
  • • Stephen Curry confirmed he actively tried to recruit LeBron to Golden State this summer before James chose Philadelphia. "If anybody didn't try, I think they'd be a fool," Curry said. On his own future, Curry, entering his 18th season with his contract up in 2027, was unambiguous: "I want to be a lifer with Golden State."
  • • The new ownership group buying the Los Angeles Lakers has circulated an investor presentation projecting the franchise's valuation will grow from $12.5 billion to at least $30 billion within a decade, with more optimistic assumptions putting the figure as high as $62 billion. The plan, put together by Thrive Capital's Joshua Kushner, identifies roughly $150 million in near-term incremental revenue by 2028, partly by reclaiming around 6,000 broker-held season tickets for direct single-game sales. However, a source close to the Kushner-Bob Iger group told Dan Woike that the Lakers are not planning a rapid ticket price hike; the goal is cutting out outside brokers and capturing that revenue directly.
  • • Jalen Brunson is at "80 to 85 percent" following wrist surgery in July but will have no restrictions when Knicks training camp opens in the coming weeks.
  • • The Miami Heat are holding their 15th roster spot open, keeping it available in case a difference-making free agent becomes available. Veteran guard Gabe Vincent has expressed interest in a reunion, but the Heat have not extended an offer. New Heat guard Davion Mitchell spoke about the defensive possibilities of a lineup featuring Giannis Antetokounmpo, Bam Adebayo, Andrew Wiggins and himself, saying the group has a shared hunger for Defensive Player of the Year.
  • • Pelle Larsson agreed to a four-year, $60 million extension with the Heat, with a mutual option for the 2030-31 season.
  • • Cam Whitmore is joining the Denver Nuggets on a two-way deal. The 2023 first-round pick averaged 10.5 points and 3.3 rebounds over 119 career games and has already been in Denver for workouts.
  • • The Minnesota Timberwolves' GM Matt Lloyd revealed just how hard the team worked to land Jonathan Kuminga this offseason: Lloyd logged 94 phone calls with Kuminga's agent between July 15 and the day the deal was signed, something his wife flagged after going through his phone. Per HoopsHype, the Wolves also signed guard Stephen Thompson to a training camp deal and waived guard Jules Bernard.
  • • Shaq graduated from the FBI National Academy's Session 299 and became an honorary FBI Special Agent alongside 265 law enforcement officials from around the country.
  • • Draymond Green pushed back hard on Danilo Gallinari's criticism of Kawhi Leonard. "I think wishing the NBA took $50 million from a player is disgusting," Green said, adding that Leonard had no actual power to execute a trade and that the franchises involved made those decisions themselves.
  • • Shams reported that the NBA is prioritizing closing on a Las Vegas expansion team owner before moving toward Seattle, with a hotter bidding market driving valuations toward $10 billion or higher in Las Vegas. Sources close to the process told Shams the league could close on the Vegas owner before the end of this year.
  • • Stephen Curry announced he has signed a long-term deal with Li-Ning after parting ways with Under Armour following 12-plus years. "I tried literally everything and there was a reason that Li-Ning stood tall at the end because the product reigned supreme," Curry said, citing Dwyane Wade and Jimmy Butler as players who set the stage for the brand in the U.S.
  • • The Cleveland Cavaliers are promoting Brandon Weems to general manager, per HoopsHype's Michael Scotto. The Orlando Magic are also hiring Ronnie Burrell as head coach of their G League affiliate, the Osceola Magic. Burrell, who won G League Coach of the Year in 2023, previously served as an assistant with the Trail Blazers and Nets.
  • • The Lakers added Meechie Johnson and Chase Ross to their training camp roster and waived Anton Watson, whom they had signed one day earlier.
  • • Tony Parker is pursuing a coaching career and went back to school to complete France's advanced coaching qualification rather than jumping straight to an NBA staff. Parker said a conversation with Zinedine Zidane about the value of starting from scratch was a major influence on the decision.
  • • Zach LaVine said he holds himself to high standards after a hand injury hampered his first season with the Sacramento Kings: "For standards for myself, I have them extremely high so I'm looking forward to this year." LaVine also had warm words for retired guard Russell Westbrook, calling him "one of the best humans I've met."
