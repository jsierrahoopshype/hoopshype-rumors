NBA Rumors Wrap: Durant compares 76ers to Warriors dynasty, Klay Thompson buyout talk heats up, Warriors re-sign Payton

Kevin Durant has seen this movie before. Speaking at a USA Basketball event Saturday, the Houston Rockets forward compared the new-look Philadelphia 76ers to his Golden State Warriors championship teams, saying the combination of LeBron James, Jaylen Brown, Joel Embiid and Tyrese Maxey gives Philly four 25-point scorers under one roof. "The last time they put three 20-point scorers on a team, they said it was unfair, which was the Warriors team I was on," Durant said. "They've got four 25-point scorers on this team. So, hell yeah, I think they're going to be a contender. They're going to be a fun team to watch. It's going to be League Pass-worthy." Durant also said he had no idea James was heading to Philly until the announcement dropped, calling LeBron "stealth like that," and added that the decision is "great for the league" given the attention James' free agency drew.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Miami Heat have strong interest in Klay Thompson, who is still under contract with the Dallas Mavericks for $17.5 million this season. The cleaner path for Miami is a buyout, with Thompson hypothetically giving up around $7 million of his salary to become a free agent. A trade is possible but would be complicated given the financials, so the Heat appear willing to wait and see how things develop in Dallas. An anonymous scout told the Sun-Sentinel that Thompson "can still make shots" and would get you 17 points on a given night, but pegged his open-market value at roughly $5 million, minimum scale territory.
  • • The Heat are also monitoring DeMar DeRozan, though an anonymous scout raised concerns about fit. "With him, Bam and Giannis, there would be no spacing," the scout said, adding that DeRozan is ball-dominant and has never come off the bench in his career outside of 12 appearances as a rookie. The scout allowed that DeRozan could function as a bench finisher if he accepted the role, but getting him to that point is the challenge.
  • • Gary Payton II is back with the Golden State Warriors on a one-year, $3.9 million deal, per Shams Charania. The signing brings Payton back for a seventh season in the Bay Area and his 11th overall in the league. He appeared in 73 games last season and has remained a valued piece of the Warriors' bench rotation. Golden State also completed the anticipated re-signing of De'Anthony Melton, continuing to bring back its veteran core this offseason.
  • • Jeremy Sochan has agreed to a one-year deal with the Portland Trail Blazers, his agent Deirunas Visockas of Gersh Sports confirmed to ESPN. The deal is non-guaranteed, and Sochan will compete for a roster spot in training camp. He averaged 9.9 points, 5.4 rebounds and 2.5 assists last season while finishing the year with the championship Knicks.
  • • Former teammate Iman Shumpert had warm words for Sochan's new chapter. "I'm so excited for him to start this journey in Portland," Shumpert said on YouTube. "I want him to prove everybody wrong so bad."
  • • The Cleveland Cavaliers have signed Mario Hezonja to a one-year, non-guaranteed deal with a trigger date, per HoopsHype. His $2.8 million salary becomes fully guaranteed if he's still on the roster past November 15th. Hezonja, who spent five years abroad after his previous NBA stint, posted on social media that he's "super happy" to be joining an organization he "always admired and respected."
  • • Shaquille O'Neal pushed back on the ring-chasing criticism surrounding LeBron's move to Philly. "He already has four rings," O'Neal said on SiriusXM NBA Radio. "It's funny the people that don't have rings, 'Oh, he's ring chasing.' He's done it his way. Now on your way out you can get with a team that's got some players to get one or two more. I would do it." O'Neal added his own terminology: "People call it ring chasing. I call it ring continuance." Former NBA player Eddie Johnson made a similar argument in a column for HoopsHype, writing that Tom Brady, Peyton Manning and Shaquille O'Neal all changed teams for another shot at titles without facing the same scrutiny: "Because every championship LeBron wins changes history. It changes where people rank him, how future generations remember him and makes the Michael Jordan debate more uncomfortable for people who've already made up their minds."
  • • Dillon Brooks offered a less sympathetic take. "I think he's doing everything he can to get a last little ring," Brooks said of LeBron joining the 76ers. "I read he almost has enough footage for a Last Dance documentary." Brooks plays for the Phoenix Suns.
  • • Chet Holmgren reflected on the Oklahoma City Thunder losing Luguentz Dort, Aaron Wiggins and Isaiah Joe in separate offseason trades. "That's how the business of this game goes," Holmgren said at a USA Basketball charity event. "It doesn't make it hurt less when you see guys go. At the same time, I'm excited for them and the opportunity they're going to have in Atlanta." Dort and Wiggins are headed to the Atlanta Hawks; Joe was dealt to the Detroit Pistons. Holmgren, who averaged 17.1 points, 8.9 rebounds and 1.9 blocks last season in earning his first All-Star nod, also addressed Oklahoma City's playoff exit: "There is no guarantee. You can put in all the work and still come up short."
  • • Victor Wembanyama is bringing the San Antonio Spurs to him. Spurs CEO R.C. Buford said roughly eight to ten players and coaches will travel to Paris to work out with Wembanyama before training camp. "They're coming over to see Victor," Buford said on TrashTalk.
  • • Rick Carlisle called Luka Doncic the most unique player he has ever coached. "From a talent standpoint and ability and everything else, he's the most unique player I've ever worked with," Carlisle said. "He's just so good. And he's a killer competitor, too." Carlisle also reflected on the 2011 Finals, recalling that Jason Kidd, at 37, picked up LeBron full court as a tactical centerpiece of their championship game plan.
  • • Ayo Dosunmu, who re-signed with the Minnesota Timberwolves, made clear the decision was personal. "This is always where I wanted to be at," Dosunmu said. "I knew once I got traded here that this was home for me, and they welcomed me. So it's just a blessing that I'm able to come back and continue my career here."
  • • Kentavious Caldwell-Pope, now with the 76ers, said reuniting with LeBron is about getting back to meaningful basketball. "I just want to get back to playing meaningful basketball," KCP said. "Playing for something like another championship. That spotlight, the playoffs, man. Ain't nothing like it." Meanwhile, 76ers forward Dominick Barlow, born in May 2003, said it's a "surreal feeling" to be teammates with LeBron since James has been in the NBA his entire life.
  • • Bogdan Bogdanovic is training in Montenegro ahead of his first season with the Rockets and says his mind is fully on Houston. "I signed with Houston, I'm happy, and we'll see what this year brings," Bogdanovic told RTHN. He acknowledged that a return to Partizan remains a long-term dream but said he cannot think about what happens two, three or four years from now.
  • • A couple of quick contract notes from Portland: Vit Krejci's deal with the Trail Blazers became partially guaranteed for $250,000 on Saturday. Separately, Ryan Nembhard will wear No. 12 for the Atlanta Hawks this season, the number previously worn by N'Faly Dante.
  • • On the international front, veteran big man Daniel Theis is in advanced talks to join Maccabi Tel Aviv. Forward Alize Johnson has signed with Paris Basketball in the French capital. And G-League guard Colby Jones has signed a one-season deal with Kids&Us Manresa in Spain after averaging 13.8 points with the Memphis Hustle last season.
