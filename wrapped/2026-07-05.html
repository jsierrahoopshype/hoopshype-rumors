NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron James' decision timeline unclear, Jaylen Brown trade becoming official, Jonas Valanciunas linked to Lakers

LeBron James' free agency is moving at its own pace, and his agent is not trying to rush it. Rich Paul told Forbes that he does not expect a decision "anytime soon," adding "I don't think it'll be the next few days" when asked whether teams should prepare for a quick resolution. Per Marc Stein, a growing belief has formed among competing teams that Cleveland, the franchise that drafted James, is increasingly the scenario to beat over early favorites Golden State. That said, the field remains wide: Stein reports the 76ers, Nuggets and Timberwolves all believe they are getting real consideration alongside the Cavaliers, Warriors and Heat. The Heat are viewed as a more viable option after landing Giannis Antetokounmpo from the Bucks and could offer a taxpayer mid-level exception in the $11-12 million range.

The Minnesota angle has real substance behind it. Wolves president Tim Connelly and Rich Paul have a strong relationship, and Paul has openly mentioned Minnesota's ownership group and Jaden McDaniels as selling points in their pitch. LeBron's odds of landing in Minnesota jumped from 2.6% to 7.0% on Polymarket over the past 24 hours, a notable if still long-shot shift.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Celtics have scheduled a press conference for Monday afternoon with president of basketball operations Brad Stevens and new majority owner Bill Chisholm to formally address the Jaylen Brown trade once the league's moratorium is lifted. Jayson Tatum posted a farewell on Instagram: "9 years! Forever grateful for all that we accomplished together, for pushing me to be a better player. From first-round exits to winning a chip together. I'm thankful for it all." Paul Pierce compared the trade's inevitability to watching a family member decline: "You saw it coming. It sucks, but when it happens it's like out of the blue, it's different."
  • On the statistical side of Brown's tenure, only 12 players have more wins in Boston Celtics history than Brown, and the youngest of those 12 are Paul Pierce and Larry Bird. Brown finished with more wins as a Celtic than Hall of Famer Dave Cowens.
  • The Lakers are eyeing multiple big men to shore up their front line. Jonas Valanciunas is drawing serious interest from Los Angeles, with the Nuggets having made him available in trade conversations since Denver's season ended. Valanciunas was a Lakers free agent target two summers ago before signing a multiyear deal in Washington. Javale McGee's heir apparent Kevon Looney also remains on the Lakers' radar.
  • Peyton Watson is seeking more than $25 million annually on his next deal, topping the figure Christian Braun secured from Denver last October. How realistic that ask is in the current market is an open question.
  • Patrick Ewing is joining the Washington Wizards coaching staff as an assistant under head coach Brian Keefe, per Shams Charania. Ewing had spent the past two seasons in an ambassador role with the Knicks, including through their 2026 title run. Veteran coach Steve Clifford, who previously worked in similar advisory capacities with Phoenix and Brooklyn, is also joining Washington as a coaching advisor. Clifford and Ewing have history together, having overlapped in Orlando and Charlotte during their respective stints there.
  • Jalen Brunson was asked about Dallas letting both him and Luka Doncic walk. When someone relayed that people online said the Mavericks "fumbled the two of you," Brunson smiled and let the pause do the work: "They made decisions and I'll leave it at that."
  • Garrett Temple is joining Dusty May's coaching staff in Dallas after a 16-year playing career. May's staff also includes former Pelicans head coach Willie Green.
  • The Bucks and Kam Jones have agreed to a two-way deal, per HoopsHype. The 38th pick in the 2025 Draft, Jones was traded from Indiana to Chicago on draft night before being released. He now lands in Milwaukee.
  • The Raptors have agreed to a two-way deal with Jaden Bradley, the 50th pick in the 2026 Draft out of Arizona, where he was the Big 12 Player of the Year, league sources told HoopsHype.
  • Andre Drummond on replacing Mitchell Robinson in New York: "Getting rid of Mitchell Robinson [was a] huge loss… I bring in a similar talent and energy… but I'm a vet, been around a long time, know what it takes to win."
  • Braden Smith sat down with HoopsHype and talked about joining forces with Ivica Zubac in Indiana, drawing on his experience playing alongside Zach Edey at Purdue. On Rick Carlisle's reputation for being tough on rookies, Smith said: "You've got to build your way up and earn that. I think that's where I will succeed and be good at because I've had to do that at every level I've been a part of."
  • In summer league action, Darius Acuff put up a bold debut for the Kings, taking 29 shots in 24 minutes and finishing with 25 points. He set up Nique Clifford for the game-winning three-pointer with five seconds left on his fourth assist of the night. Over in the Jazz summer league game, Darryn Peterson scored 28 points on 11-of-21 shooting in his debut. Ace Bailey exited the Jazz game early with back spasms and did not return.
  • Memphis handled OKC in summer league, 111-74. Cameron Boozer had 15 points and four assists in 22 minutes for the Grizzlies, Taylor Hendricks added 15 in 12 minutes, and Javon Small finished with 11 points and nine assists. For the Thunder, Brooks Barnhizer scored 13 with six rebounds, Aday Mara chipped in 10 points and four assists, and Bennett Stirtz had 10 points and three steals. Mara, adjusting to his size advantage, said: "Everything that goes through the paint, just let me get it."
  • Yaxel Lendeborg had a strong debut for the Warriors in the California Classic, scoring 19 points on 6-of-6 shooting, including four threes, while leading the team in both rebounds and assists. He graded himself a B, saying he "messed up a lot of defensive rotations."
  • FC Barcelona prospect Nikola Kusturica, 16, has reportedly been offered a $12 million NIL deal by UCLA for two seasons. Kusturica is currently playing at the FIBA U17 World Championship and is considered one of the top young prospects in global basketball.
