NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's Lakers future takes shape, Jazz pursue No. 1 pick, Myles Turner reveals Bucks chaos

The future of LeBron James with the Los Angeles Lakers is coming into sharper focus, and it does not appear to be a simple negotiation. ESPN's Dave McMenamin reported that LeBron is not approaching this as a max-or-bust situation, but that the number matters less than the message behind it: James wants to feel wanted, and he has not always felt that way from the Lakers organization. McMenamin said Rob Pelinka's job between now and whenever talks begin with agent Rich Paul is to have a realistic talent-acquisition plan attached to whatever offer gets made. Separately, a Dallas Hoops Journal report pushed back on any notion of friction between James and teammate Luka Doncic, with sources saying Doncic actively wants LeBron to re-sign and has looked up to him since childhood. Austin Reaves, meanwhile, is expected to decline his player option and re-sign with Los Angeles this summer, per the same report.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Utah Jazz are making a serious push to move up to the No. 1 pick. The Jazz reached out to the Washington Wizards about trading up to select AJ Dybantsa, according to Andscape. Jazz owner Ryan Smith reinforced that posture publicly, saying "everything should be on the table" when asked about trading up from No. 2, and adding that Austin and Danny Ainge "have also been known to do some pretty crazy stuff on draft night."
  • The Wizards hold all the leverage in that scenario. General manager Michael Winger explained why the organization traded for Trae Young, calling him "an absolute genius" and "an offensive engine" the team had lacked. Ramona Shelburne also floated the possibility that Washington could use the top pick on draft prospect Darryn Peterson rather than Dybantsa.
  • Myles Turner went on his podcast with Breanna Stewart and pulled back the curtain on a chaotic Milwaukee Bucks season under Doc Rivers. "Guys were late all the time," Turner said. "Guys were showing up to film whenever they wanted to show up. Guys were missing meetings. It was one of the craziest things I personally ever experienced." When asked who was most likely to show up late, Turner did not hesitate: "Oh, that's easy. Giannis."
  • The potential for a Giannis Antetokounmpo trade to the Miami Heat has a real structural wrinkle to it. The Heat cannot trade their No. 13 pick outright due to NBA rules around consecutive years without a first-rounder, but they could use it as part of a prearranged deal by selecting a player on another team's behalf. That means a trade framework involving Giannis and Miami could be announced before the draft, it just cannot be finalized until after.
  • The Golden State Warriors will lose their top two assistants this offseason. Terry Stotts and Jerry Stackhouse are both departing after their contracts expired. Stotts told ESPN he informed Steve Kerr late in the regular season that he would not return, and that he remains open to head coaching opportunities. Former New Orleans Pelicans head coach Willie Green, who started his NBA coaching career with Golden State in 2016 and spent three seasons under Kerr, is among the candidates to fill one of the vacancies.
  • The Chicago Bulls are building out their front office under new EVP Bryson Graham. The Bulls are finalizing a deal to hire Orlando Magic assistant GM Stephen Mervis as Senior VP of Basketball Operations, per Shams Charania. They will also bring in Acie Law IV, most recently the Brooklyn Nets' director of player personnel and a championship-winning scout with the Thunder, as VP of Player Personnel, per HoopsHype's Michael Scotto.
  • The Philadelphia 76ers are moving quickly to fill the void left by Daryl Morey's dismissal. New team president Bob Myers said at a press conference that he plans to communicate "five to seven days a week" with whoever is hired to lead basketball operations, and that the goal is to have someone in place before the June 23-24 draft.
  • The Dallas Mavericks filled their GM vacancy by hiring Mike Schmitz, the first significant move by new team president Masai Ujiri. Schmitz came up through DraftExpress, spent five years as an ESPN analyst and served as Portland's assistant GM starting in 2022. "Incredible scout. Incredible leader," Ujiri said of Schmitz. "It's a whole package."
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers held off the Detroit Pistons 117-113 in overtime in Game 5. Evan Mobley credited composure after the Cavs overcame a nine-point deficit late in regulation. Detroit had three players listed as questionable for Game 6 on Friday: Duncan Robinson (low back soreness), Kevin Huerter (left adductor strain) and Caris LeVert (right heel contusion), per HoopsHype.
  • Jayson Tatum opened up about just how difficult his Achilles recovery was in the immediate aftermath of the injury. He described needing 45 minutes to shower and get dressed, using a walker and a scooter just to move around his home. "Your tendon looked perfectly healthy. You just got unlucky," he was told by his doctor, a moment Tatum said was a "tough pill to swallow."
  • Gregg Popovich has settled into his "El Jefe" role with the San Antonio Spurs in a way that sounds different from his head coaching years. Rookie Carter Bryant told The Athletic that having Popovich around has been "amazing," and that he hears the stories about how demanding Pop could be. "He's not my head coach," Bryant said, "so I don't kind of get that same experience."
  • Isaiah Thomas has rejoined the Boston Celtics as a pro and college scout, based out of the Seattle area. The move reunites him with the organization where he became one of the most popular players in franchise history, and gives president Brad Stevens added West Coast coverage.
  • Draymond Green used his podcast to openly question what the Los Angeles Clippers plan to do with Kawhi Leonard after winning the fifth pick in the lottery. Green suggested the Clippers face a fork in the road: pair a young prospect with Kawhi, or move Leonard for draft assets and commit to a full rebuild.
  • NBA referees have called 11% more personal fouls per game in these playoffs than they did during the regular season, a gap that could rank among the largest in NBA history. Senior VP of referee development Monty McCutchen defended the increase at the draft combine, saying the physical intensity of a playoff series simply could not be sustained over 82 games.
