NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron still without offer from Lakers, Jaylen Brown trade expected, Wolves land LaMelo

LeBron James remains without a contract offer from the Los Angeles Lakers, and communication between the two sides has gone quiet since an early check-in call at the start of free agency. "Since then though there hasn't been much communication, from my understanding, between the Lakers and LeBron James' side," Shams Charania said on NBA Today. "And certainly not an offer yet." The Lakers can create up to $50 million in cap space, and James may be willing to flex on his contract figure depending on how the team uses that room around Luka Doncic. A return to Cleveland has drawn speculation, but Brian Windhorst threw cold water on it, saying the Cavaliers can only offer LeBron "four million bucks" at present, adding that he would need the Cavs to trade Jarrett Allen to create max-level money, a move Windhorst said "I don't think would improve the Cavs."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Brian Windhorst said on ESPN's Unsportsmanlike that Jaylen Brown "is going to get traded," with the Boston Celtics actively discussing him with multiple teams. Windhorst also noted that while Celtics president Brad Stevens praised Brown in recent media availability, he never ruled out a trade. The Charlotte Hornets are among the teams in discussions, per multiple reports.
  • The Minnesota Timberwolves considered both Brown and LaMelo Ball before landing on LaMelo, according to Jon Krawczynski. "The Celtics were asking more for Brown than what the Hornets wanted for Ball," Krawczynski said. The Wolves had checked in on LaMelo multiple times over the years before Charlotte finally engaged on a deal.
  • Timberwolves GM Tim Connelly and coach Chris Finch have long believed LaMelo is a natural fit alongside Anthony Edwards, Krawczynski reported. One GM who was texted about the deal told Tim MacMahon in one sentence that Connelly is "a riverboat gambler," and MacMahon added that the move creates a real hole in the roster that Finch will need to get creative to fill. Rival teams asking about Jaden McDaniels have been firmly turned away, with sources telling Yahoo Sports he is considered part of Minnesota's new big three.
  • The Wolves were also pursuing a quality point guard after sending Julius Randle to Brooklyn, a search that at one point included Cleveland's James Harden and Memphis' Ja Morant. Minnesota now has LaMelo, a $33 million trade exception, and a cap book $66 million lighter. Sandro Mamukelashvili of Toronto has been mentioned as a potential addition, though that depends on his free agent market.
  • LaVar Ball had thoughts on the LaMelo trade. "Minnesota Timberwolves, y'all did the coldest move in the game," he said on YouTube. He then lobbied the Wolves to sign Lonzo and LiAngelo Ball too, promising it would be "the most watched team ever in the NBA."
  • Charlotte, meanwhile, quickly resumed negotiations with free agent guard Coby White after the LaMelo deal cleared. White had initially turned down a Hornets offer after the Finals, seeking a starting role elsewhere, but the LaMelo trade gave him clarity on his future. His three-year, $74 million deal is fully guaranteed with no options. Illinois guard Kylan Boswell also signed a two-way contract with Charlotte.
  • Kareem Abdul-Jabbar has put four of his NBA championship rings from his time with the Lakers up for auction through Goldin Auctions, with much of the proceeds benefiting his Skyhook Foundation. The rings from 1980, 1985, 1987 and 1988 start at a minimum bid of $60,000. "Instead of gazing at the sparkle of jewels or gold plating celebrating something I did a long time ago," Abdul-Jabbar wrote on his blog, "I'd rather look into the delighted face of a child holding their first caterpillar."
  • The Golden State Warriors have signed a new jersey patch deal with AI cloud firm Iren worth more than $50 million per year on average, which would make it the richest sponsorship agreement in North American team sports history.
  • No. 1 overall pick AJ Dybantsa, who went to the Washington Wizards, said the most accurate NBA comparison he's heard for himself is Tracy McGrady. "Being 6'9, jumping over people to shoot, being a windmill, and being super athletic, I think that is kind of me," Dybantsa said. He also said he watched Jordan film after someone on TikTok ranked him above MJ, and "kind of seen the comparison a little bit" while still believing Jordan is the GOAT. Dybantsa added that he is "grateful" Washington also acquired Trae Young and Anthony Davis to surround him with veterans.
  • Per HoopsHype, Toronto Raptors guard Chucky Hepburn will sign his two-way qualifying offer and return to Toronto next season. He averaged 12.4 points, 8.6 assists and 2.4 steals per game for the Raptors 905 in the G League. The Raptors also exercised the team option on Jamal Shead for 2026-27 at $2.30 million. On the trade front, Marc Stein reported that Toronto has registered interest in re-acquiring Kawhi Leonard should he become available, though Leonard's first preference remains staying with the Clippers. Sources say the only teams Leonard would consider signing an extension with are Toronto and San Antonio.
  • Restricted free agent Mark Williams intends to sign a three-year, $38 million fully guaranteed deal to return to the Phoenix Suns, per Shams Charania. The Suns also recently re-signed guards Collin Gillespie and Jordan Goodwin. Williams will start at center, with Mamukelashvili slotted as a potential future option once available. His contract is considered tradeable if he performs well, according to John Gambadoro.
  • Zach LaVine and the Sacramento Kings are working together through his agent Rich Paul to find a trade destination, per Sam Amick. One concept is a sign-and-trade for a lower number to get LaVine to his next spot, though Amick said he is "not aware of anything that's promising" right now. LaVine is not expected to leave money on the table in free agency if a trade cannot be arranged.
  • Micah Nori, hired as the new head coach of the Portland Trail Blazers, spent 28 years as an NBA assistant before landing his first head coaching job, finishing second or third for at least five openings along the way, including Lakers and Cavaliers interviews in 2024. "Did I get discouraged? Absolutely not," Nori told the Rose Garden Report. Nori takes over a roster that already has playoff experience, which he called a rare luxury for a first-time head coach.
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers are nearing a deal to sell a 5% to 10% stake to private equity firm Blue Owl, valuing the franchise at a combined $5.5 billion including the city's incoming WNBA team. Owner Dan Gilbert retains majority control and had hired Allen & Company last year to explore selling up to 15%.
  • Milwaukee Bucks center Jericho Sims will exercise his $2.8 million player option for 2026-27, and the Atlanta Hawks exercised their $2.41 million team option on Mouhamed Gueye for next season. The Hawks and Buddy Hield have also agreed to push back the guarantee deadline on his $9.7 million contract to give both sides more time to explore options.
  • The 2026 NBA Draft averaged 4.2 million viewers on ESPN and ABC, up 10% year over year, peaking at 5.5 million during the 8:15 p.m. ET window when Dybantsa went No. 1 to Washington.
  • Austin Reaves is already No. 1 in guaranteed career earnings among all undrafted players in NBA history.
