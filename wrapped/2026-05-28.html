NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron waiting on Lakers offer, Wemby needs more in Game 6, Giannis trade strategy emerges

LeBron James is waiting on the Los Angeles Lakers to come to him, not the other way around. Per Brian Windhorst, James and his camp want the Lakers to make a formal contract offer, and if it comes in below the maximum, they expect the franchise to explain why and identify which other players would receive the remaining money. The Lakers laid off more than a dozen business-side employees Wednesday across marketing, communications, content and corporate partnerships as part of a reorganization under new franchise leadership. That restructuring adds a layer of uncertainty to a summer that was already complicated, with James's free agency unresolved and the front office still taking shape.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Bucks' situation around Giannis Antetokounmpo is drawing strategic thinking around the league. One front-office strategist told Marc Stein that if he were advising Milwaukee on a trade, he would push them to concentrate return picks in a single draft year rather than spread them out, particularly if the 2028 class projects as stronger than 2027 or 2029. Separately, Stein also reported that the NBA's new draft lottery reform could include a live, televised drawing rather than the current behind-closed-doors format, with sources expecting the lottery itself to become a public event in real time.
  • The Spurs face elimination Thursday in Game 6 against the Thunder, and coach Mitch Johnson was direct after Game 5 about what Victor Wembanyama needs to do differently. "He's got to take more than 15 shots," Johnson said. "Even with the [12] free throws, he's going to have to score more than 20 points for sure." Wembanyama was unavailable to speak to reporters after the game, though he escaped a fine for skipping the postgame press conference because, per Chris Haynes, he had been voted by the Pro Basketball Writers Association as one of the most accommodating players to media, and they let him off the hook.
  • De'Aaron Fox has been playing through a high right ankle sprain since Game 4 of the previous series, and he spoke at length about how routine and stability off the court keep him steady on it. "If I'm not making shots, I feel like I don't get sporadic," Fox told ESPN. "Even when I'm making shots, my game doesn't change. I think I stay kind of level, through it all."
  • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander fell 22 times on shot attempts in the Western Conference finals through five games, twice as often on a per-game basis as he did in the first-round sweep over the Suns. His attorneys sent a cease and desist letter to Underdog Sports, a fantasy and prediction markets company that had created a version of the board game Operation called "Unethical Hoops," in which a buzzer sounded anytime SGA was touched. The letter demanded Underdog permanently stop using Gilgeous-Alexander's name, image and likeness across all platforms.
  • For Game 6, the Thunder injury report is unchanged: Ajay Mitchell (soleus) is out, and Jalen Williams (hamstring) is listed as questionable.
  • Chet Holmgren addressed the question of whether he'd ever want a bigger offensive role with the Thunder. "Ego has been the downfall of many people's careers," Holmgren said. "Would I trade what we just accomplished last year and the opportunity that we have and the group that we have? Would I sacrifice that to go be able to shoot 20 shots a game? I don't think so."
  • Donte DiVincenzo tore his Achilles tendon late in the Timberwolves' playoff run, and a full recovery timeline remains unclear. Several within Minnesota's organization believe he could return at some point next season, though the extent of what he can give after the injury is an open question. DiVincenzo flew to New York for surgery within 24 hours of getting hurt. "There's nothing more that motivates me than trying to get in front of and play in front of our fans," he said.
  • The Atlanta Hawks are signing GM Onsi Saleh to a long-term extension and promoting him to President of Basketball Operations, per Shams Charania. Saleh, who finished runner-up for the 2026 league executive of the year award, took over as Hawks GM last offseason after previous stints with Golden State and San Antonio.
  • The Chicago Bulls have received permission to interview Cavaliers associate head coach Johnnie Bryant for their head coaching vacancy. Bryant has 12 years of NBA assistant experience and previously served as associate head coach with the Knicks.
  • The New York Knicks are donating 250 tickets per home Finals game to underprivileged youth through their Garden of Dreams Foundation, with at least 500 guaranteed across Games 3 and 4. Meanwhile, the watch party scene outside Madison Square Garden has drawn some friction among longtime fans, with some regulars complaining that social media influencers have taken over the space, using the celebrations as content rather than genuinely following the team.
  • Miles McBride, a second-round pick who averaged 2.2 points on 29.6 percent shooting as a rookie, has made it to the NBA Finals with the Knicks. Asked to reflect on the journey, he admitted self-doubt crept in at his lowest points. "For sure. For sure," McBride said. "It was some tough, long nights."
  • New Knicks coach Mike Brown spent his time after getting fired traveling with his wife: Sydney for UFC, Puerto Vallarta, St. Barts, and a few days in New York City. "If an opportunity came up, great," Brown said. "If it didn't, shoot, I felt lucky, blessed, fortunate. I had a good run."
  • Baylor's Tounde Yessoufou, a projected first-round pick, has withdrawn from the 2026 NBA Draft and will play for Rick Pitino at St. John's next season, per Shams Charania. He joins a wave of players pulling out before the deadline: Stanford's Andrej Stojakovic returns to Illinois, Milan Momcilovic is headed back to school, Vanderbilt's Tyler Tanner returns to Vanderbilt, Billy Richmond returns to Arkansas, and Flory Bidunga is heading to Louisville.
  • The NIL economy is reshaping how players think about the draft. Star-level players at high-major programs can gross $4-to-6 million per season, and agents are now forcing players to weigh those figures against projected rookie-scale salaries. Players are no longer just looking for a first-round guarantee; they want to know if the NBA deal will actually match what they'd make by staying.
  • Adam Silver told the Pat McAfee Show the NBA will implement an AI automated system to review officiating calls and also addressed the league's plans to reduce tanking, which include the new draft lottery reform limiting how far the worst teams can fall even if they go unselected.
