NBA Rumors Wrap: Spurs-Thunder WCF set, Jalen Williams eyes return, Wizards willing to deal at No. 1 **San Antonio** and **Oklahoma City** will meet in the Western Conference Finals starting Monday after the **Spurs** eliminated the Timberwolves 139-109 in Game 6 Friday, the most lopsided series win in Spurs franchise history. **Victor Wembanyama** said the moment felt surreal: "Just the words 'conference finals,' it's crazy, like something I heard my whole life, and now being in it is just special." Coach **Mitch Johnson** acknowledged the challenge ahead: "They're the defending champs. They haven't lost a game in the playoffs, so we'll have to be at our best to make it competitive." **Jalen Williams**, who has been out since April 22 with a Grade 1 left hamstring strain, said he has not had to rush his recovery and expects to be healthy for the series. Game 1 is Monday in Oklahoma City, per the published schedule. Here are more notes from around the league: • Wembanyama's postseason run has been historically dominant. He has 41 blocks in his first 10 playoff games, second-most in NBA history behind Dikembe Mutombo's 62 since blocks were recorded in 1974. The Spurs beat the Wolves by a combined 97 points across the series, the sixth-largest margin of victory in any postseason run in league history, per Law Murray. • **Stephon Castle** on facing OKC: "They're the defending champs. We know it's gonna be tough to knock them off but I think we're pretty confident we could do it." Castle also drew high praise from Wemby, who called him "amazing" for his ability to read defenses and push defenders off his body through screens. • **Anthony Edwards** did not sugarcoat the Wolves' collapse. "You're supposed to build championship habits or playoff habits during the regular season," he said after the loss. When asked if Minnesota did that: "No, we didn't build the habits during the regular season." It was the first time in franchise history the Timberwolves lost a second-round series, after entering this postseason 3-0 in that round. • **Detroit** is heading to a Game 7 in Cleveland after winning four elimination games this postseason, the most by any team in a single playoff run since the 2023 Celtics. Their average margin of victory in those games is plus-16.0, second-highest in NBA history with at least three elimination games played. **Kevin Huerter** had re-aggravated his adductor injury in Game 5 and received an injection to play in Game 6. • **Donovan Mitchell** on his rough Game 6: "I can't dwell on it. I missed shots tonight, and sure, some of them were probably tougher shots that I could have handled better. But it's more about the overall force and impact on the game." He said he is not focused on the misses heading into Game 7 Sunday night in Detroit. • **James Harden** when asked why the Cavaliers haven't played with the intensity needed to close out Detroit: "That's a great question." Harden will finish with more turnovers than made field goals for the fifth time this postseason and the 31st time in his playoff career. • **Washington** is signaling it is open to dealing the No. 1 pick, but there is a wrinkle at the top. Brian Windhorst said on ESPN: "Washington is sending out the message that they're willing to deal, but if they do a deal, people think it will most likely not be with Utah." Windhorst added there were ruffled feathers in Salt Lake City over how that messaging has been perceived. • The **Warriors** are keeping all options open with the 11th pick in the draft. Mike Dunleavy said the team wants Steph Curry to finish his career in Golden State, though formal extension talks have not started yet. On the draft pick, Dunleavy said the Warriors are open to using it, moving up, moving back, or trading it for a veteran, per HoopsHype. • **Fred VanVleet** addressed the Rockets' chaotic season head-on. "Adding KD, it's a whole new team. We didn't get one practice together," he said. "There's no continuity. Our system changed. Everything changed." On his own free agency outlook, VanVleet said: "I know I add value. I know what I bring to the table, and I know that we will be a better team with me on the team." • **Rudy Gobert** on Wembanyama's rapid rise: "I wouldn't say surprised. It's credit to his work. Now it's gonna be interesting to see how he's ready to face that next challenge and see if he takes his team to the promised land this year." Gobert said he was aware early that Wemby had the right mindset to get there quickly. • The NBA Draft Lottery set a viewership record last weekend, drawing 2.3 million viewers on ABC, up 23% from last year. USA Network also debuted its WNBA coverage with 529,000 viewers for Aces-Sparks on Mother's Day, up 18% from the WNBA's cable average last season. • **Mitchell Robinson** is drawing outside interest as he heads to free agency. Sources around the league have identified the Kings as a threat to sign him away from the Knicks. Sacramento GM Scott Perry originally drafted Robinson during his time with New York's front office and has remained a fan. • **Detroit** faces decisions this summer beyond Game 7. Tim Bontemps said it has been "a very concerning month" on the extension fronts for both **Jalen Duren** and **Ausar Thompson**, raising questions about whether those two can be part of a championship core alongside **Cade Cunningham**. • **Tyrese Haliburton** on the Pacers' draft lottery miss: "I didn't think KP had to apologize. They took a calculated risk, and I'm still rolling with the decision. I'm excited to add Zu to our crew, and I think we're going to be really good alongside him." The Pacers landed **Ivica Zubac** via the draft lottery. • The Los Angeles City Council officially declared May 15 as "Metta World Peace Day" in recognition of **Metta World Peace's** mental health advocacy work, with the resolution introduced during Mental Health Awareness Month. • The NBA's MVP for 2025-26 will be announced Sunday night on Prime Video, per Marc Stein. **Shai Gilgeous-Alexander** is widely expected to take home the award.
