NBA Rumors Wrap: Steph Curry ready for Year 18, Michael Porter Jr. extension uncertainty, Peyton Watson opens up on injury

Stephen Curry is entering his 18th NBA season with no contract extension in hand, and he sounds perfectly fine with that. The Warriors guard, who is subject to the NBA's Over-38 Rule limiting him to a maximum two-year, $136.7 million deal, told NBC Sports Bay Area that "there's no question I want to be here the rest of my career" and that the contract will get done "at the right time." Curry missed significant time last season and Golden State was eliminated in the play-in round, but he sounds motivated heading into training camp in four weeks. His wife Ayesha added that "he hasn't stopped since last season ended" and predicted "some fun surprises over the next couple of years."

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Michael Porter Jr. heads into the final year of his deal with the Nets at $40 million, and his extension situation is genuinely complicated. He showed All-Star-level play last season, but that came on a bad Brooklyn team, and contenders eyeing a trade want to know what an extension would cost before committing. An Eastern Conference executive put it plainly: "The Nuggets overpaid him when they gave him that extension, and if he wants to be paid based on that, that is not going to be an easy sell."
  • Peyton Watson opened up about the injury nightmare that defined his final year in Denver. In a wide-ranging interview, Watson revealed he re-injured his hamstring in Game 80 against Utah, suffered what doctors said looked like a Grade 2 strain identical to his first injury, and still played in the playoffs feeling less than healthy. He said the Nuggets were "between a rock and a hard place financially," and when restricted free agency arrived, most teams with cap space had already seen it dry up, clearing the path for his move to Cleveland.
  • Kyrie Irving has a $42 million player option for 2027-28, and any trade to a contender likely hinges on an informal agreement around that option, similar to the handshake deal James Harden had in Cleveland. A Western Conference executive told Heavy: "If you have some sense that, OK, we pay him $40 million this year, and next year he opts out and we can do a two-year deal or a three-year deal at a lesser number, then I think you can do something."
  • The Pelicans have shopped Trey Murphy but have not gotten serious about moving the 26-year-old wing yet. New Orleans has asked for as many as four first-round picks, citing the returns for Mikal Bridges and Desmond Bane. Realistically, the market is more likely to land at "a good young rotation player and two first-rounders."
  • Nikola Jokic played through a serious illness during Serbia's FIBA World Cup qualifier, competing after spending three days on an IV drip. Serbia's coach Dusan Alimpijevic praised his captain after the game, saying Jokic "refused to give up" despite his condition. Jokic finished with eight points and eight rebounds, well below his usual production, but the win was secured. Miami Heat coach Erik Spoelstra was in attendance to watch two of his players, Nikola Jovic and Simone Fontecchio, face off on opposite sides.
  • Deni Avdija addressed his absence from Israel's FIBA qualifier window, saying Portland "wasn't willing to take a risk" with his health coming off an injury, leaving him without the option to play. "My commitment to the national team and my country," he wrote, is not something he's willing to have questioned.
  • Victor Wembanyama led France through a strong FIBA qualifier window and is now turning his attention back to San Antonio. "There's nowhere else quite like it when it comes to putting in the work," Wembanyama told L'Equipe.
  • Luguentz Dort called his trade from Oklahoma City to Atlanta "amazing," largely because Nickeil Alexander-Walker is one of his closest friends. The two grew up playing together and against each other, and Dort said the first call he made when he got the news was to Nickeil. "He's helped me out throughout this whole transition," Dort said.
  • The Lakers ownership situation is creating a quiet organizational freeze. Multiple sources told the New York Times that it would be "foolish" to make consequential hires while the Iger and Kushner groups are still in their due diligence phase. That includes an assistant general manager position Rob Pelinka had committed to filling this offseason for personnel and scouting. Still, sources across departments said they are optimistic about the direction of the franchise under the incoming ownership.
  • Ziaire Williams, who signed with the Lakers on a one-year, $3 million deal in July, hosted his annual basketball clinic in Lancaster before camp opens. "I just thought it was a great opportunity," Williams told the Southern California News Group. "Being that big wing, versatile player that they needed, I just thought it was the best fit."
  • The Cavaliers have waived and stretched Cameron Whitmore's contract per HoopsHype. He will earn $1,819,437 annually over the next three seasons.
  • Jalen Brunson addressed the departure of Mitchell Robinson to the Celtics, saying Robinson is "my guy" even if the rivalry changes things on game nights. "Now, whenever we play him, four times a year, he's not my friend that day," Brunson told the New York Post.
  • Trey Kaufman-Renn, whom the Timberwolves drafted 59th overall in June, is fighting the NCAA in court over its "five in five" age-based eligibility model. A hearing on his motion for a temporary restraining order has been rescheduled for September 8.
  • Jay Huff has added 10 to 15 pounds of muscle this offseason and spent part of his summer scrimmaging in Los Angeles with about 10 Pacers teammates, including star point guard Tyrese Haliburton, who missed all of 2025-26. "He makes everybody around him better," Huff said of Haliburton, adding that their developing chemistry has been encouraging. On a related note, Huff's contract for next season is now fully guaranteed after he hit a minutes-played threshold last year, giving him a level of stability he has not had before in his career.
  • Former NBA player Luol Deng is being sued for more than $1 million by law firm Barnes and Thornburg, which alleges he hired them for a series of real estate deals and never paid his bills.
  • Myles Turner has pledged $1 million to Empowering the Masses, a Dallas nonprofit focused on food insecurity, through his W.A.R.M. foundation. The pledge is structured as $100,000 a year over 10 years.
