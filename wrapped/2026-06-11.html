NBA Rumors Wrap: Knicks stun Spurs in historic Game 4 comeback, LeBron seeking max from Lakers, NBA-EuroLeague talks advance

The New York Knicks pulled off one of the most stunning comebacks in NBA Finals history Wednesday night, erasing a 29-point deficit to beat the San Antonio Spurs 107-106 in Game 4 and take a 3-1 series lead. Teams were 0-96 when trailing by 17 or more points in the final nine minutes of regulation in a Finals game since 1970-71, and the Knicks just made that 1-96. OG Anunoby was the hero, going 7-for-9 in the second half with five threes, then blocking De'Aaron Fox's late layup attempt before hitting the go-ahead bucket. The Knicks can close out the franchise's third championship with a win Saturday at the Frost Bank Center in San Antonio, where Game 5 tips at 8:30 p.m. ET.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Victor Wembanyama went 3-for-14 in the second half and missed four of his five free throw attempts down the stretch as the Spurs blew their fifth double-digit lead of this postseason, tied for the most in a single playoff run in the play-by-play era, matching the 2003 Spurs. Wemby was honest after: "We worked too hard and just gave up our lead. It hurts." On the path forward, he said, "I think it's going one of two ways. The bad one would be giving up. The good one would be getting stronger from this, getting more together."
  • • Fox explained his fateful late decision to attack the basket rather than run the clock: "I just thought I'd be able to outrun OG. That's it." Anunoby's block set up the Knicks' final possession, and Fox acknowledged the play afterward: "I tried to get a layup to get up three. Force them to need a three, and OG made a good block."
  • • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson told his team to sit with the pain Wednesday night. "I told them to feel the emotion tonight, because they're competitive, and that's not humanly possible to not take all the emotion out. They're going to need to be ready to play in two days." Stephon Castle echoed his coach's belief that San Antonio has driven results in this series: "Coach Mitch said it best, we've pretty much dictated the winner and loser of all these games."
  • • Knicks coach Mike Brown said he "challenged" Anunoby at shootaround Wednesday morning, telling him he needed to be a "monster on the offensive glass" given his size and athleticism. Brown added: "I don't know if there is a play bigger in the history of Knicks basketball." When the moment came, Anunoby's response to reporters was characteristically brief: "It feels cool."
  • • Josh Hart made sure to single out Anunoby in the locker room, and the reason was personal. "I got a special shoutout for OG because he saved me," Hart said. "At least for this game but he saved me a lifetime of regret." Hart had missed a layup late that would have put the Knicks ahead.
  • • Jose Alvarado outscored the entire Spurs bench 8-6 in the second half of Game 4. The Knicks are plus-31 in 49 minutes with Alvarado on the court this series, and minus-23 in 131 minutes without him. Alvarado himself was candid postgame: "I'm not going to sugarcoat this, I was about to cry." Brown had his own tribute in the locker room: "I know a lot of you guys can't, because you're in the media, but I'mma fucking clap for Jose. Sorry, Mom."
  • • Jalen Brunson opened his postgame press conference by paying tribute to a Make-A-Wish recipient named Jonathan from North Carolina, whom he had FaceTimed the week prior. Brunson said Jonathan had since passed away, and was visibly emotional before taking questions. On the series itself, he kept it direct: "There's nothing to celebrate. It's not over yet. Not even close."
  • • Dylan Harper scored 21 points on 8-of-12 shooting in Game 4, the youngest 20-point game in Finals history. The effort went for nothing in the loss, but the number stands.
  • • The ratings backdrop for this series is remarkable. Game 3 averaged 23.8 million viewers on ABC and ESPN, the most-watched NBA Finals Game 3 in 28 years, dating back to the 1998 Bulls-Jazz series. The audience peaked at 26.3 million at 11:15 p.m. ET. Ticket demand for Game 5 in San Antonio has spiked accordingly: 37 percent of purchases are coming from New York buyers, with the get-in price up $1,000 since Wednesday's result.
  • • LeBron James and agent Rich Paul are planning to ask the Lakers for a max contract this offseason and want clarity on how the team plans to build around him if they offer less, per NBC Sports. Lakers fans expecting significant roster changes should be prepared for a relatively quiet offseason, league sources told the outlet.
  • • The NBA, EuroLeague and FIBA held "constructive discussions" Wednesday in Mies, Switzerland on a potential NBA Europe collaboration and have scheduled a follow-up meeting for early July. EuroHoops reported no significant progress, but other sources told Sports Business Journal the exchange was positive and negotiations are gaining momentum. A merger remains off the table for now.
  • • The Nuggets are parting ways with longtime broadcasters Scott Hastings and Chris Marlowe. Altitude Sports declined to renew either contract as part of a talent shakeup, with Marlowe having served as the team's regional TV play-by-play voice for 22 years.
  • • Terry Rozier's gambling case saw a procedural development in federal court. The presiding judge wants to hear from the NBA before deciding whether to lift a no-contact requirement that could affect his ability to play. The federal prosecutor said she believes the league prefers the restriction remain in place. The NBA has until Monday to file a letter with the Eastern District court.
  • • Charles Barkley, reacting to the Spurs' collapse from 29 up, called San Antonio "the dumbest basketball team in the history of civilization." Separately, on a controversy involving a comment he made about Cardi B, Barkley said he's hoping TNT fires him over it: "I got 6 or 7 years left on my contract that they know I've got no chance of doing. I would love for them to fire me and have to pay me for the next 6 or 7 years."
