NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard trade talks heat up, Jaylen Brown's future uncertain, Nuggets listening on everyone but Jokic

Kawhi Leonard's future is the dominant story of the offseason, with multiple teams circling the Los Angeles Clippers star as a trade deadline approaches. The most advanced conversations appear to involve the Toronto Raptors, who are building a package around Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick plus draft capital, per Marc Stein. Leonard's willingness to sign an extension with Toronto if traded there has given the talks real momentum, though the Clippers are still trying to maximize their return for their franchise cornerstone. One source offered a candid read on the situation: "The Clippers aren't going to commit to anything with him, so it looks like he's trying to strong-arm his way out of there." Another source noted that Leonard's history in Toronto makes the Raptors uniquely comfortable with the injury risk that has scared off other potential suitors.

The Dallas Mavericks are also in the picture. Mavericks president Masai Ujiri has interest in reuniting with Leonard, and Dallas and the Clippers have discussed a deal involving P.J. Washington, Klay Thompson and draft picks. That gives the Clippers real leverage with Toronto, which wants to avoid a bidding war but has to know another credible offer exists.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Jaylen Brown trade market is wide open. Portland, Toronto, Denver, Brooklyn, Charlotte, Atlanta and the Clippers have all expressed trade interest, per HoopsHype. On a related note, Bill Simmons said Sunday that people in Brown's vicinity at a French festival reported he was acting like he's already done in Boston: "He was acting like he's done in Boston, and that this is it, and I don't think he's very happy with this."
  • Brown pushed back on social media, citing his winning record: "Nobody has won more combined regular season and playoff games since I entered the league 10 years ago." He also took a swipe at analytics: "Analytics have / are ruining the game we playing AI hoops."
  • Portland's Deni Avdija is off the table in Brown discussions, with Scoot Henderson and Shaedon Sharpe considered available. The Celtics have also kept tabs on Aaron Gordon, though Portland's primary target remains Brown, and Gordon is their fallback option if those talks stall.
  • Nikola Jokic still wants to stay in Denver. The Nuggets have strong confidence his "Nugget forever" pledge remains intact, and Jokic has reiterated that desire in recent conversations. If he waits until next summer to sign an extension, he becomes eligible for an additional year on the deal.
  • Denver is willing to listen to offers on anyone not named Jokic. Cam Johnson, on an expiring $23 million deal, is the most likely trade candidate, with the Lakers, Celtics, Heat, Clippers and Magic all showing interest. Nuggets ownership has issued no mandate to cut costs, so how aggressively Denver moves remains unclear.
  • The Charlotte Hornets have traded Miles Bridges to the Phoenix Suns for Grayson Allen, Royce O'Neale and a 2033 first-round pick. Charlotte sent back a 2029 first and a 2027 second. Phoenix plans to extend Bridges beyond his current deal and is already looking at seven or eight free agent shooting guards to replace Allen.
  • The Golden State Warriors are attempting to trade for Washington big Anthony Davis and then sign LeBron James in free agency. While pursuing that combination, the Warriors are also expected to pursue veteran big Jock Landale and scoring guards Anfernee Simons and Collin Sexton as contingency options.
  • The New York Knicks are expected to pursue veteran center Kevon Looney if Mitchell Robinson departs amid the team's second-apron constraints. A Jordan Clarkson reunion is also "still a possibility" despite owner James Dolan's mandate to stay under the second apron.
  • The Miami Heat will have interest in Tim Hardaway Jr. when free agency opens Tuesday night. There has also been internal discussion about Mike Conley, though some in the organization would prefer a younger option at that spot.
  • Detroit's Jalen Duren is believed to be seeking close to $40 million annually, roughly 25 percent of the salary cap. The Pistons want to keep him, Cade Cunningham wants him back, and most executives around the league expect Duren to stay in Detroit.
  • The Brooklyn Nets have declined their $6.25 million team option on Ziaire Williams, making him an unrestricted free agent. The move could be a prelude to re-signing him to a longer deal using Brooklyn's room exception once the team deploys its cap space. The Lakers are among the teams that could look at Williams in free agency.
  • The Atlanta Hawks will fully guarantee Buddy Hield's $9.66 million salary. The Miami Heat have been shopping Nikola Jovic to create salary flexibility for a run at Norman Powell, but his four-year, $62 million deal has drawn little interest without draft sweeteners attached.
  • Jay Triano is a leading candidate to join Portland's new coaching staff under first-year head coach Micah Nori. Triano, who previously coached the Raptors and Suns and spent years as Canada's national team head coach, worked under Jason Kidd in Dallas last season.
  • NBA Europe is planning to launch in October 2027 with 12 permanent franchises in cities including London, Paris, Madrid, Milan and Munich. Bids for London and Paris have already topped $1 billion, and the league is projecting more than $1.5 billion in fixed and performance distributions to clubs over its first six years.
