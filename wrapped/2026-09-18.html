NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's Polymarket deal pays more than his 76ers salary, Kawhi nearing Raptors extension, Saddiq Bey locks up $55.5M

LeBron James is set to earn more from a prediction-market endorsement than from playing professional basketball this season. Front Office Sports reported that Polymarket is paying James $15 million per year under a recently announced partnership, dwarfing his $3.8 million salary with the 76ers. James is purely an endorser under the deal, not an investor, and the agreement is an ongoing engagement with multiple deliverables including social media posts and content. The deal also ropes in Derek Jeter and Eli Manning in smaller roles, with other celebrities paid only for the one commercial. The NBA is reportedly closing in on its own prediction-market agreements, though the NFL remains a holdout on the category entirely.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Kawhi Leonard and the Toronto Raptors are nearing an agreement on a contract extension, with both sides having held advanced discussions. Leonard is eligible to sign for up to two years and $126 million. The Raptors open training camp September 29 in Quebec City, and the expectation is the extension gets done before the team leaves for Quebec.
  • • Saddiq Bey is staying in New Orleans. The Pelicans forward agreed to a three-year, $55.5 million contract extension that includes a trade kicker and runs fully guaranteed through 2029-30. Bey missed the entire 2024-25 season with a torn ACL, then came back last year to average a career-high 17.7 points per game.
  • • Stephen Curry's contract situation with the Warriors remains unresolved, though a resolution appears close. Anthony Slater said clarity should come within the next week and a half on whether Curry wants an extension now or prefers to wait. Curry himself has been clear about his intentions: "I've always stated that I wanna be a lifer with Golden State," he said, adding that the contract situation "will take care of itself."
  • • Curry also addressed his new footwear partnership, explaining that Li-Ning won out after an extensive search. "I tried literally everything and there was a reason that Li-Ning stood tall at the end because the product reigned supreme," Curry said, adding that Dwyane Wade and Jimmy Butler helped set the stage with the brand.
  • • The Warriors officially welcomed Frank Vogel to their coaching staff as associate head coach. Vogel spent the past two seasons with the Mavericks as an assistant after previously serving as head coach in Indiana, Orlando, Los Angeles and Phoenix.
  • • Mark Walter, who owns the Lakers and Dodgers, is the subject of a new class-action lawsuit filed in Florida federal court. The suit names Walter and several of his insurance companies, alleging they hid a federal investigation from customers while funneling money into his other businesses. The Lakers are written into the complaint as context for Walter's alleged fundraising activity, though the franchise is not a defendant and is not accused of any wrongdoing.
  • • Jayson Tatum addressed the Jaylen Brown trade during an appearance on The Dan Patrick Show. "For me, I love that guy," Tatum said. "What we were able to accomplish together in nine years — went to the conference finals five times, we went to the championship twice, we won a championship. You can't talk about him without talking about me and vice versa."
  • • The Suns added frontcourt depth with Duop Reath, signing the former Trail Blazers center as a training camp invite. The move comes with Mark Williams sidelined by a shoulder injury that is expected to keep him out for several months. Phoenix begins camp September 29 and must trim its roster to 15 by October 19.
  • • Per HoopsHype, the Suns also signed guard David Stockton to a training camp deal. The Warriors added center Jacksen Moni on a training camp contract, and the Timberwolves brought in guard Jules Bernard on a similar deal.
  • • Jalen Brunson will guest star on Law & Order: SVU's 28th season, appearing in the fifth episode on November 5. The Knicks point guard is a longtime fan of the show and previously appeared alongside star Mariska Hargitay at the Emmys.
  • • The Knicks suspended individual game ticket sales after an internal pricing error sent costs to eye-popping levels, with the season opener against the Sixers listed at more than $1,000 minimum. MSG said it is rolling prices back to last year's levels plus an average 12 percent increase, and anyone who already purchased tickets will be refunded the difference.
  • • Donovan Mitchell was asked about his new Heat teammates and offered a straightforward read on what makes the roster dangerous. "All he gotta do is stand right there because Giannis has so much gravity. If you help on Giannis, who you gonna help off of?" Mitchell said of Klay Thompson, calling Klay a top-five shooter ever.
  • • Davion Mitchell opened up about a difficult stretch entering last season, saying he was not mentally in a good place during training camp. "I was so messed up last year in the summertime that it was kind of hard for me to just be myself even in training camp," Mitchell said, adding that coach Erik Spoelstra recognized what he was going through and gave him space. He also described what it feels like to walk into Pat Riley's office at season's end: "every single time, I just feel like my heart started pumping fast."
  • • Jordan Clarkson spoke to GQ Philippines about his time with the Knicks and his desire to return to the FIBA stage. "Another World Cup is coming up, and I want to be there," he said, adding that he hopes to one day see Jalen Green and Dylan Harper suit up for Gilas as well.
  • • Victor Wembanyama is back in San Antonio and keeping a low profile as he prepares for his fourth season. L'Equipe reported he has been training intensively with teammates while limiting public appearances, with only one American TV interview planned ahead of a season that opens with the Spurs viewed as title contenders.
  • • Jonas Valanciunas made his official debut for Zalgiris Kaunas in Lithuania's domestic league, finishing with 14 points on 6-of-7 shooting inside, along with two assists in 21 minutes. Zalgiris won 98-77.
