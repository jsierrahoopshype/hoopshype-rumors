NBA Rumors Wrap: Kyrie Irving a trade target all season, Haliburton returns to OKC, Pritchard locks in with Celtics

Kyrie Irving's time with the Dallas Mavericks may be running out before it officially ends. NBA insider Jake Fischer said on Bleacher Report that he expects Irving to be a trade target for rival teams all the way through the February 11th deadline. The argument is straightforward: Irving is nearly 15 years older than Cooper Flagg, and his contract is winding down at the exact moment Dallas is building around a teenager. Fischer believes the fit issue is hard to ignore. Whether the Mavericks pull the trigger is another matter, but the chatter around Irving is unlikely to quiet down once the regular season begins.

Meanwhile, Fischer also weighed in on another contract situation in the Carolinas. Brandon Miller is pushing for close to a max extension from the Charlotte Hornets, and Fischer said he thinks it will be "very challenging" for Charlotte to get the deal done below the five-year, $200 million threshold Miller is targeting. The Hornets are in a tricky spot, trying to build around Miller while also managing a tight financial situation.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Payton Pritchard is staying in Boston. He agreed to a two-year, $44.2 million extension with a mutual option in the 2029-30 season, per Shams Charania. Because Pritchard's current salary falls below the estimated average player salary, he was eligible to extend at 140% of that figure with 8% raises, meaning the annual numbers land around $21.2 million and $22.9 million.
  • Tyrese Haliburton returned to OKC for a preseason game and scored 24 points in the Pacers' loss to the Thunder, his first time back in the building since tearing his Achilles in Game 7 of the 2025 NBA Finals. "I thought I would feel maybe a little more emotion coming in here," Haliburton said. "It wasn't nearly as bad as I thought it was gonna be." Thunder coach Mark Daigneault also reflected on Haliburton and the Pacers' 2024 playoff run: "They were a four seed and really got on a heater in the playoffs, and he was driving that both with his individual offense and what he does to activate his teammates."
  • The Pacers are confident their trade for Ivica Zubac will help reopen their championship window, though they acknowledge Zubac brings different strengths than the departed Myles Turner. Indiana finished last season with its fewest wins in franchise history after a wave of injuries. "You can take our preseason rankings as disrespect," Haliburton said, "but at the same time, you got to understand we put ourselves in that position by the way we played last year."
  • Giannis Antetokounmpo is adjusting to life in Miami and says the pace is different from Milwaukee. He lives downtown for a bit of peace, though that only goes so far. "I am a dad, I am a regular guy, I go to Costco and I try to get stuff," Giannis said. "Once I walked in there, I had people coming up to me, like, what are you doing here? And I was like, I am just trying to feed my kids."
  • LeBron James said on his Mind the Game podcast that OG Anunoby was the real MVP of the Knicks in the Finals, even though Jalen Brunson won the award. "OG was the most steady person for that team," LeBron said. "The chase-down block on De'Aaron Fox, the tip in Game... He was consistent to continue to knock down threes. Obviously, Jalen Brunson was the head of the snake."
  • Miami Heat guard Pelle Larsson is dealing with a lingering illness that has limited him in the final week before opening night. He left the Heat's last preseason game early and said he likely came back from rest too quickly. "Once my heart rate got up, my head started hurting again," Larsson said. "Right now I'm just taking it slow, step-by-step, and can't really put a percentage on it."
  • The Nuggets plan to keep experimenting with all-bench lineups into the regular season. Coach David Adelman said he has seen success with Cam Whitmore in those groups, and the metrics have backed it up enough to continue. Adelman also said he prefers to cap his rotation at 10 players. "When you get to 11, there's gotta be a reason for it," he said. "Nobody flows well with 11."
  • Myles Turner shared a line from new Bucks coach Darvin Ham that stuck with him. Ham pointed to construction workers outside the facility busting concrete for near-minimum wage, and Turner said it gave him a renewed sense of perspective heading into the season. "We are expected to come out here, keep ourselves in shape, and play basketball to make the checks that we make," Turner said. "So I try to keep that perspective no matter where I am at."
  • The Blazers' new head coach Micah Nori opened training camp by handing out copies of "The Old Man and the Sea" to his coaching staff, then gave a pop quiz. The philosophy came from former boss Chris Finch, who is a Hemingway fan. The quiz results ranged from assistant Mike Williams getting nine of 10 correct to two others managing only two. Nori was not entirely surprised. "Your actions speak so loud I can't hear a word you are saying," he said, laughing.
  • The Washington Wizards are signaling that a rebrand is coming, just not immediately. Team executives pointed to the 2027-28 season as the target, once a three-year renovation of Capital One Arena is complete. Uniform changes appear to be on the way, with longer-term logo changes also under consideration.
  • NBA commissioner Adam Silver weighed in on prediction markets and their growing presence around the league. Silver said the league wants control over those markets, particularly around betting on things like when a coach might get fired. "We want to have control over our intellectual property," Silver said. "We want to make sure we're protecting the integrity of our competition."
  • Knicks owner James Dolan is spending this offseason in politics. He is bankrolling a PAC supporting Republican Bruce Blakeman in the New York governor's race over incumbent Kathy Hochul, and he is now calling out NYC business leaders for not joining him. According to the Times, Hochul learned Dolan had switched sides around the time the Knicks won the championship this year, when she called to congratulate him and he told her directly.
  • The Mavericks made a flurry of roster moves Tuesday, ultimately waiving Chris Mantis and signing F/C Jorge Diaz Graham to the roster. Diaz Graham, a 6-foot-10 forward from the Canary Islands, was the first pick in the G League International Draft and closed Summer League strong with two double-doubles.
  • The San Antonio Spurs waived RJ Davis, who had been on an Exhibit 10 deal. The Sacramento Kings also moved in that space, signing Damari Monsanto and Aaron Nkrumah to Exhibit 10 contracts.
  • Steve Kerr recalled a meeting he set up roughly 22 years ago between then-owner Robert Sarver and former commissioner David Stern to pitch an expansion team in Las Vegas. Stern shot it down cold. "David Stern said, 'There's no way we're having a franchise in Las Vegas because of the gambling,'" Kerr said before Tuesday's preseason game at T-Mobile Arena.
