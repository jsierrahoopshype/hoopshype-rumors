NBA Rumors Wrap: Kawhi Leonard's Raptors trade hits a snag, Sabonis wants out of Sacramento, Sixers' LeBron recruitment detailed

Kawhi Leonard's return to Toronto is taking longer than expected. Per Marc Stein, Leonard joined Raptors players in Miami for a mini-camp but has been restricted to individual workouts because his verbally agreed trade from the Clippers back to Toronto has not yet been made official. Jake Fischer and Stein report that a major reason for the delay is that the Clippers are still working through their new front office structure following NBA suspensions for owner Steve Ballmer and president Lawrence Frank. Still, Brian Windhorst said Monday night that in his conversations with people around the league, he believes the trade will go through, and as originally structured. The Clippers also have no current plans to immediately flip Brandon Ingram to recoup assets from the NBA's punishment, according to Fischer.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Domantas Sabonis wants out of Sacramento. Sabonis changed agents from Wasserman to CAA this summer and is seeking a new contract and commitment from a team outside Sacramento, according to Jake Fischer. Sabonis is extension-eligible, and in a second-apron era, his combination of an all-star profile and defensive limitations makes him a difficult fit for teams operating near the luxury tax threshold. On a related note, Fischer also noted that Sacramento was once offered Trae Young in exchange for Zach LaVine, though that deal never materialized.
  • The Philadelphia 76ers' path to LeBron James started with acquiring Jaylen Brown. Once Boston lost the race for Giannis Antetokounmpo to Miami, Brown's name became unavoidable in trade talks and Philadelphia moved quickly. After completing the Brown trade, the Sixers reached out to LeBron's agent Rich Paul, and Tyrese Maxey, Joel Embiid and Brown all took turns recruiting James over the following days and weeks.
  • James Harden is staying in Cleveland. The Cavaliers officially re-signed Harden to a multi-year contract. He averaged 23.6 points, 8.0 assists and shot a career-best from three last season, splitting time between the Clippers and Cavaliers.
  • The New Orleans Pelicans completed a trade with the Memphis Grizzlies, sending Jordan Hawkins, Micah Peavy and two future second-round picks for AJ Johnson and Taj Gibson, per Shams Charania. The move provides New Orleans nearly $6 million in savings to help absorb Bennedict Mathurin. Memphis will likely need to waive Walter Clayton Jr., D'Angelo Russell, Hawkins and Kris Murray to reach roster compliance.
  • Sacramento officially signed Ben Simmons to a one-year deal. The Kings are listing the three-time All-Star as a 6-foot-10 guard. Simmons' godfather and Arizona State associate head coach David Patrick says Simmons' hunger for the game is back and that after a year and a half away he's become more comfortable shooting from deep.
  • The Kings could have serious cap space to work with next summer. After Zach LaVine comes off the books, GM Scott Perry reportedly has center Jalen Duren's name in highlighter as a free agent target, if Duren is willing to take his qualifying offer from Detroit.
  • Denver officially signed DeMar DeRozan, per the league's transaction log. The Nuggets and Peyton Watson were far apart on a new deal this summer, with Watson's camp asking north of $25 million annually. Denver took calls from the Clippers, Bucks, Hawks and Trail Blazers on a potential Watson sign-and-trade before ultimately moving on.
  • The Lakers will unveil a statue of Phil Jackson on April 9, 2027, before a home game against the Timberwolves. Jackson will be the ninth member of the organization honored at Star Plaza outside Crypto.com Arena, joining Kareem Abdul-Jabbar, Magic Johnson, Jerry West and others.
  • Mike Conley signed with Boston because he wanted to experience playing for a franchise with history and gravitas. "The Boston Celtics are historic," Jon Krawczynski said on The Athletic's podcast, adding that Conley specifically wanted at least one year with a flagship NBA organization. Krawczynski also described how Anthony Edwards would find Conley after every game in the Wolves locker room to go over film, with Conley pointing out what Ant could have done better.
  • Per HoopsHype, the Washington Wizards are promoting Amber Nichols to Vice President of Basketball Operations and Personnel. Nichols had been Washington's Director of Amateur Evaluation and previously served as GM of the Capital City Go-Go G League affiliate. The Atlanta Hawks also made a front office move, promoting Peter Dinwiddie to executive vice president of basketball operations under Onsi Saleh.
  • Minnesota explored the possibility of bringing Sasha Vezenkov back to the NBA before signing Jonathan Kuminga, but Vezenkov indicated he was not open to a return. No formal offer was made. Vezenkov, now back in Greece with Olympiacos, said of his Kings tenure: "Sometimes I think it was not a fair chance from both sides... But I don't regret it all. It was a great experience."
  • A healthy Kyrie Irving could be a trade deadline target for contenders, with Dallas prioritizing its teenage prodigy and new president Masai Ujiri working through the team's long-term plans. Irving averaged 25.5 points and 5.1 assists in a Mavs uniform over his career there. Meanwhile, Jimmy Butler is targeting a return to Golden State's lineup in January or February, though he'll be limited to individual work when training camp opens, with plenty of checkpoints ahead before he's cleared to return from his ACL recovery.
  • Longtime Toronto Raptors broadcast analyst Jack Armstrong does not yet have a contract for the upcoming season with TSN and MLSE, with five weeks to go before the season tips off.
  • Tyler Herro and his ex Katya Henry are in a custody dispute over their two young children. Henry is seeking primary custody and requesting safeguards on Herro's access to the kids, citing his "historical conduct." Herro had initially filed his own petition to establish a parenting schedule and determine child support, then dismissed his case days later.
  • Former Knicks guard Landry Shamet was spotted working as a photographer at the U.S. Open on Tuesday night, shooting courtside during the Jessica Pegula-Emma Navarro quarterfinal match.
