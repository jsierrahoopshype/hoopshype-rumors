NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron's free agency points toward Cleveland, six-team trade sends Giannis to Miami, Lakers pursue Kuminga

Donovan Mitchell has been working behind the scenes to recruit LeBron James to Cleveland, and the effort appears to be making an impression. Per Shams Charania, Mitchell has been "hands-on" in helping the Cavs pursue roster upgrades, and has made clear internally that he would welcome James as a teammate. That aligns with Brian Windhorst's read on the situation: league sources believe the arrow is pointing toward Cleveland, though Windhorst cautioned that "it's just vibes." He also added that LeBron has been spending an unusually long stretch in Akron this summer, longer than any summer since his first year in Miami. The Heat remain very much in the picture, with Windhorst saying Miami and Cleveland are "in their own category" as destinations. Giannis Antetokounmpo, now in Miami after a blockbuster trade, is reportedly hoping LeBron follows him there.

Charania framed the Warriors situation plainly: Golden State's plan was to pair LeBron with Stephen Curry, Draymond Green and Anthony Davis, but short of trading for Davis, the Warriors don't believe they're high on LeBron's list. The Warriors haven't been told that directly, and haven't been engaging Washington on a Davis trade, but the belief internally is that a sign-and-alone deal probably isn't enough.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Bucks made it official: Giannis Antetokounmpo is headed to Miami as part of a six-team trade involving the Pistons, Clippers, Grizzlies, Wizards and Mavericks. GM Jon Horst called it a "very difficult decision" but said it was right for both sides: "This was not a 'have to,'" Horst said. "We truly believed we've found an opportunity which is unique."
  • • Dallas came away from the six-team deal with Santi Aldama, the rights to Tarik Biberović and Marcus Sasser, while sending out AJ Johnson and draft capital. Khris Middleton landed in Washington on a three-year, $17.61 million deal, with only the first year fully guaranteed. Marc Stein reported that Biberović is Dallas-bound and on course to sign despite his acquisition not yet appearing on the transaction log.
  • • The Grizzlies added Isaiah Stewart, D'Angelo Russell, AJ Johnson, a conditional future first and three future seconds in the deal, per Memphis PR. Golden State separately declined to match Quinten Post's three-year, $30 million offer sheet, allowing him to join Memphis. The Grizzlies now have a reshaped frontcourt featuring Post, No. 3 pick Cameron Boozer, Zach Edey and Stewart.
  • • The Lakers are pursuing Jonathan Kuminga and the Hawks are open to making it work. Rob Pelinka and JJ Redick met with Kuminga the day after Atlanta declined his $24.3 million option, pitching him as a starting wing around Luka Doncic. Atlanta is willing to execute a sign-and-trade built around Kuminga going to L.A. and the Hawks receiving Jarred Vanderbilt plus the Lakers' 2032 first-round pick swap. Getting Kuminga to accept a roughly $20 million pay cut to sign the veteran minimum won't be straightforward, though the Lakers have pointed to Malik Monk's trajectory as a model.
  • • James Harden is returning to Cleveland. Charania reported that Harden's camp and the Cavs are aligned on a multi-year deal, with the exact length depending on how the rest of Cleveland's offseason shakes out. Harden opted out of his deal but his representatives are giving the organization as much flexibility as possible.
  • • The Clippers have interest in restricted free agent Peyton Watson from Denver, per ESPN's Ohm Youngmisuk. The Nuggets can match any offer sheet and want to keep Watson, but a sign-and-trade is possible. The Clippers are also working to retain their own restricted free agent Bennedict Mathurin, acquired in the Zubac deal.
  • • per HoopsHype, the Nuggets are waiving center Jonas Valanciunas, who had a fully guaranteed $10 million decision due Wednesday. Denver will owe him $2 million for next season. Valanciunas has a standing two-year offer from Zalgiris in the EuroLeague and must clear the 48-hour waiver wire before becoming a free agent.
  • • The Rockets officially signed Bogdan Bogdanović. Houston also signed rookie guard Quadir Copeland to a two-way contract; Copeland will play in Las Vegas Summer League starting July 9.
  • • Larry Nance Jr. agreed to a one-year, $4 million deal with the Pacers. Mo Bamba agreed to a two-year deal to return to the Jazz. Hunter Dickinson re-signed with the Pelicans on a two-way contract, returning for his second season with New Orleans.
  • • DeMar DeRozan has interest in returning to the Raptors, but his most likely landing spots are teams that miss out on LeBron, according to Jake Fischer. DeRozan is expected to sign somewhere on a veteran minimum.
  • • Terry Rozier appeared before a federal judge after contacting someone on his no-contact list. His lawyer argued the text was simply to inform that person they were on the list. Judge LaShann DeArcy Hall was not satisfied: "You violated the court's trust when you sent this text message."
  • • Former ESPN senior NBA writer Tim Bontemps is joining the Hawks front office as a strategic adviser, reporting to president of basketball operations Onsi Saleh. Bontemps covered the NBA since 2012, including the last eight years at ESPN.
  • • Golden Knights owner Bill Foley is returning to Las Vegas from vacation to attend NBA Summer League this week, as his group continues pursuing an NBA expansion bid. "I'm planning on coming back when they do the summer league and seeing if I can connect with a few people within the NBA," Foley said.
  • • Luka Doncic is reportedly "excited" about the Lakers' new pieces and "looking forward to getting to work with this group," according to a source familiar with his thinking. The new additions include Walker Kessler, Quentin Grimes, Sandro Mamukelashvili and Collin Sexton.
  • • Mark Cuban has filed a lawsuit against current Mavericks ownership. The filing alleges "adversarial business practices" by Patrick Dumont and seeks disclosure of details around the Valley View arena development. Cuban argues his businesses were "contractually entitled to participate" in the deal despite selling his majority stake to Miriam Adelson and Dumont.
