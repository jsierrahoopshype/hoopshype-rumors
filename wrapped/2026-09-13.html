NBA Rumors Wrap: Nick Smith Jr. headed to Real Madrid, Warriors pursuing Devin Booker, Knicks sign Milicic Jr.

Nick Smith Jr. is taking his game to Europe, agreeing to a one-year deal with Real Madrid, per Michael Scotto of HoopsHype. Smith, 22, averaged 7.6 points in 17.5 minutes across three NBA seasons with the Charlotte Hornets and Los Angeles Lakers, but never quite carved out a consistent role in the league. He'll take over as Real Madrid's lead guard, stepping into a prominent role at the EuroLeague club where Luka Doncic developed before entering the NBA. His former Lakers teammates noticed: Bronny James was among the first to react on Instagram, writing "Damn thats fire bro be great!!!" while Adou Thiero and Jake LaRavia also chimed in with their support.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • The Golden State Warriors are eyeing Devin Booker if a trade becomes available. Bay Area reporter Tim Kawakami said the Warriors love Booker and would be willing to move significant assets to get him. "I know the Warriors would be first in line saying, 'We got three first-round picks we can give you,'" Kawakami said. Coach Steve Kerr is said to be especially keen, and Booker apparently enjoyed his time playing under Kerr.
  • The New York Knicks are signing Igor Milicic Jr. to an Exhibit 10 deal ahead of training camp. The 24-year-old went undrafted in 2025 and spent last season with the Philadelphia 76ers' G League affiliate, the Delaware Blue Coats, where he averaged 6.8 points and 5.1 rebounds in 13 games.
  • The Phoenix Suns' Mark Williams will miss extended time after shoulder surgery, opening the door for Khaman Maluach to step up. Suns reporter Stephen Garcia thinks Maluach is ready for the opportunity: "This is the time for him NOW."
  • Julius Randle is feeling at home back in New York, this time wearing Brooklyn Nets colors. "It's definitely a place that just feels like home," Randle said Saturday while hosting a youth basketball clinic. He's also bullish on the Nets' ceiling, saying "Whatever your expectations are, whatever expectations that are put upon us, we're gonna exceed them." On a related note, if Randle can match his 24.0 points-per-game output from 2023-24, he could become just the second player, joining Michael Ray Richardson, to earn All-Star nods with both franchises.
  • Randle also had high praise for new teammate Michael Porter: "He loves big shots, big moments, and they always seem to know exactly where to be. He does it at an extremely high level, so I'm excited."
  • The NBA has updated its salary cap projections for 2027-28, informing teams of a $176 million cap and $213 million luxury tax line. The new figure comes in $2 million higher than the league's previous projection.
  • Norman Powell, now with the Chicago Bulls, shared some candid thoughts during a recent interview. He called the Lakers the most overrated team in the NBA and praised the Heat's offseason, saying "Anytime you get a generational talent like that it automatically puts you into contention." He also called former teammate Davion Mitchell one of the hardest-working players he's been around: "Davion was the one person I seen that I say 'ok, this guy works out like me. He's committed to the grind.'"
  • Powell also said the UCLA coaching staff once tried to recruit him to try out for the football team, but he turned them down. "I don't mess with the whole industry around football," he said. "Even though I love watching it, I don't like how they treat the players."
  • Tyler Kolek isn't stressing about a backup guard's role on a championship Knicks team. "I just gotta play my game everyday," he said at a youth clinic Saturday. "Be consistent, whatever the coaches need just follow their direction and let it be what it is." Kolek also acknowledged there's more to his game than he's been able to show, adding: "Every year I try and get better."
  • Mitchell Robinson sounds motivated heading into his first season with the Celtics. "Right now, I'm just so confident in myself. I feel like this is going to be a great year for me. As you can see, I'm starting to take recovery a lot more seriously."
  • Houston Rockets coach Ime Udoka joked about the team's new shooting coach, Chip Engelland, who previously worked with Tony Parker and Kawhi Leonard in San Antonio. "I always joke that I wasn't big time enough for him to help me with my jump shot," Udoka said. "He was always busy [with] Tony Parker and Kawhi and everybody."
  • Bobby Portis is already buying into the Heat's new-look roster, posting on social media after what he called a big week with his new teammates: "Helluva week w my brothers. Team chemistry +100000 #UNDERDOG."
  • Luka Doncic's new Italian basketball venture may be getting a familiar face. According to Italian media, former teammate Maxi Kleber is in talks to join BC Roma SPQR, the club Doncic is now part of as an investor. Doncic confirmed his involvement with the club at an event in Rome in June.
  • On the WNBA side, Team USA beat Spain 76-66 to advance to the Women's Basketball World Cup gold medal game, setting up a rematch of the 2024 Paris Olympic final against France. Breanna Stewart led the way with 16 points on 4-of-6 shooting.
  • At New York Fashion Week, Shareef O'Neal debuted his first sneaker under the Shaq Brand. The shoe features a zipper closure referencing Shareef's open-heart surgery scar, an open-heart "R" logo, and four snaps on the shroud, inspired by the four chambers of the heart and Shaq's four NBA rings.
