NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers front office in chaos, Vegas expansion bids due Thursday, Sabonis trade stalled

The Los Angeles Clippers are operating without clear leadership as the franchise navigates the fallout from owner Steve Ballmer's suspension. Commissioner Adam Silver confirmed the Clippers had no representative at the NBA Board of Governors meetings over the past two days because the team has not yet named an interim governor. On the basketball ops side, sources told Chris Mannix that the front office was "leaderless" following the departures of its top three decision-makers, with general manager Trent Redden expected to take over day-to-day operations. Silver addressed the five first-round picks the league stripped from the franchise, saying "there's no question this is a setback for the team," while maintaining the sanctions were necessary to deter future misconduct. Separately, Redden called Raptors lead decision-maker Bobby Webster on Monday to confirm LA is ready to proceed with the long-delayed Kawhi Leonard trade, which was agreed to in principle on June 30 before being placed on hold.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The NBA's Las Vegas expansion process is accelerating. Next-round bids are due Thursday, with final bids expected shortly after, and proposals for the Vegas franchise are reaching or potentially exceeding $10 billion. On the arena side, Dallas Mavericks co-owner Patrick Dumont led a review of potential arena sites in Las Vegas this week, with Silver describing him as "an independent objective party" given his deep operating experience in the city.
  • Silver was more bullish on Seattle. "If we were opening in five weeks, we could be ready in Seattle," Silver said of Climate Pledge Arena, adding it may require some modifications for NBA basketball. He said the bulk of expansion discussions at this week's meetings centered on Las Vegas simply because the league can only take on so much at once.
  • The NBA's financial picture may not be as rosy as the expansion frenzy suggests. Brian Windhorst said on The Hoop Collective podcast that he has reviewed the books of multiple NBA teams and is "not sure that there are 30 healthy teams." He also reported that existing owners are budgeting to receive between $500 million and $600 million each in expansion fees, implying the two new franchises would generate between $15 billion and $18 billion combined.
  • The Sacramento Kings' asking price is blocking a potential Domantas Sabonis trade. Per HoopsHype, Bleacher Report's Jake Fischer said the disconnect between what Sacramento wants in return and what teams are willing to give is significant, with the Charlotte Hornets not motivated to meet that price. Fischer added that Charlotte could re-emerge if the asking price comes down, but said "I don't think anything is coming to fruition anytime soon."
  • Draymond Green could be on the trade block by February if Golden State gets off to a poor start. Warriors insider Marcus Thompson II floated the possibility of a trade deadline move, while coach Steve Kerr said publicly that Green "is going to have to adapt a little bit, and it's probably going to be a little frustrating at times" as the franchise enters a transition season.
  • Paul George and Mike Conley met the media at a Boston Celtics introductory event Monday. George said the negative fan reaction to the Jaylen Brown trade was "fair," adding he's "not trying to fill the void of JB" but believes he can keep the Celtics in contention. Conley said his decision was "fairly quick" because few franchises are genuinely trying to win every year, connecting Boston's current identity to its championship tradition stretching back to the Kevin Garnett era. Celtics president Brad Stevens said the organization's focus was on "what's best for us, both in the short and long term" when asked how he weighed trading Brown against potentially strengthening a rival.
  • Denver Nuggets have emerged as a team to watch in the pursuit of Cam Whitmore, per Marc Stein. Whitmore was recently waived by Cleveland after the Cavaliers acquired him as part of a five-team trade.
  • John Konchar agreed to a one-year deal with the New York Knicks, per Shams Charania. The Miami Heat had internal discussions about making an offer but are unlikely to guarantee the contract fully, as doing so could limit their flexibility on the buyout market. A source told the Miami Herald the Heat had spoken to Konchar's camp about competing with Myron Gardner for a roster spot, with a decision expected this week.
  • The Knicks are busy filling out the roster fringe. Drew Eubanks signed a training camp deal and will compete for a roster spot, while the team also signed Exhibit 10 contracts for Jaden Akins, Mitch Mascari, Toby Okani, Paul Zilinskas, Liam Robbins and Nick Jourdain, with Igor Milicic Jr. already waived and expected to head to the G League.
  • Michael Porter Jr. said his energy output in Brooklyn last season was "so much higher" than it was in Denver because of the defensive attention he received. "I think I could be the best off-ball player in the NBA," Porter said, pointing to his finishing percentage around the rim and his ability to cut and catch-and-shoot. He added that staying in better shape, like Steph Curry, is the key to sustaining that output all season. Porter also predicted Brooklyn will surpass 50 wins next season.
  • Adam Silver said the current CBA, in place since 2023, is "the best we've ever had" for fostering leaguewide competitiveness, pushing back against criticism of the luxury tax aprons. Silver framed the system as deliberately NFL-like, designed to give every team a shot regardless of market size, and said eight different champions in eight years reflects parity of opportunity even if it isn't a goal in itself.
  • Washington Wizards re-signed Anthony Gill to a one-year deal, per Michael Scotto. Gill has shot 54.5% from the field over the past six seasons with Washington and takes the team's 15th roster spot.
  • Nike and the NBA unveiled new "Specter" uniforms for eight historic franchises ahead of the 2026-27 season. The Celtics, Lakers, Warriors, Knicks, 76ers, Hawks, Pistons and Kings will wear the new threads for select games alongside their standard uniform sets.
  • Knicks ticket demand on StubHub is up 60 percent compared to this point last year, and searches are up 174 percent, driven by the franchise's first championship in 53 years. Road games in Philadelphia, Miami, Boston and Washington are the most popular purchases among Knicks fans on the secondary market.
