NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis trade talks heating up, Wizards weigh Peterson at No. 1, Lakers center hunt

The Milwaukee Bucks are pushing to trade Giannis Antetokounmpo before Tuesday's draft but have not yet received an offer they are willing to accept, and the situation may spill into free agency beginning June 30. Brian Windhorst said on ESPN's Get Up that the Bucks' predicament is mirroring the trade deadline standoff from February, when Milwaukee kept the market open, found nothing acceptable and held onto Antetokounmpo. The Miami Heat have been the most aggressive suitor for months, but Windhorst cautioned that Heat assets "none of them I consider super-premium" may not clear Milwaukee's bar. Per Marc Stein, the Bucks have been carrying themselves like a team that expects to hold both the No. 10 and No. 13 picks at Tuesday's draft, and they can only hold No. 13 if they acquire it from Miami as part of an Antetokounmpo deal. The Bucks are also hoping to attach Bobby Portis, who has two seasons and slightly more than $30 million left on his deal, to any trade that sends Antetokounmpo out of Milwaukee.

The structure of a potential Giannis-to-Miami deal is coming into focus. Stein reports the Detroit Pistons should be monitored as a potential third-team facilitator, with Tyler Herro ultimately landing in Detroit as part of the arrangement. Miami has also begun making calls in search of supplementary shooting in the event its dream frontcourt pairing of Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo comes together. Portland, which was prepared to offer up to four first-round picks at February's deadline, is no longer willing to go to those lengths after being told an extension from Antetokounmpo is considered unlikely.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Washington Wizards have not locked in their No. 1 pick, and multiple draft experts told Marc Stein they legitimately believe Washington could select Kansas guard Darryn Peterson over BYU's AJ Dybantsa. "Increased consideration" is how one well-placed insider framed Peterson's chances. The Athletic's mock draft still has Dybantsa at No. 1 but noted Washington continues due diligence and has done a significant amount of research on Peterson in recent days. Scouts who spoke to The Athletic were high on Peterson: one Eastern Conference scout said "he has the ability to be Klay-level."
  • Brian Windhorst also said on Get Up that Trae Young is expected to re-sign with Washington on a three-year, very large contract. That would pair Young with whoever the Wizards take at No. 1.
  • The Utah Jazz have offered Walker Kessler a five-year deal worth roughly $140 million, but Kessler's camp believes his value is significantly higher than that offer. The Jazz have been firm about wanting a five-year commitment and are using their right to match any offer sheet as leverage. Kessler is a restricted free agent, and Tim MacMahon reported the negotiations are "tense." The Lakers are among the teams who have done due diligence on Kessler, as sources told ESPN that both Kessler and Detroit's Jalen Duren would satisfy Luka Doncic's stated priority of landing an A-list center this offseason.
  • Luka is spending the summer in Slovenia with his daughters but is in "constant communication" with Lakers president Rob Pelinka and coach JJ Redick, per ESPN. He was cleared for full basketball activities on May 28, roughly two and a half weeks after L.A.'s playoff exit. "He's in full go mode," one source told ESPN.
  • LeBron James is a free agent target for the Golden State Warriors, who could clear room for the full $15.1 million non-taxpayer midlevel and could involve Stephen Curry in the recruitment process if needed, per ESPN. The Warriors haven't yet knocked on LeBron's door. Meanwhile, a source close to Luka told ESPN that his conversations with LeBron and Austin Reaves since the season ended have mostly revolved around golf, not basketball. A source close to the Lakers' front office told ESPN that using cap space on a trade is very much in play.
  • The Lakers are interested in retaining Marcus Smart and Luke Kennard, per ESPN. Smart has until June 29 to exercise a $5.4 million player option and is hoping for a new deal and a raise. Rui Hachimura could be the odd man out, though multiple sources told ESPN he may need to explore the open market to find his desired contract.
  • The Pistons have interest in multiple wings and guards ahead of free agency. Stein reports Detroit has eyes on Charlotte's Coby White, Oklahoma City's Isaiah Joe and possibly Sacramento's Zach LaVine, who is heading into the final year of a massive deal with a $49 million player option that must be activated by June 29. Pistons GM Trajan Langdon said Thursday that Detroit's goal is to add pieces that accentuate its core three of Cade Cunningham, Ausar Thompson and Jalen Duren.
  • The Thunder appear likely to move Lu Dort, who carries a $17.7 million team option for next season. With Oklahoma City already projected $40 million over the luxury tax line and multiple younger players at Dort's position, the most likely path is a trade for second-round picks that generates a $17 million trade exception.
  • The Pelicans are asking for a lot in return for Trey Murphy but will listen to offers, and they are actively trying to acquire a pick in the top 20 of next week's draft. The Warriors remain interested in Murphy as the more attainable wing option, though Kevin O'Connor said Golden State still covets Kawhi Leonard as its top wing target.
  • The Suns are in talks that could net them a first-round pick in Tuesday's draft; they currently hold only the No. 47 selection in the second round, per Stein. Phoenix also tendered qualifying offers to Mark Williams and Koby Brea, making both restricted free agents. Williams' qualifying offer is $9.6 million after he reached starter criteria.
  • The Clippers are seriously considering Arizona guard Brayden Burries with the fifth pick, after he impressed Los Angeles staffers during a workout Tuesday. Mikel Brown Jr. is also a top candidate at No. 5 and was scheduled to work out for the Clippers Thursday. The Mavericks, picking at No. 12, have explored trading the pick and are considering a wide range of players, from guards like Brown, Darius Acuff and Keaton Wagler to big man Yaxel Lendeborg.
  • Sean Sweeney was officially introduced as the Magic's new head coach Thursday. Sweeney, who spent years on Gregg Popovich's staff in San Antonio, said the Magic stood out during the interview process because of how deeply they questioned him. "Some interviews that I've had in the past, I did not get that same level of detail," he said. Magic president Jeff Weltman added: "In Sean Sweeney, we have somebody who, in our minds, has the potential to be one of the elite coaches in this league."
  • Tiago Splitter spoke for the first time as Chicago's new head coach, keeping expectations measured. "It's not smart by me saying we're going to win this, we're going to do that," Splitter said. "It's step by step, smart in every step." He also hinted that the "box" motivational concept he used with Portland, where the defensive player of the game drops his name into a box after a win, could make its way to Chicago.
  • Oscar Wembanyama, younger brother of Spurs star Victor Wembanyama, spoke to HoopsHype ahead of the draft about navigating the comparisons. "People recognize you without you doing nothing," he said. "I just don't care about a lot of things." On his own game, Oscar described himself as a versatile player who can guard multiple positions and said working on every facet of his game is what has made him the prospect he is today.
