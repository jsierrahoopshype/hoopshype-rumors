NBA Rumors Wrap: Mavericks fire Kidd, build around Flagg, Kyrie Irving's future uncertain, Thunder even series with Spurs

The Dallas Mavericks fired head coach Jason Kidd on Wednesday, and new team president Masai Ujiri has made clear the organization is building around 19-year-old Cooper Flagg. "Every decision we are going to make here is going to be future based," Ujiri said. "We have a 19-year-old on this team that can take us somewhere great." Ujiri described the coaching search as fully open, telling reporters the team would consider first-timers, veterans, college coaches and European candidates alike. Per Shams Charania, sources believe Kidd had supported the Luka Doncic trade that set Dallas back, and Ujiri now has full authority to reshape the franchise from top to bottom. The question hanging over all of it is Kyrie Irving, whose fit in the new era is still being sorted out.

Ujiri addressed Irving's future directly after the firing, invoking a memorable endorsement from an old friend. "Kevin Durant once told me that there's only one Kyrie Irving walking around in the world," Ujiri said. "I think we have to figure out a way how Kyrie fits with our program." He added that he had spoken with Irving as recently as the day before and expressed genuine curiosity about seeing Irving play alongside Flagg. Still, Charania reported that multiple contenders around the league are monitoring Irving's situation in Dallas, and Irving remains the last significant holdover from the Mark Cuban era.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Thunder evened the Western Conference finals at 1-1, beating the Spurs 122-113 in Game 2. Shai Gilgeous-Alexander finished with 30 points and 9 assists, Isaiah Hartenstein added 10 points and 13 rebounds, and Alex Caruso contributed 17 points and 5 assists.
  • Jalen Williams exited Game 2 in the first quarter and did not return. He was getting treatment on his left hamstring after leaving, the same hamstring he strained in the first round that caused him to miss six games before returning for the Western Conference finals opener. Dylan Harper also did not finish Game 2 due to a right leg injury.
  • De'Aaron Fox missed Game 2 with a right high ankle sprain, his second consecutive absence. Fox's injury opened the door for Harper's memorable Game 1 starting debut, when Fox walked up to the 20-year-old rookie before tip-off and said simply, "I'm not playing." Harper said Fox told him to "be me out there and not take it for granted," and he responded with seven steals in the Spurs' double-overtime win.
  • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson said of Harper after Game 1: "I thought he was phenomenal. I thought he was even better defensively." Harrison Barnes put it more plainly: "There is stuff that Dylan does that you just can't teach."
  • Victor Wembanyama's preparation for this season included a Shaolin monastery training program arranged by his agent Bouna Ndiaye. One challenge: dribbling a basketball up a dangerous mountain route to a monastery in the Song Mountains, a hike that traverses cliffside plank paths, suspension bridges and ancient forests. An average person takes seven to eight hours to complete it. Wembanyama did it in four and a half while dribbling a basketball.
  • Ndiaye set up a lunch with Lakers president Rob Pelinka to understand what made Kobe Bryant so driven, looking to apply similar principles to Wembanyama's development. "The way they think is different," Ndiaye told ESPN. "The way they play, the way they stretch themselves. Just their curiosity. How they study and watch things." Ndiaye said Wembanyama's mental strength this season is "something that I haven't seen before."
  • Carmelo Anthony said Wembanyama's play is "on a whole different level" than what the league felt about LeBron James at the same stage, describing a moment in Game 1 where Wembanyama caught a lob while standing flat-footed and dunked over Chet Holmgren. "I've never seen nothing like that," Anthony said.
  • The Timberwolves are signaling an aggressive offseason. President Tim Connelly said he would rather get fired for trying than operate in survival mode, and did not rule out pursuing a player like a 31-year-old Giannis Antetokounmpo or a 34-year-old Kyrie Irving coming off a torn ACL. "I don't know what level of risk there is when you're bounced in the second round," Connelly said.
  • The Lakers' front office rebuild took a hit when the team offered Timberwolves assistant GM Steve Senior the role of executive vice president of basketball operations, and Senior declined to leave Minnesota.
  • The Lakers want LeBron James back next season and have made that clear to him, per Shams Charania. LeBron is not expected to accept a minimum deal or a significant pay cut in free agency.
  • The Bulls coaching search has a new candidate: Jerry Stackhouse, who just finished a stint as a Warriors assistant, has emerged as a candidate in Chicago. Draymond Green also weighed in on the Jason Kidd coaching market, saying he sees the Trail Blazers as the best fit for Kidd given the Oakland connection with Damian Lillard and the presence of Scoot Henderson, though he noted the Blazers' owner has a well-publicized reluctance to pay coaches.
  • The Pelicans are planning to retain Zion Williamson, who has two years left on his contract and played 62 games this past season. New Orleans also pivoted quickly to Jamahl Mosley as its top coaching candidate once he became available after his Orlando tenure ended, with sources telling Chris Mannix that Mosley's track record developing young rosters drove the interest.
  • Knicks coach Mike Brown credited a Golden State-era tactic for helping neutralize James Harden against the Cavaliers: making Harden dribble close to 1,000 times a game by picking him up full court. That strategy opened up space for Jalen Brunson to exploit Harden defensively down the stretch.
  • Draft prospect Kingston Flemings, expected to go in the first round, survived being run over by a car at age four, suffering a broken hip and a punctured spleen. He has said the experience shaped how he approaches every day. His work ethic in high school was so intense that a Houston assistant coach described just watching one of his training days as "exhausting."
