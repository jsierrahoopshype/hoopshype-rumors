NBA Rumors Wrap: Giannis backstory revealed, Hartenstein re-signs with Thunder, Duren saga continues

The behind-the-scenes story of how Giannis Antetokounmpo ended up in Miami is coming into fuller focus, and ESPN's sprawling tick-tock makes clear just how close the Boston Celtics came to landing him. The Bucks had been operating under a hard deadline set by co-owner Jimmy Haslam: settle Giannis's future by draft night, either through an extension or a trade. By the time that deadline arrived, the answer was clear, with multiple sources confirming Antetokounmpo was not going to sign another extension in Milwaukee. General manager Jon Horst had spent months canvassing the league, only to watch at least four strong bids collapse after Antetokounmpo's camp rejected each one. In the end, Milwaukee chose the Heat's package over Boston's, with Horst preferring the flexibility Miami's offer gave the franchise in building something new.

The Celtics' near-miss carries its own complicated aftermath. Boston had been willing to part with Jaylen Brown in a Giannis deal but drew the line at also including young prospects Hugo Gonzalez and Baylor Scheierman, a decision one league source called "insane" given how publicly Brown was dangled. The team is still engaging in trade discussions for Brown, with Shams Charania reporting the Celtics have asked teams for at least four first-round picks in some cases. Brown has three years left on his supermax, and the question of which team meets that valuation remains unanswered as free agency opens.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • Isaiah Hartenstein is returning to Oklahoma City on a three-year deal worth $75 million in new money, bringing his total guaranteed earnings with the franchise to five years and $134 million. The contract includes a maximum 15% trade kicker and a mutual option that allows both sides to rework the agreement in 2028 prior to the final season, per Shams Charania.
  • The Hartenstein deal has some salary cap nuance worth watching. The mutual option likely functions like a player option with a partial guarantee, similar to James Harden's current contract structure, which shaves roughly $3 million off the Thunder's payroll and reduces their tax penalty by $21 million, leaving them about $15 million above the second apron.
  • Jalen Duren's restricted free agency is getting messy. The Pistons publicly insist they are not trading Duren and are focused solely on an extension, but Duren was underwhelmed by their initial offer and is planning to explore sign-and-trade scenarios when they become available Tuesday. The gap between the sides is described as large, and Duren prefers the sign-and-trade route over signing an offer sheet that Detroit could simply match.
  • One potential destination for Duren is Boston. Detroit is weighing a sign-and-trade of Duren for Jaylen Brown, among other conceptual outcomes, if that option remains on the table once free agency opens Tuesday evening. In the meantime, Oklahoma City traded sharpshooter Isaiah Joe to Detroit for two future second-round picks, with Detroit expected to slot Joe into the Isaiah Stewart trade exception.
  • Kawhi Leonard would not be willing to sign a contract extension if he were traded to the Pistons, per Marc Stein. Leonard has one year remaining at $50.3 million and would have the Toronto Raptors on his short list of extension-eligible destinations, with sources saying Toronto is actively exploring a trade to pair him with Scottie Barnes.
  • The Timberwolves are still working to fill out their roster after acquiring LaMelo Ball. Minnesota does not have a traditional power forward and is looking at multiple options at the four, while also seeking more shooting around Ball and Anthony Edwards. Ball's $41.25 million salary pushes the Wolves above the first apron, meaning they must do 100 percent salary matching on any future deals.
  • One team source described what Minnesota was missing before the Ball trade: "We needed a guy like LaMelo to make everybody better." Ball, who is 24, arrives with Edwards's tacit approval, as sources say Edwards has long admired his game. Charlotte's front office was widely considered the biggest winner of the draft weekend, with Naz Reid, a 2033 unprotected first, three pick swaps and three second-rounders going to the Hornets for Ball and Josh Green.
  • The Lakers have not yet made a formal offer to LeBron James as free agency approaches, according to Shams Charania, with the front office still weighing whether to pursue roster upgrades first via cap space trades and circle back to LeBron, or lock in LeBron first. The team is actively exploring multiple approaches, and free agency does not officially open until Tuesday.
  • Mitchell Robinson's free agency is drawing interest from two corners of the league. The Nets have legitimate interest in signing Robinson away from the champion Knicks, and the Lakers are also considered a likely suitor given growing pessimism that New York can retain him.
  • Jose Alvarado is declining his $4.5 million player option and returning to the Knicks on a new three-year deal worth $14-plus million. Alvarado was a key piece on New York's championship team and has been a priority retention target for the franchise all offseason.
  • Gary Trent Jr. is expected to decline his $3.9 million player option with the Bucks and hit the open market, where he has become a sought-after target for teams looking for perimeter shooting. Meanwhile, the Heat will decline the $2.41 million team option on Jahmir Young, releasing the guard who averaged 25.9 points and 8.5 assists in the G League last season.
  • Dereck Lively II addressed the durability questions that have followed him, and he was direct about it. "If I've got to take what the fans or the public have to say about me, that I'm brittle or all that, cool," Lively said. "But whenever I come back, don't forget who I am." He said he will not rush back from his foot issue and wants to be sure there is no chance of a recurrence before returning.
  • Zaire Wade, son of Hall of Famer Dwyane Wade, was arrested on suspicion of domestic violence in Burbank early Sunday morning, according to NBC4 Investigates. Burbank police were called to a home around 5:30 a.m. after a 911 call, and officers found a woman with lacerations at the scene.
  • The Utah Jazz plan to sign Tamar Bates to a two-way deal. Bates spent last season as a two-way rookie with the Nuggets and averaged 19.5 points on 55 percent shooting and 44 percent from three in the G League before a foot injury ended his year.
