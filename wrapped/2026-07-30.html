NBA Rumors Wrap: Lakers and Cavs chase Kuminga sign-and-trade, Clippers cap probe could run into 2027, Blazers relocation threat real

The race for Jonathan Kuminga is heating up, with both the Los Angeles Lakers and Cleveland Cavaliers actively pursuing him via sign-and-trade, per Shams Charania. Any deal would require the Atlanta Hawks to cooperate as the third team in a sign-and-trade framework, and Kuminga himself would need to be aligned with whichever destination emerges. The Lakers lost LeBron James to the Philadelphia 76ers this offseason, and adding Kuminga would signal a meaningful pivot toward the next chapter of the franchise. Cleveland, meanwhile, is rebuilding its roster after James chose Philadelphia over a return to his hometown, and a young, versatile forward like Kuminga would fit the direction Koby Altman's front office is heading. The competition between two big markets for the same target gives the Hawks real leverage.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • The NBA's investigation into whether the Clippers circumvented the salary cap to pay Kawhi Leonard through his sustainability company Aspiration could drag into 2027 if the parties can't agree on findings or a settlement. The NBPA has said it won't hesitate to take the matter to arbitration if it believes players' rights are affected, while the Clippers say they will "fight that to the end" and deny funneling any money to Leonard. The Raptors, who are on the other end of a trade with the Clippers that involved Leonard, Brandon Ingram and Gradey Dick, say they still intend to complete that deal, though an arbitration process could complicate matters.
  • Portland's ownership situation is growing more dire. Tom Dundon has said he has no interest in funding a Moda Center renovation, and ESPN's Brian Windhorst described him as someone with "no connection to the city of Portland" who won't be swayed by public pressure. The city could theoretically sue the Blazers if they leave, since the existing lease requires the team to maintain the arena in "first-class" condition, but that litigation threat has itself become a sticking point in ongoing negotiations. Relocation is realistic but not a done deal, per Windhorst.
  • ESPN's deep-dive on LeBron's free agency decision revealed that people close to James believed the Lakers were never serious about re-signing him, despite GM Rob Pelinka saying otherwise at his end-of-season press conference. The Celtics expressed interest, and the Clippers even floated a one-year deal worth more than $20 million that would have let James stay at his Brentwood home. The final five came down to the 76ers, Cavaliers, Heat, Warriors and Timberwolves, after Rich Paul opened conversations with 27 teams.
  • The Sixers weren't even on LeBron's radar before the Jaylen Brown trade. Rich Paul said the 76ers never made contact early in the process because neither side thought it was realistic, and it was only after Philadelphia acquired Brown that conversations shifted. Tyrese Maxey was a significant factor once those talks began, constantly calling and texting James throughout the process to answer any questions he had, per Chris Haynes.
  • Shams Charania, appearing on the Game Over podcast with Rich Paul, revealed that Cavaliers executives including Koby Altman and Brandon Weems, along with the Gilbert family, held a secret meeting with James in Akron in early July. Paul was visibly surprised Charania knew about it. Charania explained he had learned about the meeting from multiple sources but chose not to report it because the information was shared off the record.
  • One person around Jaylen Brown told Shams Charania that Brown is heading into next season with zero ego. "There's gonna be so much sacrifice that's gonna go into next season," Charania said.
  • Luka Doncic is hosting the entire Lakers roster in Slovenia for a four-day minicamp in August, according to Dave McMenamin. The itinerary includes workouts, golf and tourism in Ljubljana ahead of training camp.
  • Draymond Green is waiving his implicit no-trade clause and will receive a 15% trade kicker on his new deal with Golden State, structured as if he never declined his player option. The Warriors have no intention of trading him, but the structure keeps that option open. With that contract, Golden State will sit just over the first apron at roughly $27.7 million for Green alone, leaving three roster spots still to fill.
  • The Warriors also quietly weighed whether to pursue a trade for Anthony Davis before LeBron chose Philly. A small contingent of Golden State's front office at summer league in Las Vegas pushed for that path, involving Jimmy Butler and draft picks, but Davis's injury history and desire for a new extension convinced team brass to stay put. On Butler: the Warriors have repeatedly reassured him and his camp they don't want to trade him, and he's targeting a return as early as Christmas from his knee injury.
  • Steve Kerr told ESPN's Anthony Slater that he never believed Golden State was a real option for LeBron. "It's just that everyone kept saying we were," Kerr said. "It never seemed serious to me despite all the reports." A separate source told Slater that LeBron would have been more interested in playing with Stephen Curry on a different team than actually joining the Warriors, because of the history between the two franchises.
  • Cleveland is working through the process of securing a FIBA Letter of Clearance from Real Madrid to officially sign Mario Hezonja, per Marc Stein. Real Madrid pushed back because Hezonja didn't have his next team lined up by July 20, when his NBA out clause had to be formally exercised with a $1 million payment. The Cavs are working through those steps now.
  • Denver's opening offers to restricted free agent Peyton Watson have been in the $16-to-18 million annual range, according to league sources. The Bulls have emerged as Mathurin's other suitor beyond the Clippers, per The Stein Line, with Chicago now competing for Bennedict Mathurin.
  • DAZN is becoming a major player in NBA local rights. The platform landed exclusive digital rights to MSG and YES networks and is close to full exclusive local broadcast deals with the Timberwolves, Cavaliers, Pacers, Spurs and Grizzlies, plus DTC-only arrangements with the Magic and possibly the Hornets. That could give DAZN roughly one-third of the league by the start of the 2026-27 season, ahead of the NBA's planned centralized local broadcast hub launching in 2027-28.
  • Bam Adebayo said there's no disappointment about LeBron choosing Philly over Miami. "I'm going to bust his a--," Adebayo said with a smile. Adebayo also spoke about the Heat's acquisition of Giannis Antetokounmpo, saying he and the two-time MVP had the same agent and had talked about the possibility of teaming up before it actually happened.
  • Mark Cuban told the Dallas Morning News he has spoken to new Mavericks president Masai Ujiri exactly once, for about 120 seconds, and exchanged a handful of texts that went mostly unanswered. "His choice. I absolutely expected it when they hired Masai," Cuban said. "That's why they hired him. To run basketball."
  • The Lakers co-owner Mark Walter suffered a stroke midway through the 2024 World Series, the Wall Street Journal reported, and his health has since become a point of concern inside his business empire. Only those close to him knew at the time.
  • LaMelo Ball was sued this week by a North Carolina landlord who claims he owes more than $100,000 in missed rent, just weeks after his trade from the Hornets to Minnesota was finalized.
