NBA Rumors Wrap: Cavs linked to Giannis, Knicks Finals tickets break records, Wembanyama struggles in Game 5

The Cleveland Cavaliers are expected to pursue a trade for Milwaukee Bucks star Giannis Antetokounmpo this offseason, with Evan Mobley as a potential centerpiece. Brian Windhorst said he does not think it will be long before Cleveland gets attached to Giannis talks, floating a scenario where Mobley, Donovan Mitchell, and James Harden could all be part of the equation. The Bucks face a difficult negotiating position because, as Windhorst put it, everyone already knows they have to move him. Cleveland was swept Monday night by the New York Knicks in the Eastern Conference finals, giving the front office a clear mandate to retool around its core.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Kenny Atkinson is returning as Cavaliers head coach, with two league sources confirming the decision. Donovan Mitchell made his support plain: "I love Kenny. We love Kenny. We ride with Kenny. Ultimately, that's all that matters." Mitchell also said publicly he wants to return to Cleveland and work on his next deal, telling reporters to stop the speculation.
  • • The Cavaliers' Harden decision looms large heading into the offseason. Cleveland will enter as a second-apron team, and the choice is essentially whether to restructure Harden's deal or move on entirely, freeing up roughly $30 million in savings and letting Dennis Schroeder start. Windhorst also said Harden is expected to return on a new deal structured to help the team dip below the second apron.
  • • Victor Wembanyama had his worst game of the series in Game 5, going 4-for-15 from the floor with 20 points, all of which came via a perfect 12-for-12 showing at the free-throw line. The Spurs are now down 3-2 and facing elimination Thursday in San Antonio. Stephon Castle said the Thunder make it hard to get Wemby going by sending multiple bodies at him on every possession.
  • • Shai Gilgeous-Alexander praised Alex Caruso after Game 5: "He's one of if not the best competitor in the NBA night in and night out." Shai also said he is definitely not on Twitter, especially not this time of year, though as one observer pointed out, he has not posted in two years anyway.
  • • Jalen Williams missed Game 5 again with a hamstring injury. Chet Holmgren, meanwhile, was asked about Jared McCain shooting 19 shots in the game. His response: "19 of em? Atta boy."
  • • Jared McCain carries a worn scrap of Page 21 from "The Inner Game of Tennis" tucked inside his copy of the book, a pre-game ritual he has maintained since high school. After scoring a playoff career-high 24 points in Game 5, he said: "Being able to just take that confidence and be fearless, I love this so much."
  • • Julian Champagnie scored 13 consecutive points between the 8:21 and 4:38 marks of the first quarter in Game 5, per ESPN Research. He was the first Spur to score at least 13 straight in a playoff game since Kawhi Leonard ran off 16 in a row against Memphis in 2017.
  • • Spurs coach Mitch Johnson said the defensive collapse in Game 5 came down to not putting themselves in position to succeed on enough possessions. Louis Williams pushed back on the broader coverage of the series, asking why Johnson does not receive more credit: "When do we give him his proper due for being a part of this?"
  • • Joe Mazzulla won the 2025-26 NBA Coach of the Year award. In his statement, Mazzulla said the award belongs to his staff and reiterated his belief that it should be called the Staff of the Year. The historical footnote attached to the award is a grim one: all six coaches who won it between 2018 and 2023 were eventually fired.
  • • The Knicks are headed to the Finals for the first time in decades, and ticket prices at MSG are the highest in NBA history. The cheapest available ticket for Game 3 at Madison Square Garden is $3,410. For comparison, the average get-in price for the 2016 Finals between Cleveland and Golden State was $880.
  • • San Antonio's Becky Hammon is standing by her take that the West's two remaining teams are better than the Knicks. "Allen Iverson got MVP and he lost in the finals," she said. "I said what I said. If he proves me wrong, he proves me wrong." The comments drew attention after Jalen Brunson won the Eastern Conference Finals MVP following New York's sweep of Cleveland.
  • • A Wembanyama 1-of-1 Panini Prizm Black rookie card sold for $5.11 million in a private sale brokered by Fanatics Collect. It is the most expensive known sale of a non-autographed NBA card, the fourth-highest basketball card sale ever, and the third sports card sold for $5 million or more in 2026.
  • • A Houston jeweler whose clients include Shai Gilgeous-Alexander and who helped design the Thunder's NBA championship rings has been federally indicted. Zohaib Muhammad of ZoFrost and Co. Custom Jewelry was arrested May 12 on charges of conspiracy to commit wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering, with prosecutors alleging his business was used to launder gold acquired through scams targeting elderly people.
  • • The Lakers officially hired Rohan Ramadas as assistant general manager of strategy and data systems. He will oversee basketball analytics and strategic initiatives for the front office. Separately, Brian Windhorst said there is a pathway for LeBron James to stay in Los Angeles, with both sides expected to make decisions on his future in the next two to three weeks.
  • • New Orleans Pelicans coach Jamahl Mosley said Zion Williamson hasn't scratched the surface of what he can do. Mosley wants to open the floor more and get Zion living at the free-throw line, saying health is the other major key to getting more out of him.
  • • Texas Tech guard Christian Anderson, a projected top-20 pick, is staying in the 2026 draft after positive feedback from the combine and pre-draft workouts. Center prospect Flory Bidunga went the other direction, officially withdrawing from the draft and confirming he will play next season at Louisville.
