NBA Rumors Wrap: Cavaliers work through Hezonja paperwork, Heat target Klay Thompson, Blazers relocation fears grow

The Cleveland Cavaliers are still working to officially complete their signing of Mario Hezonja, with Real Madrid pushing back on the process over a technicality tied to his NBA out clause. One source close to the situation told Marc Stein that Cleveland is "working through" the steps needed to secure the required FIBA Letter of Clearance. The Spanish club's resistance stems from the fact that Hezonja did not have his NBA destination locked in by July 20, the deadline written into his contract for the $1 million opt-out payment to be formally exercised. Hezonja and Cleveland agreed to a one-year minimum deal on July 26, six days after that window closed. Until Madrid signs off, the deal cannot be made official.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Miami Heat have identified Klay Thompson as their top target in an ongoing pursuit of shooting. Thompson has one year remaining on his contract with the Mavericks, and Miami is exploring whether a trade or a buyout could get it done over the coming weeks.
  • • Peyton Watson remains in contract limbo with the Nuggets. Denver's initial offers have been in the range of $70 million over five seasons, roughly $16-to-18 million per year, but the Nuggets are in the second apron and trying to move contracts to create room to retain him. If a sign-and-trade becomes necessary, Denver's asking price has shifted: early in the offseason it was two first-round picks and two swaps, but that price has since moved to a high-level player plus a first-round pick. The Bucks, Hawks, and Clippers are all circling as potential sign-and-trade partners.
  • • Draymond Green has agreed to return to Golden State on a one-year, $27.7 million deal, the same contract he opted out of to give the Warriors flexibility in LeBron James' free agency. There is no no-trade clause in the deal, though Green retains trade consent rights as he would on any one-year contract. With that signing in place, the Warriors will sit just over the first apron and about $12.4 million under the second, with three roster spots still to fill, one of which is expected to go to De'Anthony Melton. Per HoopsHype, Green is the 67th player in NBA history to surpass $250 million in guaranteed career earnings, and the only one of those 67 to average fewer than 10 points per game.
  • • Green said on his podcast that he was not surprised by LeBron James' decision to sign with the 76ers. "I know a few people that know a few people and I knew the stuff was in the works," Green said. On LeBron's reluctance to join the Warriors, Green added: "I ultimately don't think Bron was ever able to get over the history, and I understand it, I get it." Anthony Slater reported a source told him LeBron would have been more interested in playing with Steph Curry on another team than joining Golden State specifically, partly because of that shared Finals history.
  • • Stephen Curry is expected to remain in Golden State for the rest of his career. Those around him have said privately that he wants to finish as a Warrior, and the Warriors want the same. There is reporting that Curry would hypothetically be open to a pay cut, but only if it would meaningfully help the team land a major free agent or make a significant trade.
  • • Jimmy Butler is targeting a return as early as Christmas from his knee injury. The Warriors have consistently told Butler and his camp that they are committed to him and have no intention of trading him, though that calculus could change depending on how his rehab goes.
  • • The 76ers sent Johni Broome and a second-round pick to the Clippers for cash considerations, a move designed to keep Philadelphia under both apron thresholds while accommodating the LeBron James and Kentavious Caldwell-Pope signings. The Sixers are now sitting with one open roster spot and $1.8 million under the first apron hard cap. They can sign a 15th player to a rest-of-season deal starting December 4 or fill the spot with a 10-day contract beginning January 5, and will likely hold that flexibility through the trade deadline.
  • • Tyrese Maxey worked out with LeBron James in Los Angeles, and ESPN's Dave McMenamin reported that Joel Embiid had several extensive conversations with LeBron before the signing. A Sixers front office member told McMenamin the internal message heading into the season will be centered on sacrifice, since all three primary options ranked in the top 25 in usage rate last season. One person close to Jaylen Brown told Shams Charania that Brown is entering next season with no ego: "There's gonna be so much sacrifice that's gonna go into next season."
  • • Bam Adebayo said there is "no disappointment" over LeBron choosing Philadelphia over Miami. "He made a decision where he feels like it's his best situation to win," Adebayo said Tuesday. "And I feel like I'm a good friend of LeBron's." Adebayo declined to address a reported altercation with former teammate Tyler Herro from earlier this month.
  • • Anthony Edwards won a legal victory in his child support case. A Los Angeles court threw out a suit filed by Ayesha Howard after she failed to provide evidence of California residency and did not appear for trial. The judge declared Howard a "vexatious litigant" and banned her from filing further claims in Los Angeles. Edwards has already paid more than $110,000 toward child support in proceedings in Atlanta.
  • • LaMelo Ball is facing a lawsuit from a North Carolina landlord who claims Ball owes more than $100,000 in unpaid rent. The suit was filed just weeks after Ball was traded from the Hornets to the Timberwolves. Ball and the landlord entered into a rental agreement in December 2020.
  • • DeMar DeRozan's free agency has a new development: the Washington Wizards have entered conversations with DeRozan over the last several days.
  • • The Bennedict Mathurin sweepstakes is down to the Clippers and the Bulls, per Marc Stein, who first reported Chicago's interest on Tuesday.
  • • James Harden's contract with Cleveland is expected to be capped at two guaranteed years. The exact salary remains fluid given the Cavaliers' continued interest in Jonathan Kuminga and their goal of staying out of the second apron for a second straight season.
  • • Brian Windhorst had a blunt read on the Portland Trail Blazers' ownership situation. "Tom Dundon is not interested by his own words in giving any money for this renovation," Windhorst said. "It's realistic but it's definitely not a done deal... Tom Dundon is not someone to be trifled with." Relocation is on the table, but Windhorst stopped short of calling it inevitable.
  • • Mark Cuban opened up about his estrangement from the Mavericks, saying "the pivot away from the Mavs was not my choice." Cuban, who retains a 27% stake in the franchise, told the Dallas Morning News he has spoken with new president Masai Ujiri once for about 120 seconds and exchanged a handful of texts, most of which went unanswered or received two- or three-word replies. "That's why they hired him. To run basketball," Cuban said.
  • • The New Orleans Pelicans have agreed to a one-year deal with center Christian Koloko, who will compete for the final roster spot after stints with four different franchises over the last four seasons.
