NBA Rumors Wrap: LeBron James draws dozen suitors, Lakers pursue Duren and Kessler, Rockets move to sign Smart

LeBron James officially opted out of his Lakers contract Tuesday and wasted no time generating a full-blown sweepstakes, with roughly a dozen teams expected to make their pitch to him and agent Rich Paul. The Warriors have emerged as perhaps the most serious alternative to a Lakers return, and per Marc Stein, Golden State has lined up additional roster moves to create financial flexibility if James signals interest in making the Bay Area his next stop. The Heat are also viewed as a legitimate contender, with Shams Charania identifying Miami as one of the teams that "really stand out," citing the Giannis-and-Bam pairing, warm weather, and LeBron's history there. Per Chris Haynes, James will be patient and open-minded in choosing his destination, with competing for a championship the top priority. One team executive summed up the situation simply: "It's going to happen on LeBron's timeline."

The Warriors' pursuit of LeBron has an additional layer. League sources told ESPN that a potential Golden State move for Anthony Davis could serve as a motivational tactic to attract James, given the two won a title together in 2020 and remain close friends. Any Davis deal would likely require plugging Jimmy Butler into an offer to the Wizards for salary-matching purposes, and a long-term extension for Davis has not been explicitly demanded by LeBron or Paul, per the SF Standard, though it is widely assumed. The Timberwolves also reached out to express interest Tuesday, though Minnesota has precious little cap space and does not believe it is near the front of the line.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • The Lakers are pursuing multiple options to reshape their roster post-LeBron. Jalen Duren had a productive meeting with Los Angeles on Tuesday and is scheduled to meet with them again Wednesday morning, with the Lakers telling the 22-year-old they view him as a max-level player. The Kings also met with Duren on Tuesday and are exploring sign-and-trade scenarios, though Detroit has no interest in a deal involving Domantas Sabonis.
  • • Duren's side has also expressed interest in a sign-and-trade to the Celtics, separate from the Lakers and Kings scenarios, according to Jake Fischer. Meanwhile, Lakers president Rob Pelinka has been in meetings with multiple free agents, including Duren and restricted free agent Walker Kessler, with the team prioritizing size, shooting, and backcourt help.
  • • Kessler is drawing significant offers. The Utah Jazz restricted free agent holds multiple offers in the mid-to-high $30 millions annually, with his choice of contract structure including player options. The league's expectation is that the Lakers will ultimately land commitments from Philadelphia free agent Quentin Grimes and Toronto free agent Sandro Mamukelashvili while continuing to chase Kessler as their top target.
  • • The Rockets are trying to trade Dorian Finney-Smith to clear the cap space needed to offer Marcus Smart a multiyear deal around $7 million annually. Without moving Finney-Smith, Houston could still sign Smart to a two-year, $13 million deal. Adding to the Rockets' offseason activity, Bogdan Bogdanovic has agreed to a one-year deal with Houston, giving the team a playoff-tested shooter for his 10th NBA season.
  • • The Pistons' free agency situation is busy on multiple fronts. Tobias Harris, Detroit's second-most productive player in the playoffs behind Cade Cunningham, is visiting with five teams at the start of free agency, making his return to Detroit uncertain. Harris is also on San Antonio's radar, along with Lakers free agent Rui Hachimura and Clippers free agent John Collins, per Stein. Hachimura is regarded as increasingly likely to depart LA and is drawing interest from Brooklyn and Detroit in addition to San Antonio.
  • • Kristaps Porzingis signed a two-year, $40 million extension with the Warriors, but the structure carries significant flexibility for Golden State. Only $3 million is guaranteed in Year 2, and the Warriors have until July 7 to waive him. The deal was structured as an extension rather than a Bird Rights contract, which means Porzingis is immediately trade-eligible with no restrictions.
  • • Draymond Green called newly drafted teammate Yaxel Lendeborg a "f— liar" on his podcast after Lendeborg claimed he never heard from Green following his selection at No. 11 in the 2026 draft. Green shot back that it was "the Michigan way", a nod to both players' ties to the university.
  • • Dean Wade agreed to a four-year, $39 million deal with the 76ers, with a partial guarantee in Year 4. Several teams competed hard for Wade, including the Cavaliers, Lakers, Clippers, and Pistons. Separately, Philadelphia free agent Quentin Grimes will not return to the 76ers.
  • • Keon Ellis agreed to a two-year, $18 million fully guaranteed deal with the Nets. The contract contains a full mutual option for Year 2, meaning either side can opt in for a new deal conversation next summer without either party being forced out. Per HoopsHype, that Year 2 option is a team option, giving Brooklyn additional flexibility.
  • • Ousmane Dieng agreed to a three-year, $17.5 million deal to return to the Bucks after a strong finish to last season following his trade from OKC. The contract includes a team option in Year 3, per HoopsHype. Meanwhile, Tim Hardaway Jr. signed a one-year, $6.5 million deal with the Heat to provide spacing alongside Giannis Antetokounmpo.
  • • Robert Williams signed a three-year, $44 million deal with the Trail Blazers, with heavy performance incentives built in. Year 2 carries only $5 million guaranteed and Year 3 is non-guaranteed, but both years become fully guaranteed if Williams plays 50-plus regular-season games in the prior season. Separately, seven-footer Branden Carlson agreed to a one-year, $2.5 million deal with Portland after two seasons in the Thunder system.
  • • Zach Collins agreed to a two-year, $17 million extension to stay with the Bulls, with his camp beating the 11:59 p.m. deadline Monday night to complete the deal as an extension rather than let Collins enter free agency. Luke Kennard signed a two-year, $13 million deal with the Suns, with a player option on Year 2.
  • • The Celtics' offseason has a notable subplot. Boston has long carried the feeling that they could revisit a potential Jaylen Brown trade, having previously offered him in a deal for Kevin Durant before deciding to stay together. Per Jake Fischer, "the toothpaste is out of the tube" on Boston being willing to move Brown, with Giannis as a potential target. Separately, Brian Windhorst said James Harden opted out and is willing to work with the Cavaliers to help improve their roster.
  • • The NBA Europe expansion bidding closed Tuesday, with multiple bids exceeding the league's preferred $500 million to $1 billion range. Ownership groups from Athens, Barcelona, Berlin, Istanbul, London, Lyon, Madrid, Manchester, Milan, Munich, Paris, and Rome all submitted bids. Former Bucks owner Marc Lasry is among the bidders, and sources say he is likely not the only former NBA owner in the running.
  • • Steph Curry has been named a finalist for the Muhammad Ali Humanitarian Award. And DeAndre Jordan is re-signing with the Pelicans for what will be his 19th NBA season.
