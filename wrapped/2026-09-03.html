NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers stripped of 5 picks, fined $30M in Kawhi circumvention ruling, Raptors trade imminent

The NBA came down hard on the Los Angeles Clippers on Wednesday, stripping the franchise of five first-round picks and issuing a $30 million fine to owner Steve Ballmer following a yearlong investigation into salary cap circumvention tied to Kawhi Leonard, per Shams Charania. Ballmer and team president Gillian Zucker have each been suspended from all team activities for one year, while lead basketball decision-maker Lawrence Frank received a six-month suspension. The NBA found that the Clippers steered endorsement deals with four team business partners — Aspiration Partners, Boingo Wireless, Daktronics, and Lockton Insurance — toward Leonard's representatives as a condition of doing business with the franchise, with investigators concluding Ballmer and Zucker's denials were, at best, inaccurate. Leonard himself was ordered to pay $700,000 in restitution, though the investigation found he did not knowingly participate in any cap circumvention. The Clippers have no formal appeal avenue within the NBA and would have to fight the ruling in court if they choose to pursue it.

The Clippers responded with a scorched-earth statement, saying: "We vehemently reject the NBA's findings, which are the result of a heavily biased investigation seeking to justify a predetermined narrative rather than facts and evidence." A separate statement from the organization called the process "a witch hunt" and said what the league told them privately differed from what was announced publicly. The Clippers also say the league's investigation cost nearly $50 million, all of it paid for by Ballmer. Multiple sources have long said that if significant punishment came down, Los Angeles would strongly pursue arbitration through external channels.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The five picks to be forfeited run from 2029 through 2033. The 2029 pick is the Indiana Pacers' unprotected first-rounder the Clippers acquired in the Ivica Zubac trade, and it will not be returned to Indiana. The 76ers own top-three protected first-round swap rights with the Clippers in 2029, and an NBA spokesperson confirmed those rights remain intact.
  • Leonard's longtime representative Dennis Robertson was banned from all league activities for five years. The NBA's report found that Clippers president of business operations Zucker emailed Boingo, Daktronics, and Lockton in June 2020 to introduce them to Leonard's representative, and within two months he had signed multimillion-dollar consulting deals with all three companies. Investigators also found that the Clippers made hundreds of payments on behalf of Leonard and his family for personal travel, tickets, and gifts that Leonard was supposed to have repaid under NBA rules but did not, with those payments approved by Frank.
  • Leonard, through his new agent Harrison Gaines, issued a statement accepting responsibility: "I accept full responsibility for lapses in judgment by people within my inner circle and regret the distraction this situation has caused the fans and my family." He added: "As I return to Toronto, I am focused on what I can control, closing this chapter, and moving forward with a clean slate." The expectation around the league is that his trade to the Raptors will be finalized within the next few days.
  • The SEC has separately opened an investigation into Daktronics, the South Dakota scoreboard manufacturer that built the Halo Board inside Intuit Dome, regarding its dealings with Leonard. The company's acting CFO confirmed it on an earnings call Wednesday, saying: "The Securities and Exchange Commission is seeking information from us concerning the company and Mr. Leonard. We take these requests seriously and are cooperating."
  • Joe Smith, who was at the center of the Timberwolves' own secret-contract scandal in 2000, called the penalties "pretty fair." He noted the key differences between his situation and Leonard's: "Mine was a little more severe because I had to face suspension or I had to go to another team and that's why I ended up in Detroit. But I mean, I can deal with that. He has to pay a fine. I didn't have to pay a fine, he has to pay a fine. The team is getting punished."
  • The Cleveland Cavaliers have officially signed center Khalifa Diop to a four-year, $9.26 million deal, per HoopsHype. The 24-year-old was Cleveland's 39th pick in the 2022 draft and spent three seasons with Baskonia in Spain's Liga ACB and the EuroLeague, averaging 3.8 points on 67.0 percent shooting, 4.2 rebounds, and 0.7 blocks in 16.4 minutes last season. The Cavaliers will hold a training camp in Andalusia, Spain, from September 30 through October 5, making the timing of his arrival a natural fit for the organization's plans abroad.
  • Russell Westbrook has joined upstart basketball league Project B as a co-founder and chief strategy officer, taking an equity stake and a seat on the board of directors. Project B has been working with Maverick Carter, LeBron James's longtime business associate, and has added former WNBA star Alana Beard as chief basketball officer and former NBA player and executive Landry Fields to run the men's division. Westbrook said: "With Project B, we're challenging the norm and bringing a new vision to the intersection of premium sport, culture, and entertainment."
  • The San Antonio Spurs announced DAZN as their new local broadcast partner for the 2026-27 season. All regular-season games not on national TV will stream exclusively through the platform, available to fans across West, Central, and South Texas. A monthly pass will run $19.99, a season pass $119.99, with a premium tier available for an extra $5 a month or $20 a year.
  • Draymond Green pushed back on comments made by Jonathan Kuminga's agent, Aaron Turner, who discussed the financial details of Kuminga's contract situation on a podcast. "Absolutely unnecessary to talk about this man's pockets on a podcast," Green said on his show. "Talking about a guy's money and how much they lost and what they gained and like talking about somebody's 11 and a half million dollars, 'that's it'... 'THAT'S IT'?! This ain't Monopoly money!"
  • Josh Giddey said the Bulls have enough to be a playoff team this season but acknowledged consistency was Chicago's undoing last year. "We went through stretches where we looked like a really really good team, then we went through stretches where we didn't look like a good team," he said. "Being able to be consistent, having a deep team where we can go to a bunch of different guys to give us different looks — I think those are important things."
  • Jalen Brunson said the Knicks are focused on approaching the coming season with the same hunger they had before winning a title, not celebrating what already happened. "We don't want to speak of the trophy, we don't want to talk about the trophy, we don't want to see the trophy," Brunson said. "We want to come in with the same mindset, if not even tougher."
  • Jaylen Brown said the new-look 76ers roster, which features four players rated above 90 in NBA 2K27, is something fans should enjoy while it lasts. "It's very rare to have a team constructed to the likes of which we might have this year," he said. "It won't be like that forever."
  • Several NBA teams have sent scouts to Bendigo, Australia, for the 2026 NBL Blitz to watch 2027 draft prospect Dash Daniels. The Thunder, Warriors, Magic, Trail Blazers, and Rockets are already on the ground, with the Heat expected to arrive later in the week.
  • The Indiana Pacers are currently on track to pay the luxury tax for the first time since the 2004-05 season. The only other franchises that have never crossed the tax threshold since 2005 are the Grizzlies, Pistons, Hornets, and Kings.
  • The Atlanta Hawks made several G League front-office moves, promoting Ryan Silverstein to Skyhawks GM while Hawks assistants Sanjay Lumpkin and Reggis Onwukamuche will become head coach and associate head coach, respectively. Current Skyhawks GM Aaron Evans moves to the Hawks' front office as a pro scout, and coach Steve Klei returns to the Hawks bench as a full-time assistant.
  • The Utah Jazz have waived guard Matt Murrell shortly after signing him.
