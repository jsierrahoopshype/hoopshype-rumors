NBA Rumors Wrap: Jaylen Brown bids farewell to Boston, Bulls regret Essengue pick, Wembanyama leads France

Jaylen Brown said goodbye to Boston in style Sunday, taking the stage at the 741 Frequency Fest to a thunderous reception from fans who chanted his name as he prepared to begin his career with the Philadelphia 76ers. "I just want to say thank you to Boston. Thank you to the city. I love you," Brown told the crowd, adding: "When I got to this city I said I'd go to war for this city. I've met so many great people here: people that helped me in the community, had motivated me on my journey, people that supported me even when I didn't support myself at times." The sendoff extended well beyond the stage: Boston mayor Michelle Wu presented Brown with a street sign reading "Jaylen Brown Way," and the Commonwealth of Massachusetts gifted him a key to the state. It was the kind of farewell that doesn't happen often, and it underscored just how much Brown meant to a city that watched him develop from a raw teenager into a champion.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Chicago Bulls' front office drama around the Noa Essengue pick runs deeper than previously known. A source told the Chicago Sun-Times that Bulls scouts preferred fellow French prospect Joan Beringer over Essengue, but dissenting voices in the organization were routinely ignored or pushed out under former GM Arturas Karnisovas. One NBA source said their organization didn't even have Essengue in the top 20 of that draft.
  • Essengue's standing with the new regime is shaky. According to the Sun-Times, he has been spoken to about his lack of physicality and warned about his demeanor on the court. Because the current front office didn't draft him, there's no sense of institutional loyalty keeping him safe.
  • The Karnisovas-era miscalculations weren't limited to Essengue. A source told the Sun-Times that Karnisovas didn't consider Tyrese Haliburton a serious prospect and doubted he'd even crack the top four in talent on the Bulls' roster at the time. Haliburton, of course, became one of the better point guards in the league.
  • Victor Wembanyama led France past Sweden 79-69 in Stockholm, finishing with 18 points, eight rebounds and three blocks on 6-of-7 shooting. The win was France's seventh in eight games in the FIBA World Cup 2027 European Qualifiers. Maxime Raynaud and Matthew Strazel added 13 points each, while Rudy Gobert sat out.
  • Wembanyama said he felt real frustration leaving the French national team setup. "I didn't expect it to be this much, but seeing these guys again, whom I've known for so long, feels so good," he told L'Equipe. He added that he's "still very excited to be back in San Antonio and to get back to work. There's nowhere else like it to work."
  • Evan Fournier said Wembanyama's presence with the French national team has been a personal motivator. "The fact that he's here, I won't lie, has been a motivation for me to keep going," Fournier told L'Equipe. He also said he sees his role with the squad evolving: "I can be the player who makes sure everything is in order. If we lack aggression, if we need to establish a half-court offense, put the ball where it needs to be, I can make sure of that."
  • Dennis Schroder ended Poland's unbeaten run in the FIBA World Cup qualifiers, scoring a team-high 18 points and five assists as Germany survived 96-94 in overtime. Johannes Thiemann contributed 14 points and 12 rebounds for the Germans.
  • Ivica Zubac went for 24 points, 11 rebounds and two blocks as Croatia edged the Netherlands 101-99. Dario Saric matched Zubac's 24 points and added six assists in the narrow win.
  • Lauri Markkanen posted a double-double as Finland rolled past Estonia 96-67 in the FIBA World Cup qualifiers. The Utah Jazz forward finished with 21 points, 10 rebounds, two assists, two blocks and a game-high 26 efficiency rating in 26 minutes.
  • Stephen Curry extension talks with the Golden State Warriors remain a storyline to watch heading into the season. The New York Times reports that if Curry signs before the season opens, it would signal that he still trusts owner Joe Lacob and the front office to maximize what remains of his career.
  • Trey Kaufman-Renn, who is on the Minnesota Timberwolves' roster, has asked a court to force the NCAA to grant him a sixth season of college eligibility, citing a knee injury that cost him most of his 2021-22 freshman year at Purdue. A hearing is set for Tuesday morning in Clark County, Indiana. The motion notes that more than one college program has expressed interest in him for the 2026-27 season, though a return to Purdue would be complicated.
  • Philadelphia 76ers big man Adem Bona described his reaction to learning LeBron James would be his teammate in vivid terms. "I just started screaming at the top of my voice in the car," Bona said. "We just started jumping in the car, and the car started like moving, bouncing literally."
  • Kentavious Caldwell-Pope is off to quite a start in Philadelphia. Minutes after his wife signed the lease on a new penthouse in the city, lightning struck the building and flooded the unit. His electrician reportedly told him there was a 1-in-400-million chance of that happening given the unit's location.
  • New York City mayor Zohran Mamdani was asked by a child at a camp whether he could one day become king. Mamdani said he was happy as mayor because New York already has a king: Jalen Brunson. "Jalen Brunson is the king," Mamdani told the kid while they roasted marshmallows.
  • Ja Morant has sold his Eads, Tennessee mansion for $3.2 million, a modest gain over the roughly $3 million he paid for it in 2022. The 13,000-plus square-foot estate sits about 62 feet from the home he purchased for his parents in 2019.
