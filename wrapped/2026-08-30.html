NBA Rumors Wrap: Timberwolves land Kuminga with Konchar stretch, Pistons won't move Duren, Curry extension window opens

Minnesota has cleared the financial path to officially sign Jonathan Kuminga, completing a multi-part roster move that started with a trade. The Timberwolves sent Josh Green and cash to the Utah Jazz for Cody Williams and John Konchar, per Shams Charania, then used the waive-and-stretch on Konchar to get under the second apron. The math worked out tight: Minnesota needed to shed $2.1 million to create the necessary space and ended up $415,566 under the second apron after saving $2.5 million. Kuminga's agent, Aaron Turner, addressed the contract situation directly, saying "there was never an offer from the Warriors for 5 years, $150M... to take it a step further, the Warriors never made an offer on an extension." Turner also suggested next summer will be a better free-agency market for his client, with Sacramento and Chicago among the teams that had been pursuing a sign-and-trade. Kuminga lands in Minnesota on the taxpayer midlevel exception, a roughly $6.5 million salary that Draymond Green publicly flagged as "a bench role salary" given the Wolves' existing commitments to LaMelo Ball, Anthony Edwards, Rudy Gobert and Jaden McDaniels.

Konchar is set to become a free agent Monday at 5 PM ET after clearing waivers, per Marc Stein. Utah, meanwhile, gets Green, a 3-and-D wing the Jazz wanted to add, and Williams stays in Minnesota.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Detroit Pistons have no interest in moving Jalen Duren, the last unresolved restricted free-agency situation of the offseason. Tim MacMahon said the Pistons "absolutely positively 100% intend to keep" Duren and have made clear to any team exploring a sign-and-trade that they're wasting their time.
  • The window for Stephen Curry to sign an extension with Golden State is now open. He's eligible for up to a two-year, $136.7 million extension through June 30; because of the Over-38 rule, two years is the maximum allowed in an extension, though he could sign for three seasons if he waits until free agency. Meanwhile, an ESPN Films documentary on Curry, Portrait of an Artist: Stephen Curry, opens exclusively on IMAX screens Oct. 9.
  • Brandon Williams will wear No. 12 for the Warriors this season, the number last worn by Braxton Key in 2025.
  • The Los Angeles Clippers have withdrawn their qualifying offer for Bennedict Mathurin, making him an unrestricted free agent. As for Cam Whitmore, the Clippers could technically pursue him, but that does not appear to be something they'd be interested in.
  • Shortly after Kuminga's deal with Minnesota became clear, Bruce Brown emerged as a potential Lakers target. "I think he's in the mix for the Lakers, a team that has had some interest in him for several years now," an NBA insider said during a Bleacher Report livestream.
  • Kevin Durant said Westbrook's retirement hit him, but he's not going anywhere. Durant holds a $46 million player option for 2027-28 and averaged 26 points per game last season while earning second-team All-NBA. "It's a young man's game," Durant said. "Even though I feel like I can play, you are always hear, 'You're 38, you're 39.' I'm going to continue to put everything into the game until I can't no more."
  • Durant also weighed in on Jalen Brunson's decision to take a discount to stay with the Knicks, saying he would not have done the same. "I would have sat down and talked to all my people like I usually do, Rich, all my friends, and I'd be like, 'Why? Are you serious? Why do you need to take that risk when these dudes are billionaires?'" Durant said. He added that he plays padel with Drake and collects jerseys from every opponent he faces, including 10-day guys.
  • Victor Wembanyama remains the only player on a rookie deal to have signed a rookie extension this offseason. Brian Windhorst said he spoke with an executive recently and heard nothing on any other extensions for players still on their rookie contracts.
  • Tony Parker is confident Wembanyama and the Spurs will win a championship. "The potential is amazing. I think it's just a matter of time before they win the championship," Parker said Saturday at an NBA event in Manila. San Antonio lost to the Knicks in the NBA Finals last June.
  • Neemias Queta left Portugal's FIBA qualifier against Spain early with an injury and is in doubt for Monday's match against Georgia. The Celtics are monitoring the situation.
  • Jason Kidd said he would never rule out returning to coaching. "Maybe somewhere down the future, but also right now I'm just at home really trying to enjoy my family," Kidd said, adding that he stays in regular contact with Jalen Brunson and Giannis Antetokounmpo.
  • Chicago Sky guard Natasha Cloud declined to address Enes Kanter Freedom by name after the pair's verbal altercation on Aug. 23, calling him "irrelevant." "What I will focus on is what actually affects women and children," Cloud said, then spent two minutes detailing domestic violence, sexual assault and food insecurity as the issues she believes deserve attention. Separately, the America First Policy Institute filed a public records request related to Freedom's removal from Wintrust Arena, and Freedom challenged Megan Rapinoe to a $5 million charity soccer match during a Fox News appearance.
  • Jonas Valanciunas made his debut for Zalgiris Kaunas in a preseason game, posting 20 points and nine rebounds in 19 minutes during a 97-85 win over Rigas Zelli.
  • Brad Stevens spoke publicly about the difficulty of making unpopular decisions, with attendees connecting his comments to the Jaylen Brown trade. "Data is an important part of learning about what you're capable of, and learning about your team, but it is a part of it. Not all of it," Stevens said. Joe Mazzulla offered his own take separately, saying his main goal as Celtics coach is "to be forgotten," because that would mean things only got better after him.
  • Knicks guard Miles McBride brought the Larry O'Brien trophy to his alma mater Moeller High School in Cincinnati on Aug. 28, where the Crusaders beat Groveport-Madison 45-7.
