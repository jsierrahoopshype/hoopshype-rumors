NBA Rumors Wrap: NBPA eyes work stoppage, Warriors wait on Butler, Lakers-Bucks trade talks stall

The NBA's new players union executive director is escalating his fight against the league's salary cap structure just months into the job. David Kelly, who took over the NBPA from Andre Iguodala in July, told Sports Illustrated he is "100 percent" preparing players for a work stoppage and said he would go "(as) far as necessary" to extract changes to the apron system. The current CBA runs through 2030, with an opt-out window opening in the summer of 2029. Kelly said he was open to finding middle ground with the league but made clear that the union's opposition "could" lead to a stoppage, while adding that he doesn't "hope to have one." The apron system, which levies stiff financial and roster-building penalties on teams that exceed spending thresholds, was agreed to by the union just three years ago, before Kelly was involved.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Lakers and Bucks have held exploratory trade discussions on a framework that would send Dalton Knecht and second-round draft capital to Milwaukee in exchange for big man Jericho Sims, per Marc Stein. Sources briefed on the talks have expressed pessimism that the deal will actually go through.
  • The Warriors are waiting on Jimmy Butler, who is recovering from ACL surgery and targeting a return around February. Steve Kerr framed the situation this way: "We get Jimmy back around February instead of losing him in February. So, what's our job? Lay the best foundation, best platform for success before that." Butler, meanwhile, said he's "feeling great" and that "as soon as I can take the floor, I promise you I'm getting back out there and competing."
  • On the trade front, Golden State never offered Butler in potential deals for Anthony Davis or Jaylen Brown this summer, citing the pick packages required and contracts attached. The Bucks had also previously balked at a package that would have included Butler's or Draymond Green's contract in a potential deal for Giannis Antetokounmpo. Golden State had also discussed Zach LaVine as an alternative acquisition target.
  • The Buss sibling legal battle over the Lakers continued Friday, with Joey and Janie Buss filing a 29-page opposition in Los Angeles Superior Court challenging Jeanie Buss's petition to remove them as co-trustees and void the family's 5-1 vote to sell its remaining stake in the franchise. The filing argues the siblings exercised rights expressly granted to them as beneficiaries and calls out what it describes as Jeanie's hypocrisy: "There is deep irony in [Jeanie] alleging that it is [Joey and Janie] who are putting their self-interests first." Sources told ESPN that Bob Iger and Josh Kushner are expected to take control of the franchise following an NBA board of governors vote in November.
  • Portland Trail Blazers owner Tom Dundon has privately floated the prospect of relocation in conversations with league officials and other owners, sources told The Athletic, even as he publicly says keeping the team in Portland is "the priority." The NBA told Dundon to focus on making the Blazers work in Portland, and the league made clear it resisted his overtures. Negotiations over a Moda Center remodel and new lease must be resolved by year's end.
  • NBA Commissioner Adam Silver left owners with the impression that a Seattle SuperSonics expansion team is no longer a lock, reintroducing Mexico City as a possible destination at last month's board of governors meeting. The Holloway-led Seattle group remains the front-runner among two known bidders, but its offer is substantially lower than the bidding war for the Vegas franchise, which could exceed $10 billion. Teams have already been budgeting around an expected $500–600 million expansion fee windfall per franchise.
  • The NBA and major sportsbooks have agreed to ban multiple new categories of bets, per Shams Charania, including parlay bets on the under for the same player's stats, "first of" markets such as opening tip or a specific player's first shot, and under bets on players expected to play fewer than 12 minutes. The new restrictions come after active communication between the league and gaming companies following recent betting scandals.
  • Cam Christie is returning to the Clippers on a two-way contract after clearing waivers, his agent Kevin Bradbury of LIFT Sports Management told HoopsHype. The 21-year-old was the 46th pick in the 2024 NBA Draft and has spent his first two seasons in Los Angeles.
  • The Hornets have waived veteran Pat Connaughton. That decision puts second-year forward Tidjane Salaun, the No. 6 overall pick in the Jeff Peterson era, in potential trade jeopardy, per Marc Stein, though Stein also notes Salaun has taken real strides this summer.
  • The Grizzlies have been exploring trade markets for GG Jackson II and Walter Clayton Jr., a notable shift from Memphis's original stance on Clayton.
  • Jaylen Brown and Jayson Tatum have barely spoken since Brown's departure to Philadelphia. When asked at Sixers media day, Brown was vague but the message was clear: "No, not really. I went my way and he went his way." Brown also said he never expected to win the 2024 Finals MVP, adding that it "mattered way more to some people than it ever mattered to me."
  • Basket Zaragoza have publicly challenged Aday Mara's move to the Thunder, insisting the rookie center remains under contract with the Spanish club for 2026-27. Oklahoma City pushed back, with a spokesperson saying that because Mara "played three years of NCAA college basketball," no clearance from the Spanish team is required, as advised by the NBA.
  • The Knicks have until Monday afternoon to make a trade to retain Bruce Brown. The team is expected to explore deals involving third-year wing Pacome Dadiet and third-year guard Tyler Kolek, though New York will be asking teams to absorb salary without sending back equal money.
  • Anthony Edwards enters the season with an Olympic gold medal, four All-Star selections and a career-best 28.8 points per game last year. His motivation, he says, is singular: "It's just the ring." Edwards also pushed back on the idea of the NBA's parity era giving anyone reason for optimism: "I don't really have hope. I just got will."
  • The NBA mourned the passing of Hall of Famer Cliff Hagan, a five-time All-Star who spent his entire 10-year NBA career with the St. Louis Hawks and played a key role in the franchise's 1958 championship. Hagan also won an NCAA title at Kentucky and later served as a player-coach in the ABA.
