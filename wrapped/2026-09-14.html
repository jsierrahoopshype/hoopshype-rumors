NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers' Ballmer accepts penalties, Amen Thompson locked up, Kawhi trade still in limbo

Steve Ballmer broke his silence Sunday, issuing a public statement accepting the NBA's punishments against the Los Angeles Clippers for salary cap circumvention, per Shams Charania. "I want to apologize to our fans, employees, and my fellow NBA team owners for the distraction and distress this matter has caused, for which I accept responsibility as principal owner," Ballmer wrote, adding that the franchise has paid the league's fine and will not pursue legal action. Ballmer will not attend the Board of Governors meetings in New York City on Monday while serving his suspension. The statement carries a tone of finality, but the ripple effects on the Kawhi Leonard trade situation are not so clean.

Despite Ballmer's compliance, a deal sending Leonard to the Toronto Raptors does not appear close. Jake Fischer reported that the Clippers are not quite ready to get back on the phone with Toronto, even though the NBA league office has said it would process a trade call without an interim governor in place. The Clippers also have not determined who will serve as interim governor ahead of Monday's meetings. Toronto's offer of two unprotected first-round picks remains on the table and is reportedly more than any other team offered for Leonard.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Houston Rockets have locked up Amen Thompson on a five-year, $208 million contract extension, and by all accounts it came together without much drama. "It was pretty easy," Rockets GM Rafael Stone said of the deal. "He wants to be here and we want to be with him."
  • Aaron Gordon spoke openly about his former Denver Nuggets teammate Peyton Watson, who signed with the Cleveland Cavaliers this offseason. "That's my dawg, but it's deeper than basketball with Peyton and I," Gordon said. "Our family members go to the same church. Peyton's just like my brother." Gordon added that after an injury-riddled season in which he played only 33 regular-season games and just three in the first-round playoff loss to the Timberwolves, he spent the offseason with a purpose: "I did a lot of soul-searching and training, so I got my mind right and my body right. I'm ready to go."
  • Hugo Gonzalez addressed the trade rumors that surrounded him throughout the Boston Celtics' offseason. "I mean, it's just something that you know when you're going to the NBA," Gonzalez told CLNS Media. "I wouldn't be the first person to get traded or the last person to get traded."
  • Mitchell Robinson sounds like a man ready for a fresh start in Boston. "Right now, I'm just so confident in myself," the Celtics center said. "I feel like this is going to be a great year for me."
  • Ziaire Williams said he sees a legitimate path to a starting role with the Los Angeles Lakers after Jonathan Kuminga chose to sign with the Minnesota Timberwolves. "Yeah, I do," Williams said when asked if he views himself as a starter. He also said he spent the summer focused above all on durability: "I feel like availability is the best ability."
  • Marcus Smart's departure from the Lakers this offseason appears to have been mutual. Lakers insider Jovan Buha said the details are murky, but his read is that Smart was ready to move on. "My understanding is that to some degree, Smart was ready to move forward in a different situation," Buha said. Smart has since joined the Rockets.
  • Magic Johnson took to social media to back a call for a Jerry Buss statue outside Crypto.com Arena. "I 100% agree that Dr. Jerry Buss is the greatest franchise owner in sports history and deserves a statue in front of Crypto arena soon in his honor," Johnson wrote, responding to a column by Lakers beat writer Khobi Price.
  • Jalen Brunson will appear via pre-tape at the start of Monday's 78th Emmy Awards ceremony, with Knicks superfan Mariska Hargitay hosting. Hargitay also confirmed that a guest role for Brunson on "Law and Order: SVU" is happening. "He is just open and ready. I don't think there is much Jalen can't do," she said.
  • Team USA won the 2026 FIBA Women's World Cup, beating France 97-79 for their fifth consecutive gold medal. Breanna Stewart dominated with 22 points and 10 rebounds and was named tournament MVP, her second such award after also winning in 2018. Jackie Young added 18 points and four threes, while Caitlin Clark scored 12 points in 13 minutes off the bench. Young also made history, becoming the first basketball player ever to win a high school state championship, an NCAA title, a WNBA title, Olympic gold in both 5x5 and 3x3, and a FIBA World Cup gold.
  • Speaking of Clark, she had a quick answer when asked about her relationship with Angel Reese after the World Cup. "We've always been friends," Clark said. "Don't try to do that."
  • Legendary Italian coach Ettore Messina is returning to the NBA, joining the Atlanta Hawks as a consultant after ending his long tenure with Olimpia Milan. The 66-year-old has been one of Europe's most celebrated coaches for decades.
  • The Miami Heat could have some family-tree appeal in Devin Carter, who has cleared waivers. Carter is the son of former Heat guard and assistant coach Anthony Carter, and the Heat have both the cap space and roster room to make a move.
  • The New Orleans Pelicans have signed Christian Shumate to a training camp contract. The undrafted forward out of McNeese State was a four-time All-Southland selection and won the conference's Defensive Player of the Year award.
  • Former NBA center Alex Len has signed a two-year deal with Baskonia Vitoria-Gasteiz in Spain. Brandon Knight, meanwhile, has signed with the Perth Wildcats for the NBL season, arriving from Puerto Rico where he averaged 18 points and 6.3 assists per game for Mets de Guaynabo.
  • Norman Powell, who made his first All-Star team at age 33 last season with the Chicago Bulls, reflected on how long the moment took to arrive. "It was one of the personal goals that I had set out for myself when I was a little kid," Powell said. "It's still a great feat, even though it's in my 11th year."
