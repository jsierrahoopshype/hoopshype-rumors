NBA Rumors Wrap: Curry's contract decision looms, KAT-Knicks talks stall, Tanter remembered

Stephen Curry says he is not going anywhere, but a contract extension with the Golden State Warriors remains unsigned as training camp opens next week in Hawaii. The Warriors have presented Curry with a full menu of options, including max deals, slightly-below-max structures that would give the team more flexibility, one- and two-year arrangements, and player options, per Shams Charania. Curry, who is recovering from patellofemoral pain syndrome that sidelined him late last season, said Wednesday at a community event in Oakland that he feels "fantastic" and pledged the situation will not create any locker room tension. "I don't want there to be no drama," he told ESPN. "No talking points. This team should be competitive and go win." He also made clear he has no interest in hitting free agency next summer and hinted at a willingness to take less than the max if it helped build a contender around him.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Karl-Anthony Towns and the Knicks have stalled on extension talks, with a deal before training camp now considered unlikely. New York has discussed starting salaries in the $45-50 million per year range and has made clear it does not want to enter the second luxury tax apron, which makes Towns' existing $61 million player option for the 2027-28 season the sticking point. "This is a cloud going into training camp," Shams Charania said. Josh Hart and Miles McBride are also eligible for extensions, adding to the front office's balancing act.
  • • The Lakers lost longtime public-address announcer Lawrence Tanter, who died Wednesday after suffering a stroke late in the regular season that forced him to miss the team's final six home games and the postseason. Tanter had served as the voice of Lakers games for 43 years and was inducted into the Southern California Sports Broadcaster Hall of Fame. Shortly before entering hospice care last month, he texted ESPN a final message: "Quote me. 'It's been a blessing.'" Lakers governor Jeanie Buss called him "a true original that added his unique style to Showtime," and former Lakers center Mychal Thompson asked the team to wear an LT patch this season.
  • • Luka Doncic published a personal essay titled "To Win It All: On Basketball and Belonging," reflecting on his arrival in Los Angeles and his goals with the franchise. "I'm not sure that I ever could have imagined being here and wearing the purple and gold," he wrote. "It's a wonderful surprise and it feels amazing and now I desperately want what all Lakers want. To win it all. I won't rest until we win more championships."
  • • Jalen Duren and the Pistons are working through a complicated contract extension situation. Detroit is seeking weight-stipulation incentives as part of any new deal, a condition that caught Duren and his camp off guard. The organization had privately flagged weight concerns internally after Detroit's Game 7 loss to the Cavaliers in the second round. One league source described the 6-foot-10 Duren as looking "like a Greek statue," suggesting the perception gap between the two sides is real.
  • • Marcus Smart turned down a bigger offer from the Lakers to sign with the Rockets, citing family ties in Houston. Smart did not hold back on the offer he declined, calling it "disrespectful." "First off, I played so well that the offer that was ultimately given didn't match the way that I played," he told the Houston Chronicle. "Especially when you see guys who played way worse than I did, the offers they're getting."
  • • Kevin Durant spoke warmly about his relationship with Rockets coach Ime Udoka heading into camp. "I feel like we can be even closer as a coach-player," Durant said. "Somebody that I always can lean on throughout a game and vice versa." He also welcomed the return of Fred VanVleet and Steven Adams as veteran stabilizers.
  • • The Raptors have agreed to a two-year, $115 million contract extension with Kawhi Leonard, with a guaranteed third year at Leonard's player option, according to Toronto Star sources. The deal kicks in for the 2027-28 season. Leonard is 35.
  • • Dereck Lively II has not been cleared for Mavericks training camp as he continues to recover from foot surgery performed last December, per Tim MacMahon. His status for the start of the regular season has not been determined. No setbacks have been reported and he is slowly ramping up to begin basketball activity.
  • • Denver is among the teams with the strongest interest in guard Ryan Nembhard on a two-way deal, according to Marc Stein. Nembhard has not yet been officially waived by Charlotte but is expected to target teams with a backup point guard need once he clears waivers.
  • • Bill Simmons said on his podcast that LeBron James was headed to Cleveland before ultimately signing with Philadelphia. "Listen, he was going to Cleveland. I don't know what happened. I can't wait for the story. Something happened," Simmons said.
  • • The Celtics signed guard Hayden Gray, who finished last season with Utah. Boston also added forward Gabe McGlothan to an Exhibit 10 deal after the Celtics' G League affiliate in Maine previously acquired his rights in a trade.
  • • Tyrese Haliburton explained the story behind his viral video congratulating 50 Cent on a Las Vegas residency. He texted it privately, thinking it was too funny not to send. "I went to sleep, and when I woke up, my phone was blowing up," Haliburton said. "By the time I could even tell him to take it down, it was too late."
  • • Stephen Curry revealed in a PEOPLE interview that he was dealing with serious vision problems during Golden State's championship runs. "It was 2018, and I didn't know I was really dealing with crazy blurred vision or distorted images," Curry said. "It was just the way that I saw things, and we compensated around it." The issue went undetected until then-GM Bob Myers noticed something was off while watching a road broadcast.
  • • Several training camp roster moves from around the league: the Magic signed Ricky Council IV and Jarron Cumberland; the Cavaliers added Curtis Jones, Kadari Richmond, Rashaun Agee and Zack Austin; the Trail Blazers waived Mark Armstrong and Samson Johnson and signed guard Barry Dunning to a training camp deal; the Nuggets waived Boopie Miller; the Spurs waived Jayden Nunn; the Jazz signed forward Ibrahima Diallo; and the Timberwolves added Payton Sandfort and Nate Santos.
