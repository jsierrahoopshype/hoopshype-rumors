NBA Rumors Wrap: Bucks open Giannis trade market, Wembanyama avoids suspension, Sixers face front-office reckoning

The Milwaukee Bucks are officially soliciting trade offers for two-time MVP Giannis Antetokounmpo, with the front office openly entering the market as the NBA draft combine gets underway, per Shams Charania. Co-owner Jimmy Haslam has already stated publicly that a resolution will come before the June draft, and sources confirmed nothing has changed in Antetokounmpo's belief that both sides need to move on. The Bucks are maintaining their asking price from the February deadline: a young blue-chip talent and/or a surplus of picks. Antetokounmpo is essentially on an expiring deal, with just one guaranteed season remaining before a 2027 player option, which gives him enormous leverage over where he lands. GM Jon Horst says ownership is aligned on working parallel paths, leaving the door open to a major trade that could convince Giannis to stay, while simultaneously fielding offers to move him.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • The teams that pursued Antetokounmpo at the February deadline included the Timberwolves, Cavaliers, Celtics, Knicks and Lakers, and how those franchises finish the playoffs will shape how aggressively they re-engage. The Bucks have up to three first-round picks and tradeable contracts in Bobby Portis and Kyle Kuzma as potential sweeteners in any deal. Per the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel, roughly 10 teams could realistically make a run at him, with the asking price expected to be "everything" in terms of young players and picks.
  • Victor Wembanyama will face no suspension or fine for elbowing Naz Reid in Game 4 Sunday night, and will play in Game 5 Tuesday in San Antonio. The ejection itself made history: Wembanyama was removed with 8:39 left in the second quarter, the earliest an All-Star has been ejected from a playoff game in the play-by-play era dating to 1997-98.
  • Reid, who got up immediately after the elbow and played through it, opened up about the family losses that have shaped his resilience this season. His sister Toraya was killed in a domestic violence incident before training camp. "A lot of hurt throughout my family," Reid said. "I'm trying to be me and alleviate some of that pain." He said his mother's daily hour-and-45-minute walk to work is what taught him to get back up.
  • Stephon Castle has been one of the more surprising defensive matchup stories of the Spurs-Timberwolves series. While Castle draws Anthony Edwards at times, he has actually spent more time guarding Julius Randle, a much bigger assignment. "He's very physical, it's a tough matchup," Castle said after Game 4. "He's more of a four, you know, a bigger player. So he uses his body a lot."
  • The Philadelphia 76ers' front office is facing a serious reckoning. The jobs of president of basketball operations Daryl Morey and head coach Nick Nurse are both expected to be evaluated this offseason, though ownership had not made final decisions as of Sunday's elimination. Behind the scenes, things were messy: on April 1, several players met with the coaching staff before shootaround to express frustration over the season's direction and what they described as insufficient locker room control. The meeting was tense enough that it briefly threatened to derail shootaround.
  • Joel Embiid reportedly clashed with the front office over what he called "ducking the tax" at the trade deadline, and he and others in the locker room were unhappy with the decision to trade Jared McCain to the Thunder without bringing back meaningful help. Despite all of that, ESPN's Brian Windhorst was blunt about Embiid's future: "He has 3 years, $192M left on his contract. I can promise him he will definitely be back. He is not going anywhere."
  • OG Anunoby is expected to be ready for Game 1 of the Eastern Conference Finals. "OG Anunoby will play in Game 1. I feel very comfortable saying that, just because of where this thing was in the day or two days after it happened," Ian Begley said on The Putback. Barring any setbacks, the Knicks should have him available.
  • In Cleveland, Thomas Bryant has been listed as questionable for Game 4 Monday against the Pistons with low back spasms, per HoopsHype.
  • The Cavaliers' Game 3 win over Detroit had a subplot worth noting: head coach Kenny Atkinson and referee Curtis Blair share a history that goes back decades. The two were teammates at Richmond from 1988 to 1990, forming a backcourt together for the Spiders. Atkinson said the relationship is entirely professional now. "We're not joking about Dick Tarrant," he said, referencing their old college coach. "What I'd love to do when this is all said and done is spend a week with Curtis and his family and hear about our journeys."
  • The Grizzlies will pursue a Ja Morant trade regardless of where they landed in the draft lottery, a source confirmed to ESPN's Tim MacMahon. "The lottery results will have no impact on the approach moving forward with Ja Morant," MacMahon said on YouTube. "They're going to try to find a home for Ja Morant regardless." Marc Stein reported that an agent at the draft combine floated the idea of Memphis trading down from No. 3 as part of a Morant deal, but Stein called such a scenario "almost certainly too drastic."
  • The Jazz hold the No. 2 pick and Austin Ainge says everything is on the table, including AJ Dybantsa. "We will figure out who is one and two on our board and be ready," Ainge told the Salt Lake Tribune. "There are at least two good players in this draft." He also told Marc Stein that Utah would consider trading down from the pick. "We're open," Ainge said. "We'll always listen."
  • Washington Wizards president Michael Winger told Stein directly that Washington will at least consider trading down from the No. 1 pick. Winger framed it as "not a savior moment" given the Wizards already have Trae Young, Anthony Davis and a collection of recent lottery picks on the roster.
  • Bucks co-owner Wesley Edens has been confirmed as the target of an alleged extortion scheme detailed in a new Wall Street Journal report. A woman named Changli "Sophia" Luo was indicted for allegedly attempting to shake down Edens for more than $1 billion by threatening to release videos and photos after the two had a brief relationship in 2023. Luo has pleaded not guilty on four counts including blackmail.
