NBA Rumors Wrap: Wizards land No. 1 pick, Wembanyama ejected in Game 4, Embiid's future in doubt

The Washington Wizards won the 2026 NBA Draft Lottery on Sunday night, earning the No. 1 overall pick and the right to select AJ Dybantsa, the BYU wing who is broadly expected to go first. It is the third time in eight years a team has won the lottery the same year it traded for Anthony Davis, following the Pelicans in 2019 and the Mavericks in 2025. Washington president Michael Winger told Marc Stein the Wizards will at least consider trading down from the top spot, calling this "not a savior moment" for a franchise that already has two former All-Stars and a deep crop of lottery picks. The pick at No. 2 is less settled than it appears: several team executives said they would consider alternatives to Kansas guard Darryn Peterson at No. 2, with Duke's Cam Boozer and UNC's Caleb Wilson also in the conversation. The Utah Jazz hold the second pick, and Danny Ainge said he, too, is open to trading down.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Victor Wembanyama was ejected 8:39 into the second quarter of San Antonio's 114-109 Game 4 loss to the Timberwolves, the earliest an All-Star has been tossed from a playoff game in the play-by-play era. Spurs coach Mitch Johnson called the physicality Wemby faces "disgusting" and said the league needs to do more to protect him, while stopping short of excusing the ejection itself. "At some stage he should be protected," Johnson said. "And if not, he's going to have to protect himself." Johnson also said suspending Wemby for Game 5 would be "ridiculous," given there was "zero intent."
  • • Wemby's teammate Dylan Harper backed the sentiment, saying he understood the frustration. "I don't think it was intentional, but it was more like they keep grabbing me, I'm trying to protect myself cuz no one else going to protect me." Anthony Edwards offered a more neutral take: without Wemby on the floor, the rim opened up considerably for Minnesota. "He's eight feet tall so, yeah, he gonna cover up the rim."
  • • Rudy Gobert had some postgame fun with the size advantage his team enjoyed. "I shouldn't laugh but I think size matters," Gobert told reporters after the win. When Naz Reid reacted audibly from across the locker room, Gobert didn't back down. "I'm sorry, but it's true," he doubled down. "Size matters."
  • • Edwards had another massive night in the win and has now tied Dwyane Wade for the fourth-most 30-point postseason games before age 25, trailing only LeBron James, Kobe Bryant and Kevin Durant on that list. He also became just the sixth player in the play-by-play era with 300-plus fourth-quarter postseason points before turning 25. After the game, Edwards kept it personal. "Today is Mother's Day, so I just wanted to win for my mom," he said. "I couldn't lose this game for her." His mother died in 2015.
  • • The Knicks swept the Sixers 144-114 on Sunday to advance to the Eastern Conference Finals for a second straight year. New York has joined the 2025 Thunder, 2008 Celtics and 1987 Lakers as the only teams in NBA history with multiple 30-point series-clinching wins in a single postseason. All three of those previous teams won the title.
  • • The crowd at Wells Fargo Center was notably stacked with Knicks fans, prompting some pointed postgame commentary. Josh Hart said he wasn't surprised: "I used to think Philly was a sports town, I don't know if it is anymore." The explanation, per Yaron Weitzman, is straightforward: it is significantly cheaper to buy a ticket in Philly than to get into MSG.
  • • Joel Embiid said after the loss that his knee has turned a corner following years of uncertainty. "We came into the season thinking that there was not much left based on the last two years. And now I'm sitting here not even worrying about my knee," he said, adding that complications from his appendectomy cascaded into hip and adductor issues. He was less certain about his future in Philadelphia. "I don't know who's going to be here. I don't even know if I'm going to be here," he told reporters. Asked about legacy, he kept it grounded: "I want to win more than anyone. It sucks to lose. I haven't won anything, so that hurts. But to go home and raise my son, raise my daughter, look at my wife in the eyes and understand I'm a good man. Really, that's all that matters."
  • • Kelly Oubre Jr. said he hopes a more efficient, measured season earns him a better market in free agency. "I've averaged 20 points in this league and still find myself barely getting any contracts," he said, adding that he loves it in Philly. Meanwhile, Tyrese Maxey said his offseason plan involves a more immediate concern: he is about to become a father for the first time.
  • • Rookie VJ Edgecombe came away from his first playoff run with a clear target. "Coming into next year a team won't ever leave me open," he said, acknowledging that playoff closeouts arrived at a different speed than what he saw in the regular season.
  • • Memphis holds the No. 3 pick after Sunday's lottery, and one agent in Chicago floated the idea of the Grizzlies trading down as part of a Ja Morant deal. Marc Stein called that scenario "almost certainly too drastic," pointing to president Zach Kleiman's history of trading up rather than down in the draft.
  • • The Bucks' Jon Horst spoke briefly after the lottery, confirming that both options on Giannis Antetokounmpo remain open. "The two paths of trading/keeping Giannis are obviously both still on the table," Horst said, adding he was relieved the team did not fall further in the lottery.
  • • Masai Ujiri spoke glowingly about Cooper Flagg now that the Mavericks own the top pick. "We've got a guy who makes it easy for us," Ujiri said. "His name is Cooper Flagg. Wherever you go around the world, that's the gem you're looking for." New GM Mike Schmitz, just three days into the job, called the moment "chaotic in a beautiful way."
  • • LaMelo Ball and partner Ana Montana welcomed a son, LaOne, in January and are now publicly sharing the news alongside the launch of I Am Fertility, a nonprofit supporting women through the fertility and IVF process.
