NBA Rumors Wrap: Clippers' Kawhi Leonard trade intact despite draft penalties, Sixers' swap rights preserved, Ben Simmons open to long-term Sacramento stay

The Los Angeles Clippers' trade sending Kawhi Leonard to the Toronto Raptors is expected to proceed despite the league's sweeping punishment of the franchise, per John Hollinger of The Athletic. The Raptors had initially slowed the deal over concerns that Leonard might face a lengthy suspension or have his contract voided as part of the NBA's investigation, but those fears have been put to rest. Toronto has no realistic Plan B for the 2026-27 season if the deal falls apart, and the rest of the offseason is effectively over, leaving both sides with strong incentive to close it. The Clippers, with their owner suspended for a year and their front office in flux, still need to identify someone authorized to finalize the agreement. The league's penalties included five forfeited first-round draft picks spanning 2029 through 2033, but those are distinct from picks already owed to other teams or previously traded away.

The Philadelphia 76ers also got clarity on what the punishment means for them. The pick the NBA stripped from the Clippers in 2029 is one owed to Los Angeles by the Indiana Pacers from the Ivica Zubac trade, not the Clippers' own 2029 first-rounder. That distinction matters because the Sixers hold a swap right on that Clippers pick from the 2023 James Harden deal, and a league source confirmed it remains completely intact.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • • Draymond Green said he was relieved Leonard avoided a personal suspension as part of the NBA's ruling. "I'm happy the team got the bulk of the penalty and not Kawhi," Green said, before using the moment to call for a broader re-examination of why the salary cap restricts players from earning money through team or league sponsors.
  • • The fallout for the Clippers' front office may not be over. Pablo Torre, appearing on a YouTube program, said he does not expect Gillian Zucker or Lawrence Frank to keep their jobs, adding that owner Steve Ballmer may feel personally betrayed by either or both of them. Kenny Smith told TMZ he had no issue with the severity of the league's punishment: "You're vehemently cheating. You're not just toeing the line. You're actually cheating and you're cheating at a high level," Smith said.
  • • Ben Simmons is open to staying in Sacramento long-term, according to a report from Marc Stein. A source close to the situation said Simmons appreciates the smaller market environment: "You go to Sacramento, it's about basketball," the source said. "I'd put their fans up there with anyone in the world."
  • • Collin Sexton explained why he chose to sign with the Lakers this offseason. "I would say just opportunity to win, being on big stage and being in the situation where I can try to come in and impact winning," Sexton told ESPN LA. Former player Matt Barnes had a more colorful take on the Lakers' rebuilt roster, saying in a TMZ Sports video that it is the "whitest Laker team since the Minneapolis Lakers." Barnes followed that up by praising the team's core, calling Luka Dončić and Austin Reaves "two of the best scoring guards in the league."
  • • The sale of the Lakers to former Disney CEO Bob Iger and venture capitalist Joshua Kushner was not something outgoing owner Mark Walter had planned. Lakers president Stan Kasten acknowledged that the decision to sell, coming just 14 months after Walter agreed to buy the team, "was not a planned thing." Walter suffered a stroke during the Dodgers' 2024 World Series run and struggled to speak clearly for months before returning to public view.
  • • Glen Davis spoke at length about his time in prison and the insurance fraud investigation that sent him there while others named in the same case did not serve time. "They literally wanted to make me out to be an example," Davis said. "Kendrick Perkins, you see him on ESPN. Tony Allen — he was literally the third person on the indictment, with his wife. He's not walking in jail." Davis named several other former players, including Gilbert Arenas and Matt Barnes, as having been investigated but not imprisoned. In a separate interview, Davis also reflected on his time in solitary confinement: "Nothing but time to reflect, like nothing but time every single day thinking about all the mistakes and everything that I've done."
  • • Julius Randle was asked whether he considers himself a champion after the Knicks won the title. "Ah no I appreciate that. What the organization accomplished is great. Been a long time coming," Randle said, before saying he looks forward to "creating that in Brooklyn." Derrick Rose, speaking to Sports Illustrated, said he had the Knicks winning the whole time and called Rick Brunson before the Finals to tell him New York was going to beat Wembanyama if they got there.
  • • A federal appeals court ruled that Bonneville International was within its rights to fire longtime Kings play-by-play voice Grant Napear after he responded to DeMarcus Cousins with an "All Lives Matter" post during the 2020 protests following George Floyd's death. Napear had spent 31 years calling Kings games before the tweet ended his career there.
  • • A second group has entered the public conversation around Seattle NBA expansion, with BlackSun Private Equity claiming it could raise $8.1 billion for a bid. The proposed arena site on Tulalip tribal land near Marysville drew skepticism, with rush-hour commutes from downtown Seattle to the location routinely exceeding an hour.
  • • Jared Sullinger announced his retirement at age 34, closing out a career that began with the Celtics as a first-round pick. He is returning to Ohio State as Director of Player Development. "I'm hanging up the jersey, but I'm not walking away from the game. I'm just changing seats. Forever a Buckeye," Sullinger wrote.
  • • Peyton Watson's departure from Denver drew a candid response from Nuggets assistant coach Ognjen Stojakovic. "Of course, losing someone like that, it's not easy," Stojakovic said. "We can be sad about that or we can be like, okay, it is what it is and we're going to do next step."
  • • Derik Queen said one of his goals heading into his first NBA season with the Pelicans is to become a better three-point shooter. Miles McBride reflected on former Knicks coach Tom Thibodeau, saying simply: "Thibs did a great job, he built a great foundation."
  • • LeBron James has signed an endorsement deal with prediction market platform Polymarket, with a focus on football content. A source told Front Office Sports the announcement post was the first step in a larger campaign set to launch early next week. The deal is similar in scope to his DraftKings arrangement, first signed in 2024.
