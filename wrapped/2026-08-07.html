NBA Rumors Wrap: Anthony Davis and Wizards table extension talks, Sixers' Brown opens up on Boston exit, Nuggets in Watson sign-and-trade talks

Anthony Davis and the Washington Wizards have agreed to table contract extension talks until at least 20 games into the 2026-27 season, per Shams Charania. The decision gives Davis time to evaluate the organization, gauge Washington's competitiveness and assess the front office's ability to add talent, while the Wizards get to see how Davis performs and how the roster develops before committing to a long-term deal. Davis holds a $62.8 million player option for the 2027-28 season, and if he declines it, a maximum extension would be worth $275 million over four years through the 2030-31 season, when Davis will be 37. The Athletic reported that any meaningful talks were always expected to happen during the season, making today's extension eligibility date less significant than it appeared.

Here are more notes from around the league:

  • • Jaylen Brown opened up on his departure from Boston, saying he threw his phone across the room when he found out he had been traded to Philadelphia. Tyrese Maxey was the first to reach out, followed quickly by VJ Edgecombe. "It has to be based in respect and work," Brown said of fitting in with a roster that includes LeBron James, Joel Embiid and Maxey. "It doesn't have to be anybody's team."
  • • Brown said he and Jayson Tatum have "not really" spoken since the trade, though he said it is "nothing but respect" on his end and he wishes Tatum well except when Boston plays Philadelphia. Brown also said he may one day share the full details of how the trade went down, but for now his focus is on what is ahead.
  • • 76ers GM Mike Gansey said that trade talks for Brown accelerated after the Dean Wade signing and that even he did not expect it to come together. "We kept at it. Boston kept calling, we kept calling. Credit Brad. We kept this thing quiet," Gansey said, adding that he believes Embiid will be "as important" to the team's success as LeBron and Brown.
  • • Embiid himself is having his healthiest offseason in years. "This is like the first summer I think in a while that he's been healthy," Gansey said. "He's been in the gym working out. He's been lifting weights. He's extra motivated." As for games played, Gansey said the team will map out a plan with the coaching and performance staffs but that the players themselves want to play every game.
  • • Embiid reached out to Brown the day the trade was announced. The two have been friendly over the years, Brown said, and he is looking forward to "earning everyone's trust and respect and grinding." Multiple Western Conference executives told ESPN they expect Brown's defensive versatility to translate well against the Knicks.
  • • The Nuggets are in active sign-and-trade talks for Peyton Watson with the Cavaliers, Bucks and Clippers. If no deal materializes, Watson could return to Denver on a one-year, $6.5 million qualifying offer that would make him an unrestricted free agent next summer. The Bucks are interested but are reluctant to include a first-round pick as they rebuild their asset base following the Giannis Antetokounmpo era.
  • • Lonnie Walker IV has signed a training camp deal with Denver, competing for one of two open roster spots this fall. His exit from Maccabi Tel Aviv required special dispensation since the club's NBA opt-out deadline was July 15 and Denver's offer did not surface until August.
  • • Karl-Anthony Towns is eligible for a four-year, $272 million max extension from the Knicks. The two sides made no progress on an extension last offseason. Towns will earn just over $57 million this season with a player option worth just over $61 million the year after, and while New York's decision-makers are expected to offer an extension, a max deal from the cash-conscious Knicks is far from certain.
  • • Victor Wembanyama has been working with Hakeem Olajuwon again this offseason, continuing the post-game development they began last summer. Pau Gasol, speaking on a separate podcast, said Wemby is most decisive inside the paint, where his wingspan disrupts the game in ways his outside shooting cannot replicate. Wembanyama also returned to Texas for workouts with teammates after signing his extension in France.
  • • Wolves coach Chris Finch explained the rationale behind bringing in LaMelo Ball: Anthony Edwards was getting doubled relentlessly in the playoffs and needs to operate more off the ball. Finch cited that Kon Knueppel shot 7 percent better from three with LaMelo on the floor than off it. The Wolves have also expressed interest in Jonathan Kuminga, but with only a minimum offer available, it is hard to see how they get involved if Kuminga is seeking north of $20 million per year.
  • • Heat guard Davion Mitchell has been working out with Giannis Antetokounmpo in Greece this offseason, saying the sessions were "amazing" and that Giannis "just wants to win." Former Bucks teammate Malcolm Brogdon said Giannis fits Miami's profile on both ends but that the relationship between Giannis and Pat Riley will require a balancing act, since Riley does not typically allow players to run the show.
  • • Multnomah County voted 4-1 to approve up to $101.6 million toward renovating Moda Center, kicking off formal negotiations between the county, city of Portland and the Blazers. Portland's city council votes on its $120 million share next Wednesday. The Blazers are seeking at least $573 million total from local and state sources. County chair Jessica Vega Pederson said she wanted the vote to signal to the NBA just how serious the region is about keeping the team.
  • • Stephen Curry, Draymond Green and coach Steve Kerr have come to terms with the fact that the Warriors are not championship contenders with their current roster. The priority for all three is going out together with dignity rather than mortgaging the franchise's future. Golden State could have matched or exceeded Philadelphia's offer for Jaylen Brown, but talks with Boston never advanced past preliminary discussions.
  • • The Lakers have interest in Dallas forward PJ Washington, and Luka Doncic is reportedly in favor of a reunion. The obstacle is Washington's four-year, $89 million extension, which begins at $20 million this season. A Western Conference executive told Heavy.com the Lakers do not want that contract, saying Dallas paid too much and Washington was not as productive last season.
  • • Three-time dunk champion Mac McClung has signed with Basket Girona in the Spanish ACB for his first foray into European basketball. The club's founder and president is former NBA All-Star Marc Gasol.
  • • Nikola Jokic is on Serbia's preliminary roster for the FIBA World Cup qualifying windows in August. Bogdan Bogdanovic, despite earlier expectations, remains unavailable for this window.
