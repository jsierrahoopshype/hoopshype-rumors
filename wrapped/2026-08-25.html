NBA Rumors Wrap: Shaedon Sharpe tears meniscus, Klay Thompson details in Miami, Kuminga Lakers talks resume

Shaedon Sharpe's 2026-27 season is in serious jeopardy before it even begins. The Portland Trail Blazers guard sustained a torn meniscus in his knee during an offseason workout and is expected to miss approximately six months, per Shams Charania. The injury is a significant blow to a young Blazers team already in rebuild mode, and Sharpe's extended absence will push his return deep into the regular season at best. Portland will need to lean on other pieces while he recovers, and his timeline will be one of the most closely watched injury situations as the season approaches.

Here are more notes from around the league:
  • The Lakers and Hawks have revived sign-and-trade talks around Jonathan Kuminga, with guard Jaden Hardy now in the conversation at $6 million. Earlier discussions centered on Jared Vanderbilt at $12.4 million, but those stalled because Atlanta, sitting only $3.8 million below the luxury tax, won't add salary without a quality draft pick attached, and Los Angeles wouldn't go there. Atlanta also enters training camp with 17 players under contract, which adds another layer of complexity to any deal.
  • Klay Thompson will hold his introductory press conference with the Heat on Tuesday at 3 p.m. EST. His two-year deal starts at $5.6 million with a player option of $5.88 million in year two, and Miami still has room to sign two players to minimum contracts. Thompson will wear No. 11, last worn by Jaime Jaquez Jr. Pat Riley called him "one of the most iconic players to ever suit up in the NBA" and said the Heat are "blessed that Klay chose" them.
  • The Sacramento Kings are honoring Hall of Fame coach Rick Adelman with a commemorative black band on the upper-left shoulder of their jerseys throughout the 2026-27 season. The Trail Blazers will do the same, and will host a dedicated tribute game on Dec. 15 against the Nuggets, whose head coach David Adelman is Rick's son.
  • Enes Kanter Freedom was ejected from a WNBA game after a courtside confrontation with Chicago Sky guard Natasha Cloud. Freedom said the incident started when he raised his hands to celebrate a Sophie Cunningham three-pointer and Cloud approached him, shouting that Cunningham "isn't going to sleep with" him. Security escorted Freedom out as he approached the court. Sky coach Stephanie Marsh said Cloud was worried about being a distraction, but told her: "We all embrace her as someone that protects our team and protects people that need to be protected."
  • Cloud did not speak at the postgame press conference, but posted on Threads afterward: "To every trans kid/person, You are apart of MY community & tribe. You belong. You're loved. You're seen. You're perfect as you are. & you will always be protected by me." Freedom, for his part, said the episode made him more motivated to enter the WNBA draft: "I cannot wait to go against Natasha now."
  • The WNBA has declared Freedom and Royce White ineligible to play in the league, with league sources calling their draft declarations "simply publicity stunts" and saying the league will "not allow outside pressure or manufactured controversy to dictate league processes." Freedom has said he is communicating with two former NBA players, three European players and three G League players who are privately considering similar declarations.
  • A former Kings assistant athletic trainer has filed a lawsuit against the organization and its senior director of athlete health, Jaspreet Randhawa, alleging pregnancy discrimination, sexual harassment, retaliation and wrongful termination. The suit alleges Randhawa told her "You're f---ed" when she revealed her pregnancy and that the team removed her from road games during her second trimester before demoting her in April 2023.
  • The full autopsy report on former Grizzlies forward Brandon Clarke, who died of an accidental heroin and cocaine overdose on May 11, reveals he had a history of drug use that included prior stints in rehabilitation. Toxicology testing also detected oxycodone, anti-anxiety medication, muscle relaxers and mitragynine within therapeutic levels. Clarke was 29. His agent had been unable to reach him and asked his roommate to check on him; the roommate had assumed Clarke was napping or at basketball practice.
  • VJ Edgecombe is heading into his second season with the Sixers alongside LeBron James and Jaylen Brown, and says any concern about a reduced role isn't keeping him up at night. "It's fine if they don't respect me, I'll just get a bunch of open shots," he said at camp. He also admitted he'll need to pace himself around James: "I'll have a bunch of questions, so I'm going to have to control myself when it comes to asking questions."
  • Zach LaVine opened up in a wide-ranging Q&A with HoopsHype about the lessons his career has taught him. "The careers are short, and it's hard to make the NBA," he said. "It's even harder to stay, and it's even harder to be successful." He credited veterans including Kevin Garnett, Mo Williams and Thaddeus Young with instilling a relentless mindset early in his career in Minnesota.
  • Spurs star Stephon Castle's trainer says the second-year guard has spent the offseason focused on his shot, with the goal of syncing his shooting mechanics to account for defensive fatigue. "He's such a high-volume guy defensively that sometimes his sequence in his shooting patterns is off," the trainer told R.Org Sports. Castle also noted in a separate interview that if he could play in any NBA era, he'd pick the 1990s: "I just think the game is so different now."
  • Nets two-way player Chaney Johnson says Brooklyn is hungry to improve after a 20-win season, and pointed to second-year guard Nolan Traore as the player who has grown the most this offseason. "He's a different guy now," Johnson said. "He done put on some mass. He's walking around with a sleeveless hoodie on, looking at the biceps." Traore missed Summer League recovering from a knee scope but has been active in the gym.
  • Horace Grant raved about Bulls rookie Caleb Wilson in an exclusive interview with the Action Network, calling him "a freakin' beast" after Wilson's strong Summer League showing following the No. 4 overall pick. Grant also offered an endorsement of new Bulls head coach Tiago Splitter, whose previous stop was in Portland, where he coached Grant's nephew Jerami Grant: "The Bulls are going to have a very young core group of guys, so I think he's the perfect guy for the job."
  • The Bulls also hired Quincy Pondexter as head coach of their Windy City G League affiliate. Pondexter played for Chicago in 2017-18, later coached at Washington and USC, and is the nephew of former Bull Cliff Pondexter. He also grew up playing high school basketball alongside Robin and Brook Lopez.
  • veteran forward DeWanna Bonner is a free agent after reaching a buyout with the Mercury with fewer than 10 games left before the WNBA postseason. The six-time All-Star ranks second all-time in games played, second in free throws made and third in scoring in league history.
  • Former NBA guard TyTy Washington has signed with ASVEL Villeurbanne in France, beginning his first European chapter and making his EuroLeague debut at 24.
